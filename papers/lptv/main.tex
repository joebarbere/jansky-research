\begin{abstract}
Long-period radio transients (LPTs) --- sources pulsing on minute-to-hour periods
\citep{hurleywalker2022,caleb2024} --- are a young class whose progenitors span neutron stars and
white-dwarf binaries \citep{rea2026}. We extend a provenance-carrying LPT catalogue to \lvNLpt\
confirmed members with the three 2026 discoveries \citep{deruiter2026,vaster2026}, each coordinate
verified against its source-name convention. At \lvNLpt\ members the hinted $\sim$78-minute period
boundary between white-dwarf-binary and other LPTs is \textbf{still not significant} (permutation
$p = \lvPeriodSplitP$). We then perform what has not been done before: systematic multi-epoch forced
Stokes-V (circular-polarization) photometry at every LPT position across the RACS-low1, RACS-low2,
and RACS-mid epochs \citep{mcconnell2020,racslow2}. Per-source polarization is known for a handful of
LPTs and RACS-low2 published a blind V catalogue that did not target them, but no uniform V survey of
the class exists. The tested forced-photometry pipeline (a synthetic circularly-polarized injection
is recovered; a blank field yields an honest limit; the photometry re-measured 2026-08-14 with
the position genuinely locked, after review showed the original was a 12\arcsec\ peak search).
Across the \lvRealNMeasured\ RACS-covered LPTs we find \emph{three} single-epoch
circularly-polarized detections at the catalogue positions: ASKAP J1745$-$5051 ($I\approx22$~mJy,
$15\%$ circular, $21.6\sigma$, consistent with its magnetic-CV nature); ASKAP
J165130.3$-$450520 ($67\%$ circular at $11.9\sigma$ --- formerly a positional candidate, now
at 0.85\arcsec); and ASKAP J183950.5$-$075635, a single-night $I=164$~mJy, $40\%$-circular
pulse (MJD 60335) formerly mis-vetoed as confusion --- there is no catalogued neighbour
within 2\arcmin, and the survey's own blind V catalogue (a different, off-state epoch) is
empty there. All other epochs and sources are limits (median best-epoch $3\sigma$ limit
\lvRealMedianVLimit\,mJy; per-epoch median 0.73~mJy). All three signals are single-epoch
burst states. The non-detections are limits on circularly polarized \emph{flux}, not on
fractional polarization --- the undetected sources are undetected in Stokes~I too, so the
implied $|V|/I$ limits are 49--114\% and even fully circular emission is not excluded for
most of the sample. RACS snapshots catch LPT pulses with their circular polarization intact;
the uniform V-limit table, three burst detections, and the reusable vetted machinery are the
deliverable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Since GLEAM-X J1627 \citep{hurleywalker2022} the long-period radio transients have grown from a
curiosity (GCRT J1745$-$3009; \citealt{hyman2005}) into a class of $\sim$15 members with periods
from minutes to hours, some now tied to white-dwarf binaries and others still consistent with
neutron stars \citep{rea2026}. Two questions follow the growing sample. First, does the
period distribution separate the white-dwarf-binary members from the rest --- a hinted
$\sim$78-minute boundary? Second, do the sources have a persistent, circularly-polarized radio
counterpart? Circular polarization is the cleanest coherent-emission diagnostic, and a steady
component (versus purely bursty emission) would discriminate models; but the counterpart
cross-matches published so far are Stokes-I only, and the one wide-field Stokes-V catalogue
\citep{racslow2} was blind, not targeted. We address both: a verified v3 catalogue, and the first
systematic multi-epoch forced-V photometry at every LPT position.

\section{The v3 catalogue}
\label{sec:cat}
We add the three 2026 discoveries to the vendored, per-value-provenanced table, transcribing each
value from its discovery paper and checking the RA/Dec against the source-name sexagesimal (all
agree to $<$1\arcsec). The catalogue now holds \lvNLpt\ confirmed LPTs (median period
\lvMedianPeriodMin\,min), \lvNWdBinary\ of them white-dwarf binaries or candidates. Re-running the
period-split test --- a 20{,}000-draw permutation test on the white-dwarf-binary-versus-rest median
$\log$-period difference (a normal-approximation rank test is unreliable at this $N$) --- gives
$p = \lvPeriodSplitP$: the hinted $\sim$78-min boundary is not significant at \lvNLpt\ members.
Notably the $p$-value \emph{rose} from $\approx0.27$ at the previous $N{=}13$ to
$\lvPeriodSplitP$ --- the extra data moved the split \emph{away} from significance, because the
two long-period VASTER additions (6.5 and 4.7 hr) have binary status not yet reported and so
enter the ``rest'' group at long periods, diluting the binaries-sit-longer trend. The 36-min
ASKAP J1424 does not sharpen it either; we report the non-detection of the boundary rather than
over-interpret a small sample.

\section{Stokes-V forced photometry}
\label{sec:v}
For each LPT we query the CASDA ObsCore for every RACS restored image containing the position with
both a Stokes-I and a Stokes-V product, stage a SODA cutout of each, and run forced $I{+}V$
photometry at the catalogue position (the tested \code{measure\_circular\_pol}, locking the
measurement so a non-detection becomes an honest limit). A V detection must clear both $5\sigma$
and a $0.6\%$-of-$I$ instrumental-leakage veto (the characteristic ASKAP on-axis leakage level).
A second, \emph{confusion} veto guards against the forced-photometry peak locking onto a nearby
bright source: a detection is flagged suspect when the peak is both off-centre ($>$4\arcsec) and a
gross Stokes-I outlier ($>$10$\times$ the source's median across its own epochs) --- the signature
of a confusing source rather than the LPT. Upper limits are the deepest $3\sigma$ across a
source's epochs, and we flag any inter-epoch handedness (LCP$\leftrightarrow$RCP) change among
detections. Validation: offline, a synthetic RACS-like cutout with an injected $|V|/I = 0.6$
source is recovered (class \lvInjClass) and a blank field yields no false detection; the same
forced-photometry code recovers known circular emitters in the parent \code{stokesv} slice.

\section{Results and Conclusions}
Across the \lvRealNMeasured\ RACS-covered LPTs (Table~\ref{tab:vtable}), one source yields a
secure circularly-polarized detection and one a candidate, each in a \emph{single} epoch, with
the remaining epochs of both and all other targets yielding limits (median $3\sigma$ Stokes-V
limit \lvRealMedianVLimit\,mJy; \lvRealNFlips\ handedness changes among detections). ASKAP
J1745$-$5051, the accreting-white-dwarf LPT, is the \textbf{secure} case: bright ($I\approx22$~mJy),
on-centre ($0.7\arcsec$), at $|V|/I\approx15\%$ with $21.6\sigma$ in $V$ --- $25\times$ above the
$0.6\%$ on-axis leakage floor and above even a pessimistic several-per-cent off-axis leakage, and
leakage would not switch on for a single epoch the way this burst does; the $15\%$ fraction is
consistent with its magnetic-CV nature. ASKAP J165130.3$-$450520, one of the two 2026 VASTER
additions, \textbf{strengthens under forced photometry}: with the measurement locked to the
catalogue position (offset 0.85\arcsec, against 3.2\arcsec\ for the earlier peak search) it is
$67\%$ circular at $11.9\sigma$ in the single epoch where Stokes~I rises to $3.6$~mJy. The
positional ambiguity that made it a candidate is resolved; we report it as a detection.

The third source is the most instructive. Under the earlier peak search, ASKAP
J183950.5$-$075635's bright epoch ($240$~mJy at a $5.3\arcsec$ offset) was vetoed as ``a
nearby confusing source''. That veto was wrong twice over. First, $5.3\arcsec$ is a fifth of
the RACS-low beam --- not a separable neighbour. Second, and decisively: forced photometry at
the catalogue position reads $I=164$~mJy, $V=+65$~mJy ($|V|/I = 40\%$, $199\sigma$) in that
single epoch (MJD 60335), with all nine other epochs consistent with zero --- including the
following night --- and the survey's own blind leakage-corrected V catalogue contains no
source within $60\arcsec$ (it derives from a different epoch, when the source was off), and
no I-catalogue source brighter than 4~mJy within 2\arcmin\ (so there is no neighbour to
confuse with). A single bright, strongly circularly polarized, night-scale transient at the
position of this source is not a contaminant: it is what this source \emph{does} --- its
published pulses are of exactly this brightness, duration and polarization. We therefore
report it as a single-epoch pulse recovery in survey imaging, caught in the $\sim$39\% of
its 6.4-h period that our epochs sample. The
picture is therefore three detections and thirteen sets of limits --- and the negative half
must be stated for what it is. The non-detections are limits on circularly polarized
\emph{flux} ($\sim$0.5~mJy best-epoch, 0.73~mJy per-epoch median), not on fractional
polarization: the undetected sources are undetected in Stokes~I too (median $I/\sigma_I =
1.9$ per epoch), so the implied $|V|/I$ limits are 49--114\% and the census does not exclude
even fully circular emission for most of the sample. CHIME J1634+44 --- published at
$\sim$100\% circular polarization, the class's natural archetype --- is among the
non-detections at $<$0.41~mJy: a flux non-recovery, not a polarization statement. What the
census does establish is that RACS snapshots \emph{can} catch LPT pulses, three times over,
with the burst-state circular polarization intact --- the coherent-emission diagnostic in
survey imaging. Because RACS integrates $\sim$15 min per field, a source active for a small fraction of its
period is diluted by $\sim T/w$ even when strongly polarized in burst, and a limit on the
snapshot mean is $T/w$ times weaker on the pulse flux. Phase coverage varies enormously
across this sample: the short-period sources ($P<20$~min) are sampled many times over, while
the four longest-period sources have 27--43\% single-visit phase coverage --- for them a
non-detection is the \emph{expected} outcome of an off-state and constrains little. The
J183950 recovery is the existence proof: ten epochs, one pulse. Two follow-ons are immediate through the
same pipeline: the deeper, wider RACS-low2 V epochs as they are released \citep{racslow2}, and
phase-resolved polarimetry of the burst windows which snapshot imaging cannot reach. The v3
catalogue, the two serendipitous burst detections, and the reusable vetted V machinery are the
contribution.
