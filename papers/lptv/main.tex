\title{A v3 Long-Period-Transient Catalogue and the First Systematic Multi-Epoch
Stokes-V Forced Photometry at All LPT Positions}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Long-period radio transients (LPTs) are sources pulsing on minute-to-hour periods
\citep{hurleywalker2022,caleb2024}, a young class whose proposed progenitors span neutron
stars and white-dwarf binaries \citep{rea2026}. We extend a provenance-carrying LPT
catalogue to \lvNLpt\ confirmed members with the three 2026 discoveries
\citep{pritchard2026,vaster2026}, each coordinate verified against its source-name
convention. At \lvNLpt\ members the hinted $\sim$78-minute period boundary between the
white-dwarf-binary members and the rest remains not significant (permutation
$p = \lvPeriodSplitP$). We also present the first targeted, multi-epoch, uniform forced
Stokes-V (circular-polarization) photometry at every LPT position, using the RACS
\citep{mcconnell2020} epochs that cover each source: RACS-low2 \citep{racslow2}, RACS-mid,
and the 2023--24 low-band pass; RACS-low2's own V catalogue is blind and did not target
the class, and per-source polarization is otherwise known for only a handful of LPTs. The
forced-photometry pipeline recovers a synthetic circularly-polarized injection and
yields an honest limit on a blank field, with the source position locked rather than
peak-searched.

Across the \lvRealNMeasured\ RACS-covered LPTs we find three single-epoch
circularly-polarized detections at the catalogue positions, and adjudicating each against
its discovery paper shows all three are independent re-measurements of already published detection
epochs, not new pulses: ASKAP J1745$-$5051 in SBID 20398, the RACS-mid epoch its
discovery search ran on ($I=21.6$~mJy vs the published $22.9\pm2.3$; $15\%$ circular,
$21.6\sigma$); ASKAP J165130.3$-$450520 in the one archival epoch its discovery paper
reports (3.6~mJy, $67\%$ circular at $11.9\sigma$ vs their $\sim$4~mJy, $\sim$60\%); and
ASKAP J183950.5$-$075635 in its discovery observation itself ($40\%$ circular vs the
published 37\%), the latter formerly mis-vetoed as confusion by the earlier peak search.
The RACS census therefore contains zero new detections; the recoveries validate the
locked photometry end to end on real LPT pulses spanning 3.6--164~mJy. All other epochs
and sources are limits (median best-epoch $3\sigma$ limit \lvRealMedianVLimit\,mJy;
per-epoch median 0.73~mJy). All three detections are single-epoch burst states. These non-detections are limits on
circularly polarized flux, not on fractional polarization: at every non-detection position
Stokes~I is itself consistent with zero (median forced $I/\sigma_I = -0.14$ per epoch), so
the census places no constraint on $|V|/I$ for any undetected source.

Extending the sweep to the VAST survey's fortnightly 12-min epochs (966 epochs across
the 10 VAST-covered LPTs, 2022--2026, with full-precision epoch times) yields four further
V detections: two are again published epochs (ASKAP J1832$-$0911 in its observing
campaign; ASKAP J1755$-$2527 in its discovery observation), and two are pulses of ASKAP
J183950.5$-$075635 absent from its discovery paper's observation table, in the gap
between that campaign's 2024 March and June pointings. On the published ephemeris one
falls at pulse phase $0.489 \pm 0.016$, in the interpulse window, with a flux ratio to
the other pulse (18\%) inside the published interpulse band, though its circular fraction
($18 \pm 3\%$) sits above the published interpulse range ($\lesssim$10\%): a candidate
interpulse, the first at interpulse phase in ASKAP imaging to our knowledge. The other,
main-pulse-like (36\% circular, inside the published 30--60\% band), falls at
$0.951 \pm 0.017$. A search of the works citing the discovery paper finds no report of
either epoch. RACS and VAST snapshots catch LPT pulses with their circular polarization
intact; the uniform V-limit table, the validated recoveries, the two new J1839$-$0756
pulses, and the reusable vetted machinery are the products of this survey.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Since GLEAM-X J1627 \citep{hurleywalker2022} the long-period radio transients have grown from a
curiosity (GCRT J1745$-$3009; \citealt{hyman2005}) into a class of $\sim$15 members with periods
from minutes to hours, some now tied to white-dwarf binaries and others still consistent with
neutron stars \citep{rea2026}. Two questions follow the growing sample. First, does the
period distribution separate the white-dwarf-binary members from the rest, at a hinted
$\sim$78-minute boundary? Second, do the sources have a persistent, circularly-polarized radio
counterpart? Circular polarization is the cleanest coherent-emission diagnostic, and a steady
component (versus purely bursty emission) would discriminate models; but the counterpart
cross-matches published so far are Stokes-I only, and the one wide-field Stokes-V catalogue
\citep{racslow2} was blind, not targeted. We address both: a verified v3 catalogue, and the first
systematic multi-epoch forced-V photometry at every LPT position.

\section{The v3 catalogue}
\label{sec:cat}
We add the three 2026 discoveries to the vendored, per-value-provenanced table, transcribing each
value from its discovery paper and checking the RA/Dec against the source-name sexagesimal (the
three additions agree to $<$1\arcsec). The same check applied to the inherited rows caught one
error, in the name rather than the coordinate: the row for ASKAP J1832$-$0911 carried an
erroneous full-precision designation 59\arcsec\ from its coordinate, while the coordinate itself
matches the published VLBA position \citep{wang2025} to 2\arcsec; v3 corrects the name.
Thirteen of the sixteen rows store positions at 3-decimal-degree precision (a 3.6\arcsec\
quantum), which the forced photometry below inherits as a positional systematic. The catalogue now holds \lvNLpt\ confirmed LPTs (median period
\lvMedianPeriodMin\,min), \lvNWdBinary\ of them white-dwarf binaries or candidates. Re-running the
period-split test (a 20{,}000-draw permutation test on the white-dwarf-binary-versus-rest median
$\log$-period difference; a normal-approximation rank test is unreliable at this $N$) gives
$p = \lvPeriodSplitP$: the hinted $\sim$78-min boundary is not significant at \lvNLpt\ members.
The $p$-value rose from $\approx0.27$ at the previous $N{=}13$ to
$\lvPeriodSplitP$; the extra data moved the split away from significance because the
two long-period VASTER additions (6.5 and 4.7 hr) have binary status not yet reported and so
enter the ``rest'' group at long periods, diluting the binaries-sit-longer trend. The 36-min
ASKAP J1424 does not sharpen it either; we report the non-detection of the boundary rather than
over-interpret a small sample.

\section{Stokes-V forced photometry}
\label{sec:v}
For each LPT we query the CASDA ObsCore for every RACS restored image containing the position with
both a Stokes-I and a Stokes-V product, stage a SODA cutout of each, and run forced $I{+}V$
photometry at the catalogue position (the tested \code{measure\_circular\_pol}, locking the
measurement so a non-detection becomes an honest limit). A V detection must clear both $5\sigma$
and a $0.6\%$-of-$I$ instrumental-leakage veto (the characteristic ASKAP on-axis leakage level;
\citealt{racslow2}). The pipeline retains a confusion veto from its earlier peak-search
configuration (suspect when the measured peak is $>$4\arcsec\ off-centre and a
$>$10$\times$ Stokes-I outlier); under locked-position photometry the offset is pixel-rounding
arithmetic bounded by half a pixel diagonal, so the off-centre clause can never fire and the veto
is superseded: the failure mode it guarded against (the peak wandering to a neighbour) no
longer exists. Association is instead assessed directly, by the flux recovered at the locked
catalogue pixel. Upper limits are the deepest (best-epoch) $3\sigma$ across a source's epochs.
The photometry was re-measured on 2026-08-14 with the position locked at the catalogue
coordinate, after review found the original configuration to be a 12\arcsec\ peak search.
Validation: offline, a synthetic RACS-like cutout with an injected $|V|/I = 0.6$ source
(2$\times$ the classifier boundary) is recovered (class \lvInjClass) and a single blank field
yields no false detection; this is a smoke test of the chain, not a calibration of the decision
boundaries. The parent \code{stokesv} slice recovers known circular emitters with the same
photometry code, though under its default 12\arcsec\ peak-search mode rather than the locked
mode used here.

\section{Results and Conclusions}
Across the \lvRealNMeasured\ RACS-covered LPTs (Table~\ref{tab:vtable}), three sources yield
circularly-polarized detections, each in a single epoch, with the remaining epochs of all
three and every other target yielding limits (median best-epoch $3\sigma$ Stokes-V limit
\lvRealMedianVLimit\,mJy). ASKAP
J1745$-$5051, the accreting-white-dwarf LPT, is the cleanest measurement: bright
($I=21.6$~mJy), at $|V|/I\approx15\%$ with $21.6\sigma$ in $V$, $25\times$ above the
$0.6\%$ on-axis leakage floor, and leakage would not switch on for a single epoch the way
this burst does; the $15\%$ fraction is consistent with its magnetic-CV nature. It is also
not serendipitous: the detection epoch is SBID 20398, the RACS-mid observation on which the
discovery paper's untargeted circular-polarization search ran, and that paper's own table
reports the source there at $22.9\pm2.3$~mJy \citep{rose2026}, consistent with our
independent forced measurement. ASKAP J165130.3$-$450520, one of the two 2026 VASTER
additions, strengthens under forced photometry: the measurement locked to the catalogue
pixel recovers $I=3.6$, $V=2.4$~mJy (83\% and 95\% of the earlier peak-search values) at
$11.9\sigma$ in $V$ and $67\%$ circular in its single bright epoch. That the locked pixel
recovers nearly all of the peak-search flux places the polarized emission at the catalogue
position to well within a beam; the 3.2\arcsec\ peak wander that made it a candidate is
consistent with noise-driven peak displacement at this signal-to-noise. We report it as a
detection, but again not a new one: the discovery paper's archival search covered 61
ASKAP observations of this field and reports exactly one detection, a RACS epoch on
2024-11-21 at $\sim$4~mJy with $\sim$60\% circular polarization \citep{vaster2026}. That is
this epoch (MJD 60635), and our 3.6~mJy at 67\% is consistent with their measurement; their
timing solution places it at pulse phase $\approx -0.1$.

The third source is the most instructive. Under the earlier peak search, ASKAP
J183950.5$-$075635's bright epoch ($240$~mJy at a $5.3\arcsec$ offset) was vetoed as ``a
nearby confusing source''. That veto was wrong twice over. First, $5.3\arcsec$ is about a
third of the $\sim$15\arcsec\ RACS-low beam \citep{mcconnell2020}, not a separable
neighbour; indeed the forced-to-peak flux ratio (0.68 in both $I$ and $V$, identically) is
exactly the beam response of a single point source measured $\sim$5\arcsec\ off its true
position, part of which is the catalogue coordinate's own 3.6\arcsec\ quantization. Second,
and decisively, the epoch is the discovery observation of this source. SBID 57929 is
the 2024-01-26 RACS-low block in which its published pulse (0.70~Jy decaying to 0.03~Jy
across the 15 minutes, 37\% circularly polarized) was first found \citep{lee2025}. The
committed adjudication (\code{results/lptv\_j183950\_adjudication.json}) collects the
cross-checks: the published decay averaged over the block is
$(700-30)/\ln(700/30) = 213$~mJy, against our peak-search 241~mJy (13\% agreement; the forced
164~mJy is that flux beam-attenuated by the $\sim$5\arcsec\ offset); our $|V|/I = 40\%$ is
consistent with the published 37\%; and the survey's own blind V catalogue is empty within
60\arcsec\ (a different, off-state epoch) with no I-catalogue source above 4~mJy within
2\arcmin, so no neighbour existed to confuse with. We are explicit about what this is and is
not. Because the discovery block necessarily enters our epoch list, re-finding the pulse was
guaranteed, not blind, so this epoch cannot count toward any pulse-rate estimate. What it
does provide is an end-to-end check on real data that the locked photometry chain recovers a
known Jy-level LPT pulse with its polarization intact ($199\sigma$ formally, though at this
brightness the error budget is dominated by systematics the local-annulus rms cannot see),
and the demonstration that the earlier peak-search veto produced a false rejection. The
picture is therefore three detections and twelve sets of limits (a sixteenth target lies
outside the RACS footprint and yields none), and the negative half must be stated for what
it is. The non-detections are limits on circularly polarized flux ($\sim$0.5~mJy
best-epoch, 0.73~mJy per-epoch median), not on fractional polarization: at every
non-detection position Stokes~I is itself consistent with zero (median forced
$I/\sigma_I = -0.14$ per epoch), so a $|V|/I$ ratio is unconstrained. The census does not
exclude even fully circular emission for any undetected source. CHIME J1634+44, published
at $\sim$100\% circular polarization \citep{chime1634} and the class's natural archetype, is
among the non-detections at $<$0.41~mJy: a flux non-recovery, not a polarization statement.
What the census does establish is that RACS snapshots can catch LPT pulses with the
burst-state circular polarization intact, though every one of the three recoveries is of
an epoch already reported by the corresponding discovery paper
(\code{results/lptv\_detection\_provenance.json} collects the adjudications), so the RACS census
demonstrates the method rather than adding detections. Because RACS integrates $\sim$15 min per field
\citep{mcconnell2020}, a source active for a small fraction of its period is diluted by
$\sim T/w$ even when strongly polarized in burst, and a limit on the snapshot mean is $T/w$
times weaker on the pulse flux. Phase coverage varies enormously across this sample: the
short-period sources ($P<20$~min) are sampled many times over, while the four longest-period
covered sources accumulate only 27--75\% cumulative phase coverage across all their epochs
(a single 15-min visit covers $\sim$4\% of a 6.5-h period, and the cumulative figure assumes
the visits land at independent phases), so for them a non-detection is the expected
outcome of an off-state and constrains little. Two follow-ons are immediate through the same
pipeline: the fortnightly VAST epochs (Section~\ref{sec:vast}), and phase-resolved
polarimetry of the burst windows which snapshot imaging cannot reach. The v3
catalogue, the uniform V-limit table with its three validated recoveries, the two new
J1839$-$0756 pulses of the VAST extension (Section~\ref{sec:vast}), and the reusable
vetted V machinery are the contribution.

\section{The VAST extension}
\label{sec:vast}
RACS gives each position only $\sim$10 epochs; the VAST survey \citep{murphy2021} monitors
its fields roughly fortnightly with 12-min integrations and publishes Stokes V under the
same product conventions, so the identical forced photometry extends directly. We swept the
966 VAST I$+$V observations of the sample positions matched in the CASDA obscore (10 of 16
LPTs are in the VAST footprint; MJD 59897--61223, i.e. through 2026 July): 647 measured,
212 returning no valid photometry (the position falls outside the mosaic or in a blanked
region, cause not recorded per row), and 107 scheduling blocks that CASDA has
never publicly released (obscore release date null, verified per SBID); the denominator
is stated because a limit quoted over unreleased epochs would be a limit on nothing. The
yield is source-dependent; for the source that matters most below, ASKAP
J183950.5$-$075635, 44 of its 92 VAST pointings yielded a measurement. Unlike the RACS sweep, epoch times are
recorded at full precision ($\sim$1\,s), so phase arithmetic against published ephemerides
is possible from the committed CSV; sweep reduction is \code{lptv.summarize\_vast}
(tested), output \code{results/lptv\_vast\_metrics.json}.

Four epochs pass the $5\sigma$ $+$ leakage vetoes. Two are, once again, published epochs
independently re-measured: ASKAP J1832$-$0911 at 250~mJy (12-min average of the 1522~mJy
pulse in its campaign's SBID 60804; \citealt{wang2025}) with $|V|/I = 5.6\%$, and ASKAP
J1755$-$2527 at 25~mJy, 25\% circular, in SBID 47253, its discovery observation and
timing reference epoch \citep{mcsweeney2025}. The other two are pulses of ASKAP
J183950.5$-$075635 that do not appear in its discovery paper's observation table
\citep{lee2025}: plain VAST survey epochs (SBIDs 62032 and 62646, 2024 May 3 and June 7)
falling in the gap between that campaign's March 15 and June 26 pointings. The table's
SBID list, the ephemeris provenance, and every published number this section leans on are
recorded in \code{results/lptv\_vast\_adjudication.json}, alongside a novelty search
(2026-08-15): an arXiv title/abstract search for the source and a full-text e-print
check of the works citing \citet{lee2025} whose scope could include these epochs (a VAST
transient sample, an LPT review, an LPT timing analysis), which finds no report of
either epoch. Folding on the
published ephemeris ($P = 23{,}221.740 \pm 0.332$~s; zero point the de-dispersed sub-pulse
at MJD 60358.2452, an anchor good to $\pm$0.013 in phase since it is only ``near'' a
main-pulse centre):

\begin{itemize}
\item SBID 62032: $I = 12.2$~mJy at $21\sigma$, $V = 2.1$~mJy (18\% circular; $6.4\sigma$
locally, $5.4\sigma$ after the 647-epoch trials correction) at phase $0.489 \pm 0.016$
(period term 0.004, exposure smearing 0.009, ephemeris-anchor systematic 0.013, in
quadrature), $0.4\sigma$ from the interpulse window ($\approx$0.496, from the published
``roughly 3.2 hours after the main pulse''). Two published properties corroborate and one
is in tension: its flux ratio to the SBID-62646 pulse (17.8\%) sits inside the published
10--20\% interpulse-to-main-pulse band (both being 12-min averages of different epochs,
not a clean per-pulse ratio), and the discovery paper notes no interpulse in its own later
ASKAP epochs; but its circular fraction ($18 \pm 3\%$) is $\sim$2.8$\sigma$ above the
published interpulse range ($\lesssim$10\%; main pulses 30--60\%), so we report it as a
candidate interpulse. Instrumental and confusion origins are excluded: 18\%
circular is 29$\times$ the leakage floor, the same position gives $|V| < 1\sigma$ in the
43 other measured VAST epochs, and the catalogue queries recorded in
\code{results/lptv\_j183950\_adjudication.json} show no neighbour to confuse with.
\item SBID 62646: $I = 68.2$~mJy, $V = 24.4$~mJy at $18.9\sigma$, with a circular fraction
(36\%) inside the published main-pulse band (30--60\%), at phase $0.951 \pm 0.017$,
i.e. 19~min before the nominal ToA. That offset is 3.8$\times$ the anchor systematic and
11--23$\times$ the published timing jitter: it exceeds the quoted systematics and is
unexplained. (The interpulse identification above does not lean on phase precision this
model cannot support: 0.489 is $\sim$0.5, thirty times any plausible systematic,
from the nearest main pulse.)
\end{itemize}

The later epochs constrain less than they appear to: by 2026 the period uncertainty alone
scrambles phase to $\pm$0.04--0.05, comparable to the on-pulse windows, so on/off-pulse
assignment is no longer meaningful. The twenty 2026 epochs are all consistent with zero in
V (fifteen with $3\sigma_V \le 1.5$~mJy; the five shallowest reach 3.0~mJy), but each
12.1-min epoch covers only 0.031 of the 6.45-h period, so their cumulative phase coverage
is $1-(1-0.031)^{20} = 0.47$: even at mid-2024 pulse strength the expected number of
on-pulse catches is $20 \times 0.031 = 0.63$ and zero catches has $p = 0.53$ (all three
numbers from \code{lptv\_vast\_metrics.json}; the window is the exposure alone, and adding
the pulse's own width would only make the null less surprising). These are flux limits at
effectively random phase, consistent with the published decay rather than evidence of it. Finally, GPM
J1839$-$10 \citep{hurleywalker2023} is detected in Stokes~I at 5--31$\sigma$ in eight
epochs spanning 2023 April to 2026 July with $|V|$ always $<1.2\sigma$, consistent with
its published low circular fraction. This is a purity cross-check: a 31$\sigma$
Stokes-I source producing no significant V confirms that I$\to$V leakage is not
manufacturing detections at these positions (V sensitivity is demonstrated by the
four detections themselves).
