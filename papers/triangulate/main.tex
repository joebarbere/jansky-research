\title{Triangulating a Solar Type III Radio Source in 3D with STEREO-A and STEREO-B,
and Cross-Checking the Geometric Distance Against the Plasma-Frequency Distance}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
A type III electron beam radiates near the local plasma frequency, so its emission frequency encodes
its heliocentric distance, but only through a density model. Two spacecraft give the distance
independently: each measures the direction of arrival of the emission, and the two lines of
sight intersect at the source, with no density model at all. We build a small tested tool
that triangulates a type III in 3D from the public STEREO/WAVES \citep{bougeret2008} Level-3
direction-finding product \citep{cecconi2008,krupar2012}, intensity-weighted vector-averaging the
per-frequency direction on each spacecraft and then taking the least-squares intersection of the two
rays, and cross-checks the geometric heliocentric distance against the plasma-frequency distance of
the \citet{leblanc1998} model. Applied to a 2013-05-15 burst
(\code{\triSource}; STEREO-A and STEREO-B \triSep$^\circ$ apart), \triNtri\ frequency channels
(\triFlo--\triFhi~MHz) triangulate to a source at HEEQ longitude \triLon$^\circ\pm$\triLonScatter,
latitude \triLat$^\circ\pm$\triLatScatter\ (near the ecliptic), spanning
\triRlo--\triRhi~$R_\odot$ (\triRloAU--\triRhiAU~AU). The comparison's honest summary is
additive, not multiplicative: the triangulated distances sit a nearly constant
$\triDiffMed\pm\triDiffStd~R_\odot$ \emph{outside} the Leblanc plasma level while the plasma
distance varies by a factor of 13, with a regression slope of \triOlsSlope\ --- the signature
of a constant $\sim$\triBiasDeg$^\circ$ direction-finding bias at the lever arm, not of a
density enhancement, and a zero-model-error synthetic at this exact baseline reproduces an
offset of this size from the documented $18$--$25^\circ$ apparent-source scatter alone. After
that one constant offset the model reproduces the triangulated distances to
\triRmsAdd~$R_\odot$ rms ($\sim$12\% in radius) over 0.07--0.5~AU; the
frequency-dependent ratio (\triRatioMin--\triRatioMax, median \triRatio) is reported as the
contrast, since a single ``scale factor'' describes no channel. This is a method and
reproducibility contribution, recovering a known result rather than reporting a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The drift of a type III burst from high to low frequency traces an electron beam outward through the
corona and solar wind, because the emission sits near the local plasma frequency and the plasma
frequency falls with density and hence with heliocentric distance. Turning the observed frequency into
a distance, however, always passes through a density model (Newkirk in the corona, Leblanc in
the heliosphere): the distance is only as good as the assumed $n_e(r)$. Two spacecraft remove that
dependence entirely. Each spacecraft's direction-finding measures where on the sky the emission comes
from; two such directions, from two known positions, fix the source in 3D as the intersection of the
lines of sight, pure geometry with no density model \citep{reiner2009}. That makes the geometric distance
independent of the plasma-frequency distance, so the two can be compared as a cross-check, and
the geometry additionally yields the source's heliographic longitude and latitude, which a single
1-D drift cannot.

With the public STEREO/WAVES Level-3 direction-finding product and a small tested tool, we test whether
an amateur can triangulate a type III source in 3D and use it to independently check the density-model
distance, with the direction-finding noise budget surfaced honestly.

\section{Methods}
\code{triangulate} is a pure-NumPy module. From each spacecraft's Level-3 product it reads, per time
and frequency, the arrival-direction azimuth and colatitude in the heliocentric HEEQ frame, the flux,
and the spacecraft's HEEQ position. For each frequency it (i) converts each sample's
(azimuth, colatitude) to a unit vector and takes the intensity-weighted vector mean over a
burst window on each spacecraft, the correct circular mean, immune to the azimuth wrap that breaks
scalar angle averaging; (ii) triangulates the two rays (spacecraft position $+$ direction) as their
least-squares closest point, the midpoint of the shortest connecting segment; and (iii) keeps the
channel only when both rays point forward (the source lies in front of both spacecraft), the
miss distance (the connecting segment's length) is below a threshold (60~$R_\odot$, which
exceeds the smallest distance inferred below, so the cut is permissive rather than selective
at the high-frequency end; a sweep of the threshold is reported with the results), and each spacecraft
contributed enough good samples. The kept geometric radii are compared against
\code{windwaves.emission\_radius}, the Leblanc plasma-frequency radius at the same harmonic, reusing
the heliospheric density model of the interplanetary-type-III slices; the committed
(harmonic\,$\times$\,density-scale) grid propagates the $f_p^2\propto n_e$ degeneracy those
slices share (fundamental at $4\times$ density is exactly harmonic at $1\times$), rather than
footnoting it. Time is carried as seconds since the file date's UTC midnight on both spacecraft
--- the daily Level-3 CDFs are sparse, so a per-file origin could silently misalign the two
burst windows by exactly the constant angular bias this analysis measures; the committed
per-file epochs show the two origins differ by 25~s here, negligible inside the $\pm$900~s
window. The summary longitude is a circular median (centred on the circular mean before the
median is taken): this event sits on the $\pm180^\circ$ branch cut, where a scalar median
lands at the edge of one longitude cluster rather than between them. Every analysis function is
tested against a synthetic two-spacecraft event built by placing a radially outflowing source at
a known longitude and latitude --- including one \emph{on} the branch cut at the real event's
separation --- and giving each spacecraft the true arrival direction plus injected Gaussian
angular scatter, a dialled constant bias, or a shifted time origin, so the estimator's failure
modes are exercised, not just its successes.

\section{Data}
We use the STEREO/WAVES High Frequency Receiver Level-3 direction-finding CDFs (one per day per
spacecraft) from NASA SPDF (public, no auth). The product is the SVD goniopolarimetric inversion of
\citet{cecconi2008} and \citet{krupar2012}. Triangulation needs both spacecraft, so it is reproducible
only for the 2007--2014 two-spacecraft era (STEREO-B was lost in 2014); the tool runs on any A$+$B day.

\section{Results}
In the 2013-05-15 event (active region AR~11748, an X-class-flare period), with STEREO-A and STEREO-B
\triSep$^\circ$ apart as seen from the Sun, \triNtri\ frequency channels (\triFlo--\triFhi~MHz)
triangulate to a forward intersection with a median ray miss of \triMiss~$R_\odot$. The source sits
at HEEQ longitude \triLon$^\circ\pm$\triLonScatter\ (circular median; the channels straddle the
$\pm180^\circ$ cut), latitude \triLat$^\circ\pm$\triLatScatter\ (near the ecliptic), and the
geometric heliocentric distance runs from \triRlo\ to \triRhi~$R_\odot$ (\triRloAU\ to
\triRhiAU~AU), genuinely interplanetary (Figure~\ref{fig:tri}). Every per-channel solution ---
including both spacecraft's mean direction vectors and sample counts, with the spacecraft
positions in the results JSON --- is committed, so each triangulation is reproducible offline.

\paragraph{The comparison is additive, and that is the result.} All \triNtri\ triangulated
distances lie \emph{outside} the Leblanc plasma level, by a nearly constant
$\triDiffMed\pm\triDiffStd~R_\odot$ (jackknife error on the median $\pm$\triDiffJkSe) while
$r_\mathrm{plasma}$ itself varies by a factor of 13 across the band; the regression of
$r_\mathrm{geom}$ on $r_\mathrm{plasma}$ has slope \triOlsSlope\ and intercept
\triOlsIntercept~$R_\odot$. A one-parameter additive model ($r_\mathrm{geom}=r_\mathrm{plasma}
+\triDiffMed$) leaves an rms residual of \triRmsAdd~$R_\odot$; the one-parameter multiplicative
alternative ($r_\mathrm{geom}=\triRatio\,r_\mathrm{plasma}$) leaves \triRmsMul~$R_\odot$ ---
five times worse. The ratio is correspondingly not a constant: it runs from \triRatioMin\ at the
lowest frequency to \triRatioMax\ at the highest (median \triRatio$\pm$\triRatioJkSe), so a
single ``factor'' describes no channel, and the log--log slopes differ
(\triSlopeGeom\ geometric versus \triSlopePlasma\ for Leblanc). Two consequences follow. First,
the near-unity regression slope leaves no room for a density enhancement, which would
\emph{multiply} the radii; the additive offset is instead the signature of a constant
$\sim$\triBiasDeg$^\circ$ pointing bias at the $\sim$\triLever~$R_\odot$ lever arm. Second, that
bias needs no new physics: a zero-model-error synthetic at this exact \triSep$^\circ$ baseline,
with the source placed at the unmodified Leblanc radii, produces additive offsets of
\triCalibNineDiff, \triCalibEighteenDiff, and \triCalibTwentyFiveDiff~$R_\odot$ for $9^\circ$,
$18^\circ$, and $25^\circ$ of injected direction scatter (committed as the calibration grid) ---
and the apparent source size of a type III reaches tens of degrees at these frequencies
\citep{krupar2014}, so the observed \triDiffMed~$R_\odot$ is fully consistent with
direction-finding scatter alone. Framed this way the additive comparison is a
\emph{recover-a-known for the Leblanc shape}: after one constant instrumental offset, the model
tracks the triangulated distances to
\triRmsAdd~$R_\odot$ rms ($\sim$12\% of the median radius) from 0.07 to 0.5~AU.

For continuity with correlation-based practice we also report Pearson $r=\triCorr$
(channel-block jackknife $\pm$\triCorrJkSe; $r=\triCorrLog$ in log--log space, the space the
curvature lives in) --- but we no longer present it as the cross-check, because it cannot fail:
any monotone power law in frequency correlates with the Leblanc curve at $r>0.95$, and a bare
$1/f$ matches the geometric points as well as the model does. The statistic that can fail is the
log--log slope: \triSlopeGvsP\ for $r_\mathrm{geom}$ against $r_\mathrm{plasma}$, against 1.0
for a pure scale offset --- and it does fail, which is how the additive framing above was
forced. The committed (harmonic\,$\times$\,density) grid bounds the assumption the comparison
rests on: under fundamental emission the offset becomes \triGridHOneDiff~$R_\odot$ (ratio
\triGridHOneRatio), and fundamental at $4\times$ density is identically harmonic at $1\times$
(\triGridHOneSFourRatio) --- the degeneracy is exact, so the harmonic choice is a convention
here, not a measurement. Neither headline depends on the miss-distance cut: the committed sweep
moves the correlation at most to \triSweepThirtyCorr\ (at 30~$R_\odot$, its largest excursion)
and tightening to 15~$R_\odot$ keeps \triSweepFifteenN\ of \triSweepSixtyN\ channels at ratio
\triSweepFifteenRatio; no kept channel misses by more than 32~$R_\odot$, so the 60 and
100-$R_\odot$ rows are identical --- the analysis cut never actually binds.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
This tool is a tested method and reproducibility contribution, locating a type III source in 3D
from two public STEREO/WAVES direction-finding spectra and using the geometry to independently
check the density-model distance that the drift-to-distance slices rely on, with the
direction-finding noise surfaced rather than hidden. The honest limitations: (i) The robust
results are the source direction and the additive comparison; the constant
$\sim$\triBiasDeg$^\circ$ offset the comparison reveals is at the size the measured calibration
grid produces from apparent-source scatter alone, but a genuine pointing calibration error in
the Level-3 inversion would look identical, and two rays cannot distinguish them --- either way
the absolute radial scale carries the offset and we do not read it as calibrated. (ii) Only the
low band (\triFlo--\triFhi~MHz) yields direction-finding solutions for this event, so this
reaches the interplanetary source, not the coronal one. (iii) With two spacecraft there is one
closest-point solution per frequency, and the miss distance, not a fitted error ellipsoid, is
its consistency check --- and the median miss (\triMiss~$R_\odot$) is comparable to the additive
offset itself, so individual channels are consistent with the model while the ensemble offset is
one-signed on all \triNtri\ channels. (iv) The harmonic assumption is a convention within the
committed (harmonic\,$\times$\,density) grid, exactly degenerate with the density scale.
(v) The two-view geometry needs STEREO-B and so is reproducible only on 2007--2014 data. None of
these undermine the recover-a-known result (a type III is located in 3D and, after one constant
offset, its geometric distances confirm the Leblanc profile to $\sim$12\% over the triangulated
band), but they bound what two lines of sight to a large, fuzzy source can claim. Every number
above is written by the pipeline into the macros this paper \code{\textbackslash input}s
(including the calibration and degeneracy grids), and
\code{python -m jansky\_research.triangulate -\,-date 20130515} regenerates them from the public
SPDF CDFs.
