\title{Recovering the 16.35-day Activity Period of FRB~20180916B from the Public
CHIME/FRB Catalog with a Lightweight Rayleigh Periodogram}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
A handful of repeating fast radio bursts (FRBs) show periodic activity windows; the archetype,
FRB~20180916B, has a $16.35\pm0.15$-day cycle \citep{chime2020periodic}. We ask the modest question whether that period
can be recovered from the public First CHIME/FRB Catalog alone, using a small, tested, CPU-only
tool. We implement a phase-folding Rayleigh $Z^2_1$ periodogram \citep{buccheri1983}, run over
all \fpNsources\ catalog repeaters with one epoch per transit (same-transit bursts are duplicate
phase samples at multi-day periods, and collapsing them lowers the peak statistic from an inflated
33.4 to $Z^2_1=\fpZtwo$). Only \fpNsearch\ sources have enough bursts ($\geq\fpMinBursts$) to
search. FRB~20180916B peaks at $P=\fpPeriod\pm\fpPerr$~days on \fpNepochs\ transit epochs
(\fpNbursts\ bursts), consistent with the published value. The validation's strength is
quantified by the number the argument actually rests on: the probability that a null
realization's grid peak lands within the published $\pm0.15$~d of 16.35~days by chance, measured
by Monte Carlo as $\fpUniPcoinc$ under a uniform null and $\fpCluPcoinc$ under a clustered
(aperiodic, interval-bootstrapped) null: real evidence at roughly the $10^{-2}$ level, and
about two orders of magnitude weaker than the analytic false-alarm probability an earlier
version of this paper printed, which burst clustering (not aliasing) invalidates. The other
searchable source shows no significant period, with the sensitivity stated: an injected twin of
the detected source (duty cycle \fpDuty) would have been recovered there only \fpTwinPower\ of
the time. This is a reproducibility and tooling contribution, a recovered known result rather
than a new periodicity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Fast radio bursts are millisecond-duration, highly dispersed radio flashes. A subset repeat, and a
smaller subset repeat periodically: bursts cluster at a preferred phase of a multi-day cycle.
FRB~20180916B was the first, with a 16.35-day activity period \citep{chime2020periodic}; a longer
$\sim$157-day candidate period has been reported for FRB~121102 \citep{rajwade2020}. Detecting such
periods normally relies on dedicated monitoring campaigns with an exposure model. Here we test the
weaker but reproducible claim that the flagship period is already recoverable from the public
First CHIME/FRB Catalog \citep{chimecat1} with a minimal tool. This is a validation exercise rather than a
search for new periods.

\section{Method}
\code{frbperiod} is a pure-NumPy module (built on the \code{jansky} helper package; \citealt{jansky})
implementing the Rayleigh $Z^2_1$ statistic \citep{buccheri1983}. For a trial period $P$, arrival
times are folded to phase $\phi_i = 2\pi\,(t_i/P \bmod 1)$ and
\begin{equation}
Z^2_1 = \frac{2}{n}\left[\Big(\textstyle\sum_i\cos\phi_i\Big)^2 + \Big(\sum_i\sin\phi_i\Big)^2\right],
\end{equation}
which is large when bursts concentrate at one phase and $\sim2$ for random phases; under the
no-signal hypothesis $Z^2_1\sim\chi^2_2$. Arrival times are first collapsed to one epoch per
transit: CHIME sees a source once per sidereal day, so distinct catalogued bursts minutes apart
in one transit are duplicate phase samples at any multi-day trial period, and treating them as
independent inflates $Z^2_1$ (the same pseudo-replication the catalogue's sub-burst rows would
cause, collapsed one level up). We scan \fpPmin--\fpPmax~days on a \fpNgrid-point grid, take
the peak, and attach three probabilities: the analytic
$\mathrm{FAP}=1-(1-e^{-Z^2_1/2})^{n_\mathrm{indep}}$, reported only as an approximate guide (it
assumes independent trials); a Monte-Carlo exceedance probability under two measured nulls,
uniform arrivals, and a clustered null whose inter-epoch intervals are bootstrapped from the
other searchable repeater (never from the target's own intervals, which for a periodic
source inherit the signal and would wrongly appear to destroy the detection); and the
coincidence probability, how often a null realization's grid peak lands within the published
uncertainty of the known period. The clustered null is the honest one: measured, burst
clustering raises the null's median grid-max $Z^2_1$ from \fpUniMedZ\ to \fpCluMedZ, while
the transit-comb aliasing the earlier version of this paper blamed barely moves it. A
detection means grid-max analytic FAP $<\fpFapThreshold$ (any \fpMinBursts-threshold between
4 and 11 selects the same two searchable sources). Every function is tested against synthetic
periodic sources with known injected periods, so the suite runs offline.

\section{Data}
We use the public First CHIME/FRB Catalog \citep{chimecat1}, keeping one row per event and the
\fpNsources\ sources flagged as repeaters. CHIME is a transit instrument that sees each source
roughly once per sidereal day with strongly non-uniform exposure. Sources with fewer than
\fpMinBursts\ catalog bursts are not searched; only \fpNsearch\ of the \fpNsources\ repeaters clear
that bar, the central limitation of a catalog-only search.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Recovery of the known period.} For FRB~20180916B the periodogram peaks at
$P=\fpPeriod\pm\fpPerr$~days (epoch-bootstrap uncertainty; the grid step is not the
measurement error) on \fpNepochs\ transit epochs from \fpNbursts\ catalogued bursts
(Figure~\ref{fig:pgram}), consistent with the published $16.35\pm0.15$~days
\citep{chime2020periodic}. The agreement with an independently measured value is the
validation, and its strength is now a measured number: the chance of a null realization's peak
landing within $\pm0.15$~d of 16.35 is $\fpUniPcoinc$ (uniform null) to $\fpCluPcoinc$
(clustered null), roughly $3\sigma$-equivalent evidence. The peak statistic is
$Z^2_1=\fpZtwo$ (down from an inflated 33.4 before the transit collapse); its Monte-Carlo
exceedance probability is $\fpUniPexceed$ under the uniform null but $\fpCluPexceed$ under the
clustered one, and the analytic $\mathrm{FAP}=\fpFAP$ sits between them; aliasing, the
worry the earlier version of this paper named, is measurably not what breaks the analytic
number; burst clustering is.

\paragraph{The null in the second source, with its sensitivity.} \fpNullName\ (\fpNullNbursts\
bursts on \fpNullNepochs\ transits) shows no significant period: best peak
$Z^2_1=\fpNullZtwo$ at \fpNullPeriod~d, analytic $\mathrm{FAP}=\fpNullFAP$. That null is
honest only with its power stated: injecting a twin of FRB~20180916B (period \fpPeriod~d, the
measured duty cycle \fpDuty) into this source's epoch count and span, the search's own
detection rule recovers it only \fpTwinPower\ of the time, so a real analogue would usually
have been missed, so ``no period detected'' here means ``sensitive only to duty cycles
$\lesssim$0.2'', not ``no period exists''. The remaining \fpNunsearchable\ repeaters have too
few catalog bursts to constrain anything.

\section{Limitations}
The caveats are structural rather than incidental. (i)~Exposure-blind significance: the
analytic FAP is an upper bound on confidence, not a detection probability, because transit sampling
is aliased and non-uniform. (ii)~Daily aliasing: catalog-only periodograms alias near 1~day
and its beats; the 16-day peak sits safely above that and matches the known value, but blind
short-period peaks are not trustworthy. (iii)~Topocentric times: no barycentric correction was
applied; for a 16-day period the Roemer modulation is $\sim$0.03\% and negligible (the correct
recovery is consistent with that, though it could not confirm it), but this matters for sub-day
searches. (iv)~Sparse sampling: with the catalog's burst counts, \fpNunsearchable\ of
\fpNsources\ sources yield no constraint, and even the searchable ones are marginal by
professional standards.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is a tested, offline-capable periodicity-search tool plus a validation whose
evidence is quantified: it recovers FRB~20180916B's published period from public data at a
measured coincidence probability of $\sim10^{-2}$, and finds nothing significant in the one
other searchable source, a null whose measured power against a twin of the detection is
\fpTwinPower, stated rather than implied. The analysis regenerates from a
clean checkout in one command, and every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros
the manuscript \code{\textbackslash input}s, so it is auditable end to end. Finding genuinely new
repeater periods would require the denser, exposure-modelled data the discovery teams use, not
Catalog~1.
