\title{A Steady-Control Floor for Multi-Decade VLBI Flux Variability:
A Reproducible Tool Applied to Compact AGN in the Astrogeo Database}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The Astrogeo VLBI database holds decades of dual-band ($S=2.3$, $X=8.4$~GHz) correlated-flux
measurements for thousands of compact radio sources, so each source has a multi-decade, parsec-scale
light curve. We build a small tested tool that turns these into the standard transient-survey
variability statistics, $\eta$ (weighted reduced $\chi^2$ versus a constant) and $V$ (coefficient of
variation; \citealt{scheers2011,swinbank2015,rowlinson2019}), plus a two-point $S/X$ spectral index. The
central methodological point is that for VLBI correlated flux density the per-session error is
dominated by amplitude calibration and by $(u,v)$-coverage / resolved-structure differences, and is
much larger than a naive few-percent floor. Applied to a curated set of \viN\ well-studied AGN, the absolute $\eta$ test labels every source
``variable,'' including the steady compact symmetric object (CSO) controls, so $\eta$ is not a usable
discriminant. We instead calibrate an empirical variability floor from the CSO controls (median
$V=\viFloor$), stating its selection function: a median threshold sits above half of any steady
population by construction, so ``above the floor'' means inconsistent with the controls' central
amplitude, not individually significant. On this set the recover-a-known is clean: OJ~287
(\viTopName) shows the most significant variability ($\eta=\viTopEta$, $V=\viTopV$ over
\viTopEpochs\ epochs), the
famous blazars are recovered as variable, and \viNabove\ of the \viNnoncontrol\ non-control AGN lie above the median floor, a count propagated through the floor's uncertainty: \viNaboveJkHi$\to$\viNaboveJkLo\
across the drop-one control jackknife, \viNaboveCtrlMax\ at the most conservative (control-maximum)
threshold, \viNaboveEpochMatched\ under an epoch-matched floor, and CTA~102 above the median floor
by under $10^{-4}$ in $V$. This is a method and reproducibility contribution: a
tested tool and a control-floor discriminant with its uncertainty carried to the headline count. It
is not a discovery claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Compact, flat-spectrum AGN vary in the radio on timescales from days to decades, and the parsec-scale
cores resolved by very long baseline interferometry (VLBI) are where much of that variability
originates. The Astrogeo database (\citealt{petrov2025}; the Radio Fundamental Catalog and its
underlying image archive, maintained by L.\ Petrov) is a uniquely deep public record: thousands of
compact sources, each observed in many geodetic and absolute-astrometry sessions over decades, almost
all in dual-band $S/X$. The per-session total correlated flux densities therefore constitute long,
if irregularly sampled, light curves. We ask the most reproducible version of a variability study: with
the public per-epoch flux files and a small tested tool, can one recover the known variability of
well-studied AGN with the dominant VLBI systematic honestly handled?

The systematic is the crux. Transient surveys quantify variability with $\eta$ (the reduced $\chi^2$
against a constant flux: a significance) and $V$ (the coefficient of variation: an amplitude;
\citealt{scheers2011,swinbank2015,rowlinson2019}). Both require a per-point flux error. Geodetic VLBI
provides no formal flux error, and its correlated flux density mixes intrinsic flux with the
session-dependent $(u,v)$ coverage and resolved-out structure. We show that this makes absolute $\eta$
useless and motivates calibrating a variability floor from sources known to be intrinsically steady.

\section{Methods}
\code{vlbi} is a pure-NumPy module that composes the tested \code{jansky} / \code{jansky\_research}
helpers (\citealt{jansky}): it reuses the VLASS-slice variability statistics (\code{variability\_metrics}:
$\eta$, $V$, the $\chi^2$ $p$-value) and the two-point spectral-index helper. For each source it reads the
per-epoch Astrogeo \code{\_cfd.tab} correlated-flux files, takes the integrated flux density
\code{Fl\_int} (Jy) per session, and forms a per-band light curve; a light curve needs at least four
finite epochs to be tested. The per-point error is a calibration-fraction floor,
$\sigma=\sqrt{(f\,\mathrm{Fl\_int})^2+\mathrm{Fl\_noi}^2}$ with $f=0.05$, in quadrature with the image
noise. We stress that $f=0.05$ is only a common starting assumption, not a value prescribed for
these heterogeneous networks; the control-floor analysis below measures the effective scatter directly.

\paragraph{The control-floor discriminant.} For an intrinsically steady source the per-session $V$ is
not zero; it is set by amplitude calibration and by $(u,v)$/structure differences between sessions.
Compact symmetric objects (CSOs), which lack a Doppler-boosted core, are the natural steady controls and
are established flux calibrators (\citealt{fassnachttaylor2001} found $\sim$0.7\% rms over an 8-month
campaign, which supports steadiness on year timescales; multi-decade steadiness is an extrapolation
this data set probes rather than assumes. \citealt{kiehlmann2024} adopt a $\lesssim$10\% variability
criterion in their bona-fide CSO catalog, which contains all four of our controls). We take the
controls' median $V$ as the empirical floor and flag non-control sources above it. The floor's
selection function must be stated: as a median it sits above half of any intrinsically steady
population by construction (\viNctrlAbove\ of our \viNctrl\ controls lie above their own floor), so
``above the floor'' means inconsistent with the steady controls' central amplitude, not individually
significant at a stated false-positive rate.

Every function is tested offline against a synthetic dual-band population, and the control-floor
selector itself is validated on a fixture carrying both injected variables and known-steady controls:
completeness \viSynFloorCompleteness\ for flare amplitudes well above the floor, degrading as the
injected amplitude approaches it, but purity only \viSynFloorPurity\ on that steady-dominated blind population, the measured form of the selection-function caveat above. On the curated set the low
blind purity is immaterial (the non-controls are independently known variables), but it rules out
using the floor as a blind-survey classifier.

\section{Data}
We apply the tool to a curated validation set of \viN\ well-studied compact AGN (not a blind
survey): famous Doppler-boosted blazars expected to vary, plus four CSO controls (OQ~208, 2021+614,
0108+388, NGC~3894). Variability is computed from the $X$-band light curves (denser and more precisely
calibrated than $S$); $S$-band is used only for the spectral index. \viTestable\ sources have $\geq$4
$X$-band epochs (\viEpochYearMin--\viEpochYearMax) and enter the analysis. Two controls are imperfect: OQ~208 is documented as
atypically variable for a CSO (40--60\% in components; \citealt{wu2013}) and 2021+614 shows long-term
centimetre variability (\citealt{taylor2000}); we test the floor's sensitivity to both.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Absolute $\eta$ fails; the controls show why.} Against the assumed 5\% error, every
testable source has $\eta\gg1$ and rejects constancy at $p\leq\viBelowFloorP$ (the least
significant, 3C~273), including all four CSO controls. Since a genuinely
steady CSO cannot be intrinsically variable at these levels, this is direct evidence that the per-session
error is badly under-estimated: the controls imply an effective scatter of $\sim$\viEffScatterPct\%,
$\viScatterRatio\times$ the assumed floor. The noise-debiased modulation index makes the same
measurement directly: under the assumed 5\% model the control median is $m_d=\viMedMdCtrl$, and on a
genuinely steady source $m_d$ is the scatter the error model fails to capture. Absolute $\eta$/$\chi^2$ is therefore not a usable variability discriminant for VLBI
correlated flux, and the relative log\,$\eta$--log\,$V$ outlier cut used in blind transient surveys
returns nothing here (\viNcand\ candidates) because the curated set has no steady background population.

\paragraph{The control floor recovers the known variables, with its uncertainty propagated.} Taking
the CSO controls' median as the empirical floor, $V=\viFloorFive$ (\viNctrl\ controls,
Table~\ref{tab:sources}), \viNabove\ of the non-control AGN lie above it (Figure~\ref{fig:etav}).
The floor carries its own uncertainty and we carry it to the count. The full drop-one control
jackknife spans \viFloorJkLo--\viFloorJkHi\ (count \viNaboveJkHi$\to$\viNaboveJkLo); the individual
control amplitudes span \viCtrlVmin--\viCtrlVmax, and at the most conservative threshold the controls can supply (the control maximum, set by OQ~208, itself documented as variable) the count falls to \viNaboveCtrlMax. Each source's $V$ also carries sampling scatter (a per-source
bootstrap over epochs, committed in the candidate table): \viNaboveZthree\ of the \viNabove\ exceed
the median floor by more than $3\sigma_V$, and the marginal source, \viMarginalName\ (CTA~102), sits at $V=\viMarginalV$ against the floor's $\viFloorFive$, above at the committed five-decimal precision, a tie for any practical purpose ($z=\viMarginalZ$).
The recover-a-known is clean: the most significantly variable source is the blazar OJ~287
(\viTopName), with $\eta=\viTopEta$ and $V=\viTopV$ over \viTopEpochs\ epochs at a mean $X$-band flux
of \viTopFlux~Jy, and the other archetypal blazars (BL~Lac, AO~0235+164, PKS~1510$-$089) follow.
That ranking is by $\eta$; ranked instead by the amplitude $V$ this paper argues for, BL~Lac leads
and OJ~287 is second (Table~\ref{tab:sources}). The amplitude scale is anchored in the literature:
OJ~287's strong, factor-of-several radio variability is documented in the long-term monitoring of
\citet{komossa2023}, consistent with the large $V$ recovered here. Comparing amplitudes without selecting on them (the median over all \viNnoncontrol\ testable non-controls, not the
above-floor subset, whose median exceeds the control median for any input by construction) gives $V=\viMedVnonctrl$ against the controls' \viMedVctrl; we report this as a
property of this curated sample, not of the blazar population. The population-median $S/X$ index is
$\alpha=\viMedAlpha$ (flat), the expected signature of self-absorbed compact cores. However, because
$S$ and $X$ sample angular scales differing by $\sim$3.6$\times$, $\alpha$ mixes spectrum and
differential resolution and should be read cautiously for partially-resolved objects (3C~84, 3C~120).

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is method and reproducibility: a tested tool that builds multi-decade VLBI light curves
from public Astrogeo files and a control-floor variability discriminant whose uncertainty and
selection function are stated and propagated. The limitations are real. (i) The sample is curated and
small, chosen to contain known variables, so the \viNabove/\viNnoncontrol\ recovery validates the
pipeline (a recover-a-known), not a blind detection rate; the marginal source, CTA~102, is a tie
with the floor for any practical purpose ($z=\viMarginalZ$). (ii) The floor rests on only \viNctrl\
controls (individual $V$ from \viCtrlVmin\ to \viCtrlVmax), two of which are themselves mildly
variable; the jackknife and control-maximum variants above carry that weakness to the count, and the
floor is not tightly constrained. (iii) VLBI correlated flux
is not intrinsic flux: $(u,v)$ coverage and resolved structure vary session to session, which is exactly
the scatter the control floor absorbs, and which dilutes genuine variability for sparsely-sampled or
extended sources (3C~273, with only four epochs, falls below the floor). (iv) $\eta$/$V$ use $X$-band
only. (v) $V$ is confounded with epoch count: over the whole sample, Spearman
$\rho=\viSpearmanRho$ (permutation $p=\viSpearmanP$), with an OLS trend
$V=\viOlsA+\viOlsB\,\log_{10}N_{\rm ep}$ that spans the sample's dynamic range (more epochs sample more of both the variability and the $(u,v)$ scatter) while the floor is calibrated on
controls with 4--53 epochs and applied to sources with up to \viTopEpochs. An epoch-matched floor
(the controls' own trend evaluated at each source's epoch count) gives \viNaboveEpochMatched\ of
\viNnoncontrol; four controls cannot pin a two-parameter trend, so this is a sensitivity variant,
not a replacement headline. A blind, magnitude-limited catalog with the floor calibrated per
epoch-count and declination bin is the natural next step. Every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros this manuscript
\code{\textbackslash input}s, and the analysis regenerates from a clean checkout on public data.
