\title{An Independent Replication of the Sofue \& Kohno Inner Milky Way Rotation Curve:
the Terminal-Velocity Method from Raw HI4PI Data, and the Degeneracy Behind the
Local Dark-Matter Density}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofue2025} derived the definitive modern inner rotation curve (RC) of the Milky Way
by the terminal-velocity method (TVM) applied to HI4PI and CO longitude--velocity data,
published it as machine-readable tables, and decomposed it into bulge, disc, and NFW halo,
quoting a halo-only local dark-matter density $\rho_\mathrm{DM} = 0.107$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ ---
a factor $\sim$3 below the $\sim$0.3--0.5 consensus, which they frame as a lower limit. I
replicate the analysis independently at both ends. (1) From their \emph{published tables}:
their $\rho_\mathrm{DM}$ arithmetic reproduces exactly (through the $4\pi$ convention their
halo equations absorb), but an unconstrained refit of the same curve, same model family,
fits marginally better (\irRmsOurs\ vs \irRmsTheirs~km\,s$^{-1}$ rms) at
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoOurs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$, and all \irScanN\ variants of a
profile/range/weighting sensitivity scan land in \irRhoMin--\irRhoMax: the published curve
is fully compatible with the consensus density, and the factor-3 gap is disc--halo
degeneracy width, not tension. (2) From \emph{raw HI4PI data} (\irNsight\ tangent-point
sightlines in both quadrants): their Table~2 curve reproduces at the
$\sim$\irTabTwoAbs~km\,s$^{-1}$ level, their east--west asymmetry replicates in period and
phase, and a head-to-head of terminal-velocity estimators measures the fixed-threshold
rule to read $+\irBiasMed$~km\,s$^{-1}$ high of Gaussian decomposition --- with the
calibrated dispersion corrections of the two estimators (\irSigGauss\ vs
\irSigThresh~km\,s$^{-1}$) bracketing the paper's adopted 15~km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The inner-Galaxy rotation curve underpins the Milky Way's mass model, and the
terminal-velocity method --- reading the extreme line-of-sight velocity of HI/CO emission at
the tangent point --- remains its workhorse inside the solar circle
\citep{mcclure2016,chemin2015}. \citet{sofue2025} provide the current reference analysis:
TVM applied to HI4PI \citep{hi4pi} and four CO surveys, Gaussian decomposition for the
terminal velocities, a dispersion correction calibrated at the solar circle, publication of
the RC as ASCII tables, and a mass decomposition whose halo component implies a local
dark-matter density well below consensus determinations \citep{readdm}. Replication of such
reference analyses is rare; this paper attempts one at two levels --- their published tables
(testing the decomposition and its headline density) and the raw public survey (testing the
measurement chain) --- with an independent open-source pipeline
\citep{janskyresearch}.

\section{Anchor: decomposing their published curve}
The arXiv source of \citet{sofue2025} includes the RC tables (inner curve at
$\delta R = 50$~pc, \irInnerRows\ rows; unified TVM+VERA+VLBA+Gaia curve to
\irRmax~kpc, \irUniRows\ rows). Fitting the unified curve with their model family (their
eqs.~19--27: $4\times10^{6}\,M_\odot$ point mass, Plummer bulge and disc, NFW halo) gives
Figure~\ref{fig:decomp}. Their Table~1 parameters describe their curve at
\irRmsTheirs~km\,s$^{-1}$ rms and --- converted through their
$\rho_0 = V_h^2/(G h^2)$ convention, in which their $V_h$ absorbs $\sqrt{4\pi}$ relative to
the standard normalisation --- reproduce their published
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoTheirs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ exactly. An unconstrained least-squares
refit prefers a more concentrated halo at \irRmsOurs~km\,s$^{-1}$ rms with
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoOurs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$; the bulge is stable across all solutions
(394~km\,s$^{-1}$/316~pc vs their 406/333), and a sensitivity scan over halo profile
(NFW/Burkert), fit range (all $R$ vs $R>2$~kpc), and error weighting yields
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoMin$--\irRhoMax\ across all \irScanN\ converging variants. None
reproduces their corner, suggesting an additional constraint in their fit not stated in the
paper. The conclusion is the quantified form of their own caveat: the curve constrains the
disc--halo split only to a width that spans the full range from their lower limit to the
consensus density.

\section{Replication from raw HI4PI data}
From the ten HI4PI galactic-plane tiles (CDS; 2.6~GB), $|b|<3^{\circ}$ longitude--velocity
diagrams (Figure~\ref{fig:lv}) yield \irNsight\ tangent-point sightlines at
$\ell = 5$--$89.5^{\circ}$ and $270.5$--$355^{\circ}$. Terminal velocities are measured two
ways on identical spectra: Gaussian decomposition of the envelope window (greedy seeding +
joint refinement; their method, Figure~\ref{fig:lvb}), and the fixed 2~K threshold crossing
used by simple treatments. Each estimator receives the paper's \S3.3 dispersion correction,
calibrated (per estimator) so the binned curve meets $\Theta_0$ at $R_0$; since $V$ is
linear in $\sigma_v$ the calibration is closed-form.

Three results (Figures~\ref{fig:rc}--\ref{fig:ew}):

\emph{(i) The estimator bias, measured.} The threshold rule reads
$+\irBiasMed$~km\,s$^{-1}$ (median; 16th--84th percentiles $\irBiasPlo$ to
$+\irBiasPhi$) high of Gaussian decomposition on the same \irNsight\ real spectra --- same
sign as, and larger than, the $\sim$7~km\,s$^{-1}$ literature figure for threshold-vs-fitting
offsets \citep{mcclure2016}. The calibration route confirms it independently: the two
estimators require $\sigma_v = \irSigGauss$ and \irSigThresh~km\,s$^{-1}$ respectively, a
difference equal to the direct bias --- and the paper's adopted
$\sigma_\mathrm{HI} = 15$~km\,s$^{-1}$ falls between them, indicating their decomposition
lands between component-centre and envelope-crossing in practice.

\emph{(ii) Their Table~2 reproduces at the $\sim$4\% level.} After calibration, the binned
HI-only curve matches their published inner RC to mean
$|\Delta V| = \irTabTwoAbs$~km\,s$^{-1}$ (median $\irTabTwoMed$; \irTabTwoN\ bins,
$R>2$~kpc). The residual is a shape difference plausibly from their HI+CO merge and finer
latitude windows; the flat, non-Keplerian form and the bar-region inner peak reproduce
fully.

\emph{(iii) The east--west asymmetry replicates in period and phase.} Fitting their damped
sinusoid (their eq.~29) in the mid-disc window (2--8~kpc; inside 2~kpc the bar rails any
smooth fit) gives amplitude \irEwAmp~km\,s$^{-1}$, period \irEwPeriod~kpc, phase
\irEwPhase~kpc against their $\sim$14~km\,s$^{-1}$ (at 4~kpc), 4.4~kpc, 4.0~kpc --- the same
phenomenon at softer amplitude from HI alone, with mid-disc
$\delta V$ rms \irEwRms~km\,s$^{-1}$; the damping length is unconstrained over our window.

\section{Discussion}
Caveats bound both legs. The anchor refit does not replicate their exact fitting procedure
(weighting and algorithm are not fully specified); the sensitivity scan brackets defensible
choices instead. The raw-data leg is HI-only against their HI+CO merge; TVM itself is not a
clean mass tracer inside the bar \citep{chemin2015}, which both analyses exclude from
interpretation; and terminal-velocity systematics at the $\sim$10~km\,s$^{-1}$ level
\citep{tvmcaution} are precisely what the estimator head-to-head quantifies. Within those
bounds: the reference inner RC replicates from raw public data at the few-percent level;
its published dark-matter density arithmetic is exactly correct \emph{and} its curve is
equally consistent with the consensus local density --- the informative statement is the
degeneracy width, which the published tables themselves encode.
