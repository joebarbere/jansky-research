\title{An Independent Replication of the Sofue \& Kohno Inner Milky Way Rotation Curve:
the Terminal-Velocity Method from Raw HI4PI Data, and the Degeneracy Behind the
Local Dark-Matter Density}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
\citet{sofue2025} derived the definitive modern inner rotation curve (RC) of the Milky Way
by the terminal-velocity method (TVM) applied to HI4PI and CO longitude--velocity data,
published it as machine-readable tables, and decomposed it into bulge, disc, and NFW halo,
quoting a halo-only local dark-matter density $\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoTheirs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ ---
a factor $\sim$3 below the $\sim$0.3~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ consensus value, which they frame as a lower limit. I
replicate the analysis independently at both ends. (1) From their \emph{published tables}:
their $\rho_\mathrm{DM}$ arithmetic reproduces to the precision they quote (through the
$4\pi$ convention their halo equations absorb), but a bounded refit of the same curve, same
model family, fits better (\irRmsOurs\ vs \irRmsTheirs~km\,s$^{-1}$ rms;
$\chi^2/N = \irChiOurs$ vs \irChiTheirs) at
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoOurs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ (1$\sigma$ \irRhoLo--\irRhoHi\ from the fitted
halo parameters), and the improvement is concentrated beyond 8~kpc, where their published
halo sits systematically below their own curve. Their \irRhoTheirs\ is therefore not the value their
curve demands: the same data and model family admit a solution a factor $\sim$2.3 higher at
better $\chi^2$, and the consensus density lies inside the refit's 1$\sigma$ interval. (2) From \emph{raw HI4PI data} (\irNsight\ tangent-point
sightlines in both quadrants): their Table~2 curve reproduces at the
$\sim$\irTabTwoAbs~km\,s$^{-1}$ level, their east--west asymmetry replicates qualitatively (same sign and
comparable phase, but a period \irEwPeriodOffPct\% longer), and a head-to-head of terminal-velocity estimators measures the fixed-threshold
rule to read $+\irBiasMed$~km\,s$^{-1}$ high of Gaussian decomposition --- with the
calibrated dispersion corrections of the two estimators (\irSigGauss\ vs
\irSigThresh~km\,s$^{-1}$) bracketing the paper's adopted 15~km\,s$^{-1}$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The inner-Galaxy rotation curve underpins the Milky Way's mass model, and the
terminal-velocity method --- reading the extreme line-of-sight velocity of HI/CO emission at
the tangent point --- remains its workhorse inside the solar circle
\citep{mcclure2016,chemin2015}. \citet{sofue2025} provide the current reference analysis:
TVM applied to HI4PI \citep{hi4pi} and four CO surveys, Gaussian decomposition for the
terminal velocities, a dispersion correction calibrated at the solar circle, publication of
the RC as ASCII tables, and a mass decomposition whose halo component implies a local
dark-matter density well below consensus determinations \citep{readdm}. Replication of such
reference analyses is rare; this paper attempts one at two levels --- their published tables
(testing the decomposition and its headline density) and the raw public survey (testing the
measurement chain) --- with an independent open-source pipeline
\citep{janskyresearch}.

\section{Anchor: decomposing their published curve}
The arXiv source of \citet{sofue2025} includes the RC tables (inner curve at
$\delta R = 50$~pc, \irInnerRows\ rows; unified TVM+VERA+VLBA+Gaia curve to
\irRmax~kpc, \irUniRows\ rows). Fitting the unified curve with their model family (their
eqs.~19--27: $4\times10^{6}\,M_\odot$ point mass, Plummer bulge and disc, NFW halo) gives
Figure~\ref{fig:decomp}. Their Table~1 parameters describe their curve at
\irRmsTheirs~km\,s$^{-1}$ rms and --- converted through their
$\rho_0 = V_h^2/(G h^2)$ convention, in which their $V_h$ absorbs $\sqrt{4\pi}$ relative to
the standard normalisation --- reproduce their published
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoTheirs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ to the three significant figures they quote.
A bounded least-squares refit ($50 \le v_\mathrm{bulge} \le 800$~km\,s$^{-1}$,
$3 \le h_\mathrm{halo} \le 100$~kpc, and similarly wide bounds on the remaining parameters)
prefers a more
concentrated halo at \irRmsOurs~km\,s$^{-1}$ rms with
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoOurs$~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$, whose own $1\sigma$ halo-parameter
uncertainties give \irRhoLo--\irRhoHi~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$ --- an interval that contains the
consensus density. That interval, not the variant scan below, is what supports compatibility
with $\sim$0.3; the bulge is nearly identical between the two solutions
(\irVBulgeOurs~km\,s$^{-1}$/\irABulgeOurs~pc vs their 406/333), A sensitivity scan over halo profile (NFW/Burkert), fit
range (all $R$ vs $R>2$~kpc), and error weighting fits \irScanNFitted\ variants, but only
\irScanN\ of them are usable: in the other \irScanNRailed\ at least one parameter finishes on
a fit bound, which means the data did not determine it. (Excising $R<2$~kpc removes every
point that constrains the bulge, so $v_\mathrm{bulge}$ and $a_\mathrm{bulge}$ slide along the
Plummer degeneracy ridge until they hit walls; three variants also drive the halo scale
radius onto its lower bound.) Across the \irScanN\ variants with all parameters interior,
$\rho_\mathrm{DM} = \irRhoMin$--\irRhoMax. None reproduces their corner. Three readings are
consistent with that: an additional constraint in their fit beyond what the paper states, a
different local optimum of this degenerate six-parameter problem, or a mismatch between our
reimplemented bulge/disc functional forms and theirs --- only their \emph{halo} equation is
independently validated here, by the \irRhoTheirs\ reproduction. The conclusion is the
quantified form of their own caveat: their published curve does not pin the disc--halo split,
and the refit that fits it best puts $\rho_\mathrm{DM}$ a factor $\sim$2.3 above their value.

\section{Replication from raw HI4PI data}
From the ten HI4PI galactic-plane tiles (CDS; 2.6~GB), $|b|<3^{\circ}$ longitude--velocity
diagrams (Figure~\ref{fig:lv}) yield \irNsight\ tangent-point sightlines at
$\ell = 5$--$89.5^{\circ}$ and $270.5$--$355^{\circ}$. Terminal velocities are measured two
ways on identical spectra: Gaussian decomposition of the envelope window (greedy seeding +
joint refinement; their method, Figure~\ref{fig:lvb}), and the fixed 2~K threshold crossing
used by simple treatments. Each estimator receives the paper's \S3.3 dispersion correction,
calibrated (per estimator) so the binned curve meets $\Theta_0$ at $R_0$; since $V$ is
linear in $\sigma_v$ the calibration is closed-form.

Three results (Figures~\ref{fig:rc}--\ref{fig:ew}):

\emph{(i) The estimator bias, measured.} The threshold rule reads
$+\irBiasMed$~km\,s$^{-1}$ (median; 16th--84th percentiles $\irBiasPlo$ to
$+\irBiasPhi$) high of Gaussian decomposition on the same \irNsight\ real spectra --- same
sign as, and larger than, the $\sim$7~km\,s$^{-1}$ literature figure for threshold-vs-fitting
offsets \citep{mcclure2016}. The calibration route recovers the same offset, though it is not an
independent check: because $V$ is linear in $\sigma_v$ and both estimators are calibrated on
the same sightlines with the same weights, the difference between the two required
dispersions ($\sigma_v = \irSigGauss$ and \irSigThresh~km\,s$^{-1}$) is algebraically the
same statistic restricted to the solar-circle window. It is consistent with the direct bias
rather than equal to it. The paper's adopted $\sigma_\mathrm{HI} = 15$~km\,s$^{-1}$ falls
between the two, which is consistent with their decomposition landing between
component-centre and envelope-crossing --- though it is equally consistent with this
pipeline's Gaussian $|v_\mathrm{term}|$ reading low at the solar circle, the same sign and
scale as the Table~2 offset above.

\emph{(ii) Their Table~2 reproduces at the $\sim$4\% level.} After calibration, the binned
HI-only curve matches their published inner RC to mean
$|\Delta V| = \irTabTwoAbs$~km\,s$^{-1}$ (median $\irTabTwoMed$; \irTabTwoN\ bins,
$R>2$~kpc). The residual is a shape difference plausibly from their HI+CO merge and finer
latitude windows, and it is almost pure offset: the calibration pins the zero point on the
7.0--8.15~kpc solar window, so this figure measures a slope difference, not an overall
agreement (without the calibration the median offset is
$\irTabTwoFixedSigma$~km\,s$^{-1}$). The flat, non-Keplerian form reproduces. The bar-region
inner peak does \emph{not}: inside 2~kpc, where their curve is CO-dominated and ours is
HI-only, this curve runs $\irInnerDv$~km\,s$^{-1}$ low on average over \irInnerN\ bins, and
their $\irPeakTheirs$~km\,s$^{-1}$ peak at $R = \irPeakTheirsR$~pc appears here as
$\irPeakOurs$~km\,s$^{-1}$ at $R = \irPeakOursR$~pc. TVM is not a clean mass tracer inside
the bar, and HI is not the right tracer there; this is a limitation of the replication, not
a disagreement with their result.

\emph{(iii) The east--west asymmetry replicates qualitatively.} Fitting their damped
sinusoid (their eq.~29) in the mid-disc window (2--8~kpc; inside 2~kpc the bar rails any
smooth fit) gives amplitude \irEwAmp~km\,s$^{-1}$, period \irEwPeriod~kpc, phase
\irEwPhase~kpc against their directly stated $\simeq$15~km\,s$^{-1}$ mid-disc amplitude and eq.-29
period/phase of 4.4/4.0~kpc. The phase agrees, but the period is \irEwPeriodOffPct\% longer,
which is not a replication of the period: over a 2--8~kpc window spanning barely one cycle it
is weakly constrained, the damping length rails against its fit bound, and the fit is seeded
at their published values, so the phase agreement is partly anchored by construction. The
sinusoid accounts for under half the variance (fit residual \irEwFitRms\ against mid-disc
$\delta V$ rms \irEwRms~km\,s$^{-1}$). The honest statement is that the same phenomenon is
present, at the same sign and comparable phase and a softer amplitude, from HI alone.

\section{Discussion}

The anchor result is not a null. Their Table~1 solution and the refit are usually compared by
rms (\irRmsTheirs\ vs \irRmsOurs~km\,s$^{-1}$), which makes them look like alternative corners
of one degeneracy; in $\chi^2$ they are not ($\irChiTheirs$ vs $\irChiOurs$), and the
difference is not spread over the curve. Beyond 8~kpc --- exactly where
$\rho_\mathrm{DM}(R_0)$ is set --- their published halo sits $\irOuterBiasSig\sigma$ per point
below their own unified curve across \irOuterN\ independent points, a one-sided offset rather
than scatter. Correcting that bias is what raises the implied local density by a factor
$\sim$2.3. So the useful statement is sharper than "the split is degenerate": their published
curve prefers more local dark matter than their published decomposition reports.

Caveats bound both legs. The anchor refit does not replicate their exact fitting procedure
(weighting and algorithm are not fully specified). The sensitivity scan was intended to
bracket defensible choices, but \irScanNRailed\ of its \irScanNFitted\ variants finish with a
parameter on a fit bound and cannot be read as fits at all; the \irScanN\ interior variants
span only \irRhoMin--\irRhoMax, so the scan is a weaker instrument than the refit's own
$1\sigma$ interval (\irRhoLo--\irRhoHi) and is not what carries the compatibility claim
here. The raw-data leg is HI-only against their HI+CO merge; TVM itself is not a
clean mass tracer inside the bar \citep{chemin2015}, which both analyses exclude from
interpretation; and surrogate-model studies show terminal-velocity curves degrade substantially once a bar
forms \citep{tvmcaution} --- the estimator-level systematics quantified here are one layer of
that error budget. Within those
bounds: the reference inner RC replicates from raw public data at the few-percent level;
its published dark-matter density arithmetic is correct to the precision quoted, \emph{and}
its curve is equally consistent with the consensus local density --- but not because the
split is unconstrained. The published halo underfits the outer curve it was fitted to, and a
refit that removes that bias lands at \irRhoOurs~GeV\,cm$^{-3}$.
