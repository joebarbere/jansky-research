\begin{abstract}
The e-CALLISTO network of low-cost solar radio spectrometers \citep{benz2009} has recorded
45--870~MHz dynamic spectra worldwide since 2002, an unintended long-baseline record of the
radio-frequency-interference (RFI) environment. The only published RFI-trend study on the
archive \citep{prieto2020} is a two-epoch (2012 vs.\ 2019) campaign that found increased
interference levels at the Spanish sites; it stops in 2019, the year Starlink deployment scaled. We examine the
continuous archive over 2012--2026, sampling one representative spectrum per station-month from
the open FITS record at \rfRealNStations\ long-running stations and reducing each to a
burst-immune occupancy metric, a per-channel time median that a transient solar burst or satellite
pass does not move. e-CALLISTO log-power drifts with uncalibrated station gain, a common-mode
systematic that would fake a trend, so our primary metric is self-normalizing: the excess level at
the intrinsic narrowband Starlink unintended-emission (UEM) lines (125/135/150/175~MHz;
\citealt{divruno2023}) over their immediately adjacent channels. This cancels common-mode
station gain and, unlike a fixed FM control, survives the per-station RFI-avoidance notches that leave many
e-CALLISTO instruments without the FM band. We test the trend against the public Starlink
on-orbit count \citep{planet4589}. The result is a systematics-limited null. Across
\rfRealNMonths\ usable station-months, the two stations that sample UEM lines disagree in sign:
HUMAIN's fitted line-excess trend is positive ($\rfRealHUMAINSlope$
[$\rfRealHUMAINSlopeLo$, $\rfRealHUMAINSlopeHi$] per year), but a step analysis of the same
committed series shows it is not a trend at all: single-month discontinuities of
\rfRealHumStepTwoDelta\ (at \rfRealHumStepTwoYr) and $+$\rfRealHumStepThreeDelta\ (at
\rfRealHumStepThreeYr) separate plateaus within which no regime rises (largest within-regime
slope \rfRealHumMaxRegimeSlope\ per year, $p=\rfRealHumMaxRegimeP$) --- the signature of
receiver reconfiguration, and the downward step spans exactly the interval in which the
constellation grew from 0 to $\sim$1900 satellites. ALMATY's excess falls
($\rfRealALMATYSlope$ [$\rfRealALMATYSlopeLo$, $\rfRealALMATYSlopeHi$] per year). A genuine
global signal should raise every station's UEM lines together; the sign disagreement
(\rfRealNRising\ rising, \rfRealNFalling\ falling; \code{coherent\_rise}=\rfRealCoherent) is
consistent with per-station and local-RFI effects dominating --- and the null carries its
measured sensitivity: injecting a common Starlink-shaped rise into both committed series, the
coherence criterion reaches 90\% power only at \rfRealAmpNinety\ log-units, larger than the
largest excursion in the data, so a modest common signal would not have been detected. The
positive pooled slope survives equalising the station month counts
(\rfRealEqualNSlope\ [\rfRealEqualNLo, \rfRealEqualNHi] per year), so it is dominated by
HUMAIN's step amplitude, not its month count. We claim no Starlink attribution and report the
validated burst-immune metric, the notch-robust self-normalizing line test, the
pipeline-generated coherence verdict with its power curve, and the per-station systematics
accounting.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large low-Earth-orbit satellite constellations radiate unintended electromagnetic emission (UEM)
that falls inside protected and scientifically used radio bands. Dedicated-instrument campaigns
have detected Starlink UEM directly: LOFAR sees broadband emission between 110 and 188~MHz plus
narrowband features \citep{divruno2023}, and the second-generation satellites are brighter still
\citep{bassa2024}. These are targeted observations with calibrated interferometers. What has not
been examined is whether the archival record of a distributed, uncalibrated spectrograph
network already carries the constellation-growth signal, a question of spectrum management as
much as astronomy, and one the e-CALLISTO archive is positioned to answer because it spans
the entire deployment era with the same simple receivers.

The only RFI-trend precedent on this archive \citep{prieto2020} compares two epochs, 2012 and 2019,
at Spanish sites and reports increased interference levels across the band, but
it ends in 2019, the year the first operational Starlink batches launched. The 2012--2026
e-CALLISTO record is therefore an unexamined 15-year RFI observatory covering the megaconstellation
ramp, and extending the two-epoch result into a continuous trend, attributed to the public
constellation count, is unclaimed. We do that here, with the caveat stated up front: the
archive can ask whether a post-2019, UEM-band-specific rise tracks Starlink growth, but it
cannot by itself prove causation, because general terrestrial RFI grows over the same period.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
e-CALLISTO values are uncalibrated digital log-power, and two effects
would manufacture or mask a trend: (i) station gain drift, in which receiver and antenna changes
move the whole 45--870~MHz spectrum up or down over years, and (ii) solar bursts,
broadband transients that raise all frequencies for minutes. Both are common-mode, moving
the band together, and our metrics are built to cancel both.

For a band we compute, per channel, the time median over the
15-minute sweep, the persistent occupied level, and take the median of those channel floors
as the band occupancy. A solar burst or satellite pass occupies a small fraction of the sweep, so
it does not move a time median; the metric reads the persistent RFI floor, not transients. We
verify on synthetic stacks that turning bursts on in up to 90\% of months does not change the
recovered trend (\S\ref{sec:synth}).

The natural gain-cancelling metric is a
differential, the UEM-band occupancy minus that of a fixed-transmitter control band at the
same station, so a common-mode gain drift subtracts out. The cleanest such control is the FM
broadcast band (88--108~MHz), a strong stable terrestrial transmitter. On real e-CALLISTO data,
however, this fails at the archive's best UEM-band station: HUMAIN samples no FM channels at all
(its committed control assignment is the low band, which the pipeline selects only when fewer than
four channels fall in 88--108~MHz), and notches other sub-bands too. e-CALLISTO operators configure station-specific RFI-avoidance notches,
so a fixed FM control is not universally observed. We therefore make the differential's control
station-adaptive, falling back to a sampled clean band below or above the UEM window, and
promote to primary a metric that needs no external control at all.

The primary metric is the narrowband line-vs-adjacent excess. \citet{divruno2023} identify
narrowband Starlink UEM features intrinsic to the satellites at 125, 135, 150 and 175~MHz (a
fifth feature at 143.05~MHz is reflected GRAVES space-surveillance radar, not intrinsic, and we
exclude it). A genuine intrinsic UEM line sits above the channels immediately around it,
whereas station gain or broadband terrestrial RFI raise a line and its neighbourhood together. For
each sampled UEM line we take the level within $\pm$1~MHz minus the level in the flanking channels
and average. This is self-normalizing within the UEM band: it cancels common-mode
station gain by construction and is robust to the per-station notches, since a line simply drops out
if its region is not sampled. It does not cancel differential local contamination of a line's
flanking channels, which, as \S\ref{sec:real} shows, is the residual systematic that
limits the study. Configuration stability is enforced by requiring a station to keep the same
set of sampled UEM lines across its retained months; months with a different sampled grid are
dropped so the trend is not an instrument-reconfiguration artifact. This screen keys on the sampled
line-set only, not on level continuity, so within-set receiver reconfigurations can survive it, a
limitation the per-station results make visible rather than hide.

We regress the metric against decimal year (robust Theil--Sen slope with a
Kendall-$\tau$ significance, so a single anomalous month cannot swing it) and correlate it with the
public cumulative on-orbit Starlink count \citep{planet4589}, a hand-vendored table of rounded
yearly anchors interpolated linearly (so the regressor is nonzero from early 2019 --- between the
2019.0 and mid-2019 anchors --- rises monotonically, and clamps at the last anchor for months
after 2026.0); no Space-Track credentials are required. As a qualitative pre-vs-post
baseline check we also compare the raw UEM occupancy in 2012--2013 against 2018--2019, in the spirit
of the two-epoch \citet{prieto2020} campaign, though this is the gain-contaminated raw level (not
their occupancy statistic) at a different station, so it is a consistency note, not a reproduction.

\section{Synthetic recover-a-known}
\label{sec:synth}
We build \rfSynNMonths\ synthetic monthly spectra with a known UEM-band level rise and
narrowband line excesses at the intrinsic UEM frequencies, both shaped like the Starlink count, plus
per-month common-mode gain drift and broadband solar bursts in a configurable fraction of months.
Both metrics recover the injected trend, and the recovery is scored against the stated truth: the
Theil--Sen slope of the injected line series is \rfSynTruthLineSlope\ per year and the primary
line-vs-adjacent excess recovers \rfSynLineExcessSlope\ (ratio \rfSynLineRecovery); the
secondary differential recovers \rfSynSlope\ per year ($p=\rfSynTrendP$). The correlations with
the injected Starlink shape ($r=\rfSynLineCorr$) are near unity \emph{by construction} on these
clean arms --- the metric returns the injected quantity plus noise --- so they test algebra, not
discrimination; the arm that can fail is the next one. Local RFI accruing in a line's flanking
channels only (a linear ramp, uncorrelated with the Starlink curve) is the systematic the
line-vs-adjacent difference cannot cancel. Sweeping its total amplitude against the fixed injected
line rise of 4.0: the recovered sign crosses zero at a flank ramp of only
$\sim$\rfSynFlankCross\ --- about $0.8\times$ the true line rise --- and at a flank ramp of 5.0
the recovered pair (slope \rfSynFlankAtFiveSlope\ per year, Starlink correlation
\rfSynFlankAtFiveCorr) reproduces ALMATY's measured pair almost exactly, a quantitative
demonstration that ALMATY's fall is mechanistically consistent with local flank contamination. At
the fiducial 6.0 the bias is \rfSynFlankBias\ per year. A station's falling line excess
therefore cannot, by itself, be read as a falling UEM signal. A null control built from two bands
both outside the UEM window does not trend (slope \rfSynCtrlSlope\ per year), and burst immunity
is measured on the primary metric, not asserted: the recovered slope is \rfSynBurstNone\ with no
bursts and \rfSynBurstHeavy\ with bursts in 90\% of months. These validations run offline in
continuous integration.

\section{The real archive: 2012--2026}
\label{sec:real}
We sampled one representative spectrum per station-month over 2012--2026 at three e-CALLISTO
stations spanning the era (\rfRealNMonths\ station-months entering the line analysis, from the
two line-sampling stations, after config-stability screening). The three are a fixed list chosen for
their span, not the survivors of an archive-wide stability screen, and the screening attrition is
uneven: HUMAIN keeps \rfRealHUMAINNMonths\ of \rfRealHUMAINNMonthsRaw\ months and GLASGOW
\rfRealGLASGOWNMonths\ of \rfRealGLASGOWNMonthsRaw, but ALMATY keeps only
\rfRealALMATYNMonths\ of \rfRealALMATYNMonthsRaw. ALMATY is the station whose falling
slope carries the sign disagreement below, and its screening is not span-neutral: the retained
ALMATY window starts at 2015.5 while HUMAIN's starts at 2013.1, shortening ALMATY's
pre-deployment baseline by $\sim$2.3 years. The mirror check closes that worry: HUMAIN
restricted to ALMATY's window gives slope \rfRealWinSlope\ per year with Starlink correlation
\rfRealWinCorr, essentially unchanged, so the window truncation does not manufacture the
disagreement. We reduced each month to the burst-immune line-excess
and station-adaptive differential. The three stations
sample the UEM band very differently, a difference that bears directly on the coherence test below
(Table~\ref{tab:stations}). Only
two of the three observe UEM lines with a stable core-plus-flank grid; GLASGOW samples no UEM line
region stably and drops out of the line analysis entirely.

The two line-sampling stations disagree in sign, and neither behaves like a constellation.
HUMAIN's fitted line-excess trend is $\rfRealHUMAINSlope$
[$\rfRealHUMAINSlopeLo$, $\rfRealHUMAINSlopeHi$] per year ($p=\rfRealHUMAINP$,
$r=\rfRealHUMAINStarCorr$ against the Starlink count) --- but the fit is not describing a trend.
A step analysis of the committed series finds three single-month discontinuities
($+$\rfRealHumStepOneDelta\ at \rfRealHumStepOneYr, \rfRealHumStepTwoDelta\ at
\rfRealHumStepTwoYr, $+$\rfRealHumStepThreeDelta\ at \rfRealHumStepThreeYr), each larger than
the fitted total change over the whole 13-year baseline, separating plateaus within which no
regime rises (the largest within-regime slope is \rfRealHumMaxRegimeSlope\ per year at
$p=\rfRealHumMaxRegimeP$). The \rfRealHumStepTwoDelta\ step is \emph{downward} and spans
exactly the 2019--2022 interval in which the constellation grew from 0 to $\sim$1900 satellites
--- behaviour a megaconstellation signal cannot produce and receiver reconfiguration does. The
Starlink-shape correlation exists only because the 2022--2026 plateau is higher than the
2013--2017 baseline; against a plain linear ramp the correlation is \rfRealHumShapeRamp\ and
against a 2019.4 step \rfRealHumShapeStep. HUMAIN's raw UEM occupancy rose by
\rfRealHUMAINPerez\ log-units between 2012--2013 and 2018--2019, qualitatively consistent with
the pre-Starlink rise \citet{prieto2020} found, read as a consistency note rather than a
reproduction. ALMATY trends the opposite way: its line excess (lines \rfRealALMATYLines~MHz)
falls ($\rfRealALMATYSlope$ [$\rfRealALMATYSlopeLo$, $\rfRealALMATYSlopeHi$] per year,
$p=\rfRealALMATYP$, $r=\rfRealALMATYStarCorr$) --- an interval whose upper bound sits just
below zero, and a fall the flank-contamination arm of \S\ref{sec:synth} reproduces
quantitatively.

The cross-station coherence test is the paper's primary discriminator. A real global Starlink UEM
signal should raise every station's UEM lines together. Our pipeline computes, from the
significant per-station slopes, whether they agree in sign: they do not (\rfRealNRising\ rising,
\rfRealNFalling\ falling; \code{coherent\_rise}=\rfRealCoherent). The positive network-pooled
slope (\rfRealLineExcessSlope\ per year, $p=\rfRealTrendP$, $r=\rfRealCorr$ with Starlink) is
not a count artifact --- equalising the two stations' month counts leaves it at
\rfRealEqualNSlope\ [\rfRealEqualNLo, \rfRealEqualNHi] per year over 500 subsamples --- it is
dominated by the sheer amplitude of HUMAIN's step excursion ($\pm$13 units against ALMATY's
$\pm$2), which is the correct reason to distrust it. We claim no Starlink attribution and no
megaconstellation detection. The result is consistent with per-station effects limiting any
common signal: local RFI-environment change, receiver and antenna reconfiguration (e-CALLISTO
instruments are uncalibrated and operator-tuned; the HUMAIN staircase above is the sharpest
concrete example), and station-specific occupancy of the flanking channels that normalize each
line.

The coherence test's sensitivity is measured, not asserted. Injecting a common Starlink-shaped
rise of total amplitude $A$ into both committed series (detrended, residuals circularly shifted
so the real autocorrelation and step structure survive) and applying the pipeline's own
criterion: the detection probability is \rfRealPowerAtSix\ at $A=6$ log-units --- a signal the
size of HUMAIN's fitted total change --- and reaches 90\% only at $A=\rfRealAmpNinety$, larger
than the largest excursion in the data. The null therefore excludes only a common rise of
$\gtrsim$\rfRealAmpNinety\ log-units at high confidence; below that the two-station test has
limited power. Opposite signs also do not strictly rule out a real global signal for a second
reason: the two stations sample partly different lines (Table~\ref{tab:stations}), and because
the line-excess is self-normalized against its flanks, a station whose flank channels accrue
local terrestrial RFI faster than the line can register a falling excess even under a rising
true signal --- the degeneracy the flank arm quantifies. The test therefore shows the data are
dominated by per-station systematics; it does not prove the absence of any Starlink
contribution. Because our conclusion is a null, both asymmetries are conservative.

An earlier draft of this work flagged HUMAIN as a follow-up candidate "whose rise is real,
significant, and Starlink-correlated". The step analysis retires that framing: what is real at
HUMAIN is a 2022--2026 plateau sitting above the 2013--2017 baseline, reached through
single-month reconfiguration-like steps --- including a downward one through the deployment era
--- with no rise inside any regime. If a follow-up is run, it should be satellite-pass-gated
occupancy within one instrument configuration, and per-month file provenance (which day and time
each sampled spectrum came from --- computed by the sampler but not previously recorded) should
be committed first, so a step epoch can be checked against a sampling change. We claim nothing
about HUMAIN beyond what the staircase shows.

\section{Reproducibility}

(\code{python -m jansky\_research.rfitrend --offline}). The real census streams the open e-CALLISTO
FITS archive one small gzipped file at a time, entirely in memory
(\code{scripts/rfitrend\_real.py}). Code, tests, and this paper's pipeline are in the
\code{jansky-research} repository, built on the \code{jansky} course library \citep{jansky}. Draft
prepared with the assistance of a large language model \citep{anthropicclaude}; all results are
pipeline-generated and human-verified.
