\title{Is the Extragalactic Rotation-Measure Sky Isotropic? A First Dipole Test
on $10^5$ Faraday Rotation Measures from SPICE-RACS DR2}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Every test of the cosmic dipole anomaly to date uses source counts or flux densities as the
tracer \citep{secrest2025,boehme2025,landstrykowski2025,mittal2026}; to our knowledge no published test uses Faraday
rotation measures (RMs). The kinematic (observer-motion) expectation for RM is a Doppler rescaling,
$\mathrm{RM}_{\rm obs} = \mathrm{RM}\,(1+\beta\cos\theta)^2$: a signed dipole of fractional
amplitude $2\beta \approx 2.5\times10^{-3}$, or $4\beta \approx 4.9\times10^{-3}$ in the
power statistic fitted here, roughly at the smallest per-leg fit precision this catalogue
reaches and far below the scramble-null sensitivity, and largely absorbed by local foreground
subtraction. We therefore test whether the
extragalactic RM sky is statistically isotropic at dipole order, using SPICE-RACS DR2
\citep{spiceracs_dr2}, the first catalogue with the source count this test needs. From \rmdRealN\
quality-flagged (goodRM, S/N$\ge$8) components at
$|b|\ge45\arcdeg$ we form extragalactic residuals with the catalogue's own nearest-neighbour
Galactic-RM subtraction \citep[the convention of][]{malik2026}, fit a monopole$+$dipole model to
the noise-debiased residual RM power and to $|$residual$|$, and calibrate significance with
footprint-preserving Dec-band scrambles. The survey's Dec$\,\le\,$$+49\arcdeg$ footprint is the
dominant systematic, so the scramble null is load-bearing. Validation is three-way: an injected 0.3 power
dipole is recovered on the real footprint without bias ($0.301\pm0.009$ over 20 realizations,
apex within \rmdRealInjSepTrue$\arcdeg$), survives the tail clip (still $p=$ \rmdRealInjClipP),
and is detected when painted onto the real heavy-tailed residual field. On the real sky the power statistic rejects its scramble null
(amplitude \rmdRealAmp$\pm$\rmdRealAmpSe, $p=$ \rmdRealPScramble), but the rejection is
carried entirely by the top 1\% of $|$residual$|$: clipping those \rmdRealClipN\ sources returns
the sky to consistency with isotropy ($p=$ \rmdRealClipP, stable across clip quantiles
0.95--0.995), the outlier-robust $|$residual$|$ statistic on the widest sample is likewise
isotropic ($p=$ \rmdRealWideAbsP), and every variant's apex lies $80\arcdeg$--$153\arcdeg$ from
the CMB dipole apex. The test excludes RA-projected power-dipole
amplitudes $|p|/m > \rmdRealClipNullNinetyFive$ (the clipped-core null's 95th percentile) and
constrains nothing below that; it is blind to a polar-axis dipole by construction. The
catalogued noise power carries its own small dipole (amplitude \rmdRealNoiseAmp) pointing
elsewhere, excluding mis-modelling that follows the catalogued noise map as the driver. We
conclude that the RA-projected extragalactic RM sky is isotropic at dipole order once the
$\sim$1\% outlier tail is removed, to an excluded amplitude of \rmdRealClipNullNinetyFive, with no
evidence for any CMB-aligned dipole; the tail's own dipole points at
(\rmdRealTailApexRa$\arcdeg$, $+$\rmdRealTailApexDec$\arcdeg$), toward the survey's declination
edge, a Galactic-subtraction/leakage/$n\pi$-ambiguity systematics statement, not a cosmological
one. The tooling (pure NumPy, tested, with injection
validation) is built to run on the coming POSSUM full-survey RM grid, where per-source
redshifts and lower leakage floors should sharpen the test.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The cosmic dipole anomaly (radio/quasar source-count dipoles exceeding the kinematic
expectation from the CMB dipole by factors of $\sim$2--4 \citep{secrest2025,boehme2025}) is
tested exclusively with number counts and flux densities, the observables for which
\citet{ellisbaldwin1984} derived the kinematic prediction. Faraday rotation measures are an
entirely separate observable carried by much of the same source population, yet the RM sky has
never been subjected to a catalogue-scale dipole/isotropy test. The closest prior art is
decades old and asked a different question: a uniform cosmological magnetic field would
imprint a signed mean-RM dipole, which \citet{kronberg1976,vallee1990} bounded with
$\mathcal{O}(10^2)$ RMs and \citet{kolatt1998} proposed correlation statistics for; more
recently \citet{mtchedlidze2026} simulated the primordial-field RM sky without fitting any
observed catalogue. SPICE-RACS DR2 \citep{spiceracs_dr2}, with $\sim$$3.3\times10^5$
quality-flagged RMs over 87.5\% of the sky, makes such a test possible.

Two facts bound what such a test can mean. First, the kinematic expectation for RM is
negligible at present sensitivity. An observer boost
rescales the observed RM by $(1+\beta\cos\theta)^2$ (the same wavelength-squared scaling that
produces the $(1+z)^{-2}$ redshift dilution), a signed dipole of amplitude
$2\beta \approx 2.5\times10^{-3}$ for the CMB-inferred velocity, far below this test's amplitude
uncertainties ($0.006$--$0.065$) and largely removed by local foreground subtraction, since
neighbouring sources share the factor. A dipole detected at measurable amplitude therefore could
not be kinematic, and a null does not constrain our velocity; we frame the measurement strictly
as a statistical-isotropy test.
Second, extragalactic RMs are residuals after subtracting a Galactic foreground an order of
magnitude larger \citep{mao2010}, and any signed mean-RM dipole is partially absorbed by
local foreground subtraction; the statistics tested here are dipoles in the residual
amplitude/power, which survive it. A Vall\'ee-style uniform-field limit from this
catalogue would require model-based (not local) foreground subtraction and is deferred.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:method}
We start from the DR2 goodRM sample (S/N$_{\rm polint}\ge8$; the survey's leakage, S/N,
Stokes-I-fit and channel flags all clean), and form per-source extragalactic residuals
$r_i = \mathrm{RM}_i - \mathrm{GRM}_i$ using the catalogue's own nearest-neighbour local RM
median (\code{nn\_rm\_med}, $\ge$5 neighbours) as the Galactic estimate \citep{malik2026}, at
$|b|\ge45\arcdeg$ (primary) and $|b|\ge30\arcdeg$; a 5$\arcdeg$-latitude-band median
subtraction is the cross-check path (it leaves longitude-dependent Galactic structure in the
residuals by construction, and behaves accordingly; \S\ref{sec:results}).

For each sample we fit $y_i = m + \vec p\cdot\hat n_i$ by least squares, with
$y_i = r_i^2 - \sigma_i^2$ (noise-debiased residual power; the debias is exact in expectation)
as the primary statistic and $y_i = |r_i|$ as the outlier-robust companion, reporting the
fractional amplitude $|\vec p|/m$, the apex direction, and source-bootstrap uncertainties.
Significance comes from footprint-preserving scrambles: residuals are permuted among the real
source positions within $5\arcdeg$ Dec bands, preserving the footprint and any Dec-dependent
systematic exactly while destroying all RA structure. Because band means survive every
permutation, a real dipole's polar (Dec-projected) component is shared by the observed and null
amplitudes alike, so the test constrains the RA-projected dipole and is conservative for a
general one; a dipole aligned with the celestial polar axis would be invisible to it.
Sensitivity to a CMB-aligned dipole is nearly full only because the CMB apex sits at Dec
$-6.9\arcdeg$ \citep[$(167.94\arcdeg, -6.94\arcdeg)$ equatorial;][]
{planck2020}. Diagnostics: $|r|$-clipped power fits with the clip quantile swept over 0.95--0.995
(tail-drivenness, with the choice shown not to matter), a dipole fit to the clipped-out tail
itself, a dipole fit to the catalogued noise power $\sigma_i^2$ (a ``power dipole'' sharing its
apex with the noise map would be mis-modelled noise, not sky), and the neighbour-count
requirement varied over 3--10 (it does not bind: the sample is unchanged, so the $\ge$5
criterion in the selection above describes the catalogue, not a cut). Validation is
injection-based end to end, three ways. At the real source positions, twenty realizations of an
injected $A=0.3$ Gaussian power dipole are recovered without bias (mean $0.301\pm0.009$
seed-to-seed, matching the single-realization bootstrap SE of 0.009; an earlier version of this
paper read one low draw as a partial-sky bias, which the ensemble refutes) and within
\rmdRealInjSepTrue$\arcdeg$ in direction. The same injection pushed through the 99\% clip is
attenuated but still detected (recovered \rmdRealInjClipAmp, $p=$ \rmdRealInjClipP), so the
clip cannot manufacture the isotropic-core result from a real dipole. And a dipole painted onto
the real residual field (sign-randomised, then variance-modulated) is detected at
$p=$ \rmdRealInjRealP, establishing detectability in the real, heavy-tailed distribution and
not merely in a Gaussian surrogate; its recovered amplitude (\rmdRealInjRealAmp\ for an
injected 0.3) shows the fractional-amplitude scale is not calibrated on the real field, which is
why our conclusions are stated in detection/exclusion terms rather than as amplitude
measurements.

Table~\ref{tab:legs} is the result. Four features are robust across variants. (i) The
noise-debiased power statistic rejects its scramble null in every sample/subtraction
variant. (ii) Its amplitude and apex are method-dependent: the latitude-band subtraction
(which leaves longitude-dependent Galactic structure in the residuals) yields larger
amplitudes than the nearest-neighbour subtraction (and its $|r|$ statistic also rejects,
$p=0.001$ at both cuts, the same contamination signature), the expected behaviour of residual
Galactic structure rather than sky-stationary extragalactic anisotropy. (iii) The power anisotropy
is a tail phenomenon: removing the top 1\% of $|r|$ (\rmdRealClipN\ of \rmdRealN\
sources) drops the amplitude from \rmdRealAmp\ to \rmdRealClipAmp\ and the scramble $p$-value
from \rmdRealPScramble\ to \rmdRealClipP, stably across clip quantiles 0.95--0.995
($p=$ 0.78--0.93), and the outlier-robust $|r|$ statistic on the widest, best-subtracted sample
($|b|\ge30\arcdeg$, nearest-neighbour) is likewise consistent with its null
($p=$ \rmdRealWideAbsP). The isotropy statement carries its sensitivity: the clipped-core
scramble null's 95th-percentile amplitude is \rmdRealClipNullNinetyFive, so dipoles above that are
excluded and nothing below it is constrained. (iv$'$) The clipped-out tail fitted alone has
amplitude 0.52 at $p=0.002$ with apex ($25\arcdeg$, $+65\arcdeg$), beyond the survey's
Dec $+49\arcdeg$ edge where leakage and beam-distance systematics live, and $65\arcdeg$ from
the full-sample apex the tail was previously assumed to share. One entry is marginal: the
$|r|$ statistic on the primary sample itself gives $p=0.023$ (apex $82\arcdeg$ from the CMB).
With no trials correction across the eight sky-statistic variants tested (the chance of one
$p\le0.023$ among eight independent trials is 17\%, and the nested, source-sharing legs make
the true look-elsewhere probability smaller, i.e.\ this reading is conservative in the
direction that favours our own null), and with no persistence in the $1.7\times$ larger
$|b|\ge30\arcdeg$ sample, we read it as an echo of the same tail, not as evidence of core
anisotropy. The core of the residual distribution is isotropic;
$\sim$$10^3$ high-$|r|$ sources with an anisotropic sky distribution carry the whole signal.
(iv) The catalogued noise power $\sigma^2$ has its own statistically significant but small
dipole (amplitude \rmdRealNoiseAmp, apex $\sim$$150\arcdeg$ from the power apex). Survey depth
is not uniform, but the noise dipole neither matches the power dipole's direction nor its
magnitude, excluding mis-modelled noise as the carrier. No variant's apex lies near the CMB
dipole apex (separations $80\arcdeg$--$153\arcdeg$).

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
The extragalactic RM sky at $10^5$-source scale is isotropic at dipole order in its
distributional core, and shows no CMB-aligned dipole in any variant: whatever the
source-count dipole excess \citep{boehme2025} is, it has no counterpart in Faraday-rotation
power pointing the same way at current sensitivity. The tail-carried power anisotropy we do
measure ($\sim$$10^3$ sources, $\sim$1\% of the sample, anisotropically distributed toward
apex $\sim$$(70\arcdeg,+10\arcdeg)$) is most conservatively read as the residual-systematics
floor of the present catalogue: imperfect local Galactic subtraction
\citep[cf.][]{vacca2026}, leakage survivors, and $n\pi$-ambiguity outliers are all plausible
carriers, none of which is cosmological. The permutation null also assumes within-band
exchangeability, but the RM residual field has measured degree-scale correlations in this very
catalogue (its structure function rises to $\sim$$3\arcdeg$--$4\arcdeg$), so small $p$-values
strictly read ``RA structure exists,'' not ``a dipole exists'', which is precisely the
systematics reading we adopt. Identifying those $\sim$$10^3$ sources individually
(are they clustered on particular tiles? near bright sources? do they share \code{rm\_width}
morphology?) is the immediate follow-on and may itself be a useful DR2 quality diagnostic. Two follow-ons are natural: a model-based
Galactic subtraction (which would unlock the signed mean-RM dipole and a modern
\citealt{vallee1990} uniform-field limit at $\sim$$10^3\times$ the old sample size), and
repeating the test on POSSUM's full RM grid with redshifts. The method, its injection
validation, and its honest nulls are the contribution; the tooling is a $\sim$500-line tested
module that runs on any RM catalogue with positions and errors.
