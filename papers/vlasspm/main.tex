\title{No Optically Dark Compact Radio Movers in Four VLASS Epochs}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}

\begin{abstract}
We searched the four VLASS Quick Look component catalogues for sources that move, without
using optical or infrared positions until the last step. The search recovers UV~Ceti at
\vpmRealUVMu~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$ (Gaia: \vpmRealUVGaiaMu) and finds no other mover. Positional
errors are the catalogue's elliptical Gaussian errors plus floors and a structure term
calibrated on \vpmRealNStaticPairs~million pairs of static detections, and a compactness cut calibrated on
the measured sizes of nearby Gaia stars reduces \vpmRealNCandBefore\ candidates to UV~Ceti alone;
the other \vpmRealNStaticVetted\ are static extended sources in the images. For compact sources
above 3~mJy moving at \vpmRealRateMid--\vpmRealRateMax~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$ with no Gaia or CatWISE
counterpart, the 95\% upper limit is $\vpmRealLimThree$~deg$^{-2}$ over
\vpmRealAreaDeg~deg$^2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Search}
\label{sec:search}
Radio proper motions have so far been measured for objects already known to move: stars
selected by their Gaia or optical proper motions and then found in radio surveys
\citep{driessen2023,de2024}. A blind search asks the reverse question and can find movers with
no optical counterpart, such as cool brown dwarfs or high-velocity pulsars. The VLA Sky Survey
\citep[VLASS;][]{lacy2020} has now covered the sky north of Dec $-40^\circ$ four times at
3~GHz with a 2.5-arcsec beam. We used the CIRADA component tables for epochs 1 and 2
\citep{gordon2021} and the NRAO Quick Look lists for epochs 3 and 4, all fitted by PyBDSF
\citep{pybdsf}: after the quality cuts they hold \vpmRealNEOne, \vpmRealNETwo, \vpmRealNEThree\
and \vpmRealNEFour\ components observed between \vpmRealTStart\ and \vpmRealTEnd.

A component with no counterpart within 2.5~arcsec in another epoch is an orphan, and only
orphans with no other component within 30~arcsec in their own epoch are used, because split
extended sources produce correlated orphans. Orphans in two epochs are linked when the implied
rate lies between \vpmRealRateMin\ and \vpmRealRateMax~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$ and the shift is
significant at $3\sigma$; a third epoch must then contain an orphan within $3\sigma$ of the
extrapolated straight line at its own observing date. Every triple of epochs is searched.

Each component's positional error is the elliptical Gaussian error of \citet{condon1997}, with
the effective signal-to-noise of \citet{condon1998} and the exponents as in \citet{prandoni2000},
computed from its fitted axes, position angle, beam and peak signal-to-noise. It reproduces the
catalogue errors. To it we add an astrometric floor measured from bright static sources in
declination bands (\vpmRealFloorMin--\vpmRealFloorMax~arcsec) and a structure term: a resolved
source's centroid moves between epochs by a fraction \vpmRealKStruct\ of its deconvolved size
along each of its axes. That fraction was fitted to the offsets of \vpmRealNStaticPairs~million
pairs of isolated static sources matched between epochs, so that their $3\sigma$ tail matches a Rayleigh
distribution, and checked on a held-out half of the sky: the fraction of static sources beyond
$3\sigma$ falls from \vpmRealTailMeas\ with the catalogue errors to \vpmRealTailFit\ (expected
0.011). The calibrated errors remain too narrow beyond about $4\sigma$ (a fraction
\vpmRealTailFitFive\ of static sources lies beyond $5\sigma$) and south of Dec $-35^\circ$
(\vpmRealTailSouthRatio\ times the Rayleigh fraction beyond $3\sigma$), and too wide in the core
(median normalised offset \vpmRealMedZFit, against 1.18).

Nearly all false candidates are resolved static sources, whereas stars and pulsars are
unresolved at 2.5~arcsec, so we also require compact detections, with compactness defined as the
deconvolved major axis divided by the beam major axis. A faint point source is not fitted with
zero size, and the threshold was set on injected point sources whose catalogue sizes were drawn,
by signal-to-noise, from \vpmRealNStars\ detections of stars within 100~pc in the Gaia
Catalogue of Nearby Stars \citep{gcns2021} (about \vpmRealNStarChance\ expected by chance). The
criterion was fixed before the cut was applied to the data: the smallest threshold that keeps at
least 95\% of the recovered injections overall and in every signal-to-noise bin, with the
cross-epoch rule chosen by its rejection of the general static population. Requiring two of
three detections below \vpmRealCutThr\ keeps \vpmRealKeepAll\ of the injections
(\vpmRealKeepLowSNR\ below signal-to-noise 7) and removes \vpmRealStaticRej\% of static sources;
requiring all three needs a threshold of \vpmRealCutThrAll\ and removes \vpmRealStaticRejAll\%.
Between \vpmRealRateLo\ and \vpmRealRateMid~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$ the cut keeps only \vpmRealKeepSlow\
of the injections, because the target was set per signal-to-noise bin rather than per rate, and
the star sample is too small to show whether the size noise differs between epochs.
Rotating the later epochs by a few arcminutes in right ascension gives the expected number of
chance alignments, and \vpmRealNInj\ movers injected at random sky positions with the same
errors and size noise give the completeness. On a synthetic field the pipeline recovers
\vpmSynNRecovered\ of \vpmSynNMovers\ planted movers with \vpmSynNFalse\ false candidates.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
UV~Ceti is recovered from epochs 2--4 at \vpmRealUVMu~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$ with a straight-line
residual of \vpmRealUVResid$\sigma$; it is below the epoch-1 detection limit. Gaia DR3
\citep{gaiadr3} gives \vpmRealUVGaiaMu~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$; the difference may come from the
companion BL~Cet, about 2~arcsec away and blended in the VLASS beam. Its compactness in the three epochs is \vpmRealUVCompA, \vpmRealUVCompB\
and \vpmRealUVCompC, so it passes on two detections.

Before the compactness cut the search returns \vpmRealNCandBefore\ candidates. Cutouts of the
other \vpmRealNStaticVetted\ show emission persisting at the first-epoch position; most are
extended, and \vpmRealNSouthVetted\ lie south of Dec $-35^\circ$, where the beam in some epochs
is strongly elongated. The cut removes all \vpmRealNStaticVetted\ and leaves UV~Ceti alone. The scrambled
catalogues give at most \vpmRealNullCand\ chance candidates per trial in any triple of epochs.

At 3~mJy the completeness is \vpmRealCompFloorThreeCut\ below \vpmRealRateLo~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$
and \vpmRealCompSlowThreeCut\ between \vpmRealRateLo\ and \vpmRealRateMid~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$,
because a slower mover stays within the 2.5-arcsec static match radius (Figure~\ref{fig:comp}). Averaged over \vpmRealRateMid--\vpmRealRateMax~arcsec~yr$^{-1}$ it is
\vpmRealCompThreeCut\ at 3~mJy (\vpmRealCompThreeNoCut\ without the cut) and
\vpmRealCompOneFiveCut\ at 1.5~mJy (\vpmRealCompOneFiveNoCut). With no new mover, and no Gaia DR3
or CatWISE2020 \citep{catwise2020} counterpart required, the 95\% upper limits on the surface
density of compact movers in that range are $\vpmRealLimThree$~deg$^{-2}$ at 3~mJy and
$\vpmRealLimOneFive$~deg$^{-2}$ at 1.5~mJy, or fewer than \vpmRealAllSky\ over the whole sky.
The limit applies only to movers unresolved at 2.5~arcsec: the cut removes a mover with resolved
emission, such as a pulsar with a bow-shock nebula, and a binary a few tenths of a beam wide that
is fitted as one elongated source in two epochs.
