\title{A Reproducible CPU-Only Pipeline for VLASS Multi-Epoch Radio Variability:
Quick-Look Systematics, a \vlassRealArea\,deg$^2$ Census, and the Recovery of FK~Comae~Berenices}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The Karl G.\ Jansky Very Large Array Sky Survey \citep[VLASS;][]{lacy2020} imaged the sky north of
$\delta=-40\arcdeg$ at 2--4~GHz in three epochs (2017--2024), making it a natural hunting ground for
radio variability. We ask how much of that variability is accessible from the public Quick-Look (QL)
component catalogues alone, with a small, tested, CPU-only tool. Cross-matching the three QL epochs
and selecting outliers in the standard variability metrics $\eta$ (significance) and $V$ (amplitude)
is, at the $\vlassSigma\sigma$ operating point tested here, dominated by source-extraction
artefacts: component deblending, cross-match incompleteness, and bright-neighbour confusion. A
believable candidate list requires a ladder of four stages, each of which removed false positives
the previous stages passed: a per-epoch QL flux-scale correction \citep{vlassmemo13, vlassmemo22},
a deblending isolation cut, automatic forced-photometry confirmation against the actual images with
a peak-centring gate, and archival plus next-epoch follow-up. Over a \vlassRealArea~deg$^2$ census
(\vlassRealNsources\ Epoch-1 sources, \vlassRealNusable\ usable), the $\vlassSigma\sigma$ selection
yields \vlassRealNcand\ candidates, \vlassRealNconfirmed\ survive image confirmation, and archival
follow-up (forced photometry through the independent VLASS Epoch~4 release plus
NVSS/FIRST/TGSS/AllWISE cross-matches) leaves \vlassRealNarchival: the known rapidly rotating
active giant FK~Comae~Berenices, recovered with a clean $\sim$7$\to$1~mJy decline at a
$0.9\arcsec$ match; the other image-confirmed candidate is a single-epoch imaging artefact.
Injection--recovery on the census's own selection shows completeness is set by the quiescent
signal-to-noise entering $\eta$: \vlassRealComplTen\% of $10\times$ single-epoch flares are
recovered, 50\% at $\vlassRealComplFifty\pm\vlassRealComplFiftyErr\times$, and faint flares are
mostly missed, so the surviving variable is a floor ($\ge\vlassRealFracFloor$ of the usable census,
$\sim\vlassRealFracTenCorr$ after the $10\times$ completeness correction). The result is a tooling
and methodology contribution, a validation (FK~Com) plus a completeness-bounded limit, not a
discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Repeated all-sky imaging makes radio variability a survey science: a source seen at several epochs
yields a light curve, and outliers flag flaring AGN, radio stars, and slow transients. VLASS
\citep{lacy2020} provides three 2--4~GHz epochs over $33{,}885$~deg$^2$, and its Quick-Look component
catalogues \citep{gordon2021} are public. We test the weakest, most reproducible version of the
problem: with a $\sim$few-hundred-line CPU-only tool and the public catalogues alone, how many
genuine variables can one recover, and how badly do the QL systematics bite? The honest answer is
both cautionary and positive.

\section{Methods}
\code{vlass} is a pure-NumPy/SciPy/astropy module (on the \code{jansky} helpers; \citealt{jansky}).
For each source it builds a per-epoch light curve and computes the two transient-survey metrics
\citep{swinbank2015, rowlinson2019}: the weighted reduced $\chi^2$ against a constant flux,
$\eta = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_i (S_i-\bar S_w)^2/\sigma_i^2$ (significance of variability), and the
coefficient of variation $V=\sigma_S/\bar S$ (amplitude). Sources that are outliers in both
$\log\eta$ and $\log V$ at $\vlassSigma\sigma$ are candidates. Three mitigations make the result
trustworthy:

\paragraph{Per-epoch flux scale.} The QL images underestimate peak flux, epoch-dependently
\citep{vlassmemo13, vlassmemo22}. We correct each epoch to the Perley--Butler scale (Epoch~1 by
the mean of its two campaigns' corrections, 13\%; Epoch~2 by 7.5\%; Epoch~3 by 3.1\%) and fold the
$\sim$7\% residual scatter into the errors; without this the epoch-to-epoch offset alone flags
every source.

\paragraph{Deblending isolation.} A slightly extended source is fit with a different number of
Gaussian components in each epoch, so a secondary component's flux jumps around. We drop sources with
a catalogue neighbour within $5\arcsec$.

\paragraph{Forced-photometry confirmation.} Every catalogue candidate is checked against the images:
we perform forced peak photometry at the locked position in each epoch's QL cutout (one CADC query
per source), recompute $\eta$/$V$ on the image light curve, and confirm a candidate only if it
is significantly variable (p$<0.01$) with real amplitude ($V>0.3$) and the forced peak in the
brightest epoch is centred within $2.5\arcsec$. The centring gate is essential: a bright source just
outside the locked position otherwise pins the forced peak at the search-box edge and fakes a
variable as its wings shift between epochs. The photometry does search a $4\arcsec$ box rather than
read one fixed pixel, so the per-epoch forced fluxes, local rms values, and peak offsets are all
committed with the verdicts (\code{results/vlass\_candidates.csv}), making each confirmation and
rejection auditable.

\paragraph{Archival follow-up.} Image confirmation is necessary but not sufficient (\S\ref{sec:results}),
so every image-confirmed candidate passes a final stage: forced photometry through all available
epochs including the independent Epoch~4 release, counting epochs with a $>4\times$rms detection
at the fixed position, the pixel at the propagated coordinates, because a peak searched
over a beam-sized box is biased high on blank sky and can read a bright neighbour's wings (a
genuinely forced measurement goes negative about half the time on empty sky, and ours does).
Fewer than two fixed detections means a single-epoch artefact. Nearest-counterpart separations in
NVSS, FIRST, TGSS, and AllWISE are recorded alongside; the per-epoch measurements and verdicts are
committed (\code{results/vlass\_archival.json}).

Selection completeness is measured by injection--recovery on the census's own selection: a
single-epoch flare of known factor is injected into light curves drawn from the usable population
(every source with $\ge$2 epochs) and judged against the census's committed $\eta$/$V$ thresholds.
The per-epoch error is $\sigma^2=\sigma_{\rm cat}^2+(0.07\,S)^2$; a flare changes the flux, so only
the flux-proportional systematic term is rescaled with it, and the catalogue error keeps its
quiescent value. The grid is repeated over ten independent seeds, so the completeness curve and the
50\% factor carry Monte-Carlo errors. Every function is tested against a synthetic three-epoch
population, so the suite runs offline.

\section{Data}
We use the CIRADA Epoch~1--2 and NRAO Epoch~3 QL component catalogues \citep{gordon2021,vlassmemo22}
(Epoch~1 via VizieR TAP, Epochs~2--3 via the bulk NRAO files), cross-matched within $2.5\arcsec$, and
the CADC Quick-Look image cutouts for forced photometry. A subtle trap: the VizieR Epoch-1 peak flux
is recorded in $\mu$Jy while mislabelled ``mJy/beam'', a factor of $10^3$ that, uncaught, makes
every source appear variable; we detect it from the catalogue flux floor (the $\sim$0.7~mJy detection
threshold) and rescale.

The census is anchored on Epoch-1 detection: light curves are built by matching the later epochs
onto the Epoch-1 component list, so a transient that turns on after Epoch~1 never enters the
selection and has completeness identically zero. Every statement about variables below is therefore
about sources detected in Epoch~1.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Over a \vlassRealArea~deg$^2$ cone, the Epoch-1 anchor list holds \vlassRealNsources\ sources;
\vlassRealNsingleEpoch\ of them have no cross-epoch match within $2.5\arcsec$ (no light curve, so
they are excluded) and \vlassRealNexcluded\ more are dropped as crowded, leaving \vlassRealNusable\
usable. The selection flags \vlassRealNcand\ candidates and image confirmation keeps
\vlassRealNconfirmed.

\paragraph{A real recovery.} One confirmed source matches FK~Comae~Berenices at $0.9\arcsec$,
the prototype of the FK~Com class of rapidly rotating, magnetically active single giants and a known
coronally active radio emitter \citep{gudel2002}. Its forced light curve declines monotonically
($\sim$7.2, 2.7, 1.0~mJy/beam across the three epochs; upper track of Figure~\ref{fig:fkcom}),
confirmed in both the catalogue and the images. Recovering it purely from public catalogues is the
validation that the pipeline surfaces genuine variables, not only rejects artefacts.

\paragraph{Image confirmation is not the final word.} The second image-confirmed survivor is a
single-epoch detection ($\sim$3~mJy in Epoch~1). The archival stage
(\code{results/vlass\_archival.json}) rejects it: forced photometry through the independent
VLASS Epoch~4 release finds no fixed-position $>4\times$rms detection in Epochs~2--4 (the
fixed-position flux is negative in Epoch~4, exactly as forced photometry on blank sky should
sometimes be, while the box-searched peak drifts outward toward the neighbour); its Epoch-1
Quick-Look entry is a blended component with a low peak-to-ring ratio (a sidelobe signature); and
the nearest archival counterparts (NVSS, FIRST, TGSS, AllWISE) sit $\sim$$8\arcsec$ away at a
persistent source rather than at the candidate position, which has no counterpart of its own. It is
therefore an Epoch-1 imaging artefact, not a transient. Only the next-epoch and archival follow-up
separated it from the real variable: each of the four stages removed false positives that passed
every earlier one, so no prefix of the ladder is sufficient. Several other candidates carry
blazar/QSO/BL-Lac identifications but fall below the $V>0.3$ bar: their catalogue amplitudes
($V=$ \vlassRealCandVmin--\vlassRealCandVmax) collapse to forced-photometry amplitudes an order of
magnitude smaller (median \vlassRealForcedVmed, maximum \vlassRealForcedVmax), which is this
paper's thesis in miniature: the catalogue amplitude of a QL candidate is not a measurement of the
source.

\paragraph{Completeness.} Injection--recovery on the census's own selection
(Figure~\ref{fig:compl}) shows the recovery of a single-epoch flare is set by the source's
quiescent signal-to-noise entering $\eta$, not by the three-epoch $V$ ceiling (the committed $V$
threshold sits well below a strong flare's $V$). The selection recovers almost nothing below
$3\times$, half at $\vlassRealComplFifty\pm\vlassRealComplFiftyErr\times$ (Monte-Carlo error over
ten injection seeds), and \vlassRealComplTen\% at $10\times$; recovery stays above 99\% out to the
largest factor tested, $\vlassRealComplMaxFactor\times$. An earlier version of this analysis scaled the whole per-epoch error with the injected
flare, which suppressed the flare epoch's $\chi^2$ and produced an apparent 52\% saturation; that
number measured the error model, not the selection. The \vlassRealNarchival\ surviving variable is
a floor: $\ge\vlassRealFracFloor$ of the usable census, or $\sim\vlassRealFracTenCorr$ for
$\ge$$10\times$ flares after the completeness correction, with the caveat that both numbers are
conditional on an Epoch-1 detection.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
At the $\vlassSigma\sigma$ operating point tested here (the only one this paper runs, and one
whose purity and completeness are two faces of the same cut), catalogue-only VLASS QL multi-epoch
variability selection is dominated by source-extraction artefacts, and every statistical candidate
must be confirmed against the images before it is believed, the cautionary thread shared with much
survey transient work. But over enough sky the same method also does its positive job: it recovers
a genuine variable (FK~Com) and sets an honest, completeness-bounded floor on the rest. The
contribution is the reproducible, QL-systematic-aware tool (flux-scale correction, deblending
isolation, centred forced-photometry confirmation, archival follow-up, and data-driven
completeness), not a discovery. Every census number above is written by the pipeline
into the macros that the manuscript \code{\textbackslash input}s, and the analysis regenerates from a clean
checkout. A looser catalogue cut (viable because forced photometry removes false positives), more
sky, or per-epoch forced photometry of every source would tighten the limit.
