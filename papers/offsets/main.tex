\title{Reproducing the Radio--Optical Offset Excess of AGN and Its Alignment with the Parsec-Scale
Jet: ICRF3 $\times$ Gaia DR3 $\times$ MOJAVE}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The VLBI radio position of an active galactic nucleus marks the synchrotron self-absorbed jet base,
while its Gaia optical position can be pulled toward optical jet or host structure; the two do not
coincide. Across the population the normalised radio--optical offset
$\xnorm=\sqrt{(\Delta\alpha^*/\sigma_\alpha)^2+(\Delta\delta/\sigma_\delta)^2}$ shows a heavy tail far
beyond the Rayleigh expectation for pure Gaussian astrometric noise; the heavy tail is a
well-established result \citep{mignard2016,petrovkovalev2017L,kovalev2017,lindegren2018,plavin2019}.
We reproduce it with a small, tested, CPU-only tool: ICRF3 \citep{icrf3} cross-matched to Gaia DR3
\citep{gaiadr3} via the CDS X-Match service \citep{cdsxmatch} (\offN\ matched AGN, shipped as a
per-source catalogue). $\xnorm$ is computed as a Mahalanobis distance using both catalogues'
per-source RA/Dec error correlations, so the Rayleigh null is exact by construction. The median raw
offset is \offMedian~mas, and \offFracTail\% of sources have $\xnorm>3$
($\pm$\offFracTailJkSe\ from a declination-band jackknife), against a Rayleigh expectation of
\offRayleigh\%: a \offExcess$\times$ excess, whose error model is measured rather than assumed;
the noise core of $\xnorm$ fits a Rayleigh of scale \offCoreScale\ (so any common error
underestimation is $\lesssim$10\%), and the excess survives even a threefold error inflation at
\offInflThree$\times$. We then test the offset direction: cross-matching the \offJetN\ AGN that
also have a MOJAVE \citep{lister2021} parsec-scale jet position angle, the radio--optical offset
aligns with the jet axis (median jet-axis angle \offJetMedAxis$^\circ$ versus $45^\circ$ for random
orientation; Kolmogorov--Smirnov $p=\offJetKsP$ against uniform) and, among the \offNaligned\
axis-aligned offsets, points preferentially downstream (\offNdown\ downstream versus \offNup\
upstream, binomial $p=\offDownP$ against the sign-symmetric null). This reproduces
\citet{kovalev2017} and \citet{plavin2019}. The contribution is reproducibility and tooling (a
validated offset statistic, the jet-alignment test, and a reproducible matched catalogue), not a
new discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Gaia and VLBI measure the same AGN to sub-milliarcsecond precision but at different wavelengths, and
their positions differ systematically. \citet{mignard2016} first showed the radio--optical offsets of
ICRF sources exceed what formal errors allow; \citet{petrovkovalev2017} and \citet{kovalev2017}
attributed the excess to optical structure and showed the offsets align preferentially with the
parsec-scale jet. This is now a standard diagnostic of the AGN disk--jet system \citep{plavin2019}. We
ask the weakest, most reproducible version: with the public catalogues and a $\sim$hundred-line tool,
can one recover the offset excess cleanly, and how large is it?

\section{Methods}
\code{offsets} is a pure-NumPy module (on the \code{jansky} helpers; \citealt{jansky}). For each
matched AGN it forms the offset components $\Delta\alpha^*=(\alpha_o-\alpha_r)\cos\delta_r$ and
$\Delta\delta=\delta_o-\delta_r$, the separation, and the position angle, then the normalised
offset \xnorm\ as a Mahalanobis distance: the offset vector is whitened by the combined covariance
$C=C_\mathrm{radio}+C_\mathrm{Gaia}$, where each term carries the catalogue's own per-source
RA/Dec error correlation (ICRF3 \code{Corr}, Gaia \code{ra\_dec\_corr}). This matters for the
null: with the correlations ignored, the pure-noise tail of the per-axis statistic is always
heavier than Rayleigh (the excess would be quoted against the wrong expectation), whereas
the whitened \xnorm\ is exactly Rayleigh under Gaussian noise, $P(\xnorm>x)=e^{-x^2/2}$
(\offRayleigh\% at $x=3$), so a heavier observed tail is real structure. (In this sample the
correlation-blind fraction happens to agree at the quoted precision, \offFracTailNaive\%, but
its null does not.) Every analysis function is tested against a synthetic field in which most
offsets are Gaussian noise (including a correlated-noise variant that verifies the naive
statistic overshoots the Rayleigh tail while the whitened one does not) and an injected
minority carry a structural offset, so the suite runs offline.

\section{Data}
ICRF3 \citep{icrf3} (the VLBI reference frame; positions, formal errors, and the RA/Dec error
correlation) is cross-matched to Gaia DR3 \citep{gaiadr3} through the CDS X-Match service
\citep{cdsxmatch} within a sub-arcsecond radius, taking the Gaia errors and \code{ra\_dec\_corr}
as the optical uncertainty model. The match yields \offN\ of \offNicrf\ ICRF3 sources with both a
radio and an optical position; the chance-coincidence rate is measured, not assumed: repeating
the identical query with every ICRF3 declination shifted by $+0.2^\circ$ returns \offNchance\
matches, so $\sim$0.2\% of the sample are false pairs, all of which land in the significant tail
and none of which move the headline. \offNgtFifty\ matches have raw offsets above 50~mas (maxima
of several hundred mas); nothing structural displaces a photocentre that far, so these are
false matches or host-galaxy centroids; excluding them moves the tail fraction only to
\offFracTailLeFifty\%. The full matched catalogue (positions, errors, correlations, both
statistics, jet angles) is committed as \code{results/offsets\_sources.csv}. For the
jet-alignment test we add a third catalogue, MOJAVE XVIII \citep{lister2021} (VizieR
\code{J/ApJ/923/30}), whose ``mean innermost jet position angle'' gives the parsec-scale jet
direction; it is positionally matched (within $1''$) to the same AGN, yielding \offJetN\ with a
jet PA.

\section{Results}
The median raw radio--optical separation is \offMedian~mas. \offFracTail\% of the matched AGN
have a normalised offset $\xnorm>3$, against the exact Rayleigh expectation of \offRayleigh\% for
pure Gaussian errors, a factor of \offExcess\ more sources than that expectation predicts
(Figure~\ref{fig:xnorm}). The uncertainty on that fraction is $\pm$\offFracTailSe\ (binomial) and
$\pm$\offFracTailJkSe\ from a delete-one-declination-band jackknife; the larger jackknife term
reflects a mild north--south gradient consistent with known Gaia scanning-law and ICRF3 zonal
systematics, and bounds their effect on the headline at the percent-point level. The noise core of
the distribution ($\xnorm<1.5$, \offCoreN\ sources) fits a Rayleigh of scale \offCoreScale\
(structural contamination biases this fit high, so it is an upper limit): the formal errors are
not detectably underestimated at the ensemble level, and any common underestimation is below
$\sim$10\%. The committed inflation sweep quantifies what error inflation could do:
even if every formal error were multiplied by 1.5, 2, or 3, the excess would remain
\offInflOneFive$\times$, \offInflTwo$\times$, and \offInflThree$\times$; the tail is that
heavy. The population fraction is not a single number, however: across quartiles of the combined
formal error the $\xnorm>3$ fraction runs from $\sim$38\% (best-measured quartile) to $\sim$22\%,
as expected when a fixed mas-scale structural offset is measured with varying precision, so
\offFracTail\% is a property of this population convolved with the ICRF3/Gaia error
distribution. For calibration against the literature: \citet{petrovkovalev2017L} found 6\% of
matches significant at the 99\% level in the Gaia DR1 era; the larger fraction here is what DR3's
improved precision resolves, not a disagreement.

\section{Alignment of the offset with the jet axis}
The magnitude excess shows the offsets are real; their direction shows what they trace. Of the
matched AGN, \offJetN\ also appear in MOJAVE \citep{lister2021}, which gives the flux-weighted innermost
jet position angle (measured from the core toward the approaching, downstream side). Comparing the
radio--optical offset PA to the jet PA (Figure~\ref{fig:xnorm}, right), the jet-axis angle
($\min(\Delta,180^\circ-\Delta)$, which is $0^\circ$ when the offset lies along the jet axis and would
have a median of $45^\circ$ and a within-$30^\circ$ fraction of $1/3$ if offsets were randomly oriented)
has median \offJetMedAxis$^\circ$, with \offJetFracAxis\% within $30^\circ$ of the axis; a
Kolmogorov--Smirnov test against the uniform distribution gives $p=\offJetKsP$. The offsets
therefore lie preferentially along the jet axis rather than at random position angles. Whether
they also point preferentially downstream is a separate question needing a separate null:
comparing the within-$45^\circ$-of-downstream fraction to ``random 25\%'' would re-test the axis
alignment just established, since an axis-aligned but sign-symmetric population already exceeds
25\%. The correct null is a fair coin over the axis-aligned subset, and it is decisively broken:
of the \offNaligned\ offsets within $45^\circ$ of the jet axis, \offNdown\ point downstream and
\offNup\ upstream (binomial $p=\offDownP$), as expected if the VLBI core sits upstream of the
optical centroid through opacity (core-shift) while extended optical jet emission lies downstream.
The \offNup\ upstream offsets ($\sim$29\% of the aligned population) are themselves the second
component \citet{plavin2019} identify, where accretion-disk emission dominates the optical
centroid. As a consistency check the signal tightens for the more significant offsets ($X>2$:
\offJetNsig\ sources, median \offJetMedAxisSig$^\circ$, \offJetFracAxisSig\% within $30^\circ$),
which is expected because weak offsets are dominated by astrometric noise with random PA. The
result is unchanged when jets with a poorly defined direction (wobble $\geq45^\circ$) are removed:
the committed variant keeps \offGoodjetN\ sources at median axis angle \offGoodjetMedAxis$^\circ$,
KS $p=\offGoodjetKsP$. This reproduces \citet{kovalev2017} and \citet{plavin2019}.

A few caveats bound the claim. MOJAVE is a flux-limited ($>1.5$~Jy at 15~GHz) sample of strongly
Doppler-boosted blazars with well-defined jets close to the line of sight, so the result applies to that
population and is not a statement about AGN in general. The jet PA is measured at 15~GHz while the radio
position is the ICRF3 S/X core; using the 15~GHz kinematic axis as the jet label is standard, and on
parsec scales the inner jet is essentially straight. And the full-matched-sample test is the primary
one; the $X$-cut rows are a qualitative consistency check (nested subsets, not independent
confirmations).

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
Both literature results are recovered cleanly from public catalogues with a small tested tool: the
radio--optical offset excess (AGN optical positions are displaced from their VLBI cores by real
milliarcsecond structure) and the offset alignment with the parsec-scale jet (the displacement
is predominantly downstream along the jet, with an upstream minority consistent with the
disk-dominated component of \citealt{plavin2019}). The contribution is reproducibility and tooling (validated offset and
jet-alignment statistics, a reproducible ICRF3$\times$Gaia$\times$MOJAVE matched catalogue, and the
two-panel figure), not a new astrophysical claim; the value added over the source papers is a fully
open, end-to-end-reproducible pipeline. The remaining literature extension is to model the disk versus
jet contributions to the optical centroid quantitatively \citep{plavin2019}. Every number above is
written by the pipeline into the macros \code{\textbackslash input}ted here, and the analysis
regenerates from a clean checkout on public data.
