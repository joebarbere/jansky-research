\title{A Reproducible Stokes-V Coherent-Emitter Pipeline for RACS, and What Single-Epoch
Circular Polarization Can Recover}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Circular polarization is a near-unambiguous flag of coherent radio emission: extragalactic AGN
are at most a fraction of a percent circularly polarized, while flaring M dwarfs, ultracool dwarfs,
magnetic chemically-peculiar stars, and close binaries reach tens of percent. A high $|V|/I$ in a
wide-field survey is therefore a clean coherent-emitter finder \citep{pritchard2021}. We build a small
tested, CPU-only pipeline for the ASKAP Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey \citep{mcconnell2020}: a
per-region leakage-floor + $V$-SNR + proper-motion selection, and forced photometry of a target
list in RACS-low Stokes-V images. The selection machinery is validated on a synthetic field (it
recovers \svNrecovered\ of \svNinjected\ injected emitters above a \svLeakFloorPct\% leakage floor at
purity \svPurity). The forced-photometry leg measures \svNmeasured\ of the \svNparent\ catalogued RACS-low
emitters \citep{driessen2024} in RACS-low \citep{hale2021} Stokes I$+$V cutout pairs pinned to
a single observation each (the \code{obs\_id} is committed per target). \svNvalid\ targets
yield physically valid measurements; the other \svNinvalid\ are undetected in Stokes~$I$
($<3\sigma$) in that observation, and are reported as such rather than measured. The image
Stokes~$I$ matches the catalogue only in the median (ratio \svIrec, spanning
\svIrecMin--\svIrecMax), so single-epoch $I$ variability and beam-scale differences are live
and unquantified. Significant circular polarization ($|V|/\sigma_V\ge5$ and $|V|/I\ge6\%$) is
present in \svNVcirc\ of the \svNvalid\ valid targets (\svFracVcircPct\%; Wilson $1\sigma$
interval \svFracVcircLo--\svFracVcircHi\%), a fraction unchanged from a 5\% to a 10\%
threshold. This is a reproducibility, tooling, and honest-limits contribution, not a blind
survey.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Coherent radio emission (electron-cyclotron maser or coherent plasma processes) is highly
circularly polarized, so a wide-field Stokes-V survey isolates it from the vast incoherent
(synchrotron) background. ASKAP RACS \citep{mcconnell2020} now releases Stokes-V products over the
southern sky, and dedicated searches \citep{pritchard2021,driessen2024} have built catalogues of
V-detected radio stars. We ask the most reproducible version: with the public catalogues and images
and a small tested tool, can one (i) build and validate the selection machinery, and (ii) forced-measure
circular polarization at known emitter positions in the real images, and what does a single RACS-low
epoch actually recover?

\section{Methods}
\code{stokesv} is a pure-NumPy module composing the \code{jansky} helpers and the forced-photometry
pattern of the VLASS variability slice. It implements three tested pieces:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Leakage floor.} The dominant false positive is instrumental Stokes-$I\!\to\!V$ leakage
($\sim$1\% on-axis, rising toward beam edges). We estimate it from the field: the median $|V/I|$ of
bright ($I/\sigma_I\gg1$) sources is the local leakage level, and a candidate must clear
$7\times$ that median (the RACS convention), not merely the image noise.
\item \textbf{$V$-SNR gate + proper-motion confirmation.} A candidate must be a real $V$ detection, and
a genuine radio star sits at its catalogue position propagated to the radio epoch, a
discriminant a chance background match fails.
\item \textbf{Forced photometry.} At each target we take the brightest Stokes-$I$ pixel within
12\arcsec\ of the propagated position and read $V$ at that same pixel (either sign). This is a peak search, not a fixed-pixel measurement: acceptable for catalogued $I$-detections, and the
per-target offsets are committed so a reader can check the search stayed on-source (median
\svMedOffset\arcsec, maximum \svMaxOffset\arcsec\ across the sample).
\end{itemize}
Each target's Stokes $I$ and $V$ cutouts are staged from the same observation: an
ObsCore query constrains the collection to RACS, the band to RACS-low, and requires an I and a
V product under one \code{obs\_id}, whose identifiers are committed per target. An earlier
version staged the two Stokes through independent unordered queries with no such constraint; at a southern position that pattern can pair images from different bands or epochs, and it
produced physically impossible ratios ($|V|/I>1$) that counted as detections. A physicality
gate ($0\le|V|/I\le1$ and $I>3\sigma_I$) now excludes failed measurements from every
statistic, and ``significant'' circular polarization requires the $V$-SNR gate
($|V|/\sigma_V\ge5$) the pipeline always implemented but previously never applied to real
data.
On a synthetic field of \svNtargets\ targets (a noise population plus injected emitters), the
pipeline recovers \svNrecovered\ of \svNinjected\ injected emitters at purity \svPurity. We
state this validation's scope plainly: the injected fractions sit far above the leakage floor,
so the recovery exercises the machinery, not the decision boundary, and the proper-motion gate
rejected \svNpmrejected\ cases on this field; a gate is only demonstrated by cases it rejects.

\section{Data}
Targets are the Sydney Radio Star Catalogue \citep{driessen2024} of V-detected radio stars; images are
RACS-low DR1 Stokes $I$ and $V$ \citep{hale2021}, staged as SODA cutouts from CASDA \citep{casda}. The
CASDA image query returns, alongside each science image, a co-named
\code{noiseMap}/\code{meanMap} product (also carrying \code{.i./.v.} and \code{restored}); selecting the
science \code{image.$\{$i,v$\}$.} product is essential, since the noise map otherwise reads as a flat
$\sim$0.2~mJy field and manufactures a null. CASDA is the sole public source of RACS Stokes~V (the CADC
and Data Central mirrors carry RACS Stokes~I only).

\section{Results}
\paragraph{The forced-photometry leg (same-observation RACS-low pairs).} The parent selection
(every SRSC row with \code{Survey=RACS-LOW}, $I>3$~mJy, on the RACS-low sky) holds
\svNparent\ targets; we measured \svNmeasured\ of them, each in an I$+$V pair from one
committed \code{obs\_id} (\code{results/stokesv\_targets.csv} carries every row: fluxes,
local rms values, $V$ signal-to-noise, offsets, product identifiers). \svNvalid\ pass the
physicality gate; the \svNinvalid\ that fail are Stokes-$I$ non-detections in that observation, single-epoch states, not pipeline failures, and they enter no statistic. Among
the valid targets the image-to-catalogue $I$ ratio has median \svIrec\ but spans
\svIrecMin--\svIrecMax\ (Figure~\ref{fig:sv}, left): the median is consistent with the
pipeline reading the right sources, while the spread says single-epoch stellar variability
and catalogue-vs-image flux conventions are unresolved, and we do not claim recovery beyond
the median. Significant circular polarization is present in \svNVcirc\ of \svNvalid\
(\svFracVcircPct\%, Wilson \svFracVcircLo--\svFracVcircHi\%; the four detections carry
$|V|/\sigma_V$ of 12--47, so the count is identical at a 5\% or 10\% fraction threshold;
Figure~\ref{fig:sv}, right). Whether each catalogue $V$ detection and this cutout share an epoch is not established here (the committed \code{t\_min} per target enables that comparison), so we state the fraction as a single-observation occurrence rate among
catalogued emitters, not as a duty cycle.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
We build a tested, reproducible Stokes-V coherent-emitter pipeline: the leakage-floor,
$V$-SNR, and proper-motion selection validated on synthetic data, and the forced-photometry leg now
working on real RACS-low DR1 images via CASDA. The honest limitations: (i) the occurrence fraction is a single-observation statement over
\svNvalid\ valid targets with a wide interval; a multi-epoch campaign (RACS-low1/low2 $+$
VAST) with the committed per-target \code{obs\_id}/\code{t\_min} bookkeeping is what turns
it into a duty cycle; (ii) the photometry searches a 12\arcsec\ box around the propagated
position rather than reading a fixed pixel; the committed offsets (median \svMedOffset\arcsec) support the search staying on-source for this catalogued sample, but a
blank-sky census must use the fixed-pixel mode; (iii) the 6\% circular threshold is the
synthetic model's constant, not a per-field leakage measurement, mitigated here by the
$V$-SNR gate and by the count's insensitivity from 5\% to 10\%; (iv) the absolute $V$ sign
convention varies between ASKAP pipeline epochs, so handedness is recorded but not physically
interpreted; (v) the $I$ ratio spread (\svIrecMin--\svIrecMax) is unexplained and bounds any
claim that the catalogue and image describe the same emission state. These bound what one
observation per target can claim. Every number above is written by the pipeline into the
\code{generated/macros} file, which is \code{\textbackslash input} at the top of this document;
\code{python -m jansky\_research.stokesv --offline --out <tmpdir>} regenerates the synthetic
validation, and \code{CASDA\_USERNAME=... python -m jansky\_research.stokesv --out <tmpdir>}
the real leg. Both must be given an output directory outside the repository: writing to the
repository root overwrites the committed figure and results.
