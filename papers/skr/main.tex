\title{Is SKR Detection Occurrence a Saturn-Proximity Law? A Cassini/RPWS Census
Whose Sensitivity Decomposition Proves Method-Limited}

\begin{abstract}
Saturn Kilometric Radiation (SKR), Saturn's dominant auroral radio emission, shows dual
rotation-modulation periods ($\sim$10.6/10.8~h north/south) tracked end-to-end across the
Cassini mission \citep{gurnett2009,fischer2015,ye2016,provan2019}. We port the occurrence-census
method of a sibling Jovian-decametric study to Saturn, using \skrNBins\ one-minute RPWS
key-parameter bins from the 2017 proximal (Grand Finale) orbits: the electric spectral density is
band-integrated over the SKR band, bins are flagged SKR-active by a background$+k\sigma$
threshold, and detection occurrence is folded against the Cassini--Saturn range from JPL Horizons.
Two results follow, in order of confidence. First, a validation: the intensity periodogram
peaks at \skrRealAnchorPeriod\ and \skrRealPeriodTwo~h, matching the published late-mission
values \citep{provan2019} to 0.05\% and 0.06\%, though within a 0.08~h periodogram resolution
and with a peak power a day-block permutation null does not call significant
($p=\skrRealPermP$); the match certifies the band-integrated flux series against the known
record rather than re-detecting the period. Second, the census: the SKR-active duty cycle rises
from \skrRealDutyFar\% at \skrRealRangeFar~$R_S$ to \skrRealDutyNear\% at
\skrRealRangeNear~$R_S$, a raw near/far ratio of $\skrRealNearFar\pm\skrRealNearFarJkSe$
(leave-one-orbit-out jackknife over \skrRealNOrbits\ periapsis passes). What this dataset
cannot do is decompose that trend into inverse-square detection sensitivity versus intrinsic
occurrence. Three defensible null models give corrected ratios of
\skrRealCorrNearFarFluxScaled, 1.68 and \skrRealSensCorrNearFar; the adopted
common-sensitivity census, the one construction that passes its injection controls, carries an
orbit-to-orbit scatter of $\pm$\skrRealCorrNearFarJkSe\ and a detection-rule dependence
spanning \skrRealSweepMin--\skrRealSweepMax, and a per-day background convention lands below
unity. The near and far bins also differ in sub-spacecraft latitude by
\skrRealAbsLatSpan$^\circ$, entangling viewing geometry with range. We therefore quote the raw
trend and no residual: the sensitivity decomposition is method-dominated at this range span. The
census machinery, the controls that expose each null model's bias, and the anchor are the
contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Saturn Kilometric Radiation (SKR; $\sim$100--400~kHz, cyclotron-maser emission from the auroral
regions) is modulated near Saturn's rotation period, with the striking property of two close
periods from the northern and southern auroral systems that drift and swap across equinox
\citep{gurnett2009,fischer2015}. That periodicity record is complete: Fischer et al.\ tracked it
through early 2013 \citep{fischer2015}, Ye et al.\ through the post-equinox epoch
\citep{ye2016}, and Provan et al.\ to the end of the mission \citep{provan2019}. What has not been asked is a simpler
occurrence question, by analogy to a proximity--occurrence law reported for Jovian decametric
emission from Juno: does SKR detection occurrence depend on the observer's distance from
the planet? Cassini's range to Saturn varied by more than an order of magnitude across each orbit,
so its own key-parameter archive answers the question directly. The occurrence and visibility of
SKR versus observer local time and latitude have been mapped \citep{lamy2008}; the
observer-range axis, and specifically whether a $1/r^2$ sensitivity trend leaves any
residual occurrence dependence, has not. We build that census, using the known rotation period as
a built-in validation.

\section{Data and method}
\label{sec:method}
We use the Cassini/RPWS 60-second summary key parameters
(\code{CO-V/E/J/S/SS-RPWS-4-SUMM-KEY60S-V1.0}; \citealt{rpws_key60s}), which provide per-minute
calibrated electric spectral densities on a 73-channel electric-antenna grid but no
pre-integrated SKR flux; we therefore integrate the electric spectral density over the SKR band
(0.1--1.2~MHz) ourselves (\code{read\_key\_params} $+$ \code{band\_integrated\_flux}, tested
against the fixed-width PDS3 record layout). A bin is SKR-active when its band flux exceeds a
robust background (the 25th log-flux percentile) plus $k\sigma$ with $k=3$, the sibling census's
detection rule; the rule's parameters are committed with the results. The Cassini--Saturn range
and sub-spacecraft latitude come from JPL Horizons (target Saturn, observer Cassini). We analyze
the 2017 proximal orbits, 2017 July 19 -- September 15 (\skrNBins\ one-minute bins over
\skrRealNOrbits\ periapsis passes), where the range sweeps repeatedly over
\skrRealRangeMin--\skrRealRangeMax~$R_S$. Half the near-quartile bins lie below its
\skrRealRangeNear~$R_S$ median: periapsis reaches $\sim$1~$R_S$, where far-field $1/r^2$
geometry is at its least valid and ring-plane dust impacts can contribute range-correlated
broadband signal, so a far-field-only variant (excluding $r<5~R_S$) is reported alongside every
corrected ratio.

Before any occurrence claim, we run a Lomb-Scargle periodogram of the log-intensity series in
the physically-motivated Saturn-rotation band (10.4--11.0~h, bracketing the established
$\sim$10.6--10.8~h periods), with significance judged by a day-block permutation null rather
than the analytic false-alarm probability, which assumes independent samples and underflows on
83,000 autocorrelated minutes. A broader 10.0--11.5~h search shows its strongest peak at
\skrRealBroadPeak~h (committed with the results), outside the rotation band; we exclude it from
the anchor as a plausible sampling harmonic of the 6.5-day orbit, and the exclusion is a stated
choice.

For the proximity census, we bin the SKR-active flag by Cassini--Saturn range quartile and
report the duty cycle per bin, with a leave-one-periapsis-out jackknife supplying the error bar
(the orbit, not the autocorrelated minute, is the independent unit). The dominant confound is
inverse-square sensitivity: at smaller range the same intrinsic emission clears a fixed threshold
more often. Constructing a valid null for that was the hard part of this paper: two plausible constructions
fail on injection controls. Rescaling the total flux to a common
range moves the range-independent instrumental-plus-galactic noise floor along with the signal
and is biased toward a collapse; rescaling the excess over the floor upward at far range
amplifies far-range noise deviations and is biased toward a reversal. The estimator we adopt is a
common-sensitivity census: each bin's excess over the floor is scaled by $(r/r_\mathrm{far})^2
\le 1$, only ever down, so noise is never promoted, and a bin counts only if it is
raw-active and its scaled excess still clears the raw threshold. On controls with a
range-independent floor, a flat intrinsic rate comes out flat with zero noise promotions and an
injected intrinsic trend is recovered; both controls, and both failure modes of the rejected
constructions, are in the test suite. A second confound is latitude: SKR is auroral and beamed,
and range correlates with sub-spacecraft latitude along the orbit. The near quartile sits at
median $|$lat$|$ 36$^\circ$ against the far quartile's 8$^\circ$, and higher-latitude viewing
favours SKR visibility \citep{lamy2008}. The latitude confound therefore inflates the near bins,
and any corrected residual is an upper bound on the intrinsic part for that reason alone.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
As a validation, the rotation-band periodogram peaks at \skrRealAnchorPeriod\ and
\skrRealPeriodTwo~h (Figure~\ref{fig:skr}, left), matching the published late-mission southern
($\sim$10.68~h) and northern ($\sim$10.79~h) values \citep{provan2019} to 0.05\% and 0.06\%.
Three qualifications keep this a certification of the flux series rather than a detection. The
periodogram resolution over this 59-day baseline is $P^2/T \approx 0.08$~h, sixteen times the
quoted agreement, and the two peaks are separated by 1.5 resolution elements, i.e.\ marginally
resolved. The peak power is not significant against a day-block permutation null
($p=\skrRealPermP$ over 199 permutations), so the periodogram alone would not discover this
period; it recovers a known one at the right location. And the strongest peak in a broader
10.0--11.5~h search is at \skrRealBroadPeak~h, outside the rotation band, which we read as an
orbit-sampling harmonic. What the anchor certifies is the band-integrated flux series; the
detection threshold is untouched by it.

Across \skrNBins\ bins the overall SKR-active duty cycle is 40.2\%. The raw duty cycle
rises monotonically with proximity, from \skrRealDutyFar\% at $\sim$\skrRealRangeFar~$R_S$ to
\skrRealDutyNear\% at $\sim$\skrRealRangeNear~$R_S$: near/far
$\skrRealNearFar\pm\skrRealNearFarJkSe$ over \skrRealNOrbits\ periapsis passes
(Figure~\ref{fig:skr}, right). The trend itself is well measured; its decomposition is not. The
common-sensitivity census gives a corrected ratio of \skrRealSensCorrNearFar,
indistinguishable from the raw trend, but with an orbit-to-orbit jackknife scatter of
$\pm$\skrRealCorrNearFarJkSe. It also carries a detection-rule dependence spanning
\skrRealSweepMin--\skrRealSweepMax\ over $k\in[4,10]$ and baseline percentiles 10--25 (at the
50th percentile the floor absorbs real emission, 40\% of bins being active, and the estimator
degenerates). A far-field-only variant gives \skrRealCorrNearFarFarField, and a per-day
background convention gives \skrRealCorrNearFarPerDay, on the other side of unity. The two
rejected null models
bracket it: rescaling total flux gives \skrRealCorrNearFarFluxScaled\ (biased toward a collapse:
that is the number an earlier version of this paper reported as ``the null removes essentially
the whole effect''), and rescaling the excess upward gives 1.68. When the answer moves by a
factor of $\sim$30 across defensible analysis choices, the measurement is the spread, not any
one value: this dataset does not determine how much of the raw trend is sensitivity. The
\skrRealAbsLatSpan$^\circ$ latitude difference between the near and far quartiles
\citep{lamy2008} entangles viewing geometry with range on top of that, in the direction that
inflates the near bins.

\section{Conclusions}
Cassini's RPWS key-parameter archive gives a well-measured raw proximity trend
($\skrRealNearFar\pm\skrRealNearFarJkSe$ from $\sim$21 to $\sim$8~$R_S$); this dataset cannot
deliver its decomposition into detection sensitivity versus intrinsic occurrence. Three
defensible null models span corrected ratios of \skrRealCorrNearFarFluxScaled\ to
\skrRealSensCorrNearFar, the rule sweep spans \skrRealSweepMin--\skrRealSweepMax, and the
per-day background convention lands below unity. An earlier version of this paper quoted the
\skrRealCorrNearFarFluxScaled\ as ``the null removes essentially the whole effect''; that number
is an artifact of rescaling the noise floor along with the signal, and the withdrawal is the
methodological result: a threshold census's $1/r^2$ null is only as good as its treatment of the
range-independent background, and controls that can fail must accompany it. The firm results are
the raw trend with its orbit-level error, the flux-series anchor (peak locations matching
\citet{provan2019} to 0.05--0.06\%, not independently significant at $p=\skrRealPermP$), and
the tested tooling. The follow-on with real leverage is the range baseline: earlier
higher-apoapsis orbits (to $\sim$60~$R_S$) widen the span where the estimators above disagree
by construction.
