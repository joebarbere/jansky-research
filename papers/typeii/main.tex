\title{A Slow-Drift Type II Solar Radio Burst Detector for OVRO-LWA, and the
False-Positive Dominance of an Archival Census}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Type II solar radio bursts trace CME-driven coronal shocks by their slow frequency drift, and the
published OVRO-LWA real-time burst detector \citep{ovrolwa_detector} handles only the fast type III
beam bursts, so the type II detector on this archive is an open gap. We build one: a slow-drift
ridge tracker with a fundamental--harmonic discriminator, distinguishing type II from type III by
three physical signatures (drift rate $\lesssim$1~MHz/s vs tens--hundreds; duration of minutes vs
seconds; a fundamental$+$harmonic band pair at ratio $\sim$2). A 30-realization synthetic ensemble
shows the detector clean against fast type III and narrowband RFI (0 misclassifications in 480
and 240 trials) but not against the contaminant class the real band contains: coherent
slow-drift window-filling background ridges are called type II at a rate near 0.38
(\tiiSynFpSlowBg\ of \tiiSynNSlowBg). Completeness is SNR-dependent (\tiiSynCompSnrfour\
strong, \tiiSynCompSnrtwo\ at threshold), measured on the synthetic configuration and an upper
bound for the deployed one. We then stream the full \tiiRealNTypeII-candidate real census: every observing day over
2024--2026 read straight from AWS Open Data (Stokes-I plane only, range-read and downsampled in
memory, nothing to disk; \tiiRealNEvents\ days, zero failures). The result is a null. The
\tiiRealNTypeII\ candidates (\tiiRealNDedup\ distinct structures after merging adjacent
overlapping windows; $\tiiRealDetRate$/day against a whole-cycle RSTN average of $\sim$0.11/day,
a comparison inflated by cycle phase since this census spans only the solar-maximum plateau) are
false-positive dominated: their matched-CME median speed \tiiRealMatchCmeMed~km/s sits modestly
above the background median (\tiiRealBgCmeMed~km/s) with a fast fraction of
\tiiRealMatchFracFast\ against the background's \tiiRealBgFracFast, the signature of the
flare--CME confound, far from the $\ge$900~km/s driver population real type II require; the burst
drift is uncorrelated with the matched CME speed (Pearson $r=\tiiRealDriftSpeedCorr$ over
\tiiRealNCorrPairs\ pairs); and \tiiRealWindowSat\ of the ``bursts'' fill the entire detection
window, which the ensemble validation identifies as the detector's own blind spot: it
misclassifies window-filling slow-drift background at a $\sim$0.38 rate. A blind slow-drift
spectral detector therefore does not yield a usable type II census in OVRO-LWA's RFI-heavy
15--85~MHz band, and the census and the ensemble agree on why. The detector with its measured
per-class false rates, the streaming pipeline at archive scale, and this characterization are the
contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Metric and decametric type II bursts are the radio signature of a shock climbing through the solar
corona: as the shock rises into thinner plasma the local plasma frequency falls, and the emission
drifts slowly from high to low frequency \citep{nelson1985,mann1995}. They are the diagnostic of
CME-driven shocks in the radio, and their statistics (occurrence, drift, band-splitting, and
the CME populations that drive them) are the science. The Owens Valley Long Wavelength Array
(OVRO-LWA) now records solar dynamic spectra continuously over 13.4--86.9~MHz, and a real-time
burst detector has been published \citep{ovrolwa_detector}, but it identifies only type III
bursts (fast electron-beam emission), the easy, short-lived case a detector trained on synthetic
type III can catch. The slow-drift type II is not covered. Metric type II censuses elsewhere are a
different instrument and cycle (RSTN, Cycle 24; \citealt{lawrance2024}) or small-N case studies, so
a systematic type II detector for the OVRO-LWA archive is unclaimed. We build and validate that
detector.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
A type II differs from a type III in three testable ways, each of which
the detector measures on a background-subtracted dynamic spectrum: (i) a slow frequency
drift (we track the drifting band's peak frequency per time column and fit a robust
(Theil--Sen) drift rate, classifying type II only for $|df/dt|$ between 0.01 and 2~MHz/s, the last
a guard band above the physical $\sim$1, in the empty gap below type III's tens--hundreds); (ii) a
minutes-long duration ($\ge$90~s, versus type III seconds); and (iii) a coherent
ridge, for which the frequency--time correlation must exceed 0.55, which rejects the two false positives a
bare slope admits: scattered noise/RFI peaks (low correlation) and a horizontal RFI line
(near-zero drift). A ``harmonic score,'' the fraction of ridge points with co-emission at
twice (or half) their frequency, is reported as a purity indicator but not required, since
single-lane type IIs exist. Narrowband RFI is masked by a channel-variance outlier test (a
persistent-RFI channel inflates its own scatter far above a channel a drifting burst only crosses
briefly). The synthetic type II reuses the same coronal-density drift model as the repository's
type III slice \citep{newkirk1961}, driven by a slow CME shock rather than a fast beam; the
detector measures generic drift/duration/coherence and does not invert that model, so the
validation is a genuine test of the discriminators, not a circular one.

A shock-driven type II follows CME onset, so each detection is matched to
the nearest CME onset within a $\pm$2-h window (\citealt{yashiro2004}; the sign is deliberately
unconstrained, since a metric type II precedes the CME's LASCO first-appearance time).
We then report the fraction of matched CMEs that are fast ($\ge$900~km/s) and wide ($\ge$60\arcdeg),
the established type II driver bias \citep{gopalswamy2005}.

\section{Synthetic validation}
\label{sec:results}
We run the detector on a seeded ensemble of mixed synthetic censuses: per realization, 24
type II bursts of varied difficulty (strong$+$harmonic, single-lane, and near-threshold SNR)
among 16 fast type III, 8 narrowband-RFI spectra, and 8 coherent slow-drift background ridges,
over 30 realizations. The last class is the one that can fail: fast type III (drift
tens--hundreds of MHz/s) and horizontal RFI (drift $\approx$0) sit orders of magnitude outside
the 0.01--2~MHz/s acceptance band by construction. A purity measured only against them, as an
earlier version of this validation did (reporting \tiiSynPurity\ from a single realization with
24 negatives, a number that bounds the false rate only below 12\%), cannot fail in the way
the real band fails. Against type III and RFI the ensemble confirms the detector is clean (0
misclassifications in 480 and 240 trials). Against the slow-drift ridges it is not:
\tiiSynFpSlowBg\ of \tiiSynNSlowBg\ are called type II, a per-window false rate near 0.38.
That number is the census's failure mode, measured. Completeness against injected type II is
SNR-dependent rather than a single saturated number:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\hline
injected SNR & 2 & 2.5 & 3 & 4 \\
completeness & \tiiSynCompSnrtwo & \tiiSynCompSnrtwofive & \tiiSynCompSnrthree & \tiiSynCompSnrfour \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Strong bursts are recovered completely; near the threshold the detector genuinely misses a
fraction, as it must. Two scope caveats on this curve: it is measured on the synthetic
configuration (300-s windows at 0.5-s bins against Gaussian noise) rather than the deployed one
(900-s windows at $\sim$4-s bins in the real band), every injection shares one morphology and
duration (so the duration and drift cuts have no measured completeness of their own), and each
injected burst spans its full window; the curve is therefore an upper bound on the deployed census's
completeness, not its selection function.

The CME cross-match, on this synthetic set, recovers the injected fast-and-wide driver bias
(\tiiSynFracFast\ fast, \tiiSynFracWide\ wide, median driver speed \tiiSynMedSpeed~km/s). We stress
that these fractions echo the injected CME distribution by construction: the synthetic test
validates that the temporal match picks the correct CME among decoys (the matching ``wiring''),
not the physical association itself, which can only be reproduced on real events.

\section{The real census: a false-positive-dominated null}
\label{sec:real}
The OVRO-LWA dynamic spectra are on AWS Open Data \citep{ovrolwa_data} (bucket
\code{ovro-lwa-solar}), directly downloadable; the Cloudflare challenge on the query portal
gates only the interactive UI, not the data. Each daily file is large ($\sim$1.7~GB; a 4D I/V
dynamic spectrum confirmed on first contact, axes time/frequency/1/Stokes), so we stream the data:
open the S3 FITS lazily (astropy \code{use\_fsspec}), range-read only the Stokes-I plane in
frequency chunks, and block-average to $\sim$4~s bins on the fly, so a day is processed entirely in
memory with nothing on disk, then freed. We ran every observing day 2024-04 to 2026-07
(\tiiRealNEvents\ days, zero failures), sliding a 15-minute window across each and classifying.

The candidate list is false-positive dominated. The run yields \tiiRealNTypeII\ candidates
(\tiiRealNDedup\ distinct structures once detections in adjacent 50\%-overlapping windows are
merged), \tiiRealDetRate/day. That is about four times the RSTN Cycle-24 catalogue's
$\sim$0.11/day \citep{lawrance2024}, but the comparison is inflated by construction: the RSTN
figure is a whole-cycle average while this census spans only the 2024--2026 solar-maximum
plateau, where any metric type II rate runs well above its cycle mean, so the excess is
suggestive rather than probative. The CME cross-match is the sharper test. Real type II are
driven by fast ($\ge$900~km/s) and wide CMEs \citep{gopalswamy2005}, so a real sample's
matched-CME speeds should sit far above the background CME population. The matched sample is
enriched, but weakly and in the wrong way: median \tiiRealMatchCmeMed~km/s against the
background's \tiiRealBgCmeMed, fast fraction \tiiRealMatchFracFast\ against
\tiiRealBgFracFast: a distribution that differs from background (KS
$p=\tiiRealSpeedKsP$) by exactly the modest enrichment the flare--CME confound predicts
(\S below), nowhere near the driver population. The match-rate test is uninformative at solar
maximum: of the \tiiRealNTypeII\ candidates, \tiiRealNCovered\ fall within the CME
catalogue's published coverage, and among those the match rate (\tiiRealObsMatch) sits slightly
above the chance rate (\tiiRealChanceMatch, the fraction of random times with any CME within
$\pm$2~h). An earlier version of this analysis reported a match-rate deficit; that deficit was
an artifact of counting candidates beyond the CME catalogue's coverage end as unmatched, and the
corrected comparison carries no weight either way. The null rests on the speed distribution and
the window-saturation test.

Two further tests support the null result, the second of them independent of the CME
cross-match whose power the preceding paragraph questions. First, the burst's own drift rate encodes the shock
kinematics, so a real sample would show $|df/dt|$ correlated with the matched CME speed. It is not:
Pearson $r=\tiiRealDriftSpeedCorr$. The radio properties carry no information about the ``matched''
CME; the associations are coincidence. Second, \tiiRealWindowSat\ of the ``bursts'' fill the
entire 15-minute detection window (half at exactly the window length); real type II are
minutes-long transients of varied duration, so a ridge that saturates the window every time is
persistent background structure tracked as a slow drift, and the ensemble validation
(\S\ref{sec:results}) measures the detector misclassifying exactly this class at a
$\sim$0.38 rate, so the census and the validation agree on the mechanism. A tighter
fundamental--harmonic cut does not rescue purity, though the committed sweep should be read
with its denominators: at score $\ge$0.5 the matched sample is 8 CMEs (median 352~km/s), and at
$\ge$0.7 it is 4 (median 469, above the full sample), consistent with the ``harmonic''
candidates lacking genuine band structure, but on counts too small for the word confirm.

The flare-gated subset is a confound, not a rescue. Candidates coincident with a large
(M/X-class) flare do show an apparent fast-wide-CME enrichment (the X-class subset matches
$\sim$1000~km/s CMEs). This is not the detector finding real type II: it is the well-known
flare-size--CME-speed relation. Selecting a blob coincident with a big flare automatically selects
a nearby fast CME whether or not the blob is a real type II; consistently, the matched-CME
speed tracks flare class, not any burst property, while the burst drift remains uncorrelated
with CME speed. The May-2024 Gannon-superstorm week, which in a nine-day pilot looked like a clean
recovery (most detections landing on real 1600--2010~km/s halo CMEs), is the same confound at its
extreme: that week was wall-to-wall fast, wide CMEs, so even false positives coincided with real
fast drivers. It does not generalise.

A blind slow-drift spectral detector does not yield a usable type II
census in OVRO-LWA's RFI-heavy 15--85~MHz band. An earlier version of this paper attributed that
entirely to the band (``this is not a detector defect''); the ensemble validation shows the
statement was too kind. Against contaminants that cannot enter the acceptance band the detector
is clean, but against coherent slow-drift background, the class this band is full of, it
false-positives at $\sim$0.38 per window, so the unusable census is a joint property of the
band and of a spectral-only detector's inability to separate window-filling slow structure from
a true type II. That is exactly why the archive's published real-time detector is
type-III-only. Type II detection here needs more than spectra: interferometric imaging to
confirm the emission originates at the eruption site, or human vetting. A handful (order a few) of
the \tiiRealNTypeII\ candidates may be genuine type II, but they are not separable from the
false-positive background with the spectral features available, so we claim no census, no
occurrence rate (the 28-month baseline over the solar-maximum plateau has little cycle-phase
leverage in any case), and no individual detection. The validated detector, the streaming pipeline
at archive scale, and this characterization are the deliverable.
