\title{Dispersion-Measure Redshift Intervals Place Localized Fast Radio Bursts Too Far Away}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We tested the redshift posteriors $p(z\,|\,\mathrm{DM})$ that \code{zdm} infers from fast radio
burst (FRB) dispersion measures (DM), with the parameters of \citet{hoffmann2025}, on
\dmzRealNCert\ FRBs with spectroscopic hosts not used in that fit.
The 95\% intervals contain the host redshift for \dmzRealEOneCovNinetyFive\ of the bursts,
below a floor of \dmzSynBandLoNinetyFive\ fixed in advance. The failure is one-sided:
\dmzRealEOneTailLo\ of the probability integral transforms fall below the central 68\% band and
\dmzRealEOneTailHi\ above it ($p=\dmzRealEOneKsP$), so the posteriors place these hosts too far
away. At the known redshift the DM shows no failure, though only a doubled mean host DM
would have been detected. The error more likely lies in the redshift distribution, from the
population model or from which hosts are identified; this sample cannot tell which.
\end{abstract}

\section{Test}
\label{sec:test}
For a burst at redshift $z$, the extragalactic DM is the sum of a cosmic term, whose mean and
scatter follow \citet{macquart2020}, and a host term redshifted by $1/(1+z)$. Inverting this
gives $p(z\,|\,\mathrm{DM})$. The \code{zdm} code \citep{james2022,zdm} adds each survey's
detection efficiency and an FRB population model. Its parameters were last fitted on
localized and unlocalized bursts from CRAFT, DSA-110 and FAST \citep{hoffmann2025}. The question
here is whether the intervals it quotes contain the true redshift as often as they claim.

The sample is the localized-host compilation of the \code{FRB} repository \citep{frbrepo}:
\dmzRealNLocal\ bursts after duplicates are removed. Bursts that entered the
\citet{hoffmann2025} fit, \dmzRealNProd\ in all, were set aside: their 25 DSA-110 commissioning
bursts (their Section~2.3) and every CRAFT burst to the end of 2023 (their Section~2.4). We
required a spectroscopic redshift and a host association probability of at least 0.9. That
leaves \dmzRealNCert\ bursts: \dmzRealNChime\ from CHIME
\citep[the gold sample of][]{leung2025}, \dmzRealNDsa\ from DSA-110 \citep{sharma2024},
\dmzRealNAskap\ from ASKAP \citep{shannon2025} and \dmzRealNMeer\ from MeerKAT
\citep{caleb2023}. We checked the discovery instrument and redshift of the non-CHIME bursts
against the original papers. FRB~20201124A is listed as a MeerKAT burst but was discovered by
CHIME \citep{lanman2022}; its redshift is 0.0979 \citep{fong2021}. Each burst was scored under
the \code{zdm} survey model of its discovery instrument. The Milky Way DM is the NE2001 value
\citep{cordes2002} plus 50~pc~cm$^{-3}$ for the halo, as in the fit.

For each burst we computed the probability integral transform (PIT), the posterior probability
below the true redshift. A central $q$ interval contains the truth when the PIT lies between
$(1-q)/2$ and $(1+q)/2$. The rule was fixed before any real posterior was computed. The 68\%
and 95\% coverages must lie inside the central 95\% of their distributions for calibrated
samples of \dmzSynN\ bursts, which are \dmzSynBandLoSixtyEight--\dmzSynBandHiSixtyEight\ and at
least \dmzSynBandLoNinetyFive, and a Kolmogorov--Smirnov (KS) test of the PITs against a uniform
distribution must give $p>0.01$. On \dmzSynM\ calibrated synthetic samples the rule fails
\dmzSynFalseFail\ of the time. It has power \dmzSynPowerF\ against a doubled cosmic scatter but
only \dmzSynPowerHost\ against a doubled mean host DM. Because of that low power we changed the
rule after this control and before the real run: a failure is reported, and a pass would not
have been read as evidence of calibration. Planting each burst's redshift from its own posterior
and repeating the analysis passes the rule \dmzRealPlantOne\ of the time, against
\dmzSynPlantExpected\ expected.

We scored two other estimators on the same bursts. One is the \citet{macquart2020} posterior
with the same host and scatter parameters, a prior uniform in comoving volume, and no survey
selection. The other is \code{fruitbat} \citep{batten2019} with its default simulation-based
relation \citep{batten2021}, which includes no host term. Neither was fitted to these bursts,
and both were expected to fail.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
All three estimators fail the rule (Figure~\ref{fig:pit}). For \code{zdm} the 68\% and 95\%
coverages are \dmzRealEOneCovSixtyEight\ and \dmzRealEOneCovNinetyFive, the median PIT is
\dmzRealEOneMedPit, and \dmzRealEOneNBelowHalf\ of the \dmzRealNCert\ PITs lie below 0.5. The
68\% coverage is in band; the failure rests on the 95\% coverage and the KS test. The other two
estimators fail more strongly, with 95\% coverages of \dmzRealETwoCovNinetyFive\ and
\dmzRealEThreeCovNinetyFive. Since the PITs pile up low and almost none lie in the upper tail,
the intervals are displaced toward high redshift rather than too narrow. The median of
$(z_{\rm est}-z)/(1+z)$ is \dmzRealDzOne\ for \code{zdm}, \dmzRealDzTwo\ and \dmzRealDzThree\
for the other two, and \dmzRealDzFour\ for the point estimate of the \code{FRB} package
(\dmzRealDzFourN\ bursts).

The \code{zdm} failure holds without the CHIME bursts (\dmzRealNoChimeN\ bursts,
$p=\dmzRealNoChimeKsP$). Without the ASKAP and MeerKAT bursts the 95\% coverage rises to
\dmzRealChimeDsaCovNinetyFive, inside the band, but the KS test still fails
($p=\dmzRealChimeDsaKsP$, \dmzRealChimeDsaN\ bursts). A halo DM of 25 or 75~pc~cm$^{-3}$ gives
$p=\dmzRealHaloLowKsP$ and $\dmzRealHaloHighKsP$. All \dmzRealNDsa\ DSA-110 bursts and one
CHIME burst are listed in the \code{zdm} survey files; removing them changes no PIT by more
than $\dmzRealPruneShift$. A second redshift for one burst and three DM values that differ between published tables do not change
the result.

The following checks were chosen after the result was seen. The PIT of the DM at the known
redshift, which does not depend on the redshift prior, shows no failure: the coverages are
\dmzRealDmzOneCovSixtyEight\ and \dmzRealDmzOneCovNinetyFive\ and $p=\dmzRealDmzOneKsP$ for
\code{zdm}. In simulations at these redshifts this check detects a doubled mean host DM
\dmzSynDmzPowHost\ of the time, but a doubled cosmic scatter only \dmzSynDmzPowF\ and an
unmodelled 30~pc~cm$^{-3}$ only \dmzSynDmzPowMw. The DM model is therefore not shown to be
correct, but a large error in the mean host DM would have been seen, and the failure more
plausibly lies in the effective redshift distribution. \citet{hoffmann2025} used redshifts only below
$\mathrm{DM}-\mathrm{DM_{ISM}}=183$~pc~cm$^{-3}$, because fainter hosts at higher redshift are
missed and a localized sample is then biased low in redshift at fixed DM. Below that value our
\dmzRealBinLowN\ bursts have a median PIT of \dmzRealBinLowMed\ ($p=\dmzRealBinLowKsP$); above
it, the \dmzRealBinHighN\ bursts have medians of \dmzRealBinHighMedRange\ in three bins. That is
the pattern such selection predicts, but nine bursts have little power and the trend of PIT with
DM is not significant ($\rho=\dmzRealTrendRho$, $p=\dmzRealTrendP$). A population model whose
redshift distribution is wrong at high DM would produce the same pattern.

FRB~20240304B \citep{caleb2026}, at $z=\dmzRealFrbZ$, was not in any statistic. The \code{zdm}
68\% interval, \dmzRealFrbOneLoSixtyEight--\dmzRealFrbOneHiSixtyEight, contains it; those of
the other two estimators (\dmzRealFrbTwoLoSixtyEight--\dmzRealFrbTwoHiSixtyEight\ and
\dmzRealFrbThreeLoSixtyEight--\dmzRealFrbThreeHiSixtyEight) do not. All three place it too far
away, as for the sample.

\section{Limits}
\label{sec:limits}
The result applies to bursts with identified hosts, not to the unlocalized bursts for which
these estimators are intended. Either localized bursts differ in redshift at fixed DM from the
population the model describes, or the model's redshift distribution is wrong; this sample
cannot tell which. The three estimators were scored on the same bursts and are not independent
tests. NE2001 was not compared with other Galactic models, no explicit model of host
identification was fitted, and host magnitudes, available only for the CHIME bursts, were not
used.
