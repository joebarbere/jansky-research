\title{A Reproducible Type III Drift-to-Distance Framework from the Corona to 0.4 AU,

\begin{abstract}
A type III radio burst radiates near the local plasma frequency, so its high-to-low frequency drift,
inverted through a coronal/heliospheric density model, traces an electron beam outward through the
solar atmosphere. We unify four small, separately-validated tools into one reproducible
drift-to-distance framework, validated on three separate events across nearly three decades in
frequency: ground-based e-Callisto \citep{benz2009} in the corona (Newkirk model;
\citealt{newkirk1961}), Wind/WAVES \citep{bougeret1995} in the inner heliosphere, and
STEREO/WAVES \citep{bougeret2008} to $\synRealIpRhiAU$~AU (both via the Leblanc model;
\citealt{leblanc1998}), with each leg's speed carried with the (emission-harmonic
$\times$ density-scale) systematic its own slice propagates, since $f_p^2\propto n_e$ makes the
two exactly degenerate. The ladder's reach is the low-frequency band edge through the assumed
model, not an event property. The whole ladder rests on that density model, which we check where
it can be checked: STEREO/WAVES and a STEREO-A$+$B direction-finding triangulation
\citep{cecconi2008,krupar2012} analyse the same 2013-05-15 event, so the model's distances can
be compared with purely geometric ones. The comparison's honest summary is additive: the
triangulated distances sit a nearly constant $\synRealGeomDiff\pm\synRealGeomDiffStd$~R$_\odot$
outside the Leblanc plasma level (regression slope $\synRealGeomSlope$), the signature of a
constant direction-finding pointing bias, after which the model reproduces the geometry to
$\synRealGeomRmsAdd$~R$_\odot$ rms; the comparison does not validate the model's log--log shape
over the triangulated band (slopes $\synRealGeomLogSlope$ vs 1). This is a synthesis and
reproducibility contribution: each leg is a recover-a-known, and the same-event, open-pipeline
cross-check is the new element, not a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Type III bursts are the radio signature of suprathermal electron beams streaming out along open
magnetic field. Because the emission is near the local plasma frequency $f_p\propto\sqrt{n_e}$, the
observed drift from high to low frequency maps, through a density model $n_e(r)$, to the beam's
heliocentric distance and speed. The method is classic, but its reach is set by the receiver: a
ground spectrometer stops at the ionospheric cutoff (a few R$_\odot$), Wind/WAVES reaches the
Alfv\'en surface, and only a low-frequency space receiver like STEREO/WAVES follows the beam into
genuinely interplanetary space, where multi-spacecraft observations of type III bursts have a long
history \citep{reiner2009}, and statistical surveys support predominantly harmonic emission
near the flux peak \citep{krupar2015}, the convention the ladder adopts inside its committed
(harmonic $\times$ density) grids. We have built and validated four such tools separately; here we unify them
into one framework and confront its central assumption.

That assumption is the density model. Every drift-to-distance result is only as good as the assumed
$n_e(r)$, and it is almost never checked independently. Multi-spacecraft direction-finding
offers exactly that check: two spacecraft each measure the arrival direction of the emission, and the
intersection of the two lines of sight fixes the source in 3D geometrically, with no density
model. When the same burst is seen both ways, the plasma-frequency distance and the geometric distance
are independent estimates of the same quantity, and their agreement (or not) tests the model. We
exploit a case where this is possible.

\section{The unified framework}
\code{type3synthesis} is a thin orchestration module (no new physics) over four tested slices, each a
recover-a-known on public data:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Corona}: e-Callisto dynamic spectra, inverted with the Newkirk model over the
fitted ridge band ($\synRealCoronaFitFlo$--$\synRealCoronaFitFhi$~MHz of the
$\synRealFhi$-MHz-topped detection band), reach
$\synRealCoronaRlo$--$\synRealCoronaRhi$~R$_\odot$ (radial speed $\synRealCoronaSpeed\,c$,
analysis spread $\synRealCoronaSpeedMin$--$\synRealCoronaSpeedMax$, model bracket
$\synRealCoronaGridSpeedLo$--$\synRealCoronaGridSpeedHi$).
\item \textbf{Inner heliosphere}: Wind/WAVES (RAD2), Leblanc model, to $\synRealHelioRhi$~R$_\odot$
under harmonic emission at the quiet-Sun profile ($\synRealHelioSpeed\pm\synRealHelioSpeedErr\,c$;
across the committed harmonic$\times$density grid the speed spans
$\synRealHelioGridSpeedLo$--$\synRealHelioGridSpeedHi\,c$ and the reach
$\synRealHelioGridReachLo$--$\synRealHelioGridReachHi$~AU, so its own slice declines to name the
Alfv\'en region exclusively, and so do we).
\item \textbf{Interplanetary}: STEREO/WAVES HFR, Leblanc model, to $\synRealIpRhi$~R$_\odot$
$=\synRealIpRhiAU$~AU, the HFR band edge through the model, not an event property
($\synRealIpSpeed\pm\synRealIpSpeedErr\,c$; grid
$\synRealIpGridSpeedLo$--$\synRealIpGridSpeedHi\,c$, reach
$\synRealIpGridReachLo$--$\synRealIpGridReachHi$~AU).
\item \textbf{Geometric}: a STEREO-A$+$B direction-finding triangulation of the same
interplanetary event, whose farthest point ($\synRealGeomRhi$~R$_\odot$) carries the constant
direction-finding offset measured below and is therefore reported separately, never as the
ladder's reach.
\end{itemize}
The same module emits the unified distance ladder (Figure~\ref{fig:synth}, left): heliocentric distance
versus emission frequency for all four, over the Newkirk and Leblanc model curves, spanning
$\synRealFhi$~MHz in the corona down to $\synRealFlo$~MHz at $\synRealOverallRhiAU$~AU (the
plasma-frequency reach).

\section{The geometric cross-check}
The triangulation analyses the same burst (2013-05-15) the interplanetary leg fits, and compares
the triangulated distance $r_{\rm geom}$ per channel with the Leblanc curve evaluated at the
same frequencies, $r_{\rm plasma}(f)$: a model-free measurement against the model itself
(Figure~\ref{fig:synth}, right; note $r_{\rm plasma}$ is a deterministic function of frequency,
not a second measurement, and the drift fit itself does not enter). The companion paper
(\code{papers/triangulate}) derives the honest summary, and this synthesis inherits it: all
triangulated channels sit outside the plasma level by a nearly constant
$\synRealGeomDiff\pm\synRealGeomDiffStd$~R$_\odot$ while $r_{\rm plasma}$ varies by a factor
of 13; the regression slope is $\synRealGeomSlope$ (an enhancement of the density itself would
multiply, i.e.\ slope well above 1), so the offset is the signature of a constant few-degree
direction-finding pointing bias (of the size the triangulation slice's committed noise
calibration produces from apparent-source scatter alone), and after that one constant offset
the Leblanc model reproduces the geometry to $\synRealGeomRmsAdd$~R$_\odot$ rms ($\sim$12\%).
What the comparison does not do is validate the model's log--log shape over the
triangulated band: the log--log regression slope of $r_{\rm geom}$ on $r_{\rm plasma}$ is
$\synRealGeomLogSlope$ against 1 for a pure scale factor, so the fractional discrepancy grows
from tens of percent at the low-frequency end to a factor $\sim$3 at the high end (the
frequency-dependent ratio $\synRealGeomRatio$ is quoted only as the median of that trend). The
Pearson correlation ($r=\synRealGeomCorr$) is reported for continuity but carries little
information here, since any monotone power law in frequency correlates with the Leblanc curve at
$r>0.95$. This is the element the framework adds: a same-event, model-free check that
bounds the model, confirming the Leblanc level to $\sim$12\% after one measured
instrumental offset, while showing that the check cannot separate a pointing bias from a shape
error at the tens-of-percent level. It also tests the Leblanc model only over the triangulated
band; the Newkirk coronal leg is unchecked by it.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is a synthesis: one reproducible drift-to-distance framework validated from the
corona to $\synRealOverallRhiAU$~AU across four public datasets (three instrument suites:
e-Callisto, Wind/WAVES, and STEREO/WAVES in two roles, its HFR spectra and its two-spacecraft
L3 direction-finding product), and (the new part) a same-event geometric check that bounds the
density-model distance the framework rests on. The honest limitations are as follows. (i) The
corona, inner-heliosphere, and interplanetary legs are different events, twelve years apart, so
this is one method validated across three regimes, not one beam tracked end to end: a single
event caught simultaneously by all three receivers would be the stronger demonstration and is
the natural next step. (ii) The geometric cross-check is event-specific (2013-05-15) and its
verdict is bounded: it reproduces the Leblanc level to $\sim$12\% after one constant
measured pointing offset, but it does not confirm the model's log--log shape over the band, and
it tests the Leblanc model only; the Newkirk coronal leg is unchecked by it. (iii) The
per-leg (harmonic $\times$ density) systematic is carried in the ladder itself (the grid
brackets above), not merely referenced; the remaining per-leg systematics (peak-time vs onset
speed bias, coarse-cadence fit statistics) are bounded in the component slices. (iv) The
Newkirk and Leblanc models are not designed to join continuously: over the two legs' actual
overlap band ($\synRealHandoffFlo$--$\synRealHandoffFhi$~MHz) they differ in heliocentric
radius by $\synRealHandoffPctLo$--$\synRealHandoffPctHi$\% (computed and committed),
reflecting their different observational bases (white-light active corona vs Wind type III
drifts), so the ladder is two stitched regimes, not one calibrated model. None of these
undermine the synthesis: the method is validated in each regime and its model distance is
geometrically bounded, but they limit what three single events can claim. Every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros this document
\code{\textbackslash input}s, and \code{python -m jansky\_research.type3synthesis} regenerates them;
\code{make reproduce} runs the four recover-a-known events on the public archives.
