\title{Environment-Split HI Mass Functions in FASHI DR2 Against Two Null Tests}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We split the HI mass function of FASHI DR2 by environment inside the SDSS footprint and compare
the knee offsets with two nulls: void- and group-shaped volumes placed at random, and environment
labels shuffled among galaxies at fixed redshift and detection sensitivity. With each galaxy's
$V_{\rm max}$ restricted to its environment, the void$-$wall offset of $\feRealEnvVoidOffset$~dex
exceeds the two nulls by \feRealEnvVoidQuadSigma\ and \feRealShufVoidEnvSigma$\sigma$, and we do not
report it as a detection. The group$-$field offset of $+\feRealEnvGroupOffset$~dex exceeds the
shuffle null by \feRealShufGroupEnvSigma$\sigma$ but is carried by sources whose flux aperture holds
another galaxy: at fixed optical luminosity they carry $\feRealBlendGroupOptInInnerDiff$~dex more HI
than isolated galaxies, against $\feRealBlendGroupOptInRingDiff$~dex when the neighbour lies just
outside the aperture, the signature of blending in FAST's beam. Without them the group offset is
$+\feRealBlendGroupFixedEnvClean\pm\feRealBlendGroupFixedEnvCleanErr$~dex. Both offsets also depend
on the fitted low-mass slope.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Void galaxies are expected to have a lower HI mass function (HIMF) knee than wall galaxies, and
group galaxies to lose gas to stripping \citep{solanes2001}. \citet{moorman2014}, with an estimator
that does not assume uniform density, found the ALFALFA void knee $\sim$0.14~dex lower, and the
ALFALFA low-mass slope differs between its void-dominated and Virgo fields \citep{jones2018}. The
FAST All Sky HI Survey \citep[FASHI;][]{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2} now provides \feRealNDRTwo\ sources
with a completeness $C$ and accessible volume $V_{\rm max}$ for each; the one environment study on
FASHI data used 230 group galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}. Environment splits of a flux-limited
$1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMF also select in distance, sensitivity and structure, and a single-dish beam
merges close neighbours, so we compare the splits with two nulls and test for blending. An
earlier version of this analysis, on DR1, reported a void offset of
$\feRealDROneMaxVoidKneeOffset$~dex without a null test.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
We keep the sources of Table~2 of \citet{fashi_dr2} with $C\geq\feRealCMin$, whose weights
$1/(C_i V_i)$ reproduce the published global HIMF ($\log M^*=\feRealGlobalLogMStar$,
$\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha$, against \feRealDRTwoPaperLogMStar\ and $\feRealDRTwoPaperAlpha$), and use the \feRealBlendNPool\ inside both
a padded box around the Douglass+2023 VoidFinder voids \citep{douglass2023} and the SDSS footprint
(1$^\circ$ cells holding Tempel+2017 galaxies; keeping only cells with at least
\feRealStrictMinPerCell, half the median, moves no offset by more than \feRealStrictMaxShift~dex).
Void members lie inside any void hole, and group members within the projected $R_{200}$ and
1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of a Tempel+2017 group \citep{tempel2017}. Each galaxy is weighted by
$C_i V_i\,g_E(d_{\max,i})$, where $g_E(D)$ is the share of the volume within $D$ occupied by its
environment, from $2\times10^6$ randoms classified by the same rules. Schechter fits are formally
poor (reduced $\chi^2$ up to \feRealGlobalRedChi) and their errors are scaled accordingly.

The first null moves each void rigidly, or each group with its velocity and $R_{200}$, to a
random position in the footprint. The second gives the environment label to the real number of
members in each cell of redshift ($\Delta z=0.0025$) and detection sensitivity (quintiles of the
catalogue's \code{rms\_beam}), drawn at random (\feRealShufReps\ shuffles); terciles or redshift
alone move no excess by more than \feRealShufStrataMaxDiff~dex. For blending we match each source to the nearest Tempel galaxy within half
a beam and $\pm300$~km\,s$^{-1}$ and classify it by its other neighbours in that window: inside its
flux aperture plus half a beam (blendable), only in a 3~arcmin ring beyond it, or none. We compare
the classes within cells of redshift and HI mass, or of redshift and the counterpart's absolute
$r$ magnitude. Excesses over a null are divided by the measured offset's fit error and the null
spread in quadrature.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
The void offset, $\feRealEnvVoidOffset\pm\feRealEnvVoidErr$~dex, exceeds the placements by
$\feRealEnvNullExcess$~dex (\feRealEnvVoidQuadSigma$\sigma$) and the shuffles by
$\feRealShufVoidEnvExcess$~dex (\feRealShufVoidEnvSigma$\sigma$; Figure~\ref{fig:nulls}). The
placed voids hold \feRealEnvPlaceVoidOccLo--\feRealEnvPlaceVoidOccHi\ galaxies against
\feRealNInVoid, and contain real void members at \feRealNullCOverlap\% against
\feRealVoidBaseRatePct\% of the sample, which pulls that null towards the measurement. The group
offset, $+\feRealEnvGroupOffset\pm\feRealEnvGroupErr$~dex, exceeds the shuffles by
$+\feRealShufGroupEnvExcess$~dex (\feRealShufGroupEnvSigma$\sigma$). The random group regions hold
\feRealEnvPlaceGroupOccLo--\feRealEnvPlaceGroupOccHi\ members against \feRealGroupNInGroupReal, and
across that range their mean offset runs from $\feRealEnvPlaceGroupMeanAtLo$ to
$+\feRealEnvPlaceGroupMeanAtHi$~dex, so that null is uninformative and we quote no significance
against it.

A neighbour lies within one beam and $\pm300$~km\,s$^{-1}$ for \feRealBlendGroupFixedFlagIn\% of
group members and \feRealBlendGroupFixedFlagOut\% of field galaxies. Without them the group
offset is $+\feRealBlendGroupFixedEnvClean\pm\feRealBlendGroupFixedEnvCleanErr$~dex
(\feRealBlendGroupFixedShufSigma$\sigma$ over the shuffles), though they are also the most massive
members (median $\log M=\feRealBlendGroupFixedLogMFlag$ against \feRealBlendGroupFixedLogMClean).
At fixed optical luminosity, group members with a neighbour inside the aperture have
$\feRealBlendGroupOptInInnerDiff\pm\feRealBlendGroupOptInInnerSe$~dex more HI than isolated ones,
and those with a neighbour only in the ring $\feRealBlendGroupOptInRingDiff\pm\feRealBlendGroupOptInRingSe$~dex;
field galaxies give $\feRealBlendGroupOptOutInnerDiff$ and $\feRealBlendGroupOptOutRingDiff$~dex.
Where the neighbour is within 100~km\,s$^{-1}$, $\log W_{50}$ at fixed HI mass is
$\feRealBlendGroupLwInDvLtDiff\pm\feRealBlendGroupLwInDvLtSe$~dex and $W_{20}/W_{50}$ is
$+\feRealBlendGroupWtwentyInDvLtDiff\pm\feRealBlendGroupWtwentyInDvLtSe$ relative to isolated
members. Among members that cannot be blended, those inside half of $R_{200}$ are no more massive than
those outside ($\feRealBlendGroupSegDiff\pm\feRealBlendGroupSegSe$~dex). With the low-mass slope
fixed at the sample's $\alpha=\feRealBlendGroupCommonAlpha$ the offsets become
$\feRealBlendVoidCommonEnvOffset\pm\feRealBlendVoidCommonEnvErr$ and
$+\feRealBlendGroupCommonEnvOffset\pm\feRealBlendGroupCommonEnvErr$~dex.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The void excess is \feRealEnvVoidQuadSigma--\feRealShufVoidEnvSigma$\sigma$ depending on the null, and
its size depends on the fitted slope; we do not report it as a detection. A void is emptier than a
typical randomly placed volume, so no rigid placement matches its occupancy, and the shuffle that
does has no spatial structure.

The group offset is positive, whereas stripping would lower the knee. An HI excess that appears
only when the neighbour lies inside the flux aperture, narrower line cores with broader wings for
neighbours close in velocity, and no mass segregation among members that cannot be blended all
point to blending of neighbours in FAST's beam. Because the aperture also selects the closest
pairs, a physical HI excess in the closest pairs is not excluded, and the offset remaining without
flagged sources may include blending with optically fainter neighbours. We therefore do not
attribute the group offset to environment. For environment-split $1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMFs from
single-dish surveys we recommend both nulls, reported with their occupancy, and the HI content at
fixed optical luminosity of sources with a neighbour inside the flux aperture.
