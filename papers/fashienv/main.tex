\begin{abstract}
The FAST All Sky HI Survey (FASHI), the largest blind HI-galaxy catalogue to date, publishes only a
global Schechter HI mass function (HIMF) in its DR2 \citep{fashi_dr2} and DR1 \citep{fashi_dr1};
its one environment study used 230 group galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}. We split the FASHI
DR1 HIMF by environment, cross-matching its \feN\ HI sources against the Douglass+2023 SDSS void
catalogue \citep{douglass2023} and the Tempel+2017 group catalogue \citep{tempel2017}, and fit a
Schechter function to the $1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMF in each. In the SDSS-cap overlap, where the
optical catalogues apply, the void HIMF
knee is suppressed relative to walls by $\Delta\log M^*=\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex
(\feRealVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$; void $\log M^*/M_\odot = \feRealVoidLogMStar$ from
\feRealNInVoid\ void galaxies, wall $\feRealWallLogMStar$), consistent in sign with, and somewhat
larger in magnitude than, the ALFALFA void-HIMF suppression \citep[$\sim$0.14~dex;][]{moorman2014}.
Sample variance across voids does not limit the result: a delete-one-void jackknife gives
\feRealVoidJkErr~dex, below the fit error. Two biases act instead, both in the signal's
direction: the $1/V_{\rm max}$
uniform-density assumption (violated by construction here) and a bounding-box comparison bin
holding 58\% of the catalogue; we report the offset as an upper bound on any true suppression.
Group members show the opposite offset, $\Delta\log M^*=+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex
(\feRealGroupKneeSigma$\sigma$, \feRealNGroupMembers\ members), reflecting survivor bias: the
group galaxies that retain detectable HI in a flux-limited sample are the more massive, gas-rich
survivors, so the group knee is an upper envelope, not a deficiency. The relative knee offsets
are robust, since every environment bin uses the same estimator; the
absolute faint-end slope is not, pending FASHI's full completeness function. The pipeline,
validated by injection on an environment-dependent mock, applies to the DR2 catalogue
(156{,}411 sources) once released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The shape of the HI mass function (how many galaxies of each atomic-gas mass exist per unit
volume) encodes the balance of gas accretion, consumption, and removal, set partly by
environment. Cluster and group galaxies lose HI to ram-pressure and tidal stripping
\citep{solanes2001}; void galaxies, evolving in isolation, are expected to have a HIMF with a
suppressed knee and a steeper faint end \citep{moorman2014,jones2018}. FASHI, the FAST all-sky
blind HI survey \citep{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2}, is now the largest such catalogue, but its released
products stop at the global HIMF; the one environment analysis on FASHI data \citep{fashi_groups}
used 230 group galaxies. The DR1 catalogue alone contains \feN\ HI sources, two orders of
magnitude more, and its environment-split HIMF has not been measured.

The DR2 catalogue is not yet public as of this
writing (release targeted for late 2026), so we work with the public DR1 catalogue as a first
leg, following the usual practice of large surveys whose analyses proceed on an earlier release
while a bigger one is embargoed. Moreover, FASHI carries HI masses and distances but no stellar
masses or optical diameters, so the classical Haynes--Giovanelli HI deficiency (which needs
optical morphology) and gas-fraction-at-fixed-$M_*$ statistics are out of reach; the HIMF itself,
split by environment, is the measurement these data support on their own.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
\label{sec:method}
From FASHI DR1 (VizieR \code{J/other/SCPMA/67.19511}) we take each source's position, redshift,
integrated flux, distance, and precomputed $\log M_{\rm HI}$. We classify environment two ways.

Voids: we convert positions to the comoving Cartesian frame of the Douglass+2023 VoidFinder
maximal spheres \citep[working in the survey's $h^{-1}$Mpc units, which are $H_0$-independent for
comoving distance;][]{douglass2023}. A galaxy is a void member if it lies inside any sphere, and a
wall galaxy if it lies inside the void-survey volume but outside every sphere; galaxies beyond
that volume (most of FASHI's southern and high-redshift sky) are unclassifiable and excluded,
leaving \feRealNInVoid\ void and the surrounding wall galaxies in the SDSS cap. The classifiable
volume is approximated by a padded bounding box around the void spheres. The SDSS cap is a wedge
and its bounding box is several times its volume, so the contamination is large: the wall bin holds
\feRealNWall\ galaxies, 58\% of all of DR1, and its knee \feRealWallLogMStar\ sits 0.010\,dex from
the all-sky fit \feRealGlobalLogMStar, a fifth of its own error. The comparison is therefore
closer to void versus everything than to void versus wall. The contamination dilutes the wall bin
toward the global population, so, if anything, it weakens the reported contrast; a genuine
footprint mask, rather than a bounding box, is required to remove it and is not yet available.

Groups: a galaxy within the projected $R_{200}$ and 1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of any Tempel+2017 group is
a member (\feRealNGroupMembers\ in the cap), the rest field.

The $1/V_{\rm max}$ method assumes each
source is uniformly distributed within its accessible volume, which is false for a sample
selected by its density environment. For the void bin the accessible volume is not the survey
cone but the union of void spheres inside it, and that fraction falls to zero at the near and far
edges of the box while staying finite for the wall bin. Because $V_{\rm max}$ is mass-dependent
(brighter galaxies reach further, sampling more void-free volume), the distortion is
mass-dependent too, and it suppresses $\phi$ at the high-mass end of the void bin, in the same
direction as the reported signal. A density-insensitive estimator (SWML) is the standard answer
and is not attempted here; until it is, the offset should be read as an upper bound on any true
suppression rather than a measurement of it.

For each environment we build the HIMF by the $1/V_{\rm max}$ method \citep{schmidt1968}: each
source contributes $1/V_i$ to its mass bin, where $V_i$ is the comoving volume out to the
distance at which its flux would fall to the survey limit, and we fit a Schechter function
\citep{schechter1976} in log space. The pipeline is exercised on a flux-limited mock with two
injected Schechter HIMFs (void: lower $M^*$, steeper $\alpha$; wall: the reference), which it
fits back to the injected knees and slopes and to the correct knee-offset sign
(\code{tests/test\_fashienv.py}).

This simple $1/V_{\rm max}$ estimator uses a single flux limit rather than FASHI's full
completeness function, so the absolute faint-end slopes from the $1/V_{\rm max}$ fits to the
survey data ($\alpha\approx\feRealVoidAlpha$ to $\feRealWallAlpha$ across the void and wall
bins) are steeper
than the published FASHI global HIMF. Because every environment bin uses the identical
$1/V_{\rm max}$ estimator, the relative knee offsets between environments are robust to this; the
absolute slope is not, and we do not claim to reproduce the global HIMF.

\subsection{Voids}
The void HIMF knee sits below the wall value by
$\Delta\log M^* = \feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex, a
\feRealVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$ suppression (void
$\log M^* = \feRealVoidLogMStar\pm\feRealVoidLogMStarErr$, wall
$\feRealWallLogMStar\pm\feRealWallLogMStarErr$).

Those are Poisson counting errors within one realisation of large-scale structure, which cannot
capture how much voids differ from one another, the variance that usually dominates a void
statistic. Deleting each of the \feRealVoidNOcc\ occupied voids in turn and refitting gives a
jackknife error of \feRealVoidJkErr\,dex, smaller than the fit error, so the offset strengthens
to \feRealVoidJkSigma$\sigma$ on that axis rather than weakening; a bootstrap over voids agrees
(0.031). Sample variance across voids is therefore not what limits this measurement; the limit
is set by bias rather than variance, which resampling cannot bound. The $1/V_{\rm max}$ density assumption
(Section~\ref{sec:data}) and the bounding-box comparison bin (Section~\ref{sec:data}) both act in
the direction of the reported signal. The offset is consistent in sign, and somewhat larger, than
the ALFALFA void-HIMF knee suppression \citep[$-0.14$~dex, $\sim$2$\sigma$;][]{moorman2014}, an
independent measurement from a different telescope and pipeline.
The HI measurement itself is independent of ALFALFA's (FAST rather than Arecibo), but the
environment classification for both comes from the same SDSS DR7 parent sample and largely the
same voids, so the comparison is a deeper HI sample over much the same volume rather than an
independent realisation of large-scale structure. We note also that the void
membership depends on the adopted distance--redshift relation (we use the Douglass catalogue's
own Planck2018 cosmology so the frames match); an EdS relation dilutes the offset to
$\sim$1$\sigma$, so the quoted value is specific to the matched-cosmology geometry. The void
faint-end slope ($\feRealVoidAlpha$) is marginally flatter than the wall's ($\feRealWallAlpha$),
consistent with Moorman+2014's mild flattening rather than the steepening of \citet{jones2018};
at DR1 sample size the two are within their mutual uncertainties, so we report the sign, not a
detection of the slope difference.

\subsection{Groups}
Group members show a knee above the field by $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex
(\feRealGroupKneeSigma$\sigma$; group $\log M^* = \feRealGroupLogMStar$, field
$\feRealFieldLogMStar$), statistically the stronger of our two offsets, though not our main
result, because it is survivor-biased. Taken at face value a positive
offset is the opposite of gas stripping; it is instead the expected signature of a selection
effect, not a physical enhancement: a flux-limited HI survey detects the group galaxies that have
retained substantial HI, i.e.\ the more massive, gas-rich survivors (the group knee,
\feRealGroupLogMStar, even exceeds the global \feRealGlobalLogMStar\ for this reason), while
stripped members drop out of the sample entirely. The group knee is therefore an upper envelope
on the surviving HI-rich population, and the true environmental effect (including the
undetected, stripped galaxies) is more negative than $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset$~dex. The
void--wall comparison does not suffer this bias, since voids do not preferentially strip HI,
which is why we take it as the main result despite its lower raw significance.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
Splitting the FASHI DR1 HIMF by environment, we find the void knee suppressed by
$\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex relative to walls
(\feRealVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$), an independent, FAST-based measurement of the ALFALFA void HIMF
suppression \citep{moorman2014}, and a group-member knee offset that is survivor-biased upward
and thus a lower bound on HI stripping. The relative measurements are robust, as all bins share
one estimator; the
absolute faint-end slope requires FASHI's own completeness function, and the void offset's central
value carries a distance-model dependence that we quantify. The
same pipeline applies to the DR2 catalogue (156{,}411 sources, $\sim$4$\times$ DR1 and a wider
footprint) once it is public. Halving the errors would move the
void offset toward a firm ($\gtrsim$4$\sigma$) detection and would extend the group split to
cluster-scale densities.
