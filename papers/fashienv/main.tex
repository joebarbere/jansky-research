\begin{abstract}
The FAST All Sky HI Survey (FASHI) publishes a global Schechter HI mass function (HIMF) for its
DR2 catalogue \citep{fashi_dr2}, but no environment split; the one environment study on FASHI data
used 230 group galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}. We split the DR2 HIMF by environment, using the
\feN\ of its \feRealNDRTwo\ sources above the survey's 50\% completeness limit, each weighted by
$1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$ from the catalogue's own completeness and accessible volume; as a bookkeeping
check this reproduces the published global HIMF. Against the Douglass+2023 SDSS voids
\citep{douglass2023}, the void HIMF knee lies $\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex below
the wall knee. Placing the same voids at random positions in the survey footprint, however, gives
$\feRealNullMean\pm\feRealNullStd$~dex over \feRealNullReps\ placements, so the estimator and the
survey geometry alone produce \feRealNullBiasPct\% of the measured offset, in the direction of the
signal. The part attributable to the real voids is $\feRealNullExcess$~dex, \feRealNullExcessSigma$\sigma$
against the scatter of random placements and \feRealNullExcessJkSigma$\sigma$ against a
delete-one-void jackknife: a tentative result, not a detection. Weighting by a single flux limit
instead, on the same sample, moves the offset by $\feRealWeightEffect\pm\feRealWeightEffectErr$~dex.
Group members of the Tempel+2017 catalogue \citep{tempel2017} have a knee
$+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex above the field (\feRealGroupKneeSigma$\sigma$);
we list the systematics that could produce it but do not identify its cause.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The shape of the HI mass function (how many galaxies of each atomic-gas mass exist per unit
volume) encodes the balance of gas accretion, consumption, and removal, set partly by
environment. Cluster and group galaxies lose HI to ram-pressure and tidal stripping
\citep{solanes2001}; void galaxies, evolving in isolation, are expected to have a HIMF with a
suppressed knee and a steeper faint end \citep{moorman2014,jones2018}. FASHI, the FAST all-sky
blind HI survey \citep{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2}, is now the largest such catalogue, but its released
products stop at the global HIMF; the one environment analysis on FASHI data \citep{fashi_groups}
used 230 group galaxies. The DR2 catalogue contains \feRealNDRTwo\ HI sources, nearly three orders
of magnitude more, and its environment-split HIMF has not been measured. An earlier version of this
analysis used the DR1 catalogue with a single flux-limit weighting; we report here the DR2
measurement with the survey's own completeness correction, and show in
Section~\ref{sec:weighting} how the two weightings differ. FASHI carries HI masses and distances but no stellar
masses or optical diameters, so the classical Haynes--Giovanelli HI deficiency (which needs
optical morphology) and gas-fraction-at-fixed-$M_*$ statistics are out of reach; the HIMF itself,
split by environment, is the measurement these data support on their own.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
\label{sec:method}
DR2 is not in VizieR; we take Table~2 of \citet{fashi_dr2} from the survey's public CSTCloud
share, linked from the FASHI release page. For each source we use the position, optical
redshift, integrated flux, distance, precomputed $\log M_{\rm HI}$, completeness $C$ and
$V_{\rm max}$ (the latter computed by the survey over 19{,}482~deg$^2$). We drop the
\feRealNZNonpos\ sources with $z\leq0$, which have no comoving position, and keep the
\feN\ sources with $C\geq\feRealCMin$, the sample defined by the survey's own HIMF (theirs, which also
cuts on $W_{20}$ and a noise statistic not in the public table, holds just over 109{,}000). The DR2
distance convention differs from DR1's: of the DR1 sources, \feRealMatchedPct\% have a DR2
counterpart within 1.5~arcmin and 100~km\,s$^{-1}$, and their DR2 distances are larger by a median
factor \feRealDistRatio, so absolute masses from the two releases are not interchangeable. We classify environment two ways.

Voids: we convert positions to the comoving Cartesian frame of the Douglass+2023 VoidFinder
catalogue \citep[working in its $h^{-1}$Mpc units;][]{douglass2023}. A VoidFinder void is the
union of all its holes, and a galaxy inside any hole of any void is a void member
(\feRealNInVoid\ galaxies; using only each void's maximal sphere, as in our DR1 analysis, gives
\feRealMaxSphereNInVoid\ members and an offset of $\feRealMaxSphereVoidKneeOffset$~dex, so the
choice does not matter here). A galaxy inside the void-survey volume but outside every hole is a
wall galaxy; galaxies beyond that volume (most of FASHI's southern and high-redshift sky) are
excluded. The classifiable volume is approximated by a padded bounding box around the voids. The
SDSS cap is a wedge and its bounding box is several times its volume, so the wall bin holds
\feRealNWall\ galaxies, \feRealWallPct\% of the sample, and includes sky where no voids were
searched; its knee sits \feRealWallMinusGlobal\,dex from the all-sky fit. Real void galaxies
outside the SDSS footprint therefore land in the wall bin, which dilutes the contrast.

Groups: a galaxy within the projected $R_{200}$ and 1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of any Tempel+2017 group is
a member (\feRealNGroupMembers\ in the cap), the rest field. Group velocities are the catalogue's
CMB-frame redshifts converted to the heliocentric frame of FASHI's $v_{\rm opt}$ with the Planck
dipole.

The $1/V_{\rm max}$ method assumes each source is uniformly distributed within its accessible
volume, which is false for a sample selected by its density environment, and the survey's
$V_{\rm max}$ is computed over the full footprint rather than the void or wall volume. We do not
predict the sign of the resulting bias; we measure it with a random-void null. Each of the
Douglass voids is moved rigidly, keeping its distance, its holes and its size, to a random sky
position inside the SDSS footprint, and the void/wall split is recomputed with the same weights.
Any offset these randomly placed voids produce is the estimator's and the geometry's own.

For each environment we build the HIMF by the $1/V_{\rm max}$ method \citep{schmidt1968}: each
source contributes $1/(C_i V_i)$ to its mass bin, with $V_i$ and $C_i$ the catalogue's accessible
volume and completeness, and we fit a Schechter function \citep{schechter1976} in log space. The
fits are formally poor (reduced $\chi^2$ of \feRealVoidRedChi, \feRealWallRedChi\ and
\feRealGlobalRedChi\ for the void, wall and global HIMFs); the fitter scales the parameter errors
by the reduced $\chi^2$, and the knee positions depend on the Schechter form. On a flux-limited mock
in which environment is a random label on uniformly placed galaxies, the pipeline recovers the sign
of an injected knee offset (\code{tests/test\_fashienv.py}); that mock contains no void geometry,
so the random-void null, not the mock, is the test of the geometric bias.

\subsection{Effect of the weighting}
\label{sec:weighting}
Our DR1 analysis weighted each source by the volume out to a single flux limit of
0.30~Jy\,km\,s$^{-1}$. That estimator gives faint-end slopes near $\alpha\approx\feRealOptAGlobalAlpha$
(DR2) and $\feRealDROneGlobalAlpha$ (DR1), much steeper than the published FASHI HIMF, whereas the
$1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$ weighting reproduces it ($\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha$ against $-1.31\pm0.02$ in
\citealt{fashi_dr2}), as it should when it uses the survey's own completeness. On the same
$C\geq\feRealCMin$ sample the single-limit weighting gives a void offset of
$\feRealOptASameVoidKneeOffset$~dex, so the weighting alone moves it by
$\feRealWeightEffect\pm\feRealWeightEffectErr$~dex (a paired delete-one-void jackknife on the
difference; \feRealWeightEffectSigma$\sigma$). The single-limit values on DR1
($\feRealDROneVoidKneeOffset$~dex, \feRealDROneN\ sources) and on all of DR2
($\feRealOptAVoidKneeOffset$~dex) agree, so the larger catalogue does not by itself change the
answer. We adopt the completeness-corrected values.

\subsection{Voids}
The void HIMF knee sits below the wall value by
$\Delta\log M^* = \feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex (void
$\log M^* = \feRealVoidLogMStar\pm\feRealVoidLogMStarErr$, wall
$\feRealWallLogMStar\pm\feRealWallLogMStarErr$). Deleting each of the \feRealJkNOcc\ occupied voids
in turn gives a jackknife error of \feRealJkErr\,dex, comparable to the fit error, so there is no
excess variance from void to void.

The random-void null changes the interpretation. Over \feRealNullReps\ random placements of the
voids the offset is $\feRealNullMean\pm\feRealNullStd$~dex (mean and standard deviation): the
estimator and geometry produce \feRealNullBiasPct\% of the measured value by themselves. No
placement gave an offset as negative as the measured one (one-sided $p=\feRealNullP$). The excess
attributable to the real voids is $\feRealNullExcess$~dex, \feRealNullExcessSigma$\sigma$ against
the scatter of the placements and \feRealNullExcessJkSigma$\sigma$ against the jackknife error. We
regard it as tentative. It is smaller than the ALFALFA void-HIMF knee suppression
\citep[$-0.14$~dex;][]{moorman2014}. The HI measurement is
independent of ALFALFA's, but the environment classification for both comes from the same SDSS
DR7 parent sample and largely the same voids.

Void membership depends on the distance--redshift relation used to place galaxies relative to
the voids; we use the Douglass catalogue's own Planck2018 cosmology, so that galaxies and voids
share one frame. An Einstein--de~Sitter relation gives $\feRealEdsVoidKneeOffset$~dex, but that is
not a test of the signal's robustness: it changes the void membership of \feRealEdsChangedLowZPct\%
of void galaxies at $z<0.02$ and of more galaxies than the void bin contains at $z>0.06$
(\feRealEdsChangedHighZPct\%), so it would remove a real signal as readily as an artefact. The void
faint-end slope ($\feRealVoidAlpha$) and the wall's ($\feRealWallAlpha$) are within their mutual
uncertainties, and we do not claim a slope difference.

\subsection{Groups}
Group members show a knee above the field by $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex
(\feRealGroupKneeSigma$\sigma$; group $\log M^* = \feRealGroupLogMStar$, field
$\feRealFieldLogMStar$). We do not identify its cause. Survivor bias does not explain it: the
$1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMF counts a stripped galaxy at its current, lower mass, which is the
environmental effect the estimator measures, and such a galaxy leaves the sample only below the
smallest detectable masses, far below the knee. Candidate systematics act in the direction of
the offset. At FAST's $\sim$3~arcmin beam, neighbouring group members blend, which raises their
measured HI masses; the same confusion raises wall masses relative to voids. The field bin
includes sky outside the Tempel footprint, where no group catalogue exists. Group identification
in a flux-limited redshift survey depends on distance, which correlates with HI mass. The group
split has not been through a random-placement null of its own.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
Splitting the FASHI DR2 HIMF by environment with the survey's own completeness correction, we find
a void knee $\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex below the wall knee, of which
$\feRealNullMean$~dex is reproduced by randomly placed voids; the remaining $\feRealNullExcess$~dex
is tentative (\feRealNullExcessSigma$\sigma$ against the random-placement scatter,
\feRealNullExcessJkSigma$\sigma$ against the void jackknife). The group knee lies above the field,
for reasons not established here. Weighting by a single flux limit rather than the survey's
completeness changes the void offset by $\feRealWeightEffect$~dex on the same sample. A
density-insensitive estimator, a true footprint mask, a random-placement null for the groups, and
a test for beam confusion are the next steps.
