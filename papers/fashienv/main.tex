\title{Environment-Split HI Mass Functions in FASHI DR2 and Random-Placement Null Tests}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We split the HI mass function (HIMF) of the FAST All Sky HI Survey DR2 \citep{fashi_dr2} by
environment, using the \feN\ sources above the survey's 50\% completeness limit weighted by
$1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$ from the catalogue's own completeness and accessible volume. Against the
Douglass+2023 VoidFinder voids \citep{douglass2023} the void$-$wall knee offset is
$\Delta\log M^*=\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex, and
the Tempel+2017 group-member knee \citep{tempel2017} is offset from the field by
$+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex. We test both offsets by relocating the voids and the groups at random within the survey
footprint. Randomly placed voids give $\feRealNullCMean\pm\feRealNullCStd$~dex and randomly
placed groups $+\feRealGroupNullMean\pm\feRealGroupNullStd$~dex, so splitting this sample by
arbitrary volumes shifts the fitted knee by amounts comparable to published void effects. The
group offset is reached by \feRealGroupNullNGe\ of \feRealGroupNullReps\ random placements. The
void offset lies beyond all \feRealNullCReps\ void placements, but the null does not supply a
unique baseline: its mean changes by $\feRealNullCSlope$~dex per unit overlap with the real
voids, and the residual after subtracting it is $\feRealNullCExcess$~dex under the completeness
weighting and $\feRealOptANullExcess$~dex under a single flux limit. We find no environmental
dependence of the HIMF that survives these tests, and recommend random-placement nulls for
environment-split $1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMFs.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The HI mass function is expected to depend on environment. Group and cluster galaxies lose gas
to ram-pressure and tidal stripping \citep{solanes2001}, and void galaxies, which evolve in
isolation, are expected to show a lower knee mass and a different faint-end slope
\citep{moorman2014,jones2018}. \citet{moorman2014} measured a void knee
$\sim$0.14~dex below the wall knee in ALFALFA. The FAST All Sky HI Survey
\citep[FASHI;][]{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2} now provides \feRealNDRTwo\ HI sources, but its released
products stop at the global HIMF, and the one environment study on FASHI data used 230 group
galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}.

An environment-split HIMF compares the mass function of galaxies inside a set of volumes (voids,
group regions) with that of the remaining galaxies. Any volume selection in a flux-limited sample
also selects in distance and in local structure, and the $1/V_{\rm max}$ estimator assumes that
each galaxy is uniformly distributed within its accessible volume, which an environment
selection violates by construction. The offset measured between two such sets therefore mixes
any physical difference with the response of the estimator to the selection. Here we measure the
environment-split HIMF of FASHI DR2 and test the offsets against the same splits made with
randomly placed volumes. An earlier version of this analysis, on DR1 with a single flux-limit
weighting, reported a void offset of $\feRealDROneMaxVoidKneeOffset$~dex as an independent confirmation of the ALFALFA
result; the tests below show that the offset is not attributable to the void environment.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
DR2 is not in VizieR; we take Table~2 of \citet{fashi_dr2} from the survey's public CSTCloud share,
linked from the FASHI release page. For each source we use the position, optical redshift,
integrated flux, distance, precomputed $\log M_{\rm HI}$, completeness $C$ and $V_{\rm max}$, the
latter computed by the survey over 19{,}482~deg$^2$. We drop the \feRealNZNonpos\ sources with
$z\leq0$ and \feRealNDropped\ with non-finite mass or distance or non-positive flux, and keep the
\feN\ sources with $C\geq\feRealCMin$, the sample defined by the survey's own HIMF (theirs, which
also cuts on $W_{20}$ and a noise statistic not in the public table, holds just over 109{,}000).
Each galaxy contributes $1/(C_i V_i)$ to its mass bin; as a bookkeeping check this reproduces the
published global HIMF ($\log M^*=\feRealGlobalLogMStar\pm\feRealGlobalLogMStarErr$,
$\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha\pm\feRealGlobalAlphaErr$, against $9.89\pm0.02$ and $-1.31\pm0.02$ in
\citealt{fashi_dr2}). We fit a Schechter function \citep{schechter1976} in log space to each
$1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMF \citep{schmidt1968}. The fits are formally poor (reduced $\chi^2$ of
\feRealVoidRedChi, \feRealWallRedChi\ and \feRealGlobalRedChi\ for the void, wall and global HIMFs);
the fitter scales the parameter errors by the reduced $\chi^2$, and the knee positions depend on
the Schechter form.

Voids are the Douglass+2023 VoidFinder voids in their Planck2018 cosmology, each the union of its
holes; a galaxy inside any hole is a void member (\feRealNInVoid\ galaxies). Wall galaxies are
the rest of the classifiable volume, approximated by a padded bounding box around the voids; it
includes sky where no voids were searched and holds \feRealWallPct\% of the sample. Groups are
the Tempel+2017 SDSS groups; a galaxy within the projected $R_{200}$ and 1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of a
group is a member (\feRealNGroupMembers\ in the cap), with the catalogue's CMB-frame redshifts
converted to the heliocentric frame of FASHI's $v_{\rm opt}$ and distances on the catalogue's
$H_0=67.8$ scale.

\section{Environment Splits}
\label{sec:splits}
The void knee is offset from the wall knee by
$\Delta\log M^*=\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex (void
$\log M^*=\feRealVoidLogMStar$, wall $\feRealWallLogMStar$; Figure~\ref{fig:himf}). A delete-one-void
jackknife over the \feRealJkNOcc\ occupied voids gives \feRealJkErr~dex. The value is stable
against the void definition (\feRealMaxSphereVoidKneeOffset~dex using only each void's maximal
sphere) and against the mass range fitted: the void fit uses \feRealVoidNBins\ bins and the wall
fit \feRealWallNBins, and fitting both over their \feRealCommonNBins\ common bins gives
$\feRealCommonKneeOffset\pm\feRealCommonKneeErr$~dex. It is not stable against the weighting: a
single flux limit of 0.30~Jy\,km\,s$^{-1}$, on the same sample, gives
$\feRealOptASameVoidKneeOffset$~dex, a change of $\feRealWeightEffect\pm\feRealWeightEffectErr$~dex
(paired jackknife). On DR1 with that weighting the offset is $\feRealDROneMaxVoidKneeOffset$~dex with
maximal spheres, the value our earlier analysis reported, and $\feRealDROneVoidKneeOffset$~dex with
full voids. Group members lie $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex above the field.

\section{Random-Placement Nulls}
\label{sec:nulls}
Each void is moved rigidly, keeping its distance, holes, size and a random orientation, to a random
sky position inside the SDSS footprint (occupied by Tempel galaxies), and the split is recomputed
with the same weights. In the constrained version every hole must lie inside the footprint and the
classifiable box. We also attempted placements in which voids may not overlap one another, as real
VoidFinder voids do not, but in a one-off test 687 of the 1163 voids could not be placed (recorded in the repository findings, not in the pipeline's results file): the voids fill too much of
the volume to be re-scattered disjointly, so overlap is allowed and measured. Groups are moved to
random footprint positions keeping their velocities and $R_{200}$.

\subsection{Groups}
Randomly placed groups give $+\feRealGroupNullMean\pm\feRealGroupNullStd$~dex over
\feRealGroupNullReps\ placements, and \feRealGroupNullNGe\ reach the measured offset
(Figure~\ref{fig:nulls}). The group offset is consistent with the random-placement distribution.

\subsection{Voids}
Over \feRealNullCReps\ constrained placements the offset is
$\feRealNullCMean\pm\feRealNullCStd$~dex, and none is as negative as the measured value
(\feRealNullMean~dex for placements constrained only at the void centre). The random voids are not
signal-free and not density-matched. Their void bins hold a median of \feRealNullCNInVoid\ galaxies
against \feRealNInVoid\ for the real voids, because random placements fall on average-density
structure, and a median \feRealNullCOverlap\ of their members are real-void members. The null mean
depends on this overlap: regressing the placement offsets on it gives a slope of
$\feRealNullCSlope$~dex per unit overlap and $\feRealNullCIntercept$~dex at zero overlap, more
negative than the measurement. A delete-one-void jackknife on ten placements gives a median
\feRealNullCJkErr~dex, the same as for the real voids, so the placements are no less noisy than the
measurement. Under the single flux-limit weighting the null mean is $\feRealNullCOptAMean$~dex and
the residual $\feRealOptANullExcess$~dex, against $\feRealNullCExcess$~dex under the completeness
weighting. An Einstein--de~Sitter distance relation, which changes void membership for
\feRealEdsChangedLowZPct\% of void galaxies at $z<0.02$ and scrambles it at $z>0.06$
(\feRealEdsChangedHighZPct\%), gives $\feRealEdsVoidKneeOffset$~dex, at the
\feRealEdsPercentile\ percentile of the null.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The measured void offset is more negative than any random placement, so the real voids are not
exchangeable with randomly placed volumes. That is expected whether or not HI knees differ
physically in voids: the real voids are the emptiest regions of the survey, and the placements
show that the offset depends on where the volumes lie, by $\sim$0.1~dex across the range of
overlap and by $\sim$0.1~dex between two defensible weightings. We therefore cannot attribute any
part of the offset to the void environment, and we do not claim an environmental dependence of the
HIMF. The group offset is reproduced by random placement.

The general result is that, in a FASHI-like survey geometry, splitting a flux-limited sample by
volume moves the fitted $1/V_{\rm max}$ knee by 0.1--0.2~dex with no environmental signal: randomly
placed void shapes give $\feRealNullCMean$~dex and randomly placed group regions
$+\feRealGroupNullMean$~dex. These offsets are as large as published void effects, including the
ALFALFA void suppression \citep[$-0.14$~dex;][]{moorman2014}, which was measured with a different
survey and estimator and which we have not tested. We recommend that environment-split HIMFs be
accompanied by random-placement nulls of the kind used here, reported with the null's dependence on
placement and on the weighting. The volume selection correlates with distance, and with fits of
reduced $\chi^2\sim20$--40 the fitted knee depends on the mass range each distance range
contributes; a density-insensitive estimator, a true footprint mask, and density-matched nulls are
the next steps. Two systematics that follow real density, and so are not removed by random
placement, remain untested: blending of neighbouring galaxies in FAST's $\sim$3~arcmin beam, and a
possible mismatch between the velocity frames of FASHI's $v_{\rm opt}$ and the void catalogue's
redshifts.
