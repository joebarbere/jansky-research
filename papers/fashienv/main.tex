\begin{abstract}
The FAST All Sky HI Survey (FASHI) publishes a global Schechter HI mass function (HIMF) for its
DR2 catalogue \citep{fashi_dr2}, but no environment split; the one environment study on FASHI data
used 230 group galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}. We split the DR2 HIMF by environment. Of the
\feRealNDRTwo\ DR2 sources we use the \feN\ above the survey's 50\% completeness limit
($C\geq\feRealCMin$), weight each by $1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$ from the catalogue's own completeness and
accessible volume, and recover the published global HIMF
($\log M^*/M_\odot=\feRealGlobalLogMStar\pm\feRealGlobalLogMStarErr$,
$\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha\pm\feRealGlobalAlphaErr$). Cross-matched against the Douglass+2023 SDSS
voids \citep{douglass2023} and Tempel+2017 groups \citep{tempel2017}, the void HIMF knee lies below
the wall knee by $\Delta\log M^*=\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex
(\feRealVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$), close to the ALFALFA void
suppression \citep[$-0.14$~dex;][]{moorman2014}. A delete-one-void jackknife gives
\feRealVoidJkErr~dex, below the fit error, but the offset depends on the distance--redshift
relation used to place galaxies relative to the voids: with an Einstein--de~Sitter relation,
which changes distances by a few per cent at the catalogue's depth, it falls to
$\feRealEdsVoidKneeOffset$~dex (\feRealEdsVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$). The single flux-limit weighting of our earlier DR1 analysis
gives $\feRealOptAVoidKneeOffset$~dex on the same data, so the weighting accounts for
\feRealWeightingShiftPct\% of the earlier offset. Two biases remain, both in the direction of the signal (the
uniform-density assumption of $1/V_{\rm max}$ and a comparison bin that holds \feRealWallPct\% of
the sample). We therefore report the void offset as a tentative upper bound, not a detection. Group members have a knee
$+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex above the field (\feRealGroupKneeSigma$\sigma$),
which we attribute to survivor bias in a flux-limited sample.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The shape of the HI mass function (how many galaxies of each atomic-gas mass exist per unit
volume) encodes the balance of gas accretion, consumption, and removal, set partly by
environment. Cluster and group galaxies lose HI to ram-pressure and tidal stripping
\citep{solanes2001}; void galaxies, evolving in isolation, are expected to have a HIMF with a
suppressed knee and a steeper faint end \citep{moorman2014,jones2018}. FASHI, the FAST all-sky
blind HI survey \citep{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2}, is now the largest such catalogue, but its released
products stop at the global HIMF; the one environment analysis on FASHI data \citep{fashi_groups}
used 230 group galaxies. The DR2 catalogue contains \feRealNDRTwo\ HI sources, nearly three orders
of magnitude more, and its environment-split HIMF has not been measured. An earlier version of this
analysis used the DR1 catalogue with a single flux-limit weighting; we report here the DR2
measurement with the survey's own completeness correction, and show in
Section~\ref{sec:weighting} how the two weightings differ. FASHI carries HI masses and distances but no stellar
masses or optical diameters, so the classical Haynes--Giovanelli HI deficiency (which needs
optical morphology) and gas-fraction-at-fixed-$M_*$ statistics are out of reach; the HIMF itself,
split by environment, is the measurement these data support on their own.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
\label{sec:method}
DR2 is not in VizieR; we take Table~2 of \citet{fashi_dr2} from the survey's public CSTCloud
share, linked from the FASHI release page. For each source we use the position, optical
redshift, integrated flux, distance, precomputed $\log M_{\rm HI}$, completeness $C$ and
$V_{\rm max}$ (the latter computed by the survey over 19{,}482~deg$^2$). We drop the
\feRealNZNonpos\ sources with $z\leq0$, which have no comoving position, and keep the
\feN\ sources with $C\geq\feRealCMin$, the sample defined by the survey's own HIMF (theirs, which also
cuts on $W_{20}$ and a noise statistic not in the public table, holds just over 109{,}000). The DR2
distance convention differs from DR1's: of the DR1 sources, \feRealMatchedPct\% have a DR2
counterpart within 1.5~arcmin and 100~km\,s$^{-1}$, and their DR2 distances are larger by a median
factor \feRealDistRatio, so absolute masses from the two releases are not interchangeable. We classify environment two ways.

Voids: we convert positions to the comoving Cartesian frame of the Douglass+2023 VoidFinder
maximal spheres \citep[working in the survey's $h^{-1}$Mpc units, which are $H_0$-independent for
comoving distance;][]{douglass2023}. A galaxy is a void member if it lies inside any sphere, and a
wall galaxy if it lies inside the void-survey volume but outside every sphere; galaxies beyond
that volume (most of FASHI's southern and high-redshift sky) are unclassifiable and excluded,
leaving \feRealNInVoid\ void and the surrounding wall galaxies in the SDSS cap. The classifiable
volume is approximated by a padded bounding box around the void spheres. The SDSS cap is a wedge
and its bounding box is several times its volume, so the contamination is large: the wall bin holds
\feRealNWall\ galaxies, \feRealWallPct\% of the sample, and its knee \feRealWallLogMStar\ sits
\feRealWallMinusGlobal\,dex from the all-sky fit \feRealGlobalLogMStar. The comparison is therefore
closer to void versus everything than to void versus wall. The contamination dilutes the wall bin
toward the global population, so, if anything, it weakens the reported contrast; a genuine
footprint mask, rather than a bounding box, is required to remove it and is not yet available.

Groups: a galaxy within the projected $R_{200}$ and 1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of any Tempel+2017 group is
a member (\feRealNGroupMembers\ in the cap), the rest field.

The $1/V_{\rm max}$ method assumes each
source is uniformly distributed within its accessible volume, which is false for a sample
selected by its density environment. For the void bin the accessible volume is not the survey
cone but the union of void spheres inside it, and that fraction falls to zero at the near and far
edges of the box while staying finite for the wall bin. Because $V_{\rm max}$ is mass-dependent
(brighter galaxies reach further, sampling more void-free volume), the distortion is
mass-dependent too, and it suppresses $\phi$ at the high-mass end of the void bin, in the same
direction as the reported signal. A density-insensitive estimator (SWML) is the standard answer
and is not attempted here; until it is, the offset should be read as an upper bound on any true
suppression rather than a measurement of it.

For each environment we build the HIMF by the $1/V_{\rm max}$ method \citep{schmidt1968}: each
source contributes $1/(C_i V_i)$ to its mass bin, with $V_i$ and $C_i$ the catalogue's accessible
volume and completeness, and we fit a Schechter function \citep{schechter1976} in log space. The pipeline is exercised on a flux-limited mock with two
injected Schechter HIMFs (void: lower $M^*$, steeper $\alpha$; wall: the reference), which it
fits back to the injected knees and slopes and to the correct knee-offset sign
(\code{tests/test\_fashienv.py}).

\subsection{Effect of the weighting}
\label{sec:weighting}
Our DR1 analysis weighted each source by the volume out to a single flux limit of
0.30~Jy\,km\,s$^{-1}$. That estimator gives faint-end slopes near $\alpha\approx\feRealOptAGlobalAlpha$
(DR2) and $\feRealDROneGlobalAlpha$ (DR1), much steeper than the published FASHI HIMF, whereas the
$1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$ weighting reproduces it ($\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha$ against $-1.31\pm0.02$ in
\citealt{fashi_dr2}). The weighting also moves the environment offsets. With the single-limit
weighting the void offset is $\feRealDROneVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealDROneVoidKneeErr$~dex on DR1
(\feRealDROneN\ sources) and $\feRealOptAVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealOptAVoidKneeErr$~dex on DR2, and the
group offset $+\feRealDROneGroupKneeOffset$ and $+\feRealOptAGroupKneeOffset$~dex. The DR1 and DR2
values agree, so the larger catalogue does not by itself change the answer; the weighting does.
We adopt the completeness-corrected values.

\subsection{Voids}
The void HIMF knee sits below the wall value by
$\Delta\log M^* = \feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex, a
\feRealVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$ suppression (void
$\log M^* = \feRealVoidLogMStar\pm\feRealVoidLogMStarErr$, wall
$\feRealWallLogMStar\pm\feRealWallLogMStarErr$).

Those are Poisson counting errors within one realisation of large-scale structure, which cannot
capture how much voids differ from one another, the variance that usually dominates a void
statistic. Deleting each of the \feRealVoidNOcc\ occupied voids in turn and refitting gives a
jackknife error of \feRealVoidJkErr\,dex, smaller than the fit error, so the offset strengthens
to \feRealVoidJkSigma$\sigma$ on that axis rather than weakening. Sample variance across voids is therefore not what limits this measurement; the limit
is set by bias rather than variance, which resampling cannot bound. The $1/V_{\rm max}$ density assumption
(Section~\ref{sec:data}) and the bounding-box comparison bin (Section~\ref{sec:data}) both act in
the direction of the reported signal. The offset agrees in sign and size with the ALFALFA void-HIMF
knee suppression \citep[$-0.14$~dex, $\sim$2$\sigma$;][]{moorman2014}.
The HI measurement itself is independent of ALFALFA's (FAST rather than Arecibo), but the
environment classification for both comes from the same SDSS DR7 parent sample and largely the
same voids, so the comparison is a deeper HI sample over much the same volume rather than an
independent realisation of large-scale structure. We note also that the void
membership depends on the adopted distance--redshift relation (we use the Douglass catalogue's
own Planck2018 cosmology so the frames match). With an Einstein--de~Sitter relation instead the
offset is $\feRealEdsVoidKneeOffset$~dex (\feRealEdsVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$). The matched cosmology
is the correct choice, since the void catalogue was built with it, but a change of a few per cent
in the distances is enough to remove the signal. The offset therefore rests on galaxies close to
void boundaries, where membership is decided by small distance differences, and it is fragile in
a way that neither the fit error nor the jackknife measures. The void
faint-end slope ($\feRealVoidAlpha$) is marginally flatter than the wall's ($\feRealWallAlpha$),
and the two are within their mutual uncertainties, so we do not claim a slope difference.

\subsection{Groups}
Group members show a knee above the field by $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex
(\feRealGroupKneeSigma$\sigma$; group $\log M^* = \feRealGroupLogMStar$, field
$\feRealFieldLogMStar$), statistically the stronger of our two offsets, though not our main
result, because it is survivor-biased. Taken at face value a positive
offset is the opposite of gas stripping; it is instead the expected signature of a selection
effect, not a physical enhancement: a flux-limited HI survey detects the group galaxies that have
retained substantial HI, i.e.\ the more massive, gas-rich survivors (the group knee,
\feRealGroupLogMStar, even exceeds the global \feRealGlobalLogMStar\ for this reason), while
stripped members drop out of the sample entirely. The group knee is therefore an upper envelope
on the surviving HI-rich population, and the true environmental effect (including the
undetected, stripped galaxies) is more negative than $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset$~dex. The
void--wall comparison does not suffer this bias, since voids do not preferentially strip HI,
which is why we take it as the main result despite its lower raw significance.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
Splitting the FASHI DR2 HIMF by environment with the survey's own completeness correction, we find
the void knee $\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex below the wall knee
(\feRealVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$), in agreement with the ALFALFA void suppression
\citep{moorman2014}, but reduced to \feRealEdsVoidKneeSigma$\sigma$ by a few-per-cent change in
the distance--redshift relation; and a group-member knee offset that is biased upward by survivor
selection.
The same catalogue weighted by a single flux limit gives a void offset of
$\feRealOptAVoidKneeOffset$~dex, as did our DR1 analysis ($\feRealDROneVoidKneeOffset$~dex); the
difference is due to the weighting. The void offset is at most a tentative upper bound on
any true suppression while the $1/V_{\rm max}$ density assumption, the bounding-box comparison bin
and the distance sensitivity stand; a density-insensitive estimator, a true footprint mask, and a
void-membership test that marginalises over distance errors are needed before it can be called a
detection.
