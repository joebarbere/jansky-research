\title{Environment-Split HI Mass Functions in FASHI DR2 and Random-Placement Null Tests}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We split the HI mass function (HIMF) of the FAST All Sky HI Survey DR2 \citep{fashi_dr2} by
environment, using the \feN\ sources above the survey's 50\% completeness limit weighted by
$1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$ from the catalogue's own completeness and accessible volume. Against the
Douglass+2023 VoidFinder voids \citep{douglass2023} the void$-$wall knee offset is
$\Delta\log M^*=\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex, and
the Tempel+2017 group-member knee \citep{tempel2017} is offset from the field by
$+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex. We test both offsets against the same splits made with volumes that keep the environment
catalogues' distances, sizes and velocities but sit at random sky positions in the SDSS footprint.
Such voids give $\feRealNullCMean$~dex and such groups $+\feRealGroupNullMean$~dex (standard
deviations \feRealNullCStd\ and \feRealGroupNullStd), so volume splits with this radial selection
move the fitted knee by amounts comparable to the published ALFALFA void effect, which used a
different, density-insensitive estimator. The group offset is reached by \feRealGroupNullNGe\ of
\feRealGroupNullReps\ random placements, a weak test, because the random group regions hold about a
tenth as many galaxies as the real groups. The void offset lies beyond all \feRealNullCReps\ void
placements under both weightings tried, but the random voids hold
\feRealNullCNInVoidMin--\feRealNullCNInVoidMax\ galaxies against \feRealNInVoid\ in the real
voids, so the null is not density-matched, and under the weighting that reproduces the published
HIMF the residual is $\feRealNullCExcess$~dex, \feRealNullCExcessSigmaFit--\feRealNullCExcessSigmaJk$\sigma$
against the real void bin's own noise. We cannot attribute either offset to environment, and we
recommend that environment-split $1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMFs be tested against random-placement nulls
that keep the environment catalogue's radial selection.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The HI mass function is expected to depend on environment. Group and cluster galaxies lose gas
to ram-pressure and tidal stripping \citep{solanes2001}, and void galaxies, which evolve in
isolation, are expected to show a lower knee mass and a different faint-end slope
\citep{moorman2014,jones2018}. With a two-dimensional stepwise maximum-likelihood estimator, which does not assume a uniform
density, \citet{moorman2014} measured a void knee
$\sim$0.14~dex below the wall knee in ALFALFA. The FAST All Sky HI Survey
\citep[FASHI;][]{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2} now provides \feRealNDRTwo\ HI sources, but its released
products stop at the global HIMF, and the one environment study on FASHI data used 230 group
galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}.

An environment-split HIMF compares the mass function of galaxies inside a set of volumes (voids,
group regions) with that of the remaining galaxies. Any volume selection in a flux-limited sample
also selects in distance and in local structure, and the $1/V_{\rm max}$ estimator assumes that
each galaxy is uniformly distributed within its accessible volume, which an environment
selection violates by construction. The offset measured between two such sets therefore mixes
any physical difference with the response of the estimator to the selection. Here we measure the
environment-split HIMF of FASHI DR2 and test the offsets against the same splits made with
randomly placed volumes. An earlier version of this analysis, on DR1 with a single flux-limit
weighting, reported a void offset of $\feRealDROneMaxVoidKneeOffset$~dex as an independent
confirmation of the ALFALFA result. That value depends on the void definition
(Section~\ref{sec:splits}) and was never tested against a null; the DR2 tests below cannot
attribute the DR2 offset to the void environment.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
DR2 is not in VizieR; we take Table~2 of \citet{fashi_dr2} from the survey's public CSTCloud share,
linked from the FASHI release page. For each source we use the position, optical redshift,
integrated flux, distance, precomputed $\log M_{\rm HI}$, completeness $C$ and $V_{\rm max}$, the
latter computed by the survey over 19{,}482~deg$^2$. We drop the \feRealNZNonpos\ sources with
$z\leq0$ and \feRealNDropped\ with non-finite mass or distance or non-positive flux, and keep the
\feN\ sources with $C\geq\feRealCMin$, the sample defined by the survey's own HIMF (theirs, which
also cuts on $W_{20}$ and a noise statistic not in the public table, holds just over 109{,}000).
Each galaxy contributes $1/(C_i V_i)$ to its mass bin; as a bookkeeping check this reproduces the
published global HIMF ($\log M^*=\feRealGlobalLogMStar\pm\feRealGlobalLogMStarErr$,
$\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha\pm\feRealGlobalAlphaErr$, against $9.89\pm0.02$ and $-1.31\pm0.02$ in
\citealt{fashi_dr2}). We fit a Schechter function \citep{schechter1976} in log space to each
$1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMF \citep{schmidt1968}. The fits are formally poor (reduced $\chi^2$ of
\feRealVoidRedChi, \feRealWallRedChi\ and \feRealGlobalRedChi\ for the void, wall and global HIMFs);
the fitter scales the parameter errors by the reduced $\chi^2$, and the knee positions depend on
the Schechter form.

Voids are the Douglass+2023 VoidFinder voids in their Planck2018 cosmology, each the union of its
holes; a galaxy inside any hole is a void member (\feRealNInVoid\ galaxies). Wall galaxies are
the rest of the classifiable volume, approximated by a padded bounding box around the voids; it
includes sky where no voids were searched and holds \feRealWallPct\% of the sample. Groups are
the Tempel+2017 SDSS groups; a galaxy within the projected $R_{200}$ and 1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of a
group is a member (\feRealNGroupMembers\ in the cap), with the catalogue's CMB-frame redshifts
converted to the heliocentric frame of FASHI's $v_{\rm opt}$ and distances on the catalogue's
$H_0=67.8$ scale.

\section{Environment Splits}
\label{sec:splits}
The void knee is offset from the wall knee by
$\Delta\log M^*=\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex (void
$\log M^*=\feRealVoidLogMStar$, wall $\feRealWallLogMStar$; Figure~\ref{fig:himf}). A delete-one-void
jackknife over the \feRealJkNOcc\ occupied voids gives \feRealJkErr~dex. The value is stable
against the void definition (\feRealMaxSphereVoidKneeOffset~dex using only each void's maximal
sphere) and against the mass range fitted: the void fit uses \feRealVoidNBins\ bins and the wall
fit \feRealWallNBins, and fitting both over their \feRealCommonNBins\ common bins gives
$\feRealCommonKneeOffset\pm\feRealCommonKneeErr$~dex. It is not stable against the weighting: a
single flux limit of 0.30~Jy\,km\,s$^{-1}$, on the same sample, gives
$\feRealOptASameVoidKneeOffset$~dex, a change of $\feRealWeightEffect\pm\feRealWeightEffectErr$~dex
(paired jackknife). On DR1 with that weighting the offset is $\feRealDROneMaxVoidKneeOffset$~dex with
maximal spheres, the value our earlier analysis reported, and $\feRealDROneVoidKneeOffset$~dex with
full voids. Group members lie $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex above the field.

\section{Random-Placement Nulls}
\label{sec:nulls}
Each void is moved rigidly, keeping its distance, holes, size and a random orientation, to a random
sky position inside the SDSS footprint (sky occupied by Tempel galaxies), and the split is
recomputed with the same weights. In the constrained version every hole must lie inside the
footprint and the classifiable box. The placed voids may overlap one another, which real VoidFinder
voids do not; a greedy sequential placement that forbade overlap (500 attempts per void, voids taken
in catalogue order) left \feRealNoOverlapUnplaced\ of \feRealNoOverlapNVoids\ voids unplaced, a count
that depends on the order and the attempt budget. Groups are moved to random footprint positions
keeping their velocities and $R_{200}$, so their redshift distribution is preserved. Because every
placement uses the same galaxy sample, the null distributions below are conditional on that sample.

groups (right), against the measured offsets (vertical lines).}

\subsection{Groups}
Randomly placed groups give $+\feRealGroupNullMean\pm\feRealGroupNullStd$~dex (mean and standard
deviation) over \feRealGroupNullReps\ placements, and \feRealGroupNullNGe\ reach the measured offset
(Figure~\ref{fig:nulls}). This null is poorly matched. The placed group regions hold a median of
\feRealGroupNullNInGroup\ galaxies at a median redshift of \feRealGroupNullMedZ, against
\feRealGroupNInGroupReal\ at \feRealGroupMedZReal\ for the real groups, because the real groups sit on
overdensities, and a median \feRealGroupNullOverlap\ of their members are real group members. With
bins a tenth the size of the real group bin, the null spread is wide, so the group offset being
inside it neither confirms nor excludes a group effect.

\subsection{Voids}
Over \feRealNullCReps\ constrained placements the offset is $\feRealNullCMean\pm\feRealNullCStd$~dex,
and none is as negative as the measured value; the same holds under the single flux-limit weighting,
whose placements reach at most $\feRealNullCOptAMin$~dex against a measured
$\feRealOptASameVoidKneeOffset$~dex. Placements constrained only at the void centre give
$\feRealNullMean$~dex. The null is not density-matched. Its void bins hold
\feRealNullCNInVoidMin--\feRealNullCNInVoidMax\ galaxies, against \feRealNInVoid\ in the real voids,
because randomly placed volumes sample average-density structure (a median \feRealNullCOverlap\ of
their members are real-void members). Across the placements the offset tracks the occupancy and the
median redshift of the placed volume ($R^2=\feRealNullCRtwoOccZ$); the overlap with the real voids
adds nothing once those are included ($R^2=\feRealNullCRtwoAll$), and on its own it moves the fitted
offset by only \feRealNullCOverlapChange~dex across the sampled range
(\feRealNullCOverlapLo--\feRealNullCOverlapHi). The real voids lie outside the sampled occupancy
range, so the null cannot say what a density-matched split without an environmental effect would
give. A delete-one-void jackknife on ten placements gives a median \feRealNullCJkErr~dex, the same as
for the real voids.

Under the completeness weighting, which reproduces the published HIMF, the offset exceeds the null
mean by $\feRealNullCExcess$~dex, \feRealNullCExcessSigmaFit$\sigma$ against the real void bin's fit
error and \feRealNullCExcessSigmaJk$\sigma$ against its jackknife error. Under the single flux limit the
excess is $\feRealOptANullExcess$~dex, but that weighting fails the bookkeeping check
($\alpha=\feRealOptASameGlobalAlpha$ for the global HIMF, reduced $\chi^2$ \feRealOptASameGlobalRedChi).
An Einstein--de~Sitter distance relation changes void membership for \feRealEdsChangedLowZPct\% of
void galaxies at $z<0.02$, and at $z=0.06$--0.09 the membership changes (in either direction)
outnumber the void members (\feRealEdsChangedHighZPct\%); it gives $\feRealEdsVoidKneeOffset$~dex, at
the \feRealEdsPercentile\ percentile of the null.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The measured void offset lies beyond every random placement, so the real voids are not
exchangeable with randomly placed volumes; but they are also emptier than any placement, and the
null does not say what an emptier volume would give without an environmental effect. Under the
weighting that reproduces the published HIMF the residual is \feRealNullCExcessSigmaFit--\feRealNullCExcessSigmaJk$\sigma$,
and the group null, whose regions hold a tenth as many galaxies as the real groups, is too weak to
confirm or exclude a group effect. We therefore cannot attribute either offset to environment.

The portable result concerns the null itself. Volumes that keep the environment catalogue's
distances and sizes but sit at random sky positions, which sample average-density structure,
give knee offsets of $\feRealNullCMean$~dex (void shapes) and $+\feRealGroupNullMean$~dex (group
regions) under the $1/V_{\rm max}$ estimator in this geometry. These offsets combine the
estimator's response to the radial selection with real structure at those distances, and they are
comparable to the ALFALFA void suppression \citep[$-0.14$~dex;][]{moorman2014}. That measurement used
a density-insensitive estimator, so our result does not apply to it; it applies to environment splits
made with $1/V_{\rm max}$. We recommend that such splits be tested against random-placement nulls that
keep the environment catalogue's radial selection, reported with the null's occupancy relative to
the real environment. A density-insensitive estimator, a true footprint mask and density-matched
nulls are the next steps. Two systematics that follow real density, and so are not removed by random
placement, remain untested: blending of neighbouring galaxies in FAST's $\sim$3~arcmin beam, and a
possible mismatch between the velocity frames of FASHI's $v_{\rm opt}$ and the void catalogue's
redshifts.
