\title{Environment-Split HI Mass Functions in FASHI DR2 and Random-Placement Null Tests}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We split the HI mass function of FASHI DR2 by environment and test the knee offsets against the
same splits made with voids and groups placed at random. With the survey-wide $1/(C\,V_{\rm max})$
weighting the void$-$wall and group$-$field offsets are $\feRealVoidKneeOffset$ and
$+\feRealGroupKneeOffset$~dex, but random placements alone give $\feRealNullCMean$ and
$+\feRealGroupNullMean$~dex. Restricting each galaxy's $V_{\rm max}$ to its own environment's
volume removes the group null offset and halves the void one. The void offset then exceeds its null
by $\feRealEnvNullExcess$~dex ($\feRealEnvNullExcessSigma\sigma$), and the group offset is reached
by \feRealEnvGroupNullNGe\ of \feRealEnvGroupNullReps\ placements. Neither null is density-matched,
so we attribute neither offset to environment. We recommend environment-restricted $V_{\rm max}$
together with random-placement nulls, since the first alone does not remove the void bias.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Void galaxies are expected to have a lower HI mass function (HIMF) knee than wall galaxies, and
group galaxies to lose gas to stripping \citep{solanes2001,jones2018}. With a two-dimensional
stepwise maximum-likelihood estimator, which does not assume a uniform density, \citet{moorman2014}
found the ALFALFA void knee $\sim$0.14~dex below the wall knee. The FAST All Sky HI Survey
\citep[FASHI;][]{fashi_dr1,fashi_dr2} now provides \feRealNDRTwo\ sources, and its catalogue
supplies a completeness $C$ and accessible volume $V_{\rm max}$ for each; the one environment
study on FASHI data used 230 group galaxies \citep{fashi_groups}. Weighting environment
subsamples by that survey-wide volume assumes that each galaxy could have sat anywhere in the
survey, which an environment member cannot. We measure the environment split under both the
survey-wide and an environment-restricted $V_{\rm max}$ and test each against random placements.
An earlier version of this analysis, on DR1 with a single flux-limit weighting, reported a void
offset of $\feRealDROneMaxVoidKneeOffset$~dex without any null test.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:data}
We take Table~2 of \citet{fashi_dr2} from the survey's public share and keep the \feN\ sources with
$C\geq\feRealCMin$, the sample of the survey's own HIMF. Weights $1/(C_i V_i)$ reproduce the
published global HIMF ($\log M^*=\feRealGlobalLogMStar$, $\alpha=\feRealGlobalAlpha$, against
$9.89$ and $-1.31$). A single flux-limit weighting on the same sample fails that check
($\alpha=\feRealOptASameGlobalAlpha$) and moves the void offset by
$\feRealWeightEffect\pm\feRealWeightEffectErr$~dex, so we do not use it. Each HIMF is fitted with a
Schechter function; the fits are formally poor (reduced $\chi^2$ up to \feRealGlobalRedChi) and the
fitter scales the errors accordingly.

Void members lie inside any hole of a Douglass+2023 VoidFinder void \citep{douglass2023}; wall
galaxies are the rest of a padded bounding box around the voids (\feRealWallPct\% of the sample).
Group members lie within the projected $R_{200}$ and 1000~km\,s$^{-1}$ of a Tempel+2017 group
\citep{tempel2017}, in the heliocentric frame. For the environment-restricted $V_{\rm max}$ we
draw $2\times10^6$ randoms uniform in volume over the DR2 footprint, classify them with the same
rules, and weight each galaxy by $C_i V_i\,g_E(d_{\max,i})$, where $g_E(D)$ is the fraction of the
volume within $D$ that environment $E$ occupies and $d_{\max}$ follows from $V_i=(\Omega/3)\,d_{\max}^3$ over the survey area. Groups occupy \feRealEnvGroupShellHi\% of the classifiable volume within 50~Mpc but
\feRealEnvGroupShellLo\% beyond 200~Mpc, because the group catalogue thins with distance; the void
fraction varies between \feRealEnvVoidShellLo\% and \feRealEnvVoidShellHi\% without a trend.

In the null each void is moved rigidly, with a random orientation, to a random position where all
its holes lie inside the SDSS footprint and the box; placed voids may overlap. Groups move to random
footprint positions with their velocities and $R_{200}$. Both nulls reuse the one galaxy sample, so
their spread omits sample noise.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
With the survey-wide weighting the void$-$wall offset is
$\feRealVoidKneeOffset\pm\feRealVoidKneeErr$~dex (void-jackknife error \feRealJkErr) and the
group$-$field offset $+\feRealGroupKneeOffset\pm\feRealGroupKneeErr$~dex. The random placements
give $\feRealNullCMean\pm\feRealNullCStd$ (\feRealNullCReps\ void placements) and
$+\feRealGroupNullMean\pm\feRealGroupNullStd$ (\feRealGroupNullReps\ groups; mean and standard
deviation; Figure~\ref{fig:nulls}), comparable to the published void effect.

With the environment-restricted weighting the offsets are
$\feRealEnvVoidOffset\pm\feRealEnvVoidErr$ and $+\feRealEnvGroupOffset\pm\feRealEnvGroupErr$~dex,
and the same \feRealEnvNullReps\ placements give $\feRealEnvNullMean\pm\feRealEnvNullStd$ and
$\feRealEnvGroupNullMean\pm\feRealEnvGroupNullStd$~dex. No void placement reaches the measured
offset, but the excess, $\feRealEnvNullExcess$~dex, is \feRealEnvNullExcessSigma\ times the
measured offset's fit error; we compare with that error because the null spread omits sample
noise. Of the \feRealEnvGroupNullReps\ group placements, \feRealEnvGroupNullNGe\ reach the
measured group offset ($p\approx\feRealEnvGroupNullP$), an excess of $+\feRealEnvGroupNullExcess$~dex.

Neither null is density-matched. The random void placements hold
\feRealNullCNInVoidMin--\feRealNullCNInVoidMax\ galaxies against \feRealNInVoid\ in the real voids,
and the random group regions a median \feRealGroupNullNInGroup\ against \feRealGroupNInGroupReal.
Across void placements the offset correlates weakly with occupancy (correlation
$\feRealNullCOccCorr$), emptier placements giving less negative offsets; extrapolated to the real
voids this would enlarge the residual, but the real voids lie outside the sampled range, so we do
not extrapolate.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
Weighting environment subsamples by the survey-wide $V_{\rm max}$ makes volumes of the
environments' shapes, placed at random, shift the fitted $1/V_{\rm max}$ knee by as much as
published environmental effects. Restricting $V_{\rm max}$ to each environment removes that shift
for groups, whose share of the volume falls with distance, but only halves it for voids, so the
restriction is necessary and not sufficient. The remaining void null offset combines the
estimator's response to the placed volumes with real structure at those distances.

The void residual is small against its own error. The group residual is larger, but the placed
group regions hold a tenth as many galaxies as the real groups, and blending of neighbours in
FAST's $\sim$3~arcmin beam, which grows with density and would raise the group knee, is untested,
as is the match between FASHI's $v_{\rm opt}$ frame and the void catalogue's redshifts. We
therefore attribute neither offset to environment. Environment-split $1/V_{\rm max}$ HIMFs should
use environment-restricted volumes, be tested against random-placement nulls reported with their
occupancy, and, for the group signal here, be repeated with density-matched nulls and a
blending test.
