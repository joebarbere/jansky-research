\title{Tracking a Solar Type III Electron Beam to 0.2--0.4 AU with STEREO/WAVES:
A Reproducible Drift-to-Distance Tool}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}

\begin{abstract}
A type III electron beam radiates near the local plasma frequency as it streams out along open field, so
its radio drift traces the beam through the heliosphere. The STEREO/WAVES \citep{bougeret2008} High
Frequency Receiver reaches \swRealFlo~MHz, where the emission comes from genuinely interplanetary,
super-Alfv\'enic plasma, far beyond the $\sim$10~MHz / few-$R_\odot$ corona. We build a small tested
tool that fits this drift in a STEREO/WAVES dynamic spectrum and inverts it, via the Leblanc, Dulk \&
Bougeret \citeyearpar{leblanc1998} heliospheric density model, to the beam's outward radial speed and
heliocentric reach. Applied to a 2013-05-15 burst (\code{\swSource}; \swRealNridge\ channels,
\swRealFlo--\swRealFhi~MHz, occupying \swRealNtimeCols\ one-minute time samples), the fit ---
one point per time sample, jackknifed leave-one-sample-out --- gives a mean radial speed of
$\swRealSpeedC\pm\swRealSpeedCSe$~$c$ under harmonic emission and the unscaled Leblanc profile,
tracking the beam from \swRealRlo\ to \swRealRhi~$R_\odot$ (\swRealRhiAU~AU). Both the mode and
an event-driven density enhancement rescale that picture, and the two are exactly degenerate
($f_p^2\propto n_e$), so we commit the (mode\,$\times$\,density) grid rather than a footnote:
the speed spans \swRealGridSpeedLo--\swRealGridSpeedHi~$c$ and the reach
\swRealGridReachLo--\swRealGridReachHi~$R_\odot$ across it, with the fundamental interpretation
(\swRealSpeedCFund~$c$ to \swRealRhiAUFund~AU) at the low end. The radial range itself is set by
the HFR band edges through the model; the speed is the event-specific quantity. Interplanetary
type III beams decelerate \citep{krupar2015}, so this is a mean over a possibly non-constant
motion. This is a reproducibility/method contribution and a recover-a-known, not a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Type III bursts trace energetic electron beams from the corona out into the solar wind. A coronal
analysis (using, e.g., the Newkirk model) follows the beam only to a few solar radii; a Wind/WAVES
RAD2 analysis, stopping at 1~MHz, reaches the Alfv\'en-surface region ($\sim$10--20~$R_\odot$). The
STEREO/WAVES HFR \citep{bougeret2008} reaches \swRealFlo~MHz, where the plasma is genuinely interplanetary under the average-wind density model,
so the same drift-to-distance method can follow the beam to a substantial fraction of an AU. We ask the
most reproducible version: with the public STEREO/WAVES spectra and a small tested tool, can one track a
type III beam into interplanetary space, with honest fit statistics, deceleration, and systematics?

\section{Methods}
\code{swaves} is a pure-NumPy module that reuses the dynamic-spectrum pipeline of the coronal and
Wind/WAVES slices (background subtraction, burst-window finding, the per-channel peak-time ridge
detector) together with the Leblanc heliospheric model and its inverse;
the only new code is the STEREO/WAVES one-minute HFR ASCII parser. It maps each ridge frequency to a
plasma frequency $f_p=f/\mathrm{harmonic}$, a density, and a heliocentric radius, and fits ONE point
per one-minute time sample (the column median of the mapped radii), with a leave-one-sample-out
jackknife error. The sample, not the channel, is the independent unit: the cadence stacks hundreds
of channels into \swRealNtimeCols\ samples, so a per-point fit is leverage-dominated -- and an
iterated residual clip on points was measured, on this very ridge, to collapse onto the two
crowded near-Sun samples and reject the sparse low-frequency samples that carry the drift. Because
the ridge uses intensity peaks (not onsets) the speed is biased low, and because a single line is
fit the result is a mean radial speed over the observed range, not a speed at any one radius. The
synthetic fixture -- built from the same Leblanc mapping -- runs at the real one-minute cadence as
well as fine sampling, so the time-quantisation bias is measured; it remains a round-trip check
for a constant-speed beam and does not test robustness to deceleration.

\section{Data}
We use STEREO/WAVES one-minute-averaged HFR dynamic spectra (the detected ridge spans \swRealFlo--\swRealFhi~MHz) from NASA SPDF, as daily
ASCII (public, no auth, no CDF). This run uses STEREO-A. STEREO-A's vantage ($\sim$135$^\circ$ ahead of
Earth in May 2013) does not affect the drift inversion, which depends only on heliocentric radius
through the density model, not on viewing angle.

\section{Results}
In the 2013-05-15 spectrum (an active, multi-flare period, AR~11748; the burst-window peak is at
\swRealPeakUTC, committed with the results) the detector returns \swRealNridge\ ridge channels
over \swRealFlo--\swRealFhi~MHz (the committed ridge is \code{results/swaves\_ridge.csv}).
Through the Leblanc model, under harmonic emission at $1\times$ density, the emission runs from
\swRealRlo\ to \swRealRhi~$R_\odot$ (\swRealRhiAU~AU; Figure~\ref{fig:swaves}); the outer value
is the band edge through the model, carried by the lowest-frequency channels. The one-point-per-
sample fit gives $\swRealSpeedC\pm\swRealSpeedCSe$~$c$ ($R^2=\swRealRsq$ over the
\swRealNtimeCols\ samples; the estimator bracket -- all-points fit \swRealSpeedCPoints, inverse
regression \swRealSpeedCInverse~$c$ -- is tight, so this track is genuinely straight at sample
level). Interplanetary type III beams decelerate (\citealt{krupar2015} measure
$\sim$$-12$~km\,s$^{-2}$ over 0.1--1~MHz), so the value is a mean; it sits at the top of
\citet{krupar2015}'s interplanetary median range (0.09--0.16~$c$ harmonic), as expected when the
fit also covers the faster coronal segment. A caveat the storm period demands: the window holds
one dominant drift track by inspection of the committed ridge (peak times are monotonic in
frequency for the sparse low-frequency samples), but a competing burst contaminating those few
samples would move the slope, and the jackknife -- which deletes each sample in turn -- is the
committed bound on that sensitivity.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is method and reproducibility: a tested tool that tracks a type III beam deep
into the heliosphere from a public STEREO/WAVES spectrum, with the fit statistics and systematics
propagated. The honest limitations: (i) the recovered speed is a mean over a decelerating track,
not a point measurement; (ii) the emission mode and an event-driven density enhancement are
exactly degenerate and jointly rescale the picture -- the committed grid spans
\swRealGridSpeedLo--\swRealGridSpeedHi~$c$ and \swRealGridReachLo--\swRealGridReachHi~$R_\odot$,
which is why the title carries a range; (iii) the fit rests on \swRealNtimeCols\ one-minute
samples, and its error is the sample-level jackknife, not a per-channel count; (iv) the
field-aligned correction from the Parker spiral is $\sim$1--3\% for the path-weighted track mean
(reaching $\sim$8\% at the outer endpoint alone), below the terms above. These change the quoted
distance by up to a factor of two (item ii) while leaving the qualitative result -- a type III
beam tracked well past the corona at an in-range mean speed -- intact. Every result number above
is written by the pipeline into the macros \code{\textbackslash input}ted by this file (the
committed evidence includes the ridge, the grid, and the burst epoch), and
\code{python -m jansky\_research.swaves -\,-date 20130515 -\,-spacecraft a} regenerates them from the
public SPDF ASCII.
