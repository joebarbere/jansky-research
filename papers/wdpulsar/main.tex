\title{No Persistent Radio Emission from the Pelisoli White-Dwarf-Pulsar Candidates:
A Systematic RACS and VLASS Survey of 56 AR\,Sco Analogues}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The white-dwarf pulsars AR\,Sco and J1912$-$4410 show pulsed, highly variable, and (at GHz
frequencies) circularly polarized radio emission from a spinning magnetic white dwarf.
\citet{pelisoli2025} selected \wdNCand\ AR\,Sco-like binary candidates from Gaia and
WISE (\wdNUnchar\ with no prior characterisation; \wdNClassNew\ newly classified by them), but
the list had not been searched for radio emission as a set. We report forced Stokes-I and
Stokes-V photometry at every candidate position in every public Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey
(RACS) low- and mid-band observation (887 and 1367\,MHz), plus VLASS Quick-Look cone searches
(3\,GHz) for the northern targets. The photometry pipeline is validated on a synthetic
injection recovered in both Stokes parameters and on the template AR\,Sco (deliberately
excluded from the 56), re-detected in Stokes~I in two of its five usable epochs (both
RACS-mid, best $I=\wdRealControlI$\,mJy, and undetected in both RACS-low epochs) but not in
circular polarization at the census's own $5\sigma$ bar (best V epoch $3.4\sigma$, with the
expected negative sense in all three RACS-mid epochs). Across the
\wdRealNCandCovered\ candidates with RACS coverage, no candidate is detected in Stokes I above
$5\sigma$ and none shows circularly polarized emission (\wdRealNCandVDet\ Stokes-V detections
after leakage vetting), to a median $3\sigma$ limit of \wdRealMedianVLimit\,mJy per candidate,
the first systematic radio non-detection census of the population. The one confirmed
white-dwarf pulsar in the list,
J1912$-$4410, is undetected in \wdRealJEpochs\ RACS epochs ($3\sigma$ limits
$I<\wdRealJLimI$, $V<\wdRealJLimV$\,mJy); for a source with a $\sim$1\% radio duty cycle, a
survey non-detection is not an absence, and the census bounds only the persistently
radio-loud fraction. A single candidate, the previously uncharacterised YSO J0408$+$6046, has
a 1.25\,mJy VLASS counterpart consistent with its young-stellar classification rather than an
AR\,Sco-like signature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
AR\,Sco \citep{marsh2016} and J1912$-$4410 \citep{pelisoli2023} are the two known
``white-dwarf pulsars'': close binaries in which a rapidly spinning, magnetic white dwarf
accelerates particles that radiate a pulsed, broadband, strongly variable signal from the radio
to the X-ray. Their radio emission is the cleanest discriminator: it is pulsed, often bright,
and circularly polarized at the tens-of-percent level at GHz frequencies \citep{stanway2018},
but it is also duty-cycled and highly variable, so a targeted campaign is the usual detection
route (J1912$-$4410 was confirmed object-by-object). \citet{pelisoli2025} widened the field by
selecting \wdNCand\ AR\,Sco-like candidates from Gaia$+$WISE variability and colours, but their
follow-up was optical and X-ray; the list has never been searched for radio emission as a set.
The all-sky radio archives make that search essentially free, and the outcome, detection or
limit, is scientifically useful either way: a detection would be a new white-dwarf-pulsar
candidate, and a systematic non-detection bounds the persistent radio-loud fraction of the
photometrically selected population.

RACS \citep{mcconnell2020} and VLASS \citep{lacy2020} are snapshot surveys, so a single-epoch
non-detection of a duty-cycled, variable source is expected even when the source is genuinely
radio-emitting. We therefore measure every available epoch per target and quote the deepest
single-epoch (snapshot) limit, the epoch in which the source would most likely have been
caught, rather than treating a non-detection as an absence. The recently released second epoch
of RACS-low added a circular-polarization catalogue \citep{racslow2}; this survey's Stokes-V
leg is therefore both timely and exposed to being scooped by a catalogue cross-match.

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:method}
The candidate list is Table 2 of \citet{pelisoli2025}; with no VizieR deposit, we transcribed it
from the article (56 rows, J2000 positions to $\sim$$0.1\arcsec$, provenance-flagged), validating
the transcription against the paper's per-type census (26 YSO / 16 polar / 3 CV / 2 IP /
8 unclear / 1 pulsar). This cross-check has, in a different slice, caught a transcription error in that slice's own
source table.
For each candidate we query the CASDA ObsCore for every RACS restored image containing the
position with both a Stokes-I and a Stokes-V product, stage a SODA cutout of each, and run forced
$I{+}V$ photometry \citep[the tested \code{measure\_circular\_pol};][locking the measurement to
the catalogue position turns a non-detection into an honest limit rather than a miss]{driessen2024}.
A Stokes-V detection must clear both $5\sigma$ and a $0.6\%$-of-$I$ instrumental-leakage veto
(the characteristic ASKAP on-axis leakage level); upper limits are the deepest ($3\sigma$)
single epoch across a target's epochs (a best-case snapshot depth, not an epoch-stacked one).
VLASS Quick-Look cone searches (3\,GHz, $7.5\arcsec$) cover the targets north of
$\delta=-40\arcdeg$ that VLASS reaches.

Validation is twofold. Offline, a synthetic RACS-like cutout with an injected point source is
recovered by the same photometry ($I,V$ within the noise, classified circular) and a blank
field yields no false detection. On the real sky, AR\,Sco (the template that defined the
selection, deliberately excluded from the 56) serves as the recovery test, with its efficiency
measured rather than assumed. In Stokes~I it is detected in 2 of 3 RACS-mid epochs at
$\ge 5\sigma$ (best $I=\wdRealControlI$\,mJy at \wdRealControlSnr$\sigma$) and in neither
RACS-low epoch (1.1$\sigma$ and 1.7$\sigma$ against a catalogued 8.58~mJy at 887.5~MHz), a
factor $\sim$2--4 below catalogue across the five usable epochs at two frequencies,
consistent with variability and/or the steep implied spectrum, and stated here because it is
the paper's one absolute flux check. In Stokes~V the sense is negative in all three mid epochs as expected, but the best
single epoch is 3.4$\sigma$, below the census's 5$\sigma$ bar, so under the census's own
criterion the polarization is not a detection. The control therefore calibrates the census at a
measured 2/5 per-epoch I efficiency and shows the V threshold is conservative even for the
archetype. The pipeline recovers the archetype in Stokes I while detecting none of the
candidates in either parameter, the internal control for the null result that follows.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Of the \wdNCand\ candidates, \wdRealNCandCovered\ fall in the RACS footprint and were measured
across a median of several epochs each (887 and 1367\,MHz). The survey is a clean null:
\wdRealNCandIDet\ candidates are detected in Stokes I above $5\sigma$, and \wdRealNCandVDet\
show circularly polarized emission after leakage vetting. No candidate epoch reaches even
$3\sigma$ in Stokes I (maximum $2.9\sigma$; largest $|I| = 2.0$\,mJy), consistent with noise.
The median $3\sigma$ Stokes-V limit is \wdRealMedianVLimit\,mJy (range
\wdRealVLimMin--\wdRealVLimMax\,mJy); the full per-target limit table appears as
Table~\ref{tab:limits}. By contrast the AR\,Sco control is detected at \wdRealControlSnr$\sigma$,
and its VLASS counterpart ($5.4$\,mJy, $1.4\arcsec$) checks the archive path end to end.

The one confirmed white-dwarf pulsar in the list, J1912$-$4410, is undetected in all
\wdRealJEpochs\ of its RACS epochs ($3\sigma$ limits $I<\wdRealJLimI$, $V<\wdRealJLimV$\,mJy).
J1912$-$4410 pulses at $\sim$15\,mJy for $<$4\,s with a $\sim$1\% duty cycle
\citep{pelisoli2023}, time-averaging to $\sim$0.1--0.2\,mJy, below these limits, and it is
likewise absent from the RACS-low DR1 source catalogue. A snapshot-survey non-detection of this
source is therefore consistent with its being a genuine, active white-dwarf pulsar; only the
persistent, bright, high-duty-cycle minority of such systems would appear in a survey of this
depth. VLASS adds one candidate counterpart: the previously uncharacterised YSO J0408$+$6046, a
1.25\,mJy 3\,GHz source at a $3.75\arcsec$ offset. That separation exceeds one VLASS beam, so
the match is positionally marginal (chance-coincidence $\sim$2--3\% across the northern
targets), and, because the source lies north of the RACS footprint, no circular-polarization
constraint exists for it. Its 3\,GHz flux and optically-derived young-stellar classification are
consistent with gyrosynchrotron emission rather than a white-dwarf pulsar, but we make no
polarization claim.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
The first systematic radio survey of the \citet{pelisoli2025} white-dwarf-pulsar candidates
yields no persistent radio counterparts: none of the \wdRealNCandCovered\ RACS-covered candidates
is detected in total intensity, and none shows circular polarization, to a median $3\sigma$
depth of \wdRealMedianVLimit\,mJy. Interpreted through the J1912$-$4410 calibration, this does
not disfavour the candidates: the archetype itself would be invisible to these surveys, so the
census bounds only the persistently radio-loud fraction of the sample. With 0 detections among
\wdRealNCovered\ covered candidates, the 95\% Poisson upper limit on the persistently
radio-loud fraction is $f < 6.1\%$ ($2.996/\wdRealNCovered$) above the per-target limits. This
bounds persistent emission only, since the control's measured per-epoch I efficiency is 2/5 and
a duty-cycled emitter evades snapshots by construction. Of 594 attempted epoch measurements,
\wdRealNUsed\ were usable (\wdRealNFailedEp\ hard failures after retries and \wdRealNNanEp\
all-NaN cutouts, from a position outside the staged image or an undersized RMS annulus), and
the per-target epoch counts in the table are the surviving ones. The limit table
(Table~\ref{tab:limits}) and the per-candidate forced photometry behind it are the survey's
reusable products. The sweep was re-measured on 2026-08-14 with the position locked, after
review showed the original was a 12\arcsec\ peak search: the forced census has 214/447 epochs
with $I>0$ (signed and mean-zero, as blank sky must be), against 458/458 positive before. The
leakage veto never binds at these flux levels (it would require $I \gtrsim 170$\,mJy against the
control's brightest measurement of 3.7\,mJy) and is retained only as a formal guard. Targeted,
time-resolved observations (MeerKAT/ASKAP beamformed, the route that confirmed J1912$-$4410) of
the highest-priority candidates are a natural next step, along with a re-run against the
RACS-low2 circular-polarization catalogue \citep{racslow2} as it matures.

The survey is reproducible from \code{jansky\_research.wdpulsar} (offline validation) and
\code{scripts/wdpulsar\_real.py} (the resumable CASDA sweep). The principal survey numbers are
pipeline-generated (\code{results/wdpulsar\_metrics.json} $\to$ macros and
Table~\ref{tab:limits}), with the J1912$-$4410 pulse properties (15\,mJy, $\sim$1\% duty
cycle) cited from \citet{pelisoli2023}. The candidate list is vendored as
\code{data/wdpulsar\_candidates.csv}; VLASS positions are drawn from the cached NRAO Quick-Look
catalogues.
