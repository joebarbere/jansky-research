\title{Tracking a Solar Type III Electron Beam Through the Inner Heliosphere with Wind/WAVES:
A Reproducible Drift-to-Distance Tool}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
A type III electron beam radiates near the local plasma frequency as it streams out along open coronal
field, so its radio drift from a few MHz down to tens of kHz traces the beam's outward motion. We build
a small tested tool that fits this drift in a Wind/WAVES \citep{bougeret1995} dynamic spectrum and
inverts it, via the Leblanc, Dulk \& Bougeret \citeyearpar{leblanc1998} heliospheric density model, to
the beam's radial speed and heliocentric reach (the wider-distance companion to a coronal type III
slice that used the Newkirk corona). Applied to the well-documented 2003-10-28 X17 flare burst
(\code{\wwSource}; \wwRealNridge\ channels, \wwRealFlo--\wwRealFhi~MHz, occupying
\wwRealNtimeCols\ distinct 16-s time samples), the fit gives an outward radial speed of
$\wwRealSpeedC\pm\wwRealSpeedCSe$~$c$ (leave-one-time-column-out jackknife) under harmonic
emission and the quiet-Sun Leblanc profile. That last clause carries the dominant systematic,
and we propagate it rather than footnote it: the emission mode and an event-driven density
enhancement are exactly degenerate ($f_p^2\propto n_e$, so fundamental emission at $4\times$
density equals harmonic at $1\times$), and over the committed
(mode\,$\times$\,density-scale) grid the speed spans \wwRealGridSpeedLo--\wwRealGridSpeedHi~$c$
and the outer radius \wwRealGridReachLo--\wwRealGridReachHi~$R_\odot$, a bracket that straddles
the inner-heliosphere/interplanetary boundary, so we do not claim either regime exclusively. The
radial range itself is set by the RAD2 band edges through the model, not by the event; only the
speed is event-specific. The headline speed sits below most published values at comparable
distances; the residuals show band-monotonic structure the straight-line model does not capture,
and we do not identify its cause. This is a reproducibility and method contribution and a
recover-a-known, not a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The electron beams that produce type III bursts do not stop at the top of the corona; they continue out
along the open field, and space-based receivers track the radio emission to ever lower frequencies as
the ambient density falls. With a heliospheric density model the frequency drift becomes a
height--time track whose slope is the beam's radial speed. This is the natural extension of a coronal
type III analysis (which uses the Newkirk corona, valid only to a few solar radii) to larger distances.
With the public Wind/WAVES spectra and a small tested tool, we test whether one can recover a beam's
outward track, with honest uncertainties and the right distance regime named.

\section{Methods}
\code{windwaves} is a pure-NumPy module that reuses the coronal slice's dynamic-spectrum pipeline
(background subtraction, burst-window finding, the per-channel peak-time ridge detector, and the robust
sigma-clipped fit) and \code{jansky.solar} \citep{jansky}. It adds the Leblanc density model
$n_e(r)=3.3\times10^{5}r^{-2}+4.1\times10^{6}r^{-4}+8.0\times10^{7}r^{-6}$ cm$^{-3}$ (r in $R_\odot$;
$\approx$7.1 cm$^{-3}$ at 1~AU) and its numerical inverse, mapping each ridge frequency to a plasma
frequency $f_p=f/\mathrm{harmonic}$, a density, and a heliocentric radius. The speed comes from a
linear fit with one point per distinct time sample (the column median of the mapped radii),
jackknifed leave-one-column-out for its error: the 16-s cadence stacks hundreds of channels into
\wwRealNtimeCols\ samples, so a per-point fit is leverage-dominated, and an iterated residual clip
on points was measured (on the sibling STEREO ridge) to collapse onto the crowded near-Sun columns
and reject the sparse columns that carry the drift. The all-points fit and the inverse regression
are committed alongside as the estimator bracket. The ridge uses intensity peaks rather than onset
times, which biases the speed low. The synthetic burst fixture, built from the same Leblanc mapping, now runs at the real band and cadence as well as at fine sampling, so the
time-quantisation bias is measured rather than hidden; it remains a round-trip check of the
inversion's code and not a validation of the real result.

\section{Data}
We use Wind/WAVES Level-2 RAD2 radio spectra from NASA SPDF (public, no auth; the detected ridge spans \wwRealFlo--\wwRealFhi~MHz;
\citealt{bougeret1995}). RAD1 reaches down to $\sim$20~kHz ($\approx$ the plasma frequency at 1~AU) but
is not used here; this run is RAD2 only.

\section{Results}
The burst-window peak falls at \wwRealPeakUTC\ (committed with the results), within a minute of
the X17 flare's radio onset; on a day driven by an X17 flare a day-maximum near the flare is
expected, so this locates the burst rather than validates the pipeline. Across \wwRealNridge\
detected channels occupying \wwRealNtimeCols\ time samples, the per-channel peak times trace a
high-to-low drift (the committed ridge is \code{results/windwaves\_ridge.csv}); under harmonic
emission and the unscaled Leblanc profile the mapped radii span \wwRealRlo--\wwRealRhi~$R_\odot$ (Figure~\ref{fig:ipburst}), a range fixed by the RAD2 band edges through the model, with the
outer value carried by the single lowest-frequency channel. The column-median fit gives
$\wwRealSpeedC\pm\wwRealSpeedCSe$~$c$; the estimator bracket is real and asymmetric (all-points
fit \wwRealSpeedCPoints, inverse regression \wwRealSpeedCInverse~$c$), and the committed
(mode\,$\times$\,density) grid spans \wwRealGridSpeedLo--\wwRealGridSpeedHi~$c$ with reaches \wwRealGridReachLo--\wwRealGridReachHi~$R_\odot$, under a factor-4 event-driven density enhancement, which the 2003-10-28 halo CME makes plausible, the speed enters the
\citet{reiner2015} range (0.17--0.35~$c$; \citealt{krupar2015} report 0.02--0.35~$c$ at lower
frequencies). The column-fit residuals are not consistent with a single straight track: they
trend monotonically across the band ($R^2=\wwRealRsq$ on the columns), which is what
deceleration-like curvature or a radius-dependent density mismatch would produce; we do not
identify the cause, and the fixture shows band compression at the real cadence does not produce
it. Projection is a percent-level effect over this track.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
This tool is a method and reproducibility contribution, turning a public Wind/WAVES spectrum into a
beam height--time track and radial speed, with the systematics propagated rather than listed.
(i) The distance regime is a model inference, not an instrument property: under the unscaled
Leblanc profile RAD2's band maps to $\lesssim$10~$R_\odot$, but across the committed
mode$\times$density grid the outer radius spans \wwRealGridReachLo--\wwRealGridReachHi~$R_\odot$,
crossing the conventional $\sim$20~$R_\odot$ interplanetary boundary; tracking the beam to 1~AU
needs RAD1 regardless.
(ii) The emission mode and an event-driven density enhancement are exactly degenerate
($f_p^2\propto n_e$), so no analysis of this spectrum alone can separate them; the grid is the
honest statement. (iii) The column-fit residuals carry unexplained band-monotonic structure, so
the speed is a track-average of a possibly non-constant motion; the estimator bracket
(\wwRealSpeedC--\wwRealSpeedCInverse~$c$ between regression conventions) is larger than the
jackknife error and is quoted alongside it. (iv) The field-aligned correction from the Parker
spiral is 1--3\% for the path-weighted track mean (reaching $\sim$8\% at the outer endpoint alone
for a 400~km\,s$^{-1}$ wind), below the terms above. These bound what a single RAD2 spectrum and
an average density model can claim. Every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros
this paper \code{\textbackslash input}s (the committed evidence includes the ridge, the grid, and
the burst epoch), and
\code{python -m jansky\_research.windwaves -\,-date 20031028 -\,-receiver rad2} regenerates them
from the public SPDF CDF.
