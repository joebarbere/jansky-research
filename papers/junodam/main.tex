\title{A Multi-Orbit Jovian Decametric Occurrence Census from Juno/Waves: The Range-Occurrence
Trend Is an Inverse-Square Detection Effect, and the Earth-Canonical Io Source Regions Are Not
Detected to Organise the Orbital Vantage}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Ground-based Jovian decametric (DAM) emission is organised in the plane of observer
central meridian longitude (CML) and Io orbital phase into the Io-A/B/C/D source regions
\citep{bigg1964,carr1983,marques2017}. The public Juno/Waves calibrated flux-density dataset
\citep{louis2021} enables the same census from orbit. We build an occurrence map over seven
months spanning six orbital configurations (2016--2019; \jdRealNbins\ 15-s bins, \jdRealNact\
active), using the dataset's per-channel background statistics for detection, Horizons for the
sub-Juno CML, and the Lieske ephemeris for Io phase \citep{lieske1987}. A synthetic round-trip
with the Io boxes injected recovers them (a machinery check only; a review-caught Io-phase
convention error is corrected to the Bigg 1964 / Carr et al.\ 1983 standard). The 7-month
aggregate gives two results. (i) The raw proximity trend is almost entirely detection
sensitivity. On the p90-SNR detector the near/far duty-cycle ratio across Juno--Jupiter range
quartiles ($\sim$\jdRangeNear\ to $\sim$\jdRangeFar~$R_J$) is \jdNearFarRawPninety; a
common-sensitivity census, which censors every bin to the far-range detection threshold and can
never promote noise, collapses it to \jdNearFarCens\ (an upward-rescaling null gives
\jdNearFarCorr; that variant promotes sub-threshold far-range values and is reported only for
comparison). The apparent proximity ``law'' is therefore a threshold-amplified inverse-square
visibility effect, and the $\sim$\jdNearFarCens$\times$ residual is entangled with the viewing
geometry that co-varies with range. (ii) The
Earth-canonical (CML, $\Phi_{Io}$) boxes are not detected to organise the orbital-vantage
occurrence: the aggregate contrast is $\jdRealContrast\pm\jdContrastSe$ (day-block bootstrap),
and the 95\% interval on the monthly contrast, \jdMonthCIlo--\jdMonthCIhi\ over \jdNmonths\
months, does not reject unity but equally does not reject a $\sim$1.6$\times$ enhancement, so
this is a sensitivity-limited non-detection. No rigid Io-phase shift of the box set does better
(contrast 0.75--1.18 at every offset). Per region, Io-B alone is enhanced (\jdRegB), which the
union statistic averages away. Both are consistent with the canonical regions encoding beaming toward a distant
near-equatorial observer, the effect \citet{louis2021jgr} measure directly from Juno/Waves
flux densities and/or genuine month-to-month variability (Io volcanism, plasma-torus
state); neither is separated here, and a beaming-model fit over the full v02 archive is the
planned follow-up.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Io's control of Jovian DAM \citep{bigg1964} is the textbook example of a satellite--magnetosphere
interaction, and the (CML, Io-phase) occurrence map with its A/B/C/D regions \citep{carr1983,
marques2017} is its canonical representation, built from decades of terrestrial
observations, i.e.\ from a fixed, distant, near-equatorial vantage. Juno's polar, eccentric orbit
breaks every one of those assumptions. The public Juno/Waves estimated flux densities
\citep[version 02, 2023;][]{louis2021} (daily CDFs to 40.5~MHz with per-channel background and
sigma) make the from-orbit census reproducible with public data alone.

\section{Method}
Each daily CDF is binned to 15~s; a bin is DAM-active when $\ge$10\% of the 3--40.5~MHz channels
exceed the shipped $\mathrm{Background}+5\Sigma$. The absolute scale (V$^2$m$^{-2}$Hz$^{-1}$)
never enters; an occurrence census needs only detection against the dataset's own statistics.
The sub-Juno CML comes from Horizons (\code{PDObsLon}, observer \code{500@-61}) at 15-min
sampling, unwrap-interpolated; Io phase is the conventional $\Phi_{Io} = \mathrm{CML} + 180^\circ - \lambda_{Io}$ with the
Lieske mean longitude \citep{lieske1987} and the IAU System III rotation rate
\citep{archinal2018}. Occurrence maps carry per-cell exposure, and cells visited $<$3 times are
masked, since a single orbit does not sample the plane uniformly and hiding that would manufacture
structure. The synthetic recover-a-known drives the same code with real CML/Io rates and
Bernoulli activity concentrated in the canonical boxes: injected contrast \jdSynExpContrast,
recovered \jdSynContrast, a 21\% under-recovery: box membership is evaluated at
cell centres and boundary cells mix inside with outside, contracting every contrast toward
unity. The bias direction matters, since it points toward this paper's conclusion, so the
recovered-versus-injected curve is committed down to the regime the real measurement lives in:
injected 1.25 recovers \jdSynRecNearUnity\ and injected 2.0 recovers \jdSynRecMid. Read
against that curve, the measured aggregate contrast corresponds to an intrinsic value roughly a
third larger.

\section{Results and honest limits}
Figure~\ref{fig:jd} shows the aggregate map. The range-quartile duty cycles fall from
\jdAqA\% to \jdAqD\% (near-to-far) on the band-fraction detector, a raw factor
$\sim$\jdNearFarRaw; on the p90-SNR detector the same ratio is \jdNearFarRawPninety\ (the two
detectors differ by 70\%, and every corrected number below uses the p90 detector so the
correction is never conflated with a detector change). DAM power scales as $1/r^2$, and the SNR
distribution falls steeply across the threshold, so a modest flux change is amplified into the
occurrence swing. The common-sensitivity census, which keeps a bin only if it is raw-active and
its SNR rescaled to the far-quartile range still clears threshold, collapses the ratio to
\jdNearFarCens. The upward-rescaling variant used in an earlier version of this analysis gives
\jdNearFarCorr, but it promotes sub-threshold far-range values across the floor (its
far-quartile duty cycle exceeds the raw one), so it is reported only to show the method
dependence. The corrected duty cycles are non-monotonic near the top (the second quartile
exceeds the first), so there is no clean intrinsic trend, and the residual is entangled with the
CMI beaming geometry that co-varies with Juno's range. The ``proximity law'' is thus a
threshold-amplified inverse-square visibility effect, with at most a
$\sim$\jdNearFarCens$\times$ residual we cannot separate from geometry.

The canonical Io boxes are not detected to organise the aggregate. The aggregate union
contrast is $\jdRealContrast\pm\jdContrastSe$ (day-block bootstrap over 210 days; the
\jdRealNact\ active bins form \jdNEpisodes\ contiguous emission episodes, so per-bin errors
would overstate the effective sample size by two orders of magnitude), over \jdRealCells\
cells. The per-month median is \jdCmMed\ with range \jdCmMin--\jdCmMax\ across \jdNmonths\
months, the geometric-mean monthly contrast is \jdMonthGeoMean\ with 95\% interval
\jdMonthCIlo--\jdMonthCIhi\ (sign test $p=\jdMonthSignP$, two-sided), and there is no
strengthening with distance in the resolved quartiles (\jdCqA, \jdCqB, \jdCqC, \jdCqD\ from
near to far, on few active bins in the far pair). Two pieces of structure temper the null. The
aggregate map is not featureless: enhanced cells form a coherent diagonal band across the
plane, and the per-region contrasts are \jdRegB\ for Io-B against \jdRegA, \jdRegC\ and
\jdRegD\ for A, C and D. Io-B, which sits on that band, is enhanced while the union
statistic averages it away. And a rigid shift of the box set in Io phase finds no offset that
beats zero (contrast 0.75--1.18 everywhere), which cuts both ways: no hidden convention offset
would rescue the boxes, and the flatness of the scan is itself the strongest statement that
this plane, from Juno's vantage, is only weakly organised by the canonical regions.

Several limits qualify these results. (0) The 95\% interval on the monthly contrast spans
\jdMonthCIlo--\jdMonthCIhi: ``does not reject unity'' at $n=\jdNmonths$ equally does not
reject a 1.6$\times$ enhancement, so the null is a sensitivity-limited non-detection, not a
demonstrated absence, and genuine month-to-month Io-DAM variability (Io volcanism, plasma-torus
state) is an unmodelled alternative to geometric scatter. (i) Seven months is sparse and the far
quartiles are activity-starved. The single v01-processed month (2017-02) is also the outlier
(\jdCmMax); excluding it moves the interval to 0.48--1.33 without changing any conclusion, so
the version mix is measured to be benign rather than assumed. (ii) The fixed 10\%-of-band
activity criterion is one choice among several: it suppresses narrowband interference but
dilutes narrowband DAM. (iii) The common-sensitivity census divides out the leading
inverse-square detection advantage but not the CMI beaming geometry that co-varies with Juno's
range, so the residual \jdNearFarCens$\times$ is an upper bound, not an intrinsic measurement.
(iv) The census does not separate Io-DAM from non-Io-DAM or kilometric leakage below 3~MHz.
(v) The frame convention has no external validation in this paper: the shift scan shows no
offset rescues the boxes on this data, and the synthetic round-trip validates the machinery,
but only running a ground-based catalogue through the same code would certify the convention
independently; that, and a beaming-model fit over the full v02 archive building on
\citet{louis2021jgr}, are the follow-ups with real leverage.
