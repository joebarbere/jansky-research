\title{The Pulsar P--$\dot P$ Diagram, Surface Fields, and the Death Line:
A Reproducible Recovery from the ATNF Catalogue}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
A rotation-powered pulsar's period $P$ and spin-down rate $\dot P$ fix its surface dipole field
$B\approx3.2\times10^{19}\sqrt{P\dot P}$ G, characteristic age $\tau=P/2\dot P$, and spin-down
luminosity $\dot E=4\pi^2 I\dot P/P^3$. The $P$--$\dot P$ plane is the H--R diagram of pulsars. We build
a small tested tool that recovers its population structure from the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue
\citep{manchester2005} (the frozen 2017 VizieR snapshot \code{B/psr}, public, no auth): of the
catalogue, \ppN\ pulsars have a positive measured $\dot P$ and enter the diagram. The measured
separation is between the two classes selected on period alone: millisecond pulsars at
$\log(B/\mathrm{G})=\ppLogBmsp$ (recycled) and the ordinary population at \ppLogBnormal,
\ppMspNormalSpanDex\ dex apart, with both medians stable to the period cut. The high-field tail
medians \ppLogBmagnetar, but that class is selected on $B$ itself, so its median tracks its own
defining threshold (\ppLogBmagnetarLoCut--\ppLogBmagnetarHiCut\ as the cut sweeps
$3\times10^{12}$--$3\times10^{13}$ G) and we commit it as a sweep rather than quote it as a
population property. \ppFracAlivePct\% of the sample lies above a standard polar-cap death line
(\ppFracAlivePctLo--\ppFracAlivePctHi\% across a factor-four sweep of the death-valley constant),
as expected for a radio-selected catalogue. Four named pulsars --- the Crab ($B=\ppCrabB$ G,
$\tau=\ppCrabAge$ yr), B1937$+$21, the magnetar J1550$-$5418, and the 8.5-s J2144$-$3933 --- are
read out of the fetched rows and gated against literature windows, so a units or parsing
regression fails the run instead of rewriting these numbers. This is a reproducibility/method
contribution and a recover-a-known, not a discovery, and we are explicit about the catalogue
selection, the snapshot's vintage, the death-line model dependence, and what $B$ and $\tau$ do
and do not mean.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Pulsar spin-down is the cleanest clock in astrophysics, and the period--period-derivative diagram
organises the whole population: ordinary pulsars at $B\sim10^{12}$ G and $P\sim0.1$--1 s, millisecond
pulsars recycled to the bottom-left by binary accretion, magnetars at the top-right, and a death line
below which the polar-cap voltage can no longer sustain the pair production that powers radio emission
\citep{rudermansutherland1975,bhattacharya1991,lorimerkramer2004}. We ask the most reproducible version:
with the public catalogue and a small tested tool, can that structure be recovered, with the right
caveats?

\section{Methods}
\code{ppdot} is a pure-NumPy module on the \code{jansky} helpers \citep{jansky}, reusing the project's
ATNF fetch pattern and its millisecond-pulsar period cut. From $P$ and $\dot P$ it computes the surface
dipole field $B=3.2\times10^{19}\sqrt{P\dot P}$ G, which is the standard orthogonal-rotator
estimate ($\sin\alpha=1$, $I=10^{45}$ g cm$^2$, $R=10$ km; the aligned/equatorial convention gives
$6.4\times10^{19}$, i.e.\ fields larger by $\sqrt2$); the characteristic age $\tau=P/2\dot P$; and the
spin-down luminosity. It labels each pulsar magnetar/high-$B$ ($B>10^{13}$ G), millisecond ($P<30$ ms),
or normal, in that priority, and evaluates a constant-$B/P^2$ death line ($B_{12}/P^2=0.2$;
\citealt{rudermansutherland1975,bhattacharya1991}). Every function is tested against a synthetic
three-population fixture; that round-trip checks the classifier's arithmetic on injected labels and is
not a validation of the real results.

\section{Data}
We query the ATNF Pulsar Catalogue \citep{manchester2005} from VizieR (\code{B/psr}): periods and
period derivatives for the known pulsars. \code{B/psr} is a frozen snapshot (published 2017-07-18)
that lags the live \code{psrcat}: its longest period is 11.8 s, so the ultra-long-period pulsars
discovered since (e.g.\ the 76-s PSR J0901$-$4046) are absent by construction, and the per-class
counts are epoch-shaped as well as survey-shaped. Of the \ppNcatalogue\ entries, \ppN\ have a
positive measured $\dot P$ and enter the diagram. The discards are measured, not assumed:
\ppNpdotNull\ lack a measured $\dot P$ entirely (the dominant reason), \ppNpdotNeg\ show a
negative apparent $\dot P$ --- for most, cluster acceleration in globular clusters rather than
true spin-down --- \ppNpdotZero\ record exactly zero and are dropped by the positivity cut, and
\ppNpMissing\ lack a period.
The analysed rows, with their derived quantities, classes, and death-line dispositions, are
committed as \code{results/ppdot\_pulsars.csv}.

\section{Results}
The median surface field separates the populations (Figure~\ref{fig:ppdot}):
$\log(B/\mathrm{G}) = \ppLogBmsp$ for the \ppNmsp\ millisecond pulsars and \ppLogBnormal\ for the
\ppNnormal\ normal pulsars --- \ppMspNormalSpanDex\ dex apart, and this is the robust measured
separation, since both classes are selected on period, not on $B$: the committed sweep moves the
MSP median only \ppLogBmspTwentyMs--\ppLogBmspFiftyMs\ as the period cut sweeps 20--50 ms, and
the normal median \ppLogBnormalTwentyMs--\ppLogBnormalFiftyMs. The
\ppNmagnetar\ high-$B$/magnetar objects median \ppLogBmagnetar, but that class is defined by
$B>10^{13}$ G --- a cut on the reported quantity itself --- so its median is guaranteed to
exceed 13 and tracks the threshold directly: the committed sweep gives
\ppLogBmagnetarLoCut--\ppLogBmagnetarHiCut\ as the cut moves $3\times10^{12}$--$3\times10^{13}$ G.
The full \ppBSpanDex-dex span (a factor of $\sim$\ppBSpanFactor) between the MSP and high-$B$
medians is therefore partly an author-chosen constant, and the textbook P--$\dot P$ structure
\citep{lorimerkramer2004} is carried by the diagram and the period-selected medians, not by that
one number. The millisecond population sits at the bottom-left (short $P$, tiny $\dot P$, recycled
by accretion); the high-$B$ objects at the top-right. Against the standard polar-cap death line,
\ppFracAlivePct\% of the sample is radio-loud, as expected for a radio-selected catalogue; the
committed death-valley sweep puts that at \ppFracAlivePctLo--\ppFracAlivePctHi\% for
$B_{12}/P^2$ from 0.4 down to 0.1. Four named pulsars check the derivations. Looked up by name in
the analysed sample, the Crab ($P=\ppCrabPeriod$~s) gives $B=\ppCrabB$ G and
$\tau=P/2\dot P=\ppCrabAge$ yr, both textbook; $\tau$ is appropriately longer than the true
$\sim$970-yr age, since $\tau$ assumes braking index 3 and $P_0\ll P$. B1937$+$21 gives
$B=\ppMspAnchorB$ G (the recycled floor), J1550$-$5418 gives $\ppMagnetarAnchorB$ G (a true
magnetar), and J2144$-$3933 ($\ppDeathAnchorB$ G) is the 8.5-s archetype of the below-death-line
population. Because each row is read from the fetched catalogue and gated against a literature
window in \code{run()}, a units or parsing regression fails the run --- which evaluating the
closed forms at hand-entered values cannot.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is method and reproducibility: a tested tool that recovers the P--$\dot P$ population
structure from a public catalogue, with the caveats that bound it. (i) ``Magnetar'' here is a
position-in-diagram label ($B>10^{13}$ G; \ppNmagnetar\ sources) that includes high-field
rotation-powered pulsars as well as the $\sim$30 true magnetars, which are X-ray/$\gamma$-ray, not
radio, selected --- and, per the Results, its median is set by its own threshold, which is why the
committed sweep accompanies it. (ii) The death line is one representative model; the literature
spans a ``death valley'' \citep{chenruderman1993}, and the \ppFracDead\% below our line are not
noise but real long-period pulsars that motivated that literature. The committed sweep bounds the
model dependence --- \ppFracAlivePctLo\% above the line at twice our constant, \ppFracAlivePctHi\%
at half --- so ``almost all'' survives a factor of two and not a factor of five. Our 2017-vintage
snapshot also predates the ultra-long-period discoveries that most stress death-line models, so
the below-line census here is a floor. (iii) The ATNF catalogue is the union of many flux- and
period-limited surveys, so the relative population sizes are survey-shaped, not intrinsic. The
per-class median fields barely move under a flux cut --- requiring a tabulated $S_{1400}$, or
$S_{1400}\geq0.5$ or 2 mJy, shifts no class median by more than \ppFluxCutMaxDex\ dex (committed
as a sweep); the high-$B$ median moves most, because a radio-flux requirement removes radio-quiet
high-$B$ objects. \code{B/psr} carries no survey-provenance column, so this bounds sensitivity to
a flux cut, not to survey selection proper. None of these undermine the recover-a-known (the
field separation and the diagram structure are unambiguous), but they are why this is a
reproduction, not a population measurement. Every catalogue-derived number above is written by the pipeline into the macros
this manuscript \code{\textbackslash input}s (the $\sim$30 true magnetars and the Crab's $\sim$970-yr
historical age are literature figures, not outputs of this analysis), and \code{python -m jansky\_research.ppdot} regenerates them from the
public catalogue.
