\title{From Frequency Drift to Beam Speed: A Reproducible Tool for Solar Type III Radio Bursts,
Applied to an e-Callisto Dynamic Spectrum}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
A solar type III radio burst is the signature of an electron beam streaming out through the corona: it
radiates near the local plasma frequency, which falls with height, so the burst drifts fast from high to
low frequency, and the drift rate, through a coronal density model, encodes the beam's speed. We
build a small tested tool that fits the drift ridge in an e-Callisto \citep{benz2009} dynamic spectrum
and inverts it to an exciter speed, reusing the \code{jansky} course's coronal-physics helpers (the
Newkirk \citeyearpar{newkirk1961} density model and the plasma-frequency relation). The pipeline
background-subtracts, isolates the burst time window, builds the ridge from each channel's intensity
peak time, maps frequency to heliocentric radius, and fits radius versus time with a sigma-clipped
line iterated to convergence, whose $R^2$ flags a coherent single burst against a multi-burst
storm. Applied to an isolated, catalogue-clean type III (\sbSource; the clip keeps \sbNused\ of
\sbNridge\ ridge channels spanning \sbFitFlo--\sbFitFhi~MHz at $R^2=\sbRsq$), the height--time fit
gives an exciter speed of \sbSpeedC~$c$ assuming harmonic emission and a quiet ($1\times$) Newkirk
corona, with a committed analysis-choice spread of \sbSpeedCMin--\sbSpeedCMax~$c$ (window width,
clip threshold, isolated band-edge channels) and a factor-$\sim$3 model bracket from
\sbGridFundOne$\,c$ (fundamental) to \sbGridHarmFour$\,c$ ($4\times$ Newkirk). The harmonic
interpretations sit inside the established $0.1$--$0.5\,c$ type III range; the fundamental one
falls below it, so the in-range conclusion is conditional on the emission mode, which is not
independently established for this event. A synthetic fixture confirms the inversion is
algebraically self-consistent, and the storm-interval negative control plus the three rejected
candidates are committed at the same parameterization. This is a reproducibility/method
contribution and a recover-a-known, not a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Type III bursts are the most common solar radio bursts and a classic diagnostic of energetic electron
beams escaping along open coronal field \citep{reidratcliffe2014}. The beam excites Langmuir waves at the
local plasma frequency $f_p\propto\sqrt{n_e}$, which convert to radio at $f_p$ (fundamental) or $2f_p$
(harmonic); because $n_e$ falls with height, the emission drifts rapidly from high to low frequency. With
a coronal density model the frequency drift maps to a height--time track whose slope is the exciter
speed, canonically $0.1$--$0.5\,c$ \citep{reidratcliffe2014,reidkontar2018}. The e-Callisto network
\citep{benz2009} publishes ground-based solar dynamic spectra ($\sim$20--900~MHz, 15-minute FITS) openly
over HTTP with no authentication. We ask the most reproducible version: with the public spectra and a
small tested tool, can one recover the canonical type III beam speed with the dominant systematics
reported honestly?

\section{Methods}
\code{solarbursts} is a pure-NumPy/SciPy module on the \code{jansky} helpers \citep{jansky}. From a
dynamic spectrum it (i) subtracts each channel's median baseline (e-Callisto records arbitrary,
uncalibrated digitiser units, so the analysis is purely morphological); (ii) isolates the burst by the
band-integrated power peak $\pm$ a short window, since a type III lasts seconds within a 15-minute file;
(iii) builds the drift ridge as the peak intensity time of each frequency channel, with the
per-channel noise taken from the full sweep so a brief burst does not inflate the threshold; (iv) maps
each ridge frequency to a plasma frequency $f_p=f/\mathrm{harmonic}$, a Newkirk density, and a
heliocentric radius; and (v) fits radius versus time with an iterative sigma-clipped line, reporting the
slope $|dr/dt|$ as the speed and the fit $R^2$ as a coherence flag. We stress that the ridge uses
intensity peaks, which trace the bulk of the beam, not its leading edge; in \citet{reidkontar2018}
the mean peak-time speed ($0.17\,c$) runs 15\% below the front-of-beam mean ($0.2\,c$). The
sigma-clipped fit iterates to a fixed point, so the reported slope, kept-channel mask, and $R^2$
all describe the same final fit; an earlier version stopped after a hard-coded three iterations,
under which the speed moved by $\sim$13\% and the mask and slope came from different iterations.
Every function is tested against a synthetic burst built from the same Newkirk mapping the
analysis inverts, so a clean burst round-trips. This verifies algebraic self-consistency of the
inversion, not the density model itself.

\section{Data}
We use e-Callisto spectra from Birr, Ireland (BIR; the detected ridge spans 10--79~MHz,
the file band extends somewhat higher), selecting
isolated, quality-1 type III bursts from the Monstein Solar Geophysical Data burst lists. Event
selection is the crux, and its evidence is committed
(\code{results/solarbursts\_candidates.json}): all four candidates plus the X6.9-flare storm
control were run at the same parameterization as the headline. Only \sbSource\ is a clean fit
($R^2=\sbRsq$); the three rejected candidates give $R^2$ of \sbCandOneRsq, \sbCandTwoRsq\ and
\sbCandThreeRsq\ (the second with a wrong-direction, positive drift of
\sbCandTwoDrift~MHz/s), and the storm interval gives $R^2=\sbCandStormRsq$ with an incoherent,
artificially slow ridge (nominal \sbCandStormSpeedC~$c$), the negative control that shows the coherence gate can fail. Under this uniform parameterization the rejected events' nominal speeds
(\sbCandOneSpeedC, \sbCandTwoSpeedC, \sbCandThreeSpeedC~$c$) do not all fall in the canonical
band, so passing the gate is not guaranteed by construction. e-Callisto data carry no flux
calibration, and only one station was used, so there is no second-station confirmation.

\section{Results}
The detector finds \sbNridge\ ridge channels over \sbFlo--\sbFhi~MHz; the converged sigma clip
keeps \sbNused\ of them, spanning \sbFitFlo--\sbFitFhi~MHz, the fit band, which is what the $R^2$ and the speed describe (the wider detection band includes the clipped outliers, among them
an isolated 10~MHz channel and a 78.9~MHz channel whose timing runs the wrong way for a type
III). The kept channels' drift is \sbDriftUsed~MHz/s (the unclipped 66-channel value is
\sbDrift), and the height--time track is coherent ($R^2=\sbRsq$) over heliocentric radii
\sbRlo--\sbRhi~$R_\odot$ (harmonic, $1\times$ Newkirk; Figure~\ref{fig:burst}). The implied
exciter speed is \sbSpeedC~$c$ ($\approx\sbSpeedKms$~km\,s$^{-1}$), with a committed
analysis-choice spread of \sbSpeedCMin--\sbSpeedCMax~$c$ over the burst-window width, the clip
threshold, and the isolated band-edge channels
(\code{speed\_sensitivity} in the results). The two model knobs bracket it more widely:
emission mode (fundamental $f_p=f$ vs harmonic $f_p=f/2$) and the Newkirk fold ($1\times$ quiet
to $4\times$ active region) move the speed from \sbGridFundOne$\,c$ (fundamental, $1\times$)
through \sbGridHarmOne$\,c$ (harmonic, $1\times$) to \sbGridHarmFour$\,c$ (harmonic, $4\times$),
a factor of $\sim$3; we therefore report the grid, not a single number. The harmonic points lie
within the canonical $0.1$--$0.5\,c$ band \citep{reidratcliffe2014}; the fundamental point falls
below its lower edge, so the in-band conclusion is conditional on harmonic emission, a common identification for ground-based metric bursts, but not independently established for this event
(a fundamental--harmonic pair would appear as two ridges a factor of two apart in frequency;
this spectrum shows one). As a peak-time speed, the headline lies within the range of published
peak-time speeds (the 31-burst {LOFAR} sample of \citealt{reidkontar2018} has a $0.17\,c$
peak-time mean, derived through their own density model; the model choice moves speeds by factors comparable to our grid, so this is a range comparison, not a point agreement). The
measured drift is slower than typical: the \citet{alvarez1973} and \citet{zhang2018} relations
give $\sim-6$ to $-9$~MHz/s at mid-band.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is method and reproducibility: a tested tool that turns a public e-Callisto spectrum
into a calibrated-by-assumption beam speed, with the systematics surfaced rather than hidden. The honest limitations are substantial. (i) The
emission mode and density-model fold span a factor of $\sim$3 in speed; the Newkirk model is best
constrained within $\sim$1--3~$R_\odot$, so the $4\times$ case, which reaches
\sbGridHarmFourRhi~$R_\odot$, extrapolates beyond its edge.
(ii) The ridge is built from peak times, biasing the speed below the front-of-beam value, and
radio-wave scattering and projection further suppress apparent speeds at $<$50~MHz \citep{kontar2017}, and
both biases act downward, so the true front speed is somewhat higher than our peak-time number. (iii)
$R^2$ is a useful empirical flag for ``one burst vs.\ a storm'' but not a rigorous segmentation, and is
sensitive to ridge-point spacing. (iv) The data are uncalibrated and single-station, and ridge channels
near the $\sim$10~MHz ground-based cutoff may carry ionospheric refraction. The recover-a-known's
target band is wide (a factor of 5) against a model grid spanning a factor of 3, so landing
in-band is weak evidence by itself; the committed candidate table is what makes the exercise falsifiable: the storm control and one rejected candidate produce out-of-band or wrong-direction
answers under the same pipeline, so the gate does discriminate. These limits bound what a single
ground-based spectrum can claim. Every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros
\code{\textbackslash input}ted by this file, and
\code{python -m jansky\_research.solarbursts -\,-recover} regenerates them from the public FITS.
