\title{An Independent Reproduction of the Breakthrough Listen 3I/ATLAS
Nondetection from the Public Green Bank Telescope Data}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Breakthrough Listen's 1--12~GHz technosignature search of the interstellar object
3I/ATLAS with the Green Bank Telescope reported a nondetection down to $\sim$100~mW
equivalent isotropic radiated power \citep{jacobsonbell2025} and released the data
publicly. We reprocess every recorded node of all four receiver cadences with an
independent pipeline (physical-units de-Doppler search, ABACAD on/off filter,
per-candidate drift-coherence vet, and satellite-allocation exclusion) and reproduce
the null across the full band. \aiTotRawHits\ raw hits over \aiTotNodes\ nodes reduce
to \aiTotSurvivors\ two-position survivors and \aiTotConfirmed\ confirmed candidates,
with a matching \aiEirpMw~mW L-band limit at unit dedrifting efficiency; the drift
grid is coarser than matched, costing up to $\sim$3$\times$ off-grid. The survivors
that evade each filter stage show how terrestrial and satellite interference defeats
geometric filters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Result}

On 2025 December 18 Breakthrough Listen observed 3I/ATLAS with the GBT in four bands
(L/S/C/X, 1--12~GHz) using 6$\times$5-min ABACAD on/off cadences, searched the
fine-resolution ($\sim$2.8~Hz) data with \code{turboSETI} over $\pm4$~Hz\,s$^{-1}$, and
found no technosignature candidates \citep{jacobsonbell2025}. Companion searches with
the ATA \citep{ata3i} and FAST \citep{fast3i} also report nulls. All GBT data are public
(\url{https://bldata.berkeley.edu/ATLAS}), yet, as for most technosignature nulls, no
independent reanalysis of the released files existed.

\code{atlas3i} \citep{janskyresearch} is such a reanalysis of the complete release:
\aiTotNodes\ unique 187.5~MHz compute-node cadences spanning
\aiLBandLo~MHz--\aiXBandHi~MHz (L: \aiLNodes\ nodes; S: \aiSNodes; C: \aiCNodes;
X: \aiXNodes; the recorded spans exceed the paper's analysed passbands at the band
edges). The pipeline is a brute-force shift-and-sum de-Doppler search in physical units over
$\pm4$~Hz\,s$^{-1}$, with per-channel bandpass normalization and DC-spike excision,
thresholded at $16\sigma$ against per-drift-rate robust (MAD) noise. An on/off filter
requires a hit to appear, extrapolated along its own drift rate, in every on-target
scan and no off-target scan. A postage-stamp vet re-reads all six scans around each
survivor and demands the same coherence directly of the data, and an ITU
satellite-downlink allocation exclusion follows. Every stage is validated offline on a
synthetic cadence with an injected drifting tone, an always-on interferer, and a DC
spike.

The result reproduces the paper's null over the full band (Figure~\ref{fig:survey}):
\aiTotRawHits\ raw hits $\to$ \aiTotSurvivors\ survivors $\to$ \aiTotConfirmed\
confirmed. The $16\sigma$ narrowband limit,
$\mathrm{EIRP} = 4\pi d^{2}\,S_\mathrm{min}\,\delta\nu$ with the paper's own parameters
(SEFD $\approx$ 10~Jy, 300~s scans, $d=\aiDistanceAu$~au from JPL Horizons at the
cadence epoch), is $\mathrm{EIRP} \le \aiEirpMw$~mW at L band, algebraically identical
to the paper's formulation \citep{gajjar2021} for unit dedrifting efficiency, hence the
matching $\sim$100~mW. The reproduction cost $\approx$95~h of active compute and
$\approx$3.7~TB of transfer on one desktop PC. Auditing a published technosignature
search from its public archive is within any interested party's reach.

\section{What Evades a Two-Position Filter}

The survivor population is a taxonomy of filter evasion, concentrated where the
interference lives: \aiLSurvivors\ of the \aiTotSurvivors\ survivors are in L band,
\aiSSurvivors\ in S, \aiXSurvivors\ in X (all zero-drift Ku-band satellite-TV tones
above the analysed passband), and C band, 4.3~GHz of spectrum, produced
\aiCSurvivors. Terrestrial carriers passed the on/off filter by sitting below threshold
in the off scans; the stamp vet sees them there directly. High-drift chance
coincidences in the densest hit lists fail to track their own drift rate. Of
particular interest, \aiSatCoherent\ candidates passed both geometric filters, every one in the Iridium or
Inmarsat downlink bands with LEO-Doppler-scale drifts. Satellites are genuinely in the
sky and intermittent, so no two-position filter can reject them by geometry; a
frequency-allocation axis is necessary, as BL's own pipelines encode. This independent
rediscovery of that necessity from scratch is a check on the original search's design.

Caveats: the L-band leg used symmetric on/off thresholds where the paper used
$16\sigma$/$10\sigma$ (the stamp vet's direct off-scan check subsumes the difference).
The MAD-based threshold is effectively looser than \code{turboSETI}'s normalization
\citep{choza2024} in dense RFI, so raw hit counts are not comparable between detectors,
a conservative bias for a null. The recorded band edges searched here also extend
beyond the passbands the original team analysed.
