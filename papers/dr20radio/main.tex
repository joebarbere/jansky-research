\title{The First Radio-Counterpart Census of the SDSS-V DR20 Black Hole Mapper Quasars,
Including the First Southern-Hemisphere SDSS Quasar Spectra}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
SDSS-V Data Release 20 delivers the Black Hole Mapper's spectroscopically confirmed quasar
sample, including the first optical SDSS spectra ever taken from the southern hemisphere
(BOSS on the du~Pont at Las Campanas). Because VLASS stops at $\delta = -40^\circ$ and
southern SDSS quasar spectra did not exist before DR20, the pairing ``SDSS spectroscopic
quasars $\times$ RACS south of $-40^\circ$'' was impossible until this release; no radio
cross-match of any SDSS-V quasar catalog has been published. I present the first such
census. North of $-40^\circ$, \drNorthCensus\ clean-redshift DR20 quasars matched against
the VLASS Quick Look epoch catalogs at 2.5\arcsec\ give per-epoch radio-detection fractions
of \drNorthEtwoPct\% (E2) and \drNorthEthreePct\% (E3) after subtracting a
\emph{measured} chance-coincidence rate (\drNorthFmPct\% from position-shift trials), or
\drNorthAnyPct\% matched in any epoch (raw, uncorrected). South of $-40^\circ$ --- sky where these are the
first SDSS quasar spectra --- \drSouthCensus\ quasars (all observed from Las Campanas)
matched against RACS-low DR1 at 5\arcsec\ give \drSouthPct\% (chance rate \drSouthFmPct\%).
Radio-targeted open-fiber cartons are excluded from all fractions as circular; used instead
as a positive control, RACS-selected cartons recover at \drCartonRacsPct\%
($n=\drCartonRacsN$) against their selecting survey, while LOFAR-selected cartons recover
at only \drCartonLofarPct\% ($n=\drCartonLofarN$) at 888\,MHz, and at 3\,GHz VLASS
recovers RACS-selected cartons at \drCartonVlassRacsPct\% and LOFAR-selected at
\drCartonVlassLofarPct\% --- recovery rates for the same targeting classes across
surveys differing in both frequency and depth. The comparison of the two
hemispheres above a common rest-frame 1.4\,GHz luminosity limit depends entirely on the
assumed radio spectral index, which spans a factor seven in the resulting gap over
$\drAlphaHi \ge \alpha \ge \drAlphaLo$; I therefore \emph{measure} it, using the
\drAlphaMeasN\ quasars in the survey-overlap band detected by both VLASS and RACS above the
flux where the joint-detection requirement cannot bias the distribution, obtaining
$\alpha = \drAlphaMeasFull \pm \drAlphaMeasSeFull$~(stat)~$\pm \drAlphaSysSpan$~(sys), where
the systematic is the span across three checks that each moved it --- the assumed
completeness floor, the epoch choice, and the flux range measured over --- and dominates the
statistical error by an order of magnitude. At the measured index the fractions are
\drLumNorthMeasPct\% (north) versus \drLumSouthMeasPct\% (south), a gap of \drGapMeasPp\
percentage points (\drGapMeasLoPp--\drGapMeasHiPp\ across $\pm1\sigma$ in $\alpha$). Equalising the two
surveys' limits --- VLASS quotes a per-epoch reliability threshold where RACS quotes a 95\%
completeness limit --- reduces that gap by \drGapVlassDropPct\%, to \drGapVlassConsB\
percentage points. The residual is consistent with survey-side effects (beam-size mismatch,
hemisphere targeting mix), which I identify but do not fully quantify, and I attribute none
of it to astrophysics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The radio properties of optically selected quasars have been surveyed for as long as both
kinds of survey have existed \citep{kellermann1989, ivezic2002, condon1998}, and the modern
literature pairs SDSS quasar catalogs with every major radio survey generation
\citep[e.g.][]{arnaudova2024}. Every one of those studies, however, is built on
northern-hemisphere SDSS spectroscopy: the Sloan telescope at Apache Point bounds the
spectroscopic quasar sample to $\delta \gtrsim -20^\circ$, and the deep southern sky has
never contained an SDSS spectroscopic quasar at all.

SDSS-V \citep{kollmeier2025} changed the second half of that sentence. Data Release 20
\citep[DR20;][]{dr20}, released 2026 August~3, includes Black Hole Mapper (BHM) BOSS
spectroscopy taken with the du~Pont telescope at Las Campanas Observatory (LCO) --- the
first optical SDSS spectra from the southern hemisphere. Meanwhile the radio sky south of
$\delta = -40^\circ$ is the exclusive territory of ASKAP's Rapid Continuum Survey
\citep[RACS;][]{mcconnell2020}, because VLASS \citep{lacy2020} cannot point there. The
combination ``SDSS spectroscopic quasars $\times$ RACS in the deep south'' was therefore
categorically impossible before DR20, and as of this writing no radio cross-match of any
SDSS-V BHM catalog has been published.

This paper performs that census, in both hemispheres, with three methodological
commitments. First, the chance-coincidence rate is \emph{measured}, by rigid position-shift
trials against the real radio catalogs, rather than estimated from an assumed source
density. Second, the BHM open-fiber cartons that targeted radio sources
(\code{openfibertargets\_bhm\_racsradio\_*}, \code{...\_lofarradio\_*}) are excluded from
every detection fraction --- counting objects that hold fibers \emph{because} they are
radio sources would be circular --- and are used instead as a positive control, which turns
out to measure cross-frequency spectral fading within a fixed targeting class. Third, the
north/south comparison is made above a common rest-frame luminosity limit with all
conversion assumptions stated, and the residual difference is attributed to the surveys,
not the sky, and its dominant systematic --- the assumed spectral index --- is swept rather
than fixed, so the size of any residual is reported as a range.

All code, committed evidence files, and figures are in the open-source
\code{jansky-research} toolkit \citep{janskyresearch}; every number in this paper is
generated from version-controlled result files by the same pipeline that produced them.

\section{Data}

\subsection{DR20 quasar sample}

The parent catalog is the DR20 BOSS summary file
\code{spAll-lite-v6\_2\_1-allepoch.fits.gz}. I select \code{CLASS = QSO} with
\code{ZWARNING = 0} (a clean pipeline redshift), yielding \drNorthCensus\ census quasars at
$\delta > -40^\circ$ and \drSouthCensus\ at $\delta \le -40^\circ$ after removing the
radio-targeted cartons (Section~\ref{sec:cartons}). The \code{OBS} column records the
observatory: every one of the \drSouthCensus\ deep-south quasars is an LCO observation,
confirming in-data that this sample is the new southern territory. The BHM sample is
heterogeneous by construction --- eROSITA-selected, variability-selected, and filler
cartons enter with different selection functions \citep{dr20} --- so all fractions here
describe \emph{this targeted sample}, not a homogeneous quasar population; the
hemisphere comparison inherits any north/south difference in targeting mix, a caveat
carried explicitly through Section~\ref{sec:contrast}.

\subsection{VLASS (north)}

The northern radio catalog is the CIRADA VLASS Quick Look component list
\citep{gordon2021}: epoch 2 with the recommended quality cuts (\code{Duplicate\_flag} $<$
2, \code{Quality\_flag} $\in \{0,4\}$, non-empty islands), and the epoch-3 interim
component lists with their binary quality flag. VLASS observes at 2--4\,GHz (3\,GHz
effective) with a 2.5\arcsec\ beam; the per-epoch catalog reliability threshold is
$\sim$\drSlimVlass\,mJy \citep{gordon2021}.

\subsection{RACS (south)}

The southern radio catalog is the RACS-low DR1 Stokes-I source list
\citep[887.5\,MHz, 25\arcsec\ beam;][]{mcconnell2020, hale2021}, retrieved from the CASDA
TAP service in declination strips (\drRacsSrc\ sources; the survey covers $-80^\circ
\lesssim \delta \le +30^\circ$, with the published Galactic-plane cut). \citet{hale2021}
quote a 95\% point-source completeness of $\sim$\drSlimRacs\,mJy from source-count modeling and a
more conservative $\sim$5\,mJy from injection simulations; I adopt \drSlimRacs\,mJy as
the primary limit and repeat the luminosity-matched comparison at \drSlimRacsCons\,mJy
(Section~\ref{sec:contrast}). The band $-40^\circ < \delta \le +30^\circ$ is covered by
both surveys and provides an internal cross-check.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Matching and the measured false-match rate}

Quasars are matched to the nearest radio component with \code{astropy}
\citep{astropy}: at 2.5\arcsec\ against VLASS (matched to its beam and astrometry) and
5\arcsec\ against RACS (whose 25\arcsec\ beam implies few-arcsecond positional scatter for
faint sources). The chance-coincidence rate is measured, not modeled: each of ten trials
displaces every quasar position by an independently drawn 5--30\arcmin\ offset and repeats the
match; the mean shifted match fraction (\drNorthFmPct\% VLASS E2, \drSouthFmPct\% RACS
deep south) is subtracted from every raw fraction. The synthetic-survey test suite
verifies that this procedure recovers injected counterpart fractions to within 3 percentage
points of truth --- a check on the pipeline logic, not on the precision quoted here.

\subsection{Radio-targeted cartons as a positive control}
\label{sec:cartons}

DR20 open-fiber cartons prefixed \code{openfibertargets\_bhm\_racsradio} and
\code{...\_lofarradio} hold fibers because a radio source was already known there. They are
excluded from all census fractions. As a control they are used twice. Matched against
the survey that selected them (RACS-selected cartons vs.\ RACS-low), they recover at
\drCartonRacsPct\% ($\drCartonRacsN$ objects in RACS sky) --- the expected near-complete
recovery, with the shortfall consistent with positional scatter of a 25\arcsec-beam
catalog against a 5\arcsec\ radius and catalog-version differences between the targeting
list and DR1. Matched \emph{across} frequency, the same classes recover far less often. The pattern
does not obviously track the size of the frequency jump --- the 144\,MHz class recovers at
least as well at 3\,GHz as at 888\,MHz, though on samples small enough that the two rates
differ by only about $1\sigma$ --- which is at least consistent with survey depth mattering
as much as the spectral lever: VLASS reaches \drSlimVlass\,mJy against RACS's
\drSlimRacs\,mJy. These are recovery rates across surveys differing in \emph{both} frequency
and depth, and this census cannot separate the two terms: LOFAR-selected (144\,MHz) cartons recover at only \drCartonLofarPct\%
in RACS at 888\,MHz and \drCartonVlassLofarPct\% in VLASS at 3\,GHz, while RACS-selected
(888\,MHz) cartons recover at \drCartonVlassRacsPct\% in VLASS. (Only the highest-priority
\code{FIRSTCARTON} is recorded per object; any radio-targeted object entering under a
different first carton would evade the exclusion, a residual leak bounded by the small
carton counts.) These are not same-object measurements: the 888\,MHz leg is restricted to
$\delta \le +30^\circ$ and is a small, more southerly subset of the sky covered by the
3\,GHz leg, and the carton samples are small enough ($n$ of order tens for the RACS legs)
that the two rates are consistent within their binomial uncertainties. They bound how far
steep-spectrum selection thins out toward higher frequency, and calibrate expectations for
any cross-survey census; they do not resolve a frequency dependence.

\subsection{Measuring the spectral index}
\label{sec:method-alpha}

The overlap band ($-40^\circ < \delta \le +30^\circ$) is inside both surveys' footprints, so
a quasar detected in each yields $\alpha = \log(S_\mathrm{VLASS}/S_\mathrm{RACS}) /
\log(\nu_\mathrm{VLASS}/\nu_\mathrm{RACS})$ with no assumption at all. Each survey is matched
at its own radius (\drAlphaVlassRad\arcsec\ for VLASS's \drAlphaVlassBeam\arcsec\ beam,
\drAlphaRacsRad\arcsec\ for RACS's \drAlphaRacsBeam\arcsec), the radio-targeted cartons are
excluded as everywhere else, and VLASS peak fluxes are taken as the brightest across the
epoch catalogs --- the same construction the northern census uses.

Requiring a detection in \emph{both} surveys truncates the distribution, and it does so
asymmetrically: VLASS is the deeper survey in the sense that matters here, so a steep source
sitting at the RACS limit has already dropped below the VLASS limit by 3\,GHz and is absent,
while a flat source of the same 888\,MHz flux is retained. The joint sample's median is
therefore biased toward flat spectra --- an expectation the data confirm, at \drAlphaJointMed\
against \drAlphaMeas\ for the flux-complete sample. Requiring
$S_\mathrm{RACS} > \drAlphaComplMjy$\,mJy $= S^\mathrm{VLASS}_\mathrm{lim}
(\nu_\mathrm{VLASS}/\nu_\mathrm{RACS})^{1.5}$ removes the truncation for every
$\alpha \ge -1.5$, at the cost of \drAlphaTruncPct\% of the sample.

Three checks bound how far that median can be trusted, and all three move it. \emph{(a) The
completeness floor is an assumption, not a fact.} The cut above removes truncation only for
$\alpha \ge -1.5$, and the sample's own 16th percentile (\drAlphaMeasPlo) is steeper than
that, so for the steepest sixth the truncation is still active. Re-cutting at
$\alpha \ge -2$ ($S_\mathrm{RACS} > \drAlphaFloorBCut$\,mJy) and $\alpha \ge -2.5$
($> \drAlphaFloorCCut$\,mJy) gives medians of \drAlphaFloorB\ and \drAlphaFloorC: the median
steepens monotonically as the floor is lowered, so \drAlphaMeas\ is an \emph{upper bound on
flatness} rather than an unbiased value. \emph{(b) The VLASS flux is the brightest across the
epoch catalogs}, which is correct for a detection census and biased high for a flux ratio ---
both noise and genuine variability push a maximum up, and 3\,GHz variability is largest for
compact flat-spectrum sources, so the bias is flatward. Single-epoch medians are
\drAlphaEpochA\ and \drAlphaEpochB, bracketing the max-of-epochs value. \emph{(c) The index
depends on flux.} In $S_\mathrm{RACS}$ bins the median runs \drAlphaBinA, \drAlphaBinB\ and
\drAlphaBinC\ from the faintest to the brightest, so a single number is not transferable
across the flux range --- and the flux-complete sample is only \drAlphaComplFracPct\% of the
band, selected well above the threshold regime where the K-correction actually decides
detections. The span of the median across all three checks is \drAlphaSysSpan, and it is
quoted as the systematic in Section~\ref{sec:contrast}.

A fourth systematic is identified but not quantified here: both fluxes are \emph{peak}
fluxes, from a 2.5\arcsec\ beam and a 25\arcsec\ one. Structure on intermediate scales costs
the VLASS peak more than the RACS peak, and RACS blending of neighbours adds flux at
888\,MHz, both biasing $\alpha$ steep; a 10\% error in the flux ratio is 0.08 in $\alpha$.
Repeating with integrated fluxes, or restricting to sources unresolved in both, would settle
it and is left for future work.

Two limits of the measurement should also be stated. It is a property of the
radio-\emph{detected} quasars: the undetected majority may have a different index
distribution, and nothing here constrains it. And it is measured entirely inside the overlap
band --- the deep-south census has no VLASS coverage and never can, so applying this index
south of $-40^\circ$ is an assumption that no existing survey pair can test. And the population is broad --- standard deviation \drAlphaMeasSd, 16th--84th
percentiles \drAlphaMeasPlo\ to \drAlphaMeasPhi\ --- so applying one index to every quasar is
a population-level approximation whose error is \emph{not} the error on the median.

The breadth of that approximation is therefore tested rather than assumed away, by giving
each quasar its own index drawn from the measured distribution and recomputing. (The draw is
independent of flux and redshift, so this bounds the effect of the distribution's width, not
of any correlation between index and flux --- which check (c) above shows is present.) The right statistic here is a
bias, not a variance: because the fraction averages over $\sim$$10^5$ objects, the spread
across realizations is only \drPsRealSd\ percentage points and would make any breadth of index
distribution look harmless. What the scatter does instead is shift the answer, because the
detection criterion is a threshold and a threshold is not linear in $\alpha$, so the steep and
flat halves do not cancel. The shift is substantial for each fraction on its own ---
\drLumNorthPsPct\% and \drLumSouthPsPct\% with per-source indices, against
\drLumNorthMeasPct\% and \drLumSouthMeasPct\% with the single median --- but it is nearly
common-mode, so the \emph{gap} moves by \drGapPsShiftPp\ percentage points, to \drGapPsPp.
The contrast survives the scatter; the absolute fractions above the common limit do not, and
should be read as single-index quantities.

\subsection{Common-luminosity-limit comparison}
\label{sec:method-lum}

Raw fractions from surveys at different frequencies and depths are not comparable. For the
contrast I convert each survey's flux limit (VLASS \drSlimVlass\,mJy, the catalog
reliability threshold; RACS \drSlimRacs\,mJy, the 95\% completeness limit) to a rest-frame
1.4\,GHz luminosity limit at each quasar's redshift, assuming $S_\nu \propto \nu^{-0.7}$
and Planck~2018 cosmology \citep{planck18}, and count a quasar as radio-detected only if
its matched counterpart's peak flux converts to a luminosity above the \emph{common}
(maximum) limit of the two surveys at its redshift. The spectral-index assumption is the
weakest link and is discussed with the result.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Northern census (VLASS)}

Of \drNorthCensus\ census quasars at $\delta > -40^\circ$: \drNorthEtwoPct\% have an epoch-2
counterpart and \drNorthEthreePct\% an epoch-3 counterpart after chance-rate subtraction;
\drNorthAnyPct\% match in at least one epoch. The detection fraction is elevated in the
lowest bin ($z<0.5$, where host-galaxy emission and low-luminosity AGN enter), declines
gently across $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 2.5$ (4.6\% to 4.1\%, a trend well outside the
$\sim$0.1\,pp binomial errors), and rises to $\sim$7\% beyond
(Figure~\ref{fig:fractions}, left) --- consistent with the flux-limited selection of both
catalogs rather than any claimed evolution.

\subsection{Southern census (RACS): the first of its kind}

Of \drSouthCensus\ deep-south quasars --- all du~Pont/LCO observations, constituting the
first SDSS spectroscopic quasar sample in this sky --- \drSouthMatched\ have a RACS-low
counterpart within 5\arcsec: \drSouthPct\% after subtracting the measured \drSouthFmPct\%
chance rate. This is, to my knowledge, the first radio-counterpart measurement for any
southern SDSS spectroscopic sample, and the headline number of this census. In the
dual-coverage band the RACS-based fraction is \drOverlapPct\%, against \drNorthAnyPct\%
from VLASS in the north --- same order, with the residual carrying frequency and depth
differences whose dominant term is bounded next.

\subsection{The luminosity-matched contrast}
\label{sec:contrast}

Above the common rest-1.4\,GHz luminosity limit (Section~\ref{sec:method-lum}) the
fractions are \drLumNorthPct\% (north, VLASS) versus \drLumSouthPct\% (deep south, RACS);
the overlap band gives \drOverlapLumPct\% above the same common limit. With the
conservative \drSlimRacsCons\,mJy RACS completeness limit instead, the fractions shift to
\drLumNorthConsPct\% versus \drLumSouthConsPct\%. That variant does \emph{not} test the
asymmetry: the common limit is the larger of the two surveys' luminosity limits,
which is the RACS one in both legs, so raising it rescales north and south identically: the
north/south ratio moves only from \drRatioFid\ to \drRatioCons, a shift of 0.3\%.

\textbf{The spectral index is measured, not assumed.} The K-correction is the only thing
making a 3\,GHz and an 888\,MHz survey comparable, and it is the contrast's leading
systematic: sweeping $\alpha$ from \drAlphaHi\ to \drAlphaLo\ moves the northern fraction
over \drLumNorthFlatPct--\drLumNorthSteepPct\% while the southern fraction stays at
\drLumSouthFlatPct\% until the very steep end (where it falls to \drLumSouthSteepPct\%,
because the VLASS limit overtakes the RACS one near $\alpha \simeq -0.9$ and begins to bind
the southern leg too). That sweep spans \drGapFlatPp--\drGapSteepPp\ percentage points ---
a factor of seven, which would leave the comparison uninterpretable.

It need not be assumed. The overlap band is covered by \emph{both} surveys, so a quasar
detected twice yields a two-point spectral index directly (Section~\ref{sec:method-alpha}).
Of \drAlphaBandN\ band quasars, \drAlphaJointN\ are detected by both VLASS and RACS. That
joint-detection sample is truncated --- at the RACS limit a steep source has already fallen
below the VLASS limit at 3\,GHz --- so its median, \drAlphaJointMed, is biased flat.
Above $S_\mathrm{RACS} = \drAlphaComplMjy$\,mJy the truncation cannot operate for any
$\alpha \ge -1.5$, and the \drAlphaMeasN\ quasars in that flux-complete sample give

\begin{equation}
\alpha = \drAlphaMeasFull \pm \drAlphaMeasSeFull\ \mathrm{(stat)} \pm \drAlphaSysSpan\ \mathrm{(sys)},
\end{equation}

the median with its bootstrap standard error and a systematic term that dominates it by more
than an order of magnitude. The systematic is not an estimate --- it is the span of the median
across three checks that were run because each could have failed, and each did move it
(Section~\ref{sec:method-alpha}): the assumed completeness floor, the choice of VLASS epoch,
and the flux range the index is measured over. The measurement is therefore best read as
$\alpha \simeq -0.7$ to $-0.9$: consistent with the canonical $-0.7$, and firmly excluding the
flat spectrum that would close the gap. Evaluating the comparison at the
measured index gives \drLumNorthMeasPct\% (north) against \drLumSouthMeasPct\% (south), a gap
of \drGapMeasPp\ percentage points, and propagating the $\pm1\sigma$ index moves that gap only
over \drGapMeasLoPp--\drGapMeasHiPp, and giving every quasar its own index rather than the
median moves it to \drGapPsPp\ (Section~\ref{sec:method-alpha}). The seven-fold sweep is
therefore \emph{not} the uncertainty on this measurement; it is the range the measurement
excludes.

Measuring the index closes that axis but does not make the gap a hemispheric result. Two
survey-side effects act in the same direction and are not separated here: RACS's
25\arcsec\ beam blends and shifts faint-source positions relative to a 5\arcsec\ radius (the
same effect visible in the \drCartonRacsPct\% positive control), and the BHM targeting mix
differs by hemisphere. The overlap band points the same way --- RACS recovers
\drOverlapLumPct\% there against VLASS's \drLumNorthPct\% --- though the VLASS figure is for
the full northern census, \drNorthOutsideRacsPct\% of which lies north of the RACS
footprint, so that is not a strictly matched-sky comparison either.

A third effect is larger than any of these, and unlike the spectral index it cannot be
measured away. The two surveys' quoted limits are not the same kind of number: VLASS's
\drSlimVlass\,mJy is a per-epoch \emph{reliability} threshold and RACS's \drSlimRacs\,mJy is
a 95\% \emph{completeness} limit, so at the measured index the north is cut at
\drNorthEffCut\,mJy against the south's \drSlimRacs\,mJy. Raising the VLASS limit to
\drSlimVlassConsA\ and \drSlimVlassConsB\,mJy --- the mirror of the conservative RACS
variant, and the side that can actually move the ratio --- takes the gap from \drGapMeasPp\
to \drGapVlassConsA\ and \drGapVlassConsB\ percentage points, a reduction of
\drGapVlassDropPct\%. The contrast is therefore materially but not wholly a consequence of
how the two surveys' limits are defined: it shrinks by more than a quarter under a
like-for-like limit and does not vanish. I therefore
report the gap as a measured quantity of this catalog--survey pairing at a stated pair of
limits, and explicitly do \emph{not} claim a hemispheric difference in quasar radio
properties.

\section{Limitations}

The census inherits the BHM selection function, which is neither flux-complete nor uniform
across the sky; fractions here are properties of the DR20 BHM quasar catalog, not of
quasars. The luminosity matching applies a single spectral index to a population whose measured index
has a standard deviation of \drAlphaMeasSd. Measuring the index removed the dominant
assumption but did not make the comparison assumption-free, and the residual terms are
unequal in size. The statistical error on the median moves the contrast by
\drGapMeasLoPp--\drGapMeasHiPp\ percentage points and the population scatter by
\drGapPsShiftPp\ --- both small. The systematic on the index itself is \drAlphaSysSpan, an
order of magnitude larger, and a fourth term (peak fluxes across a 2.5\arcsec\ and a
25\arcsec\ beam) is identified but unquantified. Larger than any of these is the definitional
asymmetry between the two surveys' limits, which alone accounts for \drGapVlassDropPct\% of
the gap. The scatter is also \emph{not} negligible for the fractions individually, which fall
by \drLumNorthMeasPct--\drLumNorthPsPct\ and \drLumSouthMeasPct--\drLumSouthPsPct\ percentage
points under per-source indices; it is the difference that is robust. Finally, the index is a
property of the radio-detected, comparatively bright quasars in the overlap band alone: the
undetected majority may differ, and the deep-south census --- the paper's headline sample ---
has no VLASS coverage at all, so no existing survey pair can test the transfer there. The VLASS epoch catalogs
carry known quick-look astrometric and flux caveats \citep{gordon2021}; the RACS DR1
Galactic-plane cut removes $|b| \lesssim \drRacsPlaneCutDeg^\circ$ sky from the southern
leg, and the southern census is a declination cut that does \emph{not} exclude that hole:
\drSouthUncovN\ quasars (\drSouthUncovPct\%) sit in sky RACS-low DR1 never surveyed and
enter the denominator as guaranteed non-detections, biasing the southern fraction low by
that amount. None lie below the survey's $-85^\circ$ declination floor. The bias is a
factor $\sim$50 smaller than the fraction itself and does not affect any comparison here,
but it is a bound rather than an assumption. Neither leg
is a matched-sky sample of the other: \drNorthOutsideRacsPct\% of the northern census lies
north of the RACS footprint and is unobservable from the southern survey at any depth, so
the north/south comparison is between two differently-shaped sky regions and not between
two views of the same one. The
\drCartonRacsPct\% positive control (rather than $\sim$100\%) bounds the combined effect
of positional scatter and targeting-list/DR1 version differences at about one part in
nine for the RACS leg. Quasars outside the $0 \le z < 6$ binning range (\drOutZNorthPct\%/\drOutZSouthPct\% of the northern/southern census)
enter the raw fractions but not the redshift-binned or luminosity-matched ones. None of
these affect the categorical claim: the southern census
existed for no earlier SDSS release, and its detection fraction is now measured.

\section{Summary}

(1) First radio cross-match of any SDSS-V BHM quasar catalog: \drNorthAnyPct\% of
\drNorthCensus\ northern DR20 quasars have a VLASS quick-look counterpart
(\drNorthEtwoPct\%/\drNorthEthreePct\% per epoch, corrected by a measured chance rate).
(2) First radio census of the first southern SDSS quasar spectra: \drSouthPct\% of
