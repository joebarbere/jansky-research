\title{Long-Period Transients Are Caught in a Few Per Cent of ASKAP Snapshots, and Only One
in Ten Has an Ephemeris Good Enough to Say Why}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Forced Stokes-V photometry at \ldNSources\ long-period transient (LPT) positions across
\ldNEpochs\ archival ASKAP/VAST snapshots constrains the per-snapshot probability of catching
a pulse, with a sensitivity-weighted denominator. Three sources with detections give
\ldPMin--\ldPMax; the remaining valid limits span \ldLimMin--\ldLimMax\ at an assumed 5 mJy
pulse. The quantity is the product of the active fraction and the in-period duty cycle, which
separates only with a published ephemeris: \ldNNoEpoch\ of \ldNSources\ papers publish none,
and the split is available for one source, which gives an active fraction of \ldFActive.
\end{abstract}

\section{}

Long-period transients pulse on minute-to-hour periods and are frequently silent: GPM
J1839$-$10 nulls for 50--70\% of its cycles \citep{hurleywalker2023}, and the accreting
white-dwarf binary ASKAP J174508.9$-$505149 ``turns off for several hours at a time''
\citep{rose2026}. These statements are per-source, from heterogeneous campaigns. No uniform
measurement across the class exists; the field review notes that survey selection functions
``require explicit modeling before population analysis is possible'' \citep{rea2026}.

We measure one from archival data alone. A snapshot of length $T$ on a source of period $P$
whose emitting phase has width $w$ catches a pulse with probability
$p = f_{\rm active}\,(w+T)/P$ when the source is active. Detection counts constrain the
product, not its factors. Using forced Stokes-V photometry at the catalogued positions in
\ldNEpochs\ VAST snapshots covering \ldNSources\ LPTs, we count a detection where
$|V|/\sigma_V \ge 5$ and $|V|$ exceeds the on-axis leakage floor, matching
\citet{barbere2026lptv}.

The denominator is not the number of epochs. A pulse of flux $S$ is undetectable in an epoch
whose noise is comparable to it, so each epoch contributes its own detection probability
$\Phi(S/\sigma_V - 5)$ and the exposure is their sum. At the observed pulse brightnesses this
removes roughly half the nominal exposure; using an epoch count instead would halve every
$p$. Efficiencies below $10^{-3}$ are set to zero, because summing Gaussian tails over many
hopeless epochs otherwise manufactures sensitivity that does not exist. Upper limits are the
exact one-sided 95\% Poisson values.

Three sources yield detections, giving $p = \ldPMin$--$\ldPMax$. For scale, a $\sim$730~s
snapshot on a 2656~s period covers $\sim$27\% of a cycle, so a permanently active source with
a broad pulse would sit near 0.3; the measurements are an order of magnitude below that.
Sources without detections give 95\% limits of $\ldLimMin$--$\ldLimMax$ at an assumed 5~mJy
pulse. Every limit is a function of that assumed brightness and is tabulated against it in
the committed evidence.

The binomial treatment assumes snapshots sample pulse phase independently, and VAST's
fortnightly cadence is not random with respect to any LPT period. Testing this requires no
ephemeris, since clustering is invariant under a phase shift. Of \ldNSources\ sources,
\ldNClustered\ shows non-uniform phase sampling (Kuiper $V = 0.275$, Bonferroni-corrected
$p = 0.012$) and its constraint is therefore void, and \ldNInconclusive\ has a period quoted
too coarsely for the test to run. \ldNValid\ constraints stand.

Separating $f_{\rm active}$ from the in-period duty cycle requires physical phase, and here
the published record, not the data, is the limit. \ldNNoEpoch\ of \ldNSources\ discovery
papers tabulate no reference epoch at all --- GPM J1839$-$10 knows its period to
$2\times10^{-4}$~s over a 34-year baseline and still publishes no PEPOCH. Assuming an epoch
would manufacture the phase. Of the \ldNPhaseUsable\ sources with one, ASKAP
J175534.9$-$252749.1 admits the split: \ldFActiveOn\ of \ldFActiveN\ snapshots overlap its
pulse window and one contains a detection, giving $f_{\rm active} = \ldFActive$. ASKAP
J1832$-$0911's only detection falls at phase 0.699, which shows that its published period
epoch is not a pulse arrival, so its split is not available; fitting the phase to that
detection and then deriving $f_{\rm active}$ from it would be circular. ASKAP
J183950.5$-$075635 requires a two-component model for its published interpulse, and the
machinery reproduces the phases 0.489 and 0.951 reported independently by
\citet{barbere2026lptv}.

The measurement is limited by ephemerides rather than by sensitivity or epochs. A published
reference epoch, at the cost of one table row, would convert most of these products into
activity fractions.
