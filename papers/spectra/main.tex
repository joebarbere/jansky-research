\title{An Ultra-Steep-Spectrum Cut on TGSS~ADR1~$\times$~NVSS is Neither Pure Nor Complete:
Selection on a Noisy Index and Matched-Sensitivity Truncation, Measured}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Ultra-steep-spectrum (USS) radio sources are an efficient way to find high-redshift radio galaxies
\citep{debreuck2000}. We built a small, tested, CPU-only tool that cross-matches the TGSS~ADR1
150~MHz \citep{intema2017} and NVSS 1.4~GHz \citep{condon1998} surveys, computes two-point spectral
indices, and selects USS candidates ($\alpha<\usThreshold$). In a \usRadius$^\circ$ field at
$(\usFieldRa^\circ, +\usFieldDec^\circ)$ it matches \usNmatched\ sources (median
$\alpha=\usAlphaMedian$) and flags \usNuss\ raw candidates. Scoring the whole field against the
\citet{degasperin2018} TGSS$\times$NVSS spectral-index catalogue --- built from the same
uncorrected ADR1 fluxes, so the comparison measures matching and selection, not the flux scale ---
shows no population offset ($\Delta\alpha=+\usRealDeltaAlpha\pm\usRealDeltaAlphaSE$ over
\usRealNPairs\ detection pairs, identical on both sides of the rescaled-coverage declination edge).
The candidates nonetheless sit $\Delta\alpha=\usRealFlaggedOffset$ below their reference values,
of which a zero-free-parameter model of selection on the noisy index predicts
$\usRealSelPred\pm\usRealSelPredSD$: the offset is the selection, not a calibration. Against the
reference's own USS population the raw cut is \usRealPurityPct\% pure
(\usRealNFlaggedRefUss\ of \usNuss) and \usRealCompletenessPct\% complete
(\usRealNRefUssRec\ of \usRealNRefUss), and the incompleteness is structural: a joint detection is
equally sensitive only down to $\alpha\approx\usAlphaMatch$, and flagging a source at
$\alpha=\usAlphaProbe$ requires $S_{150}\geq\usSNeededProbe$~mJy. A catalogue-level USS cut from
this survey pair is unreliable in both directions; we report the measurement as the cautionary
result, with every number pipeline-committed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The radio spectral index $\alpha$ ($S_\nu\propto\nu^{\alpha}$) is a cheap, powerful discriminant:
the steepest-spectrum sources ($\alpha<\usThreshold$) are preferentially high-redshift radio galaxies
\citep{debreuck2000, saxena2018}. Pairing a low-frequency survey (TGSS~ADR1 at 147.5~MHz;
\citealt{intema2017}) with a 1.4~GHz survey (NVSS; \citealt{condon1998}) makes USS selection
straightforward in principle. We built a reproducible tool to do it and asked whether a blind
catalogue-level run produces a candidate list one should believe. The answer, measured against the
1.4-million-source reference catalogue of \citet{degasperin2018}, is no --- and the two reasons are
not the one we first suspected.

\section{Method and data}
\code{spectra} is a pure-NumPy/astropy module (on the \code{jansky} helpers; \citealt{jansky}). It
positionally cross-matches TGSS and NVSS catalog sources within $15\arcsec$, computes the two-point
index
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{150}^{1400} = \frac{\ln(S_{147.5}/S_{1400})}{\ln(147.5/1400)},
\end{equation}
and flags $\alpha<\usThreshold$ as USS. We ran it on a \usRadius$^\circ$ cone centred at
$(\alpha,\delta)=(\usFieldRa^\circ,+\usFieldDec^\circ)$, a high-latitude extragalactic field near
the North Galactic Pole; the expected number of chance coincidences at this match radius is
\usChanceMatches\ (committed, not asserted). Every routine is tested on a synthetic field with
injected indices --- including sources injected \emph{at} the USS boundary, where the selection
operates --- so the suite runs offline: on the fixture the raw cut recovers the injected USS
population at purity \usSynCutPurity\ and completeness \usSynCutCompleteness\ under the fixture's
small, well-behaved errors, which is precisely why the real-sky failure below is informative: what
breaks it is the real error and sensitivity structure, not the arithmetic.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{The population statistics are consistent.} The run matches \usNmatched\ sources with a
median $\alpha=\usAlphaMedian$ (Figure~\ref{fig:hist}), consistent with the typical $-0.7$ to
$-0.8$ of flux-limited extragalactic radio samples and with the weighted mean of the
\citet{degasperin2018} catalogue. This is a weak check --- a median is insensitive to everything
this paper goes on to measure --- so we treat it as consistency, not validation.
\paragraph{Raw USS candidates.} \usNuss\ sources fall below the $\alpha<\usThreshold$ cut (steepest
$\alpha=\usAlphaMin$; Figure~\ref{fig:scatter}). None has a measured redshift or high-$z$
classification in NED or SIMBAD, i.e.\ candidates, not confirmations.

\section{Scoring the field against the reference catalogue}
The reference is \citet{degasperin2018}, the 1.4-million-source TGSS$\times$NVSS spectral-index
catalogue. One point must be stated first: that catalogue is built from the \emph{same uncorrected
TGSS~ADR1 fluxes} as this tool --- its authors bound the effect of the \citet{hurleywalker2017}
flux-scale scatter on their indices at $\sim$0.06 (their \S4) --- so a raw-minus-reference
difference is structurally incapable of measuring the TGSS flux scale, which is common to both
sides. What it does measure is matching, deblending, and selection. We therefore score the
\emph{whole field}, not just the candidates, and commit the comparison
(\code{results/uss\_reference\_check.csv}; Figure~\ref{fig:compare}).

\paragraph{No population offset.} Over the \usRealNPairs\ sources whose nearest reference entry is
a genuine two-survey detection (\code{Scode=S}), the mean raw-minus-reference offset is
$+\usRealDeltaAlpha\pm\usRealDeltaAlphaSE$ --- zero --- with per-source scatter \usRealScatter,
matching the combined formal errors (\usRealScatterExp). Split at the $+30^\circ$ declination edge
of the rescaled-TGSS coverage, the two sides are indistinguishable
($+\usRealDecLeMean\pm\usRealDecLeSE$ vs.\ $+\usRealDecGtMean\pm\usRealDecGtSE$): there is no
declination-edge effect in this field, and no mechanism for one --- neither catalogue applies the
rescaling.

\paragraph{The candidate offset is selection, not calibration.} The \usNuss\ raw candidates sit
below their reference values by $\Delta\alpha=\usRealFlaggedOffset$ (over the detection rows; one
candidate's reference entry is a non-detection limit, on which more below). An earlier draft of
this work attributed exactly this offset to the TGSS flux scale. The population statistics above
refute that, and a zero-free-parameter model supplies the real cause: treating the reference
indices as truth and re-selecting on truth $+$ each source's own measurement noise predicts a mean
offset of $\usRealSelPred\pm\usRealSelPredSD$ for the selected set --- selection on the extreme of
a noisy variable produces most of the observed offset with no calibration error anywhere. This is
committed as \code{selection\_bias\_mc} and is the number any candidate-list offset must be
compared against before invoking a systematic.

\paragraph{One ``reference'' row is not a measurement.} One of the \usNuss\ candidates
(\usRealNFlaggedLimit\ row) matches a reference entry with \code{Scode=L}: a TGSS non-detection
limit with $e_\alpha=0$. That entry is not a spectral-index measurement, and it disagrees with our
own significant ADR1 detection at the same position; we exclude it from every mean (including it
would steepen the candidate offset to \usRealFlaggedOffsetAll) and flag it in the committed table.

\paragraph{The cut is neither pure nor complete.} Scored against the reference's own USS
population in the same cone (its \code{Scode=S} entries with $\alpha<\usThreshold$): of our
\usNuss\ candidates, \usRealNFlaggedRefUss\ is USS in the reference (purity \usRealPurityPct\%);
of the \usRealNRefUss\ genuine reference USS sources in the field, the raw cut recovers
\usRealNRefUssRec\ (completeness \usRealCompletenessPct\%). The incompleteness is structural, not
statistical. A source enters the matched sample only if detected in \emph{both} surveys; with the
documented survey floors (NVSS $\sim$2.5~mJy, TGSS~ADR1 $\sim$24.5~mJy) the pair is equally
sensitive only down to $\alpha\approx\usAlphaMatch$, and for anything steeper NVSS is the limiting
survey: to be flagged at the cut requires $S_{150}\geq\usSNeededThr$~mJy, and at
$\alpha=\usAlphaProbe$ (our steepest candidate) $S_{150}\geq\usSNeededProbe$~mJy --- a flux
exceeded by only a fraction \usFracAboveSNeeded\ of the matched sample. USS sources are truncated
out of a joint TGSS$\times$NVSS detection by construction.

\section{Limitations}
(i)~The TGSS~ADR1 flux-scale term of \citet{hurleywalker2017} is an approximately zero-mean
position-dependent \emph{scatter} of $\sim$15--17\%, contributing $\sim$0.06--0.07 to individual
indices --- it broadens the per-source comparison (our measured scatter \usRealScatter\ matches the
formal errors) but does not systematically steepen a field, and this field shows no offset.
(ii)~At $15\arcsec$ the expected number of chance coincidences is \usChanceMatches\ across the
matched sample; the tightest USS matches are robust, the loosest less so. (iii)~A two-point index
cannot distinguish a genuine spectral turnover from a calibration or resolution artifact; the
\usNinverted\ apparently inverted-spectrum sources are not peaked-spectrum claims and would need a
third frequency. (iv)~The purity/completeness score treats the reference catalogue's indices as
truth; they carry their own errors, so a candidate within $\sim$1$\sigma$ of the cut can land on
either side of it in either catalogue.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The honest outcome is a negative, cautionary result, sharper than the one an earlier draft of this
work claimed. There is no evidence that the TGSS flux scale biases this field's indices --- the
population offset against the reference is zero and the declination-edge mechanism is refuted by
measurement. What actually breaks a raw TGSS$\times$NVSS USS candidate list is (a) selection on
the extreme of a noisy index, which manufactures a spurious steepening of the selected few that a
committed zero-free-parameter model reproduces, and (b) matched-sensitivity truncation, which
removes genuine USS sources from the joint sample by construction. Measured against the reference
catalogue's own USS population, the cut is \usRealPurityPct\% pure and \usRealCompletenessPct\%
complete: unreliable in both directions. What survives is an open, tested tool, the committed
whole-field comparison, and the two mechanisms, quantified. The analysis regenerates from a clean
checkout (\code{python -m jansky\_research.spectra --ra \usFieldRa\ --dec \usFieldDec\ --radius
\usRadius}), and every number traces to the pipeline-written macros
\code{\textbackslash input}ted into the manuscript. We report this as a negative result, not a
discovery.
