\title{Circularly Polarized Radio Emission from an M6--M7 Dwarf in a RACS Census of Gaia Ultracool Dwarfs}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We measured Stokes~$V$ at \ucdvRealNCensus\ ultracool dwarfs from the
\citet{reyle2018} Gaia DR2 sample within \ucdvRealDmax~pc, in the RACS-low2 (887.5~MHz) and
RACS-low3 (943.5~MHz) epochs of the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey. The median $3\sigma$ limit on
$|V|$ is \ucdvRealLimit~mJy~beam$^{-1}$, and at most a fraction \ucdvRealFracFifteen\ of the
snapshots sensitive to $L_{\nu,V}=10^{15}$~erg~s$^{-1}$~Hz$^{-1}$ are that bright. One
dwarf, 2MASS~J11231964$-$0509045, an M6--M7 flare star at \ucdvRealDetDist~pc, gave the only
$5\sigma$ value, $V=+\ucdvRealDetV\pm\ucdvRealDetSigmaV$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$ in RACS-low3 with
$|V|/I\approx\ucdvRealDetFrac$. A deeper EMU observation in \ucdvRealFuBestDate\ shows $V=+\ucdvRealFuBestV\pm\ucdvRealFuBestSigmaV$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$ of the same sign, and a
matched EMU observation in \ucdvRealFuMatchYear\ shows none. The star is absent from the ASKAP
radio-star catalogues we checked.
\end{abstract}

\section{Sample and measurement}
\label{sec:method}
Ultracool-dwarf radio emission is coherent and often strongly circularly polarized, so a
Stokes~$V$ survey selects it against the unpolarized extragalactic background. The blind
VLASS search of the \citet{reyle2018} candidates at 3~GHz used Stokes~$I$ alone
\citep{perger2025}, and the RACS-low2 circular-polarization catalogue measures $V$ only at
Stokes~$I$ catalogue positions \citep{duchesne2026}. We instead measure $V$ at every dwarf.

The parent sample is the \citet{reyle2018} table of Gaia DR2 \citep{gaiadr2} candidates.
Of its \ucdvRealNReyle\ entries, \ucdvRealNBoth\ fall inside both a low2 and a low3 Stokes~$V$
image on CASDA; RACS-low1 has no $V$ products there. The fetch rate measured on
\ucdvRealSzeroN\ test pairs (\ucdvRealSzeroOk\ succeeded, \ucdvRealSzeroSec~s per pair)
projected \ucdvRealFullHours~h for the full sample; a rule fixed beforehand then set a distance
limit of \ucdvRealDmax~pc, chosen from parallax alone. For each dwarf and epoch we retrieved a
$0.06^\circ$ $I$ and $V$ cutout from one observation \citep{mcconnell2020} with
\code{astroquery} \citep{astroquery}, moved the Gaia position to the observation date with its
proper motion, and read $I$ and $V$ at the pixel containing it. The noise is the standard
deviation in an annulus of 30--90\arcsec.

A target-epoch counts as a candidate if $|V|/\sigma_V\geq5$, if $|V|/I$ exceeds the local
leakage floor when Stokes~$I$ is itself detected, and if no Stokes~$I$ source brighter than
100~mJy lies within 3\arcmin. A slot whose cutout has too few bright sources to set the floor is
held for inspection. The upper limit on $|V|$ is $|V|+3\sigma_V$.

\section{Controls}
\label{sec:controls}
The thresholds and pass criteria were fixed before any target value was examined. The checks
used the \ucdvRealSzeroN\ test pairs, whose dwarfs come from the half of the sample (split by a
hash of the Gaia identifier) that sets no threshold. Forced photometry at
\ucdvRealConeN\ blank positions 2\arcmin\ from the targets gave positive $V$ in a fraction
\ucdvRealConePos\ of cases with $\mathrm{std}(V/\sigma_V)=\ucdvRealConeStd$, as expected
for noise; a peak search instead of a fixed pixel would bias this fraction towards one. Point
sources injected into the real cutouts (\ucdvRealCtwoN\ per level) were recovered at a rate of
\ucdvRealCtwoComp\ at $7\sigma_V$, with a median recovered-to-injected flux ratio of
\ucdvRealCtwoRatio\ above $10\sigma_V$. For the \ucdvRealCthreeN\ stars of the RACS-low2
circular-polarization catalogue \citep{duchesne2026}, measured in their own observations, the
median ratio of our $V$ to the catalogue value is \ucdvRealCthreeRatio, with
\ucdvRealCthreeFlips\ sign reversals; as the catalogue comes from the same images, this tests the
pixel read against a source fit rather than the flux scale. The T8 dwarf WISE~J0623$-$0456, a known periodic emitter
outside the Gaia sample \citep{rose2023}, gives $V=\ucdvRealCthreebLowtwoV$ and
$\ucdvRealCthreebLowthreeV$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$ ($V/\sigma_V=\ucdvRealCthreebLowtwoSnr$ and
$\ucdvRealCthreebLowthreeSnr$) in low2 and low3.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
The archive refused \ucdvRealNMissing\ low3 images, so \ucdvRealNSlots\ target-epochs were
measured; \ucdvRealNInsane\ cutouts failed the noise check, leaving \ucdvRealNSane\ with limits
(median $\sigma_V=\ucdvRealSigmaV$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$). The \ucdvRealNullN\ blank-sky measurements
made alongside them give positive $V$ in a fraction \ucdvRealNullPos\ with
$\mathrm{std}(V/\sigma_V)=\ucdvRealNullStd$, but their tails are heavier than Gaussian:
\ucdvRealNullNGeFour\ reach $4\sigma$ against \ucdvRealNullExpGeFour\ expected. The fixed
two-epoch comparison flags no dwarf (\ucdvRealNPairs\ pairs), but at the median noise it can only
see changes of $\gtrsim\ucdvRealTwoEpochFloor$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$.

After seeing the data we counted a snapshot as sensitive to $L_{\nu,V}$ where $5\sigma_V$ at the
dwarf's distance reaches it. Then \ucdvRealNeffFifteen\ and \ucdvRealNeffSixteen\ snapshots are
sensitive to $10^{15}$ and $10^{16}$~erg~s$^{-1}$~Hz$^{-1}$, and the 95\% limits on the fraction
above them are \ucdvRealFracFifteen\ and \ucdvRealFracSixteen\ (Figure~\ref{fig:census}a);
weighting each snapshot by the injection completeness instead gives \ucdvRealFracCwFifteen\ at
$10^{15}$. The limits are per 15-minute snapshot, assume isotropic emission ($4\pi d^2S$), and do
not separate the fraction of emitters from their duty cycle.

One slot reached $5\sigma$ and was held, its cutout having too few bright sources to set a
leakage floor. It is 2MASS~J11231964$-$0509045 (Gaia DR2 \ucdvRealDetGaia) in RACS-low3
observation \ucdvRealDetObs\ on \ucdvRealDetDate: $V=+\ucdvRealDetV\pm\ucdvRealDetSigmaV$ and
$I=\ucdvRealDetI$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$ ($I/\sigma_I=\ucdvRealDetIsnr$), with $|V|/I=\ucdvRealDetFrac
\pm\ucdvRealDetFracErr$, far above instrumental leakage (Figure~\ref{fig:census}c). With
\ucdvRealNullNGeFive\ null value beyond $5\sigma$, about \ucdvRealChanceGeFive\ such slots are
expected from noise or unrelated sources, so this epoch is a trigger rather than a detection. Its low2 value,
$V/\sigma_V=\ucdvRealDetOtherSnr$, differs at $\ucdvRealDetPairZ\sigma$, below the fixed
comparison's threshold. The Stokes~$I$ source is catalogued, unidentified, as
\ucdvRealCatName\ \citep{thomson2026}, \ucdvRealCatOffset\arcsec\ from the star's position at
that date. The star has spectral type
M6V from a near-infrared spectrum \citep{davoudi2025} and M7 from photometry \citep{reyle2018},
and a rotation period of 0.129~d \citep{newton2018}. At $L_{\nu,V}\approx\ucdvRealDetL$
~erg~s$^{-1}$~Hz$^{-1}$ it lies below the luminosities of the fraction limits.

After the census, and outside its fixed rules, we measured the star in every ASKAP observation
of its position with $I$ and $V$ images (\ucdvRealFuNObs\ observations: \ucdvRealFuNRefused\
refused, \ucdvRealFuNNan\ off the image, \ucdvRealFuNMeas\ measured,
\ucdvRealFuFirstYear--\ucdvRealFuLastYear; VAST \citealt{murphy2021}, EMU \citealt{hopkins2025};
Figure~\ref{fig:census}b). EMU observation \ucdvRealFuBestObs\ gives
$V=+\ucdvRealFuBestV\pm\ucdvRealFuBestSigmaV$~mJy~beam$^{-1}$ ($V/\sigma_V=\ucdvRealFuBestSnr$,
$I/\sigma_I=\ucdvRealFuBestIsnr$; Figure~\ref{fig:census}d). Its Gaussian-equivalent
probability, one-sided because the census fixed the sign, is $\ucdvRealFuBestP$, or
$\ucdvRealFuBestPTrials$ for the \ucdvRealFuNIndep\ observations searched; the heavy null tail
would raise this, while \ucdvRealTailNEmu\ blank positions in the same image reach at most
$\ucdvRealTailMaxEmu\sigma$. Beam-fitted $V$ centroids lie \ucdvRealCenVRacs\arcsec\ and
\ucdvRealCenVEmu\arcsec\ from the star in the two images. EMU observation \ucdvRealFuMatchObs\
(\ucdvRealFuMatchYear, same band and depth) gives $V=\ucdvRealFuMatchV\pm\ucdvRealFuMatchSigmaV$,
\ucdvRealFuMatchZ$\sigma$ below it, so the emission is intermittent. In all,
\ucdvRealFuNPos\ of the \ucdvRealFuNIndep\ reach $2.5\sigma$, all positive and none below
$-2.5\sigma$; in \ucdvRealFuVgtIObs\ $V$ exceeds $I$, so that epoch is not a detection by itself.

The star is absent from the ASKAP radio-star and circular-polarization catalogues of
\citet{pritchard2021}, \citet{pritchard2024}, \citet{driessen2024} and \citet{duchesne2026}.
Intermittent, strongly circularly polarized emission of one sign from a rapidly rotating late-M
dwarf is consistent with electron-cyclotron maser emission \citep{hallinan2008}; coherent
emission during flares is not excluded, since the snapshots do not resolve its time structure.
