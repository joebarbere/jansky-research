\title{Two-Epoch Stokes-V Photometry of the Nearest M Dwarfs in RACS-mid:
Method, a GJ 65 Recovery, and Upper Limits}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Circular polarization is a near-unambiguous flag of coherent radio emission, but a single survey
epoch undercounts the flaring-emitter population: an emitter caught between bursts shows
nothing. We present a targeted, reproducible two-epoch Stokes-V photometry pipeline for the
nearest M dwarfs. Gaia proper motions propagate each CNS5 \citep{golovin2023} position to each
observation's epoch (Barnard's star moves $\sim$40$''$ between the epochs used, so this step is
not optional); CASDA SODA cutouts of RACS-mid \citep{duchesne2023} images at MJD $\approx$59233 and
60769 (a 4.2-yr same-band pair at 1367.5~MHz) yield $I$ and $V$ near each propagated
position. A per-epoch leakage floor against the systematic that most often masquerades as circular
polarization (off-axis
$I\rightarrow V$ leakage) is implemented and exercised in simulation, but is not
applied to the real census: the floor is defined as a multiple of the field median $|V|/I$ of
bright sources, and this sample's median $I/\sigma_I$ is 2.2, so the quantity it would
be computed from is noise- rather than leakage-dominated. GJ 65 at $|V|/I = 0.58$ is two
orders of magnitude clear of any plausible leakage, so the detection is unaffected; the
quiescent limits have not been checked against a measured leakage level. On a synthetic epoch
pair with injected steady emitters, single-epoch flares, and leakage contaminants, the
pipeline's selection separates the two injected populations perfectly (\svdComp/\svdPur). The
injected emitters sit 3.5--14$\times$ above the floor and the contaminants roughly a seventh of
it, so this result establishes that the selection arithmetic is right, not that it is sensitive
near the decision boundary. On the same injections the two-epoch step flags exactly the
flares (completeness \svdVarComp), a score measured equally far from the boundary; a fraction \svdMissFrac\ of injected emitters are invisible to any single epoch.
On the real sky (\svdRealN\ nearest M dwarfs measured; \svdRealPairs\ complete epoch pairs) the
pipeline recovers the prototype coherent emitter, the GJ 65 system (BL+UV Ceti; also in the
RACS-low2 blind V catalogue, \citealt{racs8_2026}), at $V=9.26\rightarrow7.11$~mJy between
epochs. That decline is $10\sigma$ against image noise alone, but $I$ fell by 26\% and $V$ by
23\% over the same pair, leaving the circular fraction constant to 3.4\%, which is the signature
of an epoch-to-epoch flux-scale difference rather than a change of emission state; including a
per-epoch scale term of 3--5\% \citep{duchesne2023} reduces it to 5.2--3.5$\sigma$. Every other
target is quiescent with a median $5\sigma$ $V$ limit of \svdRealVlim~mJy. This is a methods
contribution and a recovery of a known emitter, together with an upper-limit census, not a
discovery claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Blind Stokes-V searches of ASKAP surveys \citep{pritchard2021,driessen2024,racs8_2026} have
established circular polarization as the cleanest wide-field flag of coherent stellar emission.
Two things a blind single-epoch catalogue cannot provide are (i) measurements below its
extraction threshold at known stellar positions, and (ii) the time-domain behaviour of the $V$
sky. Our earlier RACS Stokes-V methods work found the honest limit of a single epoch: coherent
emission is bursty, so one epoch undercounts the population. We supply both missing
pieces for the nearest M dwarfs: two-epoch $I$ and $V$ photometry on a curated CNS5 target
list, at two RACS-mid
epochs 4.2~yr apart.

\section{Method}
Targets are the nearest CNS5 M dwarfs (red Gaia colour, Dec $<+40^\circ$, sorted by parallax).
Each position is propagated with its Gaia DR3 proper motion to each image's observation MJD.
At RACS-mid resolution this is not optional: the highest-proper-motion targets move tens of
arcseconds between the epochs. For each target and epoch we stage the RACS-mid \code{taylor.0}
restored $I$ and $V$ images via CASDA SODA, locate the brightest Stokes-I pixel within
12\arcsec\ of the propagated position and read $I$ and $V$ there, with a local-annulus rms
error, and (in simulation only, see above) apply the per-epoch leakage floor (7$\times$ the field median
$|V|/I$). Two-epoch variability uses the signed fluxes, $|V_2-V_1|/\sqrt{\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2}$,
so a handedness flip registers. The synthetic epoch-pair fixture injects steady emitters,
single-epoch flares, and leakage-only contaminants: selection completeness \svdComp, purity
\svdPur, flare-flagging completeness \svdVarComp\ and purity \svdVarPur, with \svdMissFrac\ of
emitters missed by epoch~1 alone. That missed fraction is the quantified rationale for
multi-epoch $V$ work.

\section{Results on the real sky}
Of 60 targets, \svdRealN\ returned measurements in at least one epoch and \svdRealPairs\ have
both (CASDA staging failures account for the rest, an availability, not detection, effect;
the failure list ships with the reproduction script). Exactly one system exceeds $5\sigma$ in $V$: GJ 65.
It appears as four catalogue rows (BL and UV Ceti, two epochs) whose photometry is
bit-identical, because the two CNS5 entries are 2\arcsec\ apart and fall in the same beam.
This is one independent measurement, not two: the four rows reduce to \svdRealDet\ CNS5
entries, and those two entries are a single system. It is
detected at $V=9.26\pm0.15$~mJy (MJD 59233) and $7.11\pm0.15$~mJy (MJD 60769). GJ 65 is a known
radio star (it appears in the RACS-low2 blind $V$ catalogue, \citealt{racs8_2026}), so the
recovery validates the pipeline.

The $4.2$-yr decline needs more care than the $10\sigma$ that image noise alone implies.
Stokes $I$ fell from 15.99 to 11.87~mJy ($-26$\%) while $V$ fell from 9.26 to 7.11 ($-23$\%),
so the circular fraction is constant to 3.4\% (0.579 to 0.599). A genuine change of emission
state need not preserve $V/I$; a difference in the absolute flux scale between two
independently calibrated RACS-mid observations does exactly that. Adding a per-epoch scale
term in quadrature gives $5.2\sigma$ at 3\% and $3.5\sigma$ at 5\%, against the $9.99\sigma$
from image noise. Since the committed photometry table records no tile or primary-beam
position, the systematic cannot be bounded from the shipped evidence, and the honest statement
is a marginal same-band decline consistent with, but not established above, the
epoch-to-epoch flux-scale uncertainty \citep{duchesne2023}. Every other measured target is quiescent, with a median $5\sigma$ $V$ upper limit of
\svdRealVlim~mJy. At 3~pc that is a luminosity limit at or near the lower boundary of known M-dwarf coherent-burst
luminosities ($\sim$10$^{13}$~erg\,s$^{-1}$\,Hz$^{-1}$), i.e.\ consistent with duty cycles well
below unity.

\section{Honest limits}
(i) GJ 65 is a recovery, not a discovery: every candidate here was cross-checked against the
blind catalogues before the word ``new'' could be used, and none survived that bar. (ii) Two
epochs cannot distinguish a flare from secular change; they bound variability, not its timescale.
Since UV~Cet-type bursts last minutes to hours against RACS's $\sim$15-min integrations,
both GJ~65 snapshots may themselves be burst states at different levels, and the quiescent $V$ floor
of GJ~65 is unconstrained by this dataset.
(iii) staging availability, not detection, limits the census: 35\% of targets returned no data in
any epoch and roughly half of the measured targets lack a complete second epoch. Overall
$\sim$52\% of target-epoch slots failed CASDA staging in this run; the per-row failure accounting
ships with the reproduction script. (iv) The leakage floor is a
per-field statistic; a beam-position-dependent model would sharpen faint candidates. (v) The quiescent limits are
upper limits on a beam-scale maximum, not on the star. The photometry takes the brightest
Stokes-I pixel within 12\arcsec\ rather than the pixel containing the target, and on blank sky
that returns the largest of several independent beams: all 54 quiescent rows have $I>0$, at a
median $I/\sigma_I$ of 2.2, where a genuine fixed-pixel measurement would go negative about
half the time ($p = 2^{-54}$). The $V$ value is then read wherever that maximum fell. A forced
re-measurement is the outstanding work on this census. (vi) The
quiescent limits apply at the two sampled epochs only. The M-dwarf $V$ sky remains
undersampled in time, which is precisely the case for multi-epoch targeted $V$ monitoring as VAST
and future RACS epochs accumulate.
