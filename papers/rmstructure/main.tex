\title{Noise-Debiased Rotation-Measure Structure Functions from SPICE-RACS DR1:
Method, Recover-a-Known, and the Full-Sky Disc--Halo Contrast from DR2}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The SPICE-RACS programme has multiplied the Faraday rotation-measure (RM) grid; its second data
release \citep{spiceracs_dr2} ships $\sim$3.3$\times$10$^5$ quality-flagged (goodRM) rows over
87.5\% of the sky, without a systematic Galactic structure-function analysis. We supply the
reproducible tool and its validation, led by the error model: on a synthetic screen the
sky-block jackknife SE of the latitude enhancement ratio (\rmsSynRatioJkSe) reproduces the true
field-to-field scatter over \rmsSynNReal\ realizations (\rmsSynRatioEnsSd, per-seed values
committed), where an i.i.d.\ source bootstrap claims \rmsSynRatioSe\ --- $\sim$3$\times$ too
small on a correlated field. Applied to the DR2 goodRM sample deduplicated
to one row per component (\rmsRealN\ components),
the latitude enhancement ratio --- median $|$RM$|$ at $|b|<10^\circ$ over that at $|b|>60^\circ$
--- is \rmsRealRatio$\pm$\rmsRealRatioJkSe\ (jackknife over \rmsRealJkBlocks\ $10^\circ$
blocks, growing to at most $\pm$\rmsRealRatioJkSeMax\ across block sizes of 5--30$^\circ$; the
bootstrap would claim $\pm$\rmsRealRatioSe). The statistic is definition-dependent ---
plane/pole cuts of 5$^\circ$/70$^\circ$ give \rmsRealRatioAltBins, an
$|\mathrm{RM}|<300$~rad\,m$^{-2}$ cut gives \rmsRealRatioCutThree\ --- so we quote it strictly
for its stated bins. A six-bin latitude ladder
falls monotonically from $\sigma_\mathrm{RM}=\rmsRealSigPlane$ to \rmsRealSigPole~rad\,m$^{-2}$
(a different statistic on different bins than the median ratio),
the polar endpoint (\rmsRealFloorSig$\pm$\rmsRealFloorSigJkErr) at the literature
intrinsic+extragalactic floor, licensing a first-order quadrature floor subtraction. Each
plateau is an upper bound (source-intrinsic scatter unremoved; the disc bins mix secular plane
gradients), and every ladder error is now a sky-block jackknife. A quality-flag effect
reported in an earlier version of this paper --- an unflagged high-latitude half-plateau scale
of \rmsRealBreakHiUnflagged$^\circ$ against \rmsRealBreakHiFlagM$^\circ$ flagged --- does not
survive a matched-pair comparison and is retracted: the shift is preferred in only
\rmsRealBreakFracSmall\ of shared bootstrap replicates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
RM grids sample the integral $\int n_e B_\parallel\,dl$ along every extragalactic sightline; the
spatial structure of the RM sky (how RM decorrelates with angular separation) encodes
the outer scale and amplitude of magneto-ionic turbulence \citep{haverkorn2004}, and has been
mapped at NVSS density by \citet{stil2011}, whose all-sky RM structure functions this analysis
extends to a tenfold-denser grid. Our earlier
slice measured the Taylor et al. (2009) latitude profile and the coarse net RM sign per
quadrant from 37,543 NVSS RMs \citep{taylor2009}; SPICE-RACS raises the areal density to
6.7~deg$^{-2}$ \citep{thomson2023,spiceracs_dr2}, making per-latitude-bin structure functions
newly measurable. The DR2
release paper provides the catalogue but not this analysis; we build the reproducible tool.

\section{Method and recover-a-known}
For all source pairs in angular-separation bins,
$\mathrm{SF}(\delta\theta)=\langle(\mathrm{RM}_1-\mathrm{RM}_2)^2\rangle-\langle\sigma_1^2+
\sigma_2^2\rangle$: the second term removes the measurement-noise floor that otherwise dominates
faint-grid SFs. Errors come from bootstrap resampling of sources (pairs are correlated);
pair counts are capped with the subsample fraction recorded. The central validation is of the
\emph{error model}, because the paper's headline uncertainty depends on it: on a synthetic
Gaussian-blob screen with a known coherence scale, the leave-one-sky-block-out jackknife SE of
the enhancement ratio is \rmsSynRatioJkSe, reproducing the true field-to-field scatter of
\rmsSynRatioEnsSd\ (the SD over \rmsSynNReal\ independent realizations, per-seed values
committed in \code{results/rmstructure\_synthetic.json}) to within a few percent, where the
i.i.d.\ source bootstrap on one realization claims \rmsSynRatioSe\ --- an understatement of
$\sim$3$\times$, because it resamples within correlated patches. The same fixture validates
the statistics: a pure-noise sky debiases to $\mathrm{SF}\approx0$; the screen's 5$\times$ plane
amplitude boost returns an enhancement ratio of \rmsSynRatioEns$\pm$\rmsSynRatioEnsSd\ over the
realizations, a low-$|b|$/high-$|b|$ SF plateau ratio of \rmsSynPlatRatio, and a half-plateau
break consistent with the injected scale times the Gaussian-ACF factor
$2\sqrt{\ln 2}\approx1.67$
(the binned estimator resolves this only to the log-bin width, $\sim$30\% at that scale).
Two properties of the fixture bound what this validates. The recovered ratio sits below the
injected peak boost of 5 by construction, not by failure: the boost has a 5$^\circ$-scale
Gaussian latitude profile, so the $|b|<10^\circ$ band median averages well down the profile, and
blob-scale mixing across the band edge contracts it further --- the ensemble quantifies the
machinery's \emph{response}, it is not a calibration of the sky statistic. And the fixture's
patch spans only $\pm20^\circ$, so its plane/pole cut is $|b|>15^\circ$ where the science cut is
$|b|>60^\circ$: the recover-a-known exercises the same code path, not the identical statistic.

\section{Real data: DR1, then the full-sky DR2 goodRM sample}
The first, bounded leg used the DR1 catalogue on CASDA TAP; its raw S/N cut (7{,}707 rows)
exceeds the DR1 paper's fully-flagged 5{,}818 RM measurements \citep{thomson2023} because the
leakage and Stokes-I-fit flags were not applied, a lesson promoted to the main result: the DR2
leg uses the release's own \code{goodRM} flag. The full cut cascade is committed with the
results: \rmsRealNRowsRaw\ catalogue rows, \rmsRealNAfterSnr\ at \code{snr\_polint}
$\ge 8$ (the S/N column is recorded, not resolved silently), \rmsRealNAfterFinite\ with finite
RM and positive error, \rmsRealNAfterGoodrm\ after
\code{goodRM}, deduplicated to \rmsRealN\ unique components (one row per \code{cat\_id}, keeping
the observation nearest its tile centre) --- consistent with the post-deduplication 8$\sigma$
count of $2.5\times10^5$ the release quotes in its abstract \citep{spiceracs_dr2}.
Deduplication matters beyond bookkeeping: tile-overlap repeats are the same source with
independent noise, so they inject pure noise power into the smallest SF bins, and the repeat
rows amount to 35\% of the unique-source count --- weight that lands multiply in every median.

On this sample the latitude enhancement ratio is \rmsRealRatio$\pm$\rmsRealRatioJkSe,
where the error is a leave-one-block-out jackknife over \rmsRealJkBlocks\ sky blocks of
$10^\circ$ (several coherence lengths, the exchangeable unit on a correlated field), of which
\rmsRealJkBlocksEff\ intersect the plane band or polar caps and so actually move the estimate.
The jackknife's one tuning choice is varied rather than assumed: across block sizes of
5--30$^\circ$ the SE grows to at most \rmsRealRatioJkSeMax\ (all five values committed). The
fixture arbitrates the setting: swept the same way, its jackknife SE reaches
\rmsSynRatioJkSeMax\ against a true field-to-field scatter of \rmsSynRatioEnsSd, and matches
the truth at $10^\circ$ blocks ($\approx$5 coherence lengths) --- so we quote the $10^\circ$
value as the calibrated error and \rmsRealRatioJkSeMax\ as a conservative ceiling. The
i.i.d.\ source bootstrap gives $\pm$\rmsRealRatioSe; the same understatement the synthetic
ensemble exposes on the fixture, here on the real leg. That the relative jackknife error
matches the one our NVSS slice measures on a 6.6$\times$ smaller sample ($\sim$10\% against
$\sim$11\%) is itself the signature of a field-dominated error: more sources shrink shot
noise, not the number of independent sky patches. Definitional variants, mirrored from that
slice where each was free to move the answer, are committed alongside: plane/pole cuts of
5$^\circ$/70$^\circ$ give \rmsRealRatioAltBins, an $|\mathrm{RM}|<300$~rad\,m$^{-2}$ cut gives
\rmsRealRatioCutThree, and keeping only the better half of RM errors gives \rmsRealRatioErmCut.
The ratio is roughly double the $5.4\pm0.59$ (its own sky-block jackknife) we measure from
Taylor et al.\ (2009) with the same statistic. That comparison is not directly
comparable, and both dominant differences are expected to raise the SPICE-RACS value: NVSS's
two-band $n\pi$ ambiguity caps large plane $|\mathrm{RM}|$ (deflating its plane median), and the
southern coverage adds inner-Galaxy sightlines; these systematics dominate the quoted error.
The disc/halo SF plateaus are \rmsRealPlatLo\ and \rmsRealPlatHi~rad$^2$\,m$^{-4}$ (a
fluctuation-power ratio of \rmsRealPlatRatio), and the
six-bin ladder gives $\sigma_\mathrm{RM}$ falling monotonically from \rmsRealSigPlane\
($|b|<5^\circ$) to \rmsRealSigPole~rad\,m$^{-2}$ ($|b|>50^\circ$); each plateau now carries a
sky-block jackknife error next to its bootstrap one (the bootstrap is shot-noise-only, the
lesson the headline taught), with pair counts and sampled pair fractions committed with the
results. Note the power ratio and the enhancement ratio are different statistics on different
bins --- squared dispersion in $|b|<5^\circ$ over $|b|>50^\circ$ versus median $|$RM$|$ in
$|b|<10^\circ$ over $|b|>60^\circ$ --- so their magnitudes are not mutually convertible. The
polar
endpoint, \rmsRealFloorSig$\pm$\rmsRealFloorSigJkErr~rad\,m$^{-2}$, sits at the literature floor
\citep{mao2010,taylor2009}, licensing the quadrature floor subtraction for the Galactic-only
shape; the subtraction's sensitivity to that single number is quoted in Section~4.

An earlier version of this paper claimed the quality flags were material to the SF itself:
that without \code{goodRM} the high-latitude half-plateau scale fell from
\rmsRealBreakHiFlagM$^\circ$ to \rmsRealBreakHiUnflagged$^\circ$, leakage masquerading as
small-scale RM structure. A matched-pair comparison retracts that claim. Drawing one pair set
over the $|b|>10^\circ$ subset of the unflagged sample (\rmsRealNUnflagged\ deduplicated
sources in total), with the flagged curve keeping exactly the pairs whose endpoints both pass
\code{goodRM}, reproduces the two point estimates --- but a bootstrap shared between the
curves finds the unflagged break smaller in only \rmsRealBreakFracSmall\ of replicates, and
its 68\% interval, \rmsRealBreakHiUnflagLo--\rmsRealBreakHiUnflagHi$^\circ$, spans both
values. The apparent shift is a single noisy sub-degree bin carrying an 11-valued
threshold-crossing statistic over the line, not a measured change of scale; the earlier
comparison had read breaks off two independent, uncommitted pair draws with no uncertainty
attached. Both curves, their errors, per-bin pair counts, and the bootstrap accounting are now
committed with the results.

\section{Honest limits}
(i) Each SF plateau is an upper bound on the Galactic term: source-intrinsic + extragalactic RM
scatter is unremoved, and in the disc bins the large-separation SF additionally mixes secular
plane gradients (ordered-field structure over tens of degrees), so the plane
$\sigma_\mathrm{RM}$ is an effective, not purely turbulent, amplitude. (ii) The floor
subtraction rests on one number, the polar bin's \rmsRealFloorSig$\pm$\rmsRealFloorSigJkErr\
rad\,m$^{-2}$, and by construction $\sigma_\mathrm{Gal}$ in that bin is zero: the committed
results record it as null, a definition rather than a measurement. The literature floor range
permits a genuine polar Galactic term as large as
$({\rmsRealFloorSig}^2-9^2)^{1/2}\approx8.4$~rad\,m$^{-2}$, which would bias the whole
subtracted profile low; the headline enhancement ratio involves no floor subtraction at all and
is untouched by this. The subtraction's leverage is latitude-dependent. The plane bins are
insensitive in
quadrature ($\sigma_\mathrm{Gal}$ at $|b|<5^\circ$ is \rmsRealSigGalPlane\ at the measured floor
and still \rmsRealSigGalPlaneAltFloor\ under the far more conservative floor from the
neighbouring \rmsRealAltFloorSig~rad\,m$^{-2}$ bin), but the high-$|b|$ bins are dominated by
it: at $|b|=$\rmsRealSigGalMidBin$^\circ$, $\sigma_\mathrm{Gal}=$ \rmsRealSigGalMid\ at the
measured floor and spans \rmsRealSigGalMidFloorLitHi--\rmsRealSigGalMidFloorLitLo\ if one adopts
literature floor values of 15--9 rad\,m$^{-2}$ instead \citep{mao2010}. (iii) The Taylor et al.\ (2009) ratio comparison
carries frequency, $n\pi$-handling, and sky-coverage systematics (Section 3) that dominate the
quoted errors. (iv) The half-plateau break statistic is bin-width-limited ($\sim$30\% at
these scales) and validated only against the fixture's known scale, not a turbulence model.
(v) SF bootstrap errors use a unique-set source resample (conservative by $\sim$30\%); pair
fractions are quoted per unique pair (ordered draws double-count); the headline ratio's error
is the sky-block jackknife, not a bootstrap. (vi) $n\pi$ resolution is inherited from the
upstream RM-synthesis pipeline. (vii) At DR2 scale pairs are randomly sampled rather than
exhausted (an unbiased estimator of the bin means), with the sampled fraction recorded.
(viii) The \code{goodRM} selection is itself a potentially latitude-dependent filter: it folds
in leakage and depolarization criteria that could in principle preferentially remove disc
sightlines, under-representing exactly the high-fluctuation regions the ladder measures. The
committed per-bin accounting bounds this: the flag removes
\rmsRealFlagRemPctMin--\rmsRealFlagRemPctMax\% of deduplicated sources across the six $|b|$
bins, with the maximum in the \emph{polar} bin rather than the plane --- a differential of
$\sim$1 percentage point, far too small to reorder a ladder whose neighbouring bins differ by
factors of $\sim$2.
