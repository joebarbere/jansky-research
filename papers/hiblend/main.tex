\title{An Inconclusive Two-Beam FASHI--ALFALFA Test for H\,I Beam Blending}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Beam blending should raise a galaxy's H\,I flux more in the Arecibo beam
(\hbRealBeamAlfa~arcmin) than in the FAST beam (\hbRealBeamFast~arcmin). We tested this on
\hbRealNTargets\ galaxies matched between FASHI DR2 and ALFALFA $\alpha$.100, with controls
set in advance. Neighbours too far apart in velocity to blend gave a slope of
$\hbRealBetaNull\pm\hbRealBetaNullErr$ instead of zero, so the test is inconclusive. The
calibration misfit measured on isolated galaxies does not reproduce that slope, which is
concentrated at low signal-to-noise; its cause is unknown. A second attempt stopped when its
held-out calibration gate failed on medians. Its tolerance fails a perfect calibration two
times in three, but its $\chi^2$ also failed. In post-hoc bin means the
residuals are consistent with zero by $\chi^2$. No blending slope is interpreted.
\end{abstract}

\section{Test}
\label{sec:test}
In FASHI DR2 \citep{fashi_dr2}, galaxies with another galaxy inside their flux aperture carry
more H\,I at fixed optical luminosity than isolated ones (\code{fashienv},
\citealt{janskyresearch}). The excess could be blending or real gas. \citet{haynes2018}
describe confusion in ALFALFA as relatively minor, though confused sources are not hard to
find (their arXiv version, p.~15, citing \citealt{jones2016}), and \citet{jones2015} studied it by simulation; we know of no test on the
same galaxies with two beams. FASHI DR2 gives a FAST beam of \hbRealBeamFast~arcmin at
1420~MHz (its Table~1), and \citet{giovanelli2005} an ALFA beam averaging
\hbRealBeamAlfa~arcmin (arXiv preprint, pp.~8 and 22; 3.3 by 3.8~arcmin). We use these nominal
widths; broader effective beams would change both the size and the separation dependence of
the prediction. FASHI DR2 (\hbRealNFashi\ sources with finite flux, error, velocity and width) was matched to ALFALFA $\alpha$.100 code-1
detections \citep[\hbRealNAlfalfa\ sources in all;][]{haynes2018} within 1.5~arcmin and
100~km~s$^{-1}$; shifting one catalogue by 10~arcmin gives \hbRealChance\% chance matches.

A neighbour of flux $S_n$ at separation $s_n$, overlapping in velocity, adds
$S_n\exp(-4\ln2\,s_n^2/\theta^2)$ to a beam of width $\theta$. The predicted log ratio $R$ of the
ALFA and FAST fluxes peaks at \hbRealSignal~dex for one equal-flux neighbour at 2~arcmin; its
median is \hbRealRprimMed\ (primary) and \hbRealRnullMed\ (null). The statistic is the least-squares slope $\beta$ of
the observed log flux ratio on $R$, after removing the survey-to-survey flux scale: blending
predicts $\beta\approx1$, extra gas in close pairs $\beta\approx0$. The flux scale (a function of
signal-to-noise, line width, flux and declination) was fitted on the \hbRealNIsolated\ targets
with no neighbour within 6~arcmin and applied to the \hbRealNPrimary\ with a neighbour at
1--6~arcmin in the same velocity window. Here signal-to-noise is the noise-weighted geometric
mean of the two fluxes over the FASHI flux error, not the catalogue SNR. The negative control
uses \hbRealNNull\ targets whose neighbours lie more than 600~km~s$^{-1}$ away and cannot blend;
its slope must be within $2\sigma$ of zero. Errors come from a bootstrap over groups of targets
linked within 6~arcmin.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
On simulated fluxes with the real geometry, a planted $\beta=1$ was detected at $3\sigma$ in
20 of 20 trials. Blends injected into real isolated targets returned $\beta=\hbRealInjOne$
(scatter \hbRealInjOneSd\ over ten injections; \hbRealConeProdSd\ in a later rerun), within the
pre-set tolerance of 0.3. That pass was two errors cancelling. The injection added each
neighbour on the FASHI flux scale to both surveys; placed on each survey's own scale (the
calibration's predicted ratio), it gives $\beta=\hbRealScaledFrozen$ with covariates taken before
injection. But injected flux, like a real blend, also raises the calibration covariates, and the
estimator as applied then returns $\hbRealScaledProd\pm\hbRealScaledSd$, outside the
tolerance. It overstates the blending gain by about half. The negative control failed,
$\beta_{\rm null}=\hbRealBetaNull\pm\hbRealBetaNullErr$, so the primary slope
($\hbRealBeta\pm\hbRealBetaErr$) cannot be interpreted.

Diagnostics run afterwards, with their expected outcomes recorded first, did not find the cause.
Tested on isolated targets it was not fitted on, the calibration's bin means are off by up to
\hbRealVoneMaxMean~dex (Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}). Mapped onto the null targets by their
signal-to-noise, this misfit predicts $\beta_{\rm null}$ between \hbRealMisfitNullMed\ and
\hbRealMisfitNull\ (from bin medians and means). The null slope itself does depend on target
signal-to-noise: weighted to the primary sample's distribution it falls to
$\hbRealMatchedNull\pm\hbRealMatchedNullErr$, and it is $\hbRealTercLo\pm\hbRealTercLoErr$ in the
faintest third against $\hbRealTercHi\pm\hbRealTercHiErr$ in the brightest, contrasts of under
$2\sigma$.
Resampling 2--8-degree strips of right ascension instead of 6-arcmin groups leaves the failure at
\hbRealStripSigMin--\hbRealStripSigMax$\sigma$, and local galaxy density does not account for it.

A second attempt, designed after these diagnostics on the same targets but fixed before it was
run, used a linear spline in signal-to-noise and added local density. It placed a held-out gate
first: fitted on alternate 2-degree strips and tested on the rest, every decile median had to
lie within 0.010~dex of zero with $p>0.01$ over the ten. Both directions failed on both counts ($p=\hbRealVtwoPA$
and \hbRealVtwoPB), and the run stopped before $\beta$ was measured; the plan read such a stop as
showing that calibration limits the test. Later checks do not support that reading, and we
withdraw it. Given the decile errors, a perfect calibration passes the 0.010~dex rule in both
directions with probability only \hbRealAmpPass. Medians also respond to skew, while the fit
uses means. In bin means, a statistic chosen after the gate failed, the spline calibration
gives $\chi^2$ $p=\hbRealMeanPA$ and \hbRealMeanPB, against $p<10^{-5}$ for the original form,
though one bin (\hbRealMeanMaxB~dex) still exceeds the tolerance (Figure~\ref{fig:heldout}).

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
The test is inconclusive. Its negative control fails for a reason we have not found. The
calibration misfit measured on isolated galaxies does not reproduce it, the failure survives the
resampling scales we tried, and local density does not explain it. The slope is concentrated at
low signal-to-noise. A flux scale that differs for galaxies with neighbours, misfit along
another covariate, and confusion in a broader effective ALFALFA beam all remain untested. The
second attempt's gate stopped the run, and its residuals look consistent with zero only in a
post-hoc statistic. A further attempt should frame its gate as a bound on the slope that a
calibration misfit can induce, estimated as in our mapping test, with that gate's false-failure
rate computed before it is fixed. Interferometric H\,I imaging of the pairs would settle the
question directly.
