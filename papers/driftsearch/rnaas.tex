\title{Voyager 1 Re-Recovered with a Minimal Open Drift Search: a Seeded Benchmark and Two
Measured Pitfalls}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
An independent, pure-NumPy replication of the standard narrowband-drift-search validation on
the public Breakthrough Listen Voyager~1 data: locating the carrier in the data at
\dsVoyFreq~MHz (measured drift \dsVoyDriftHz~Hz\,s$^{-1}$), a brute-force de-drift search
recovers it at S/N~$=\dsVoySnr$. A fully seeded CPU-only injection--recovery benchmark
accompanies it (50\% completeness at internal S/N~$\approx\dsCfifty$, threshold \dsThreshold,
committed noise-only statistics), with two pitfalls measured in the same file: the band-center
DC spike, $\sim10^{3}\times$ brighter than the carrier, which a brightest-channel search
reports instead; and a spurious null from searching an asserted rather than located carrier
frequency, which an earlier version of the underlying analysis reported and this one retracts.
\end{abstract}

\section{}

A narrowband transmitter drifts in frequency with the line-of-sight acceleration; SETI
pipelines de-drift the dynamic spectrum to recover such tones, and the field's standard
end-to-end validation is recovering the Voyager~1 carrier in Green Bank Telescope data
\citep{enriquez2017,lebofsky2019}. That validation has, to our knowledge, no published
independent replication with a minimal open detector, and reported injection--recovery
efficiencies have no shared reference cell on which two detectors can be compared on identical
inputs. This note supplies both, in the tested \code{jansky\_research.driftsearch} module
wrapping the teaching-grade brute-force search of the \code{jansky} package, with
\code{setigen} \citep{brzycki2022} as the standard for the injection side.

The benchmark cell is fully specified and seeded: $\dsNtime\times\dsNfreq$ waterfalls of unit
Gaussian noise, a \dsNsearchDrifts-point drift grid, a single injected drifting tone per
trial, \dsNtrials\ trials per (S/N, drift) cell at a detection threshold of \dsThreshold.
Drift-averaged completeness reaches 50\% at injected S/N~$\approx\dsCfifty$ and 90\% at
$\approx\dsCninety$ (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}), with the full per-drift matrix committed. No
false positive occurred in \dsFprTrials\ noise-only trials (one-sided 95\% bound
\dsFprBound\%); the committed noise-only peak distribution (mean \dsNoiseMean, maximum
\dsNoiseMax) shows the threshold sits $\sim$18$\sigma$ above the noise-only mean, so that
bound describes a conservative operating point, not a calibration near the boundary.
Injecting half a grid step off the searched drifts moves the 50\% crossing to
\dsOffGridCfifty, within binomial scatter, so on-grid injection is not flattering the
completeness. The S/N and drift units are the synthetic model's internal units: the benchmark
compares detectors on a common reference, not on the sky, and it contains no interference or
observing cadence.

On the real data, the first measured pitfall is the band-center spectrometer DC-spike
artifact, in this file $\sim10^{3}\times$ brighter than the carrier: a brightest-channel
``detection'' reports it at S/N~$\dsVoyDcSnr$ and is wrong. The pipeline therefore locates
the carrier rather than asserting it: masking the DC region and normalising the time-averaged
spectrum by its median absolute deviation, the brightest narrowband feature sits at
\dsVoyFreq~MHz, flanked by the telemetry-subcarrier sidebands, with a directly measured
per-sample peak walk of \dsVoyDriftChan\ channels\,sample$^{-1}$
($=\dsVoyDriftHz$~Hz\,s$^{-1}$). Evaluated there, the search returns S/N~$=\dsVoySnr$ at a
best-fit drift of \dsVoyBestDrift\ channels\,sample$^{-1}$, one grid step from the measured
walk, against a blank-window \dsVoyBlankSnr.

The second pitfall is the reason this note exists. An earlier version of the underlying
analysis searched at an asserted carrier frequency of \dsVoyLegacyFreq~MHz
\citep{estevez2021} and reported a ``Voyager-1 null'' (S/N~\dsVoyLegacySnr). In this file
that frequency is blank sky, $\sim$0.92~MHz from the carrier (the scale of a
topocentric/barycentric frame difference at X band, though the mechanism is not confirmed
here); the null was a targeting error, not a property of the detector, and it is retracted,
with the wrong frequency kept in the committed evidence as the lesson. Both failure modes
carry the same moral: what separates a toy detector from purpose-built SETI software
\citep{enriquez2017} is artifact handling and carrier location, not sensitivity, and a
validation should locate its known signal in the data rather than assume where it is.
