\title{A Reproducible CPU-Only Injection--Recovery Benchmark for Narrowband Doppler-Drift
SETI Searches, with Voyager-1 Recovered Once the Search Looks in the Right Place}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Technosignature searches look for narrowband signals that drift in frequency as the transmitter
accelerates. Surveys routinely quote injection--recovery efficiencies, but there is no shared,
reproducible, CPU-only reference set on which to compare detectors. We build one on the pure-NumPy
drift search in the \code{jansky} package: synthetic drifting tones are injected over a grid of
signal-to-noise ratio and drift rate, the brute-force de-drift search is run, and the recovered
fraction is measured. With \dsNtrials\ trials per cell and a detection threshold of \dsThreshold, the benchmark
reaches 50\% completeness at injected S/N~$\approx\dsCfifty$ and 90\% at $\approx\dsCninety$; no
false positive occurred in \dsFprTrials\ noise-only trials (one-sided 95\% bound \dsFprBound\%),
though the noise-only best peaks (mean \dsNoiseMean, max \dsNoiseMax) sit so far below the
threshold that this bound was never in danger. Pointing the same detector at the Breakthrough
Listen open-data file of Voyager~1 \citep{lebofsky2019} yields two measured results. First, a
caution: the band-center spectrometer DC spike is $\sim10^{3}\times$ brighter than the carrier,
so a brightest-channel search reports the artifact at S/N~$\dsVoyDcSnr$ and is wrong. Second, a
recovery: locating the carrier in the data (the brightest non-DC narrowband feature, at
\dsVoyFreq~MHz, drifting \dsVoyDriftHz~Hz\,s$^{-1}$ by direct measurement of its per-sample peak
walk), the same brute-force search recovers it at S/N~$=\dsVoySnr$ with a best-fit drift matching
the measured walk to one grid step. An earlier version of this paper asserted the carrier frequency
(\dsVoyLegacyFreq~MHz) instead of locating it; in this file that frequency is blank sky
(S/N~\dsVoyLegacySnr\ vs.\ \dsVoyBlankSnr\ blank), and the resulting ``Voyager null'' was a
targeting error, which we retract. The benchmark, the recovery, and the DC-spike caution are the
contributions; there is no discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A narrowband radio transmitter at fixed frequency appears, at the receiver, to drift in frequency
because of the changing line-of-sight acceleration; SETI pipelines de-drift the dynamic spectrum to
recover such tones \citep{enriquez2017}. Reported detection efficiencies depend on the injector, the
detector, and the data, which makes cross-comparison difficult. The \code{setigen} package
\citep{brzycki2022} standardized signal injection. Still missing for small, portable work is a single
reproducible cell that fixes both the injection model and the detector, so two detectors can be
compared on identical inputs on any laptop. We provide that reference cell, fully specified ($\dsNtime\times\dsNfreq$ waterfalls, a \dsNsearchDrifts-point drift grid, unit Gaussian noise, seeded), and we test the underlying teaching-grade detector on real data.

\section{Method}
\code{driftsearch} wraps the brute-force Doppler-drift search in \code{jansky.seti}. It injects a
synthetic drifting tone of a given S/N and drift rate into Gaussian noise, runs the de-drift search
(whose trial grid includes the true drift), and records whether the recovered peak exceeds the
threshold. Sweeping a grid of $\dsNdrift$ drift rates (0 to $\dsDriftMax$ channels per sample) by a
range of injected S/N, with \dsNtrials\ independent realizations per cell, yields the completeness
surface $P_\mathrm{detect}(\mathrm{S/N}, \dot f)$. Noise-only realizations calibrate the threshold:
the false-positive rate is the fraction whose best peak crosses \dsThreshold\ with no signal present.
Everything is offline and seedable, so the benchmark is exactly reproducible.

The injected tone is a single line in Gaussian noise, with no ON/OFF cadence and no realistic
radio-frequency interference, so the benchmark measures raw recovery rather than interference
rejection. The S/N and drift-rate units are the internal units of the synthetic model, not calibrated
sensitivities; the benchmark compares detectors on a common reference, not on the sky.

\section{Benchmark results}
The noise-only best peak distribution is committed with the benchmark: mean \dsNoiseMean, standard
deviation \dsNoiseSd, maximum \dsNoiseMax\ over \dsFprTrials\ draws, so the threshold of \dsThreshold\ sits roughly $20\sigma$ above the noise-only mean, and ``no false positives''
(one-sided 95\% bound \dsFprBound\%) is a statement about a very conservative operating point,
not a calibration near the boundary. The drift-averaged completeness reaches 50\% at injected
S/N~$\approx\dsCfifty$ and 90\% at $\approx\dsCninety$ (Figure~\ref{fig:recovery}); the full
per-drift matrix is committed, and the per-drift 50\% crossings agree within the binomial
scatter, so ``flat across drift'' is auditable rather than asserted. Injecting half a grid step
off the searched drifts moves the 50\% crossing to \dsOffGridCfifty, within scatter because the de-drift rounds to integer channel shifts, so the on-grid injection is not flattering the
completeness.

\section{Voyager-1: the DC-spike caution, and a recovery}
Pointing the detector at the Breakthrough Listen GBT open-data file of Voyager~1
\citep{lebofsky2019, macmahon2018} yields two measured results, both committed
(\code{results/drift\_voyager.json}). The caution first: the brightest channel is the band-center spectrometer DC-spike artifact, in this file $\sim10^{3}\times$ brighter than the carrier, and a brightest-channel ``detection'' reports it at S/N~$\dsVoyDcSnr$, which is wrong. The
pipeline therefore locates the carrier rather than asserting where it is: masking the DC
region and normalising the time-averaged spectrum by its median absolute deviation, the brightest
narrowband feature sits at \dsVoyFreq~MHz, flanked by the telemetry-subcarrier sidebands, with a
per-sample peak walk of \dsVoyDriftChan\ channels\,sample$^{-1}$ = \dsVoyDriftHz~Hz\,s$^{-1}$.
Evaluated there, the same brute-force search returns S/N~$=\dsVoySnr$ at a best-fit drift of \dsVoyBestDrift\ channels\,sample$^{-1}$, one grid step from the independently measured walk, against a blank-window \dsVoyBlankSnr. The teaching-grade detector does recover the
spacecraft, at three orders of magnitude above the noise floor, once it is pointed correctly.

An earlier version of this paper reported a ``Voyager-1 null'': it searched at an asserted
carrier frequency of \dsVoyLegacyFreq~MHz \citep{estevez2021} and found noise
(S/N~\dsVoyLegacySnr). In this file that frequency maps to blank sky $\sim$0.92~MHz from the carrier (the scale of a topocentric/barycentric frame difference at X band, though we have not confirmed the mechanism), so the null was a targeting error, not a property of the detector,
and we retract it. The lesson is kept in the committed evidence (the \code{legacy\_asserted}
block) and in the pipeline's design: the carrier is now found in the data, never assumed. What
remains true, and matters more: a search that ranks by raw brightness reports the instrumental
artifact, so artifact handling, not sensitivity, is what separates a toy detector from
purpose-built SETI software \citep{enriquez2017}.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
We provide a reproducible, fully specified CPU-only injection--recovery benchmark for a
narrowband drift detector (50\% completeness at S/N~$\approx\dsCfifty$ in the model's internal
units, with the committed noise-only peak distribution making the threshold's conservatism
explicit), a real-data recovery of the Voyager-1 carrier at S/N~$\approx10^{3}$ once the carrier
is located in the data, and the DC-spike caution that an earlier version of this work first
mistook for a detection and then, by asserting the carrier frequency instead of measuring it,
converted into a spurious null. There is no discovery. The benchmark regenerates from a clean
checkout, the Voyager leg from the cached public file, and every number traces to the
pipeline-written macros \code{\textbackslash input}ted by the source file.
