\title{Recovering the Galactic Faraday Rotation Sky from the Taylor et al.\ (2009) Catalogue:
A Reproducible Pipeline}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The polarisation angle of a radio source rotates as $\chi(\lambda)=\chi_0+\mathrm{RM}\,\lambda^2$ on its
way through the magnetised interstellar medium, with the rotation measure
$\mathrm{RM}=0.81\int n_e B_\parallel\,\mathrm{d}l$ (rad m$^{-2}$). The RMs of tens of thousands of
extragalactic sources thus sample the Galactic Faraday sky (the line-of-sight integral of the
field weighted by electron density) along every sightline. We build a small tested tool that
reproduces two textbook signatures from the Taylor, Stil \& Sunstrum \citeyearpar{taylor2009} NVSS RM
catalogue (\rmN\ sources; VizieR, public, no auth; the analysed table is committed with the
results). Because the RM sky is correlated on degree scales, every quoted uncertainty is a
$10^\circ$ sky-block jackknife (\rmJkBlocks\ blocks) --- the i.i.d.\ source bootstrap, which
resamples within correlated patches, understates the headline error here by a factor of four.
The median $|\mathrm{RM}|$ is enhanced toward the Galactic plane by a factor of
$\rmRatio\pm\rmRatioJkErr$ ($\rmPlane$ rad m$^{-2}$ at $|b|<10^\circ$ versus $\rmPole$ at
$|b|>60^\circ$), the expected disk path-length signature; and the mean RM is sign-organised
across the plane (inner-Galaxy mean $\rmInnerNorth\pm\rmInnerNorthJkErr$ rad m$^{-2}$ north of
the plane, $\rmInnerSouth\pm\rmInnerSouthJkErr$ south), supported by an alias-immune statistic:
the fraction of positive RMs is $\rmInnerNorthFrac\pm\rmInnerNorthFracErr$ north versus
$\rmInnerSouthFrac\pm\rmInnerSouthFracErr$ south, against 0.5 under no organisation. Both are
consistent in sign and order of magnitude with the published maps. We are careful about what
this does not show: RM constrains only $\int n_e B_\parallel\,\mathrm{d}l$, not $B_\parallel$
itself; the coarse quadrant mask conflates regions of opposing sign; and the north/south
source-count imbalance is largely an NVSS coverage artefact. This is a reproducibility/method
contribution and a recover-a-known, not a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Faraday rotation of the polarised emission of background radio sources is the workhorse probe of the
magneto-ionic interstellar medium. Each measured RM is the path integral
$0.81\int n_e B_\parallel\,\mathrm{d}l$, so an all-sky grid of extragalactic RMs is a screen-map of the
Galactic Faraday sky \citep{taylor2009,oppermann2012}. Two large-scale features are textbook: a strong
enhancement of $|\mathrm{RM}|$ toward the Galactic plane (the disk path length grows as $\csc|b|$;
\citealt{schnitzeler2010}), and a sign-organised, quadrupole-like pattern reflecting the ordered
disk/halo field \citep{taylor2009,sun2008}. We test whether both can be recovered from the public
catalogue by a small tested tool, quoting honest uncertainties and caveats throughout.

\section{Methods}
\code{rmsky} is a pure-NumPy module built on the \code{jansky} helpers \citep{jansky}. It exposes
(and round-trip verifies) \code{jansky.polarization.rotation\_measure\_fit}, the least-squares fit
of $\chi=\chi_0+\mathrm{RM}\,\lambda^2$ by which catalogue RMs are measured --- but the catalogue
values arrive pre-fitted from VizieR, so the helper anchors the slice to the polarisation stack
without being applied to the data. From the catalogue the module computes (i) the median
$|\mathrm{RM}|$ in bins of Galactic latitude $|b|$ (the plane enhancement) and the plane/pole
ratio, and (ii) the mean RM and the fraction of positive RMs in four regions split by hemisphere
and by inner ($l<90^\circ$ or $l>270^\circ$) versus outer Galaxy. Every quoted uncertainty is a
leave-one-block-out jackknife over $10^\circ$ sky blocks (\code{rmstructure}'s estimator, built
for exactly this failure mode): the RM sky is correlated on degree scales, so the exchangeable
unit is a sky patch, not a source, and an i.i.d.\ resample of sources measures shot noise. The
i.i.d.\ bootstrap is retained only as the contrast. The median, which the rare
$n\pi$-aliased outliers cannot move, is used for the latitude profile; the mean is used for the sign
analysis, since the median would null the net-sign signal being sought, and the mean's alias
exposure is handled by the committed $|\mathrm{RM}|<300$ rad m$^{-2}$ variant and the
sign-fraction statistic, which a $\pm$\,652.9 rad m$^{-2}$ wrap can move by at most the wrapped
count. Every analysis function is tested against two synthetic skies: a $\csc|b|$ disk plus
sign-of-$b$ field (the recover-a-known round trip), and a correlated Gaussian-blob field with a
known coherence scale, on which the test suite asserts the block jackknife exceeds the i.i.d.\
bootstrap --- the failure the deterministic fixture is structurally unable to expose. These are
code checks, not physical validations; the physical validation is the real-data run below.

\section{Data}
We query the Taylor, Stil \& Sunstrum \citeyearpar{taylor2009} NVSS RM catalogue from VizieR
(\code{J/ApJ/702/1230}): \rmN\ polarised extragalactic sources at 1.4~GHz, each with an RM derived
from the two NVSS IF bands. We retrieve $(\alpha,\delta,\mathrm{RM},\sigma_\mathrm{RM})$ and
transform to Galactic $(l,b)$; the analysed table is committed as
\code{data/rmsky\_taylor2009.csv.gz} with the fetch date and VizieR table id in the results
JSON, so every number here is auditable without a network re-fetch. A nearest-neighbour
self-match finds \rmDupCount\ pairs within $5''$, so duplication is negligible rather than
assumed. The catalogue covers $\delta>-40^\circ$, so the southern Galactic sky is incomplete.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Plane enhancement.} The median $|\mathrm{RM}|$ falls monotonically from $\rmPlane$ rad
m$^{-2}$ at $|b|<10^\circ$ to $\rmPole$ rad m$^{-2}$ at $|b|>60^\circ$ (Figure~\ref{fig:rmsky},
with per-bin jackknife errors), a plane/pole ratio of $\rmRatio\pm\rmRatioJkErr$ (sky-block
jackknife; the i.i.d.\ bootstrap gives $\pm\rmRatioErr$, and the factor-four gap between the two
is itself a measurement of the sky's correlation). The ratio is bin-definition-dependent through
the $\csc|b|$ geometry, so the committed variants move it as that predicts rather than testing a
fixed truth: $\rmRatioAltBins$ for $5^\circ/70^\circ$ edges, $\rmRatioCutThree$ under
$|\mathrm{RM}|<300$ rad m$^{-2}$, and $\rmRatioErmCut$ for the better-measured half
($\sigma_\mathrm{RM}$ below its median) --- each is a plane enhancement of the same order, and
none is ``the'' ratio without its definition attached. The value is softer than the bare
$\csc|b|$ limit ($\sim$11 for these bin centres) for two physical reasons: a flat $\sim$10 rad
m$^{-2}$ floor set by intrinsic extragalactic RM (\citealt{schnitzeler2010}; \citealt{mao2010})
that does not scale with $|b|$, and Faraday depolarisation in the thick disk, which suppresses
the detectable near-plane source count and so biases the detected near-plane sample low. The
ratio is therefore a lower bound on the path-length effect.

\paragraph{Sign organisation.} The mean RM is sign-organised across the plane: in the inner
Galaxy it is $\rmInnerNorth\pm\rmInnerNorthJkErr$ rad m$^{-2}$ north of the plane
($\rmInnerNorthN$ sources) and $\rmInnerSouth\pm\rmInnerSouthJkErr$ south ($\rmInnerSouthN$) ---
significantly non-zero and of opposite sign under the block jackknife, though at a few standard
errors, not the tens the per-source errors ($\pm\rmInnerNorthErr$, $\pm\rmInnerSouthErr$) would
claim. The alias-immune sign fractions agree: $\rmInnerNorthFrac\pm\rmInnerNorthFracErr$ of
inner-north RMs are positive versus $\rmInnerSouthFrac\pm\rmInnerSouthFracErr$ inner-south
(0.5 = no organisation), and the means are essentially unchanged under the $|\mathrm{RM}|<300$
cut ($\rmInnerNorthCutThree$ / $\rmInnerSouthCutThree$), so the pattern is not carried by
$n\pi$-aliased outliers. The outer Galaxy shows the same, weaker sense
($\rmOuterNorth\pm\rmOuterNorthJkErr$ / $\rmOuterSouth\pm\rmOuterSouthJkErr$ over
$\rmOuterNorthN$ / $\rmOuterSouthN$ sources), where the southern mean is not individually
significant. The net positive-above / negative-below pattern is consistent in sign with the
published maps \citep{taylor2009,sun2008}. We do not over-read the precise amplitudes: the true
structure is a quadrupole, and our ``inner'' mask combines the $l<90^\circ$ and $l>270^\circ$
halves, which carry opposite sign at a given $b$, so the recovered means are partial
cancellations, a net-sign indicator rather than a field measurement. Moreover the inner
north/south source counts ($\rmInnerNorthN$ vs $\rmInnerSouthN$) are imbalanced largely because
the $\delta>-40^\circ$ cut removes inner-south sightlines, so the larger southern amplitude must
not be read as a physical north/south asymmetry.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is method and reproducibility: a tested pipeline that recovers two established
Galactic-Faraday-sky signatures from a public catalogue, with uncertainties and the right caveats. The
honest limitations bound the interpretation. (i) RM measures $\int n_e B_\parallel\,\mathrm{d}l$;
disentangling $B_\parallel$ needs an electron-density model (e.g.\ NE2001/YMW16) not applied here.
(ii) The two-band NVSS RMs alias by $\pm652.9$ rad m$^{-2}$ \citep{taylor2009}. Medians are
insensitive to the rare wraps, but a region \emph{mean} is not automatically so: wraps are not
sign-random and concentrate at low latitude, where the sign signal lives. We therefore do not
argue the effect away; we bound it, with the $|\mathrm{RM}|<300$ variant and the sign-fraction
statistic, both of which reproduce the pattern. (iii) The quadrant split is a coarse net-sign
probe, not a harmonic/multipole decomposition, and is degraded by the southern coverage gap.
(iv) A single 1.4~GHz band gives one RM per source with no in-band Faraday-complexity
information. None of these undermine the recover-a-known: under block-jackknife errors the
plane enhancement is highly significant and the inner-Galaxy sign organisation significant at a
few sigma, both consistent in sign and order of magnitude with the published maps --- no
published value of this exact binned ratio exists to match more precisely. These limitations
are, however, why this is a reproduction and not a measurement of the field.
Every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros this paper \code{\textbackslash input}s,
and \code{python -m jansky\_research.rmsky} regenerates them from the public VizieR catalogue.
