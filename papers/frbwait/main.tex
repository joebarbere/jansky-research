\title{A Uniform Wait-Time, Periodicity, and Duty-Cycle Census of the
CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 Repeaters}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 \citep{cat2} contains 83 repeating sources, yet its repeater timing has
only been analysed source-by-source: the companion rate census \citep{cook2026} computes
burst rates, DM drifts, and the repeater fraction, but reports no per-source wait-time
clustering shape, no periodograms, and no duty cycles, and all published periodicity results
are single-source campaigns. We compute one statistic uniformly across every Cat-2 repeater:
the Weibull wait-time shape $k$ in the \citet{oppermann2018} formalism, a Rayleigh-$Z^2$
periodogram, and an activity-window duty cycle at any significant period. The public exposure
release is time-integrated, so per-epoch exposure correction from public data is not
possible; periodogram significance instead comes from a transit-comb-preserving scramble, in
which each burst keeps its sidereal phase and gets a uniformly redrawn day number, putting
CHIME's daily-visibility aliasing into every null realisation (the Catalog-1 lesson that
exposure-blind FAPs are not rigorous, applied here mechanically; \citealt{cat1}). The pipeline re-finds the
one in-catalogue anchor, FRB 20180916B, at \fwRealAnchorPeriod\,d ($p=$ \fwRealAnchorP,
published 16.35\,d; \citealt{chime2020period}), with a 90\%-containment duty cycle of
\fwRealAnchorDuty; the recovery also survives a stricter null that scrambles whole transit
groups ($p=$ \fwRealAnchorPGrouped), which the two other nominally significant peaks do not
(their $p$ rises to 0.046 and 0.021), so the census contains exactly one period. The
population-level result is a selection function, not an astrophysical norm: a transit-censored
injection curve shows that a pure Poisson source recovers $k\approx$ \fwRealBiasKMidRate\ at
typical census rates and $k\approx$ \fwRealBiasKHiRate\ at the highest observed rate, so the
census median $k=$ \fwRealMedianK\ (95\% CI \fwRealMedianKlo--\fwRealMedianKhi) is consistent
with Poisson arrivals seen through CHIME's transit comb. Per source, the three smallest-$k$
sources lie below the curve at their own rates, and one source is significantly
super-Poissonian; both facts are reported against the curve rather than against $k=1$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Whether repeating FRB sources share a common temporal character (Poissonian storms,
long-memory clustering, periodic activity windows) is a population question, but the
population data have been analysed almost entirely one source at a time. The two known
activity periods (16.35\,d for FRB 20180916B, \citealt{chime2020period}; $\sim$160\,d for
FRB 20121102A, \citealt{rajwade2020}) came from dedicated campaigns; wait-time clustering fits exist for a handful of
loud repeaters \citep[e.g.][]{oppermann2018,frb220912a}; and the Catalog-2 companion census
\citep{cook2026} stops at rates and DM drift, noting that ``repeater burst arrival times are
stochastic; however, they cluster in time'' without modelling it. Catalog 2 \citep{cat2},
with 83 repeaters observed under one instrument, one detection pipeline, and one exposure
accounting for just over five years, is the first dataset on which a homogeneous timing census
is possible. We present such a census here.

FRB 20240114A and FRB 20240209A, the two loudest recent repeaters with claimed long periods,
post-date the Catalog-2 cutoff (2023 September 15) and are not in the table; the only
in-catalogue anchor with an established period is FRB 20180916B, and re-finding it is the
validation gate for everything else. The public exposure product accompanying the catalog
(CANFAR DOI 10.11570/25.0066) is, second, a time-integrated sky map, not a per-epoch exposure
history; we therefore never claim a per-epoch-corrected periodogram, and build the null
differently (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\section{Data and Method}
\label{sec:method}
We use the public burst table (4,539 events; sub-bursts deduplicated to the first component's
\code{mjd\_400} TOA) and its per-source upper-transit exposure hours. Every source flagged
with a \code{repeater\_name} enters the census with a rate row; sources with $\ge$10 bursts
and $\ge$30-day spans (the stated completeness cut) additionally get: (i) a Weibull fit to
their inter-burst wait times with bootstrap CIs on the shape $k$ ($k<1$ with the CI excluding
1 = clustered); (ii) a Rayleigh-$Z^2$ periodogram over 1.5\,d--span/3; (iii) a
90\%-containment activity-window duty cycle at the peak period, quoted only when the peak is
significant.

CHIME is a transit instrument: a source is visible for $\sim$minutes per sidereal day, so
burst TOAs live on a comb of period 0.99727\,d, and any exposure-blind false-alarm estimate
inherits that aliasing. Our null preserves it exactly: each burst keeps its sidereal phase
(its position within the daily transit window) while its day number is redrawn uniformly over
the source's observed span; the periodogram-peak FAP is the fraction of $10^3$ such scrambles
whose maximum $Z^2$ over the same grid reaches the observed peak, $p=(k{+}1)/(n{+}1)$. The
scramble does not, however, carry day-scale exposure structure that the public release omits:
instrument downtime and per-day exposure gaps are absent from the null because days are
redrawn uniformly. This is a stated approximation, not a solved problem.

The same transit geometry censors the wait-time data: waits between $\sim$15 minutes and
$\sim$1 sidereal day are unobservable (the source has set), so observed waits are either
intra-window or $\ge$1\,d, and the missing intermediate waits bias $k$ downward, most for the
highest-rate sources. An earlier version of this paper disclosed that bias qualitatively; here
it is measured. Pure Poisson ($k=1$) processes are generated in continuous time, censored to
the 15-minute daily transit window exactly as CHIME observes, and fitted exactly as the census
fits real sources, across the observed rate range. The recovered shape falls from
$k\approx1$ at the lowest rates through $\approx$\fwRealBiasKMidRate\ at 0.6 bursts per
exposure-hour to $\approx$\fwRealBiasKHiRate\ at 3.2, the rate of FRB 20220912A. Every $k$
in this census must therefore be read against this curve, not against 1; the curve ships in
the committed results. Validation of the period machinery is end-to-end on transit-sampled
synthetics: an injected Weibull train ($k=0.4$) with a 16.35-d, 30\%-duty activity window is
recovered in all three statistics, and a Poisson control with no window stays null (the
offline CI leg).

A second null supplements the per-burst scramble for any claimed detection. The per-burst
scramble redraws each burst's day independently, which destroys within-transit burst
multiplicity; bursts sharing a transit add coherently in $Z^2$ at every multi-day trial
period, so a high-rate source can beat the per-burst null on multiplicity alone. The
group-preserving variant redraws one day per transit group, moving each group rigidly, and is
applied to every peak that passes the primary threshold.

One statistic requires a caveat that recurs throughout the analysis: a periodogram peak with
only a few cycles across the span is degenerate with a small number of activity epochs. We report
$N_{\rm cyc}={\rm span}/P$ per source and treat peaks with $N_{\rm cyc}\lesssim5$ as
epoch-clustering candidates, not periods, whatever their formal $p$.

Table~\ref{tab:census} is the census. The anchor validates the machinery:
FRB 20180916B returns \fwRealAnchorPeriod\,d at the scramble floor ($p\le$ \fwRealAnchorP,
0 of 999 scrambles; 107 cycles) against the published 16.35\,d, and the recovery survives the
group-preserving null unchanged ($p=$ \fwRealAnchorPGrouped). Its 90\%-containment duty cycle
is \fwRealAnchorDuty\ ($\sim$3.5\,d), which sits inside the published 5-d full window
\citep{chime2020period}; the full-containment arc at the published period is
\fwRealAnchorDutyFullPub\ ($\sim$6.3\,d), wider than the published window, as expected when
113 bursts over 1748 days populate window edges that 38 bursts over 505 days did not. The
half-containment arc is \fwRealAnchorDutyHalf.

The population statement is where the injection curve changes the paper. The median Weibull
shape is \fwRealMedianK\ (bootstrap 95\% CI \fwRealMedianKlo--\fwRealMedianKhi; a sign test
of the 15 values against 1 gives two-sided $p=$ \fwRealSignP), and the transit-censoring curve
recovers $k\approx$ \fwRealBiasKMidRate\ from pure Poisson processes at the census's typical
rates: the census median carries no evidence of intrinsic clustering. Per source, the three
$k<1$ exclusions at 95\% confidence are the three highest-rate sources, the ordering the
censoring bias predicts; read against the curve rather than against 1, their $k$ values
(0.30, 0.34, 0.42) still lie below the censoring-only expectation at their rates ($\approx$0.47
at 3.2 bursts hr$^{-1}$, $\approx$0.8--0.9 below 1 hr$^{-1}$), so some intrinsic clustering
beyond the selection function remains indicated for those three, at reduced significance we do
not quantify further. One source, FRB 20191106C, is significantly super-Poissonian
($k=1.44$, CI excluding 1) where censoring pushes the other way; a one-sided ``clustered''
flag hid it in the earlier version of this census. \fwRealNPeriodic\ sources beat the
per-burst scramble at $p\le0.01$, but the two non-anchor peaks fail the group-preserving null
($p=0.046$ and $0.021$): their significance was within-transit multiplicity, not periodicity,
and FRB 20220912A's peak additionally sits at the edge of the period grid (its cycle count is
forced by the grid, flagged in Table~\ref{tab:census}). The census therefore contains exactly
one period, the known one.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
The census's main products are a selection function and an anchor. The transit-censoring
curve maps what CHIME's sampling does to a wait-time shape at every observed rate, and the
uniform per-source $k$ values are interpretable only against it; the earlier reading of this
census, that sub-Poissonian clustering is the population norm, does not survive the curve,
since the median $k$ is what Poisson arrivals look like through the transit comb. What does
survive: exactly one repeater has a scramble-significant, many-cycle, group-null-robust
activity period, the one already known; three sources show clustering beyond the mapped
selection function and one shows the opposite; and no new periodicity claim survives either
the cycles test or the group-preserving null. The $k$ values are conditioned-on-sampling
statistics, not deconvolved intrinsic shapes, and any population-synthesis use must apply the
committed censoring curve. The machinery (tested, transit-aware, both nulls published
alongside) transfers to Catalog 3 unchanged. Without a public per-epoch exposure history,
duty cycles inherit the transit-sampling approximation, and sources with the least total
exposure are the least constrained.
