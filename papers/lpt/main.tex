\begin{abstract}
The long-period radio transients (LPTs) grew from two objects in 2022 to \lptN\ by mid-2026;
we are not aware of a published population catalogue of the class carrying per-value provenance
and regenerable statistics; the review of \citealt{rea2026} discusses population constraints
and plots the class, but ships neither. We compile a cross-checked, per-value-provenanced
catalogue of all \lptN\ confirmed LPTs (periods \lptPmin~min to \lptPmax~hr;
Table~\ref{tab:lpts}, with every discovery paper cited), a process that surfaced a period
transcription error in the review's machine-readable table, and that also corrected five
defects in our own compilation before this version, which we disclose. We place the class on
the P--$\dot P$ plane against the pulsar death line and constant-field tracks. Only
\lptNmeas\ $\dot P$ measurements exist in the class. All \lptNconstr\ objects with a positive
$\dot P$ constraint sit below the death line, a count we report as the structural fact it
is: at these periods, sitting above the line would require $\dot P$ larger than any achievable
constraint by factors of \lptMinMargin\ to over $10^{6}$, so no measurement could have
falsified it; the claim-carrying subset is the \lptNdeathNonbin/\lptNnonbinConstr\
no-companion objects, and the count is \lptNdeath/\lptNconstr\ across the whole death-valley
constant range. The hinted $\sim$78-minute binary boundary \citep{rea2026} is not
established: excluding the three objects whose companion status is unknown (the primary
comparison), the binaries sit longer by $\Delta\log P=\lptSplitD$ with an exact
(fully enumerated) $p=\lptSplitP$; at that observed offset the test's power at $N=\lptN$ is
only \lptPowerPrimary, so the null constrains a clean boundary, not a modest one. The
catalogue, diagram, and statistics regenerate from one vendored CSV; this is a value-added
compilation, not a discovery claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
LPTs, periodic radio emitters with periods of minutes to hours, defy the standard pulsar
picture: coherent radio emission should cease beyond the pair-cascade death line \citep{ruderman1975}, yet
GPM~J1839$-$10 has emitted for 35+ years at $P=22$~min with $\dot P<3.6\times10^{-13}$
\citep{hurleywalker2023}. Interpretations split between ultra-long-period magnetars
\citep[e.g.][]{caleb2024,dong2025a} and white-dwarf binaries, now unambiguously confirmed for
several members \citep{deruiter2025}. The review of \citet{rea2026} synthesises the physics and plots the class; a cross-checked,
regenerable catalogue with typed $\dot P$ entries is the complementary piece. With \lptN\
objects the catalogue is worth doing carefully, and small enough that every number can be
checked against its primary source.

\section{The catalogue}
Table~\ref{tab:lpts} is the catalogue: all \lptN\ objects with period, $\dot P$ value or
limit with its type flagged, companion status (including unknown, shown as unknown
rather than folded into any class), X-ray status, and the discovery reference. The vendored
CSV additionally records positions, the discovery/measurement arXiv IDs, the quoted
uncertainties for the values this paper's comparisons rest on, and a per-object free-text
provenance flag. Converting every period to seconds is the step that caught a dropped leading
digit in the review's machine-readable file for ASKAP~J1935+2148 (2225.309~s vs the discovery
paper's $3225.313\pm0.002$~s, \citealt{caleb2024}; the retrieved review file and comparand are
recorded in the CSV's flags). Two $\dot P$ entries need care: CHIME~J0630+25's
$+5.2(1.1)\times10^{-12}$ is a measurement through a glitch (the review conservatively treats
it as a limit; we carry both framings, \citealt{dong2025a}), and CHIME~J1634+44's
$-9.03(0.11)\times10^{-12}$ is the class's only firm spin-up \citep{dong2025b}, physically
natural if the period is orbital or the emitter is torqued in a binary. \lptNx\ of the
\lptN\ objects have X-ray detections, including the first LPT X-ray discovery
\citep{wang2025}.

\paragraph{The compilation's own error history, disclosed.} "Cross-checked" describes a
process, not a guarantee, and this table's history proves it: a 2026-08 downstream audit of the
same CSV found and fixed five defects that had survived the original compilation and this
paper's first version: a $\dot P$ limit two orders of magnitude low and a
59\arcsec-offset name for ASKAP~J1832$-$0911, and three wrong discovery arXiv IDs (one
pointing at an unrelated engineering paper, one at a theory paper, one at a cosmology paper).
Every corrected cell carries its audit note in the CSV's flags column, and the repository now
pins each period, $\dot P$, type, and discovery ID in a cell-by-cell test, so any future edit
must change the pin deliberately. The transcription error we report in the review's table was
confirmed against the discovery paper's own title (a ``54-minute'' period; 2225.309~s is
37~min).

\paragraph{Data availability.} The catalogue CSV, the code that regenerates every number and
figure here, and the test suite that pins the values are in the \code{jansky-research}
repository (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21482378); \code{python -m jansky\_research.lpt}
rebuilds the table, diagram, statistics, and this paper's macros from the CSV.

\section{The P--$\dot P$ diagram and population statistics}
Figure~\ref{fig:lpt} places all \lptN\ objects on the P--$\dot P$ plane: measurements as filled
symbols, upper limits as arrows, white-dwarf binaries distinguished. Deliberately,
no dipole $B$ or characteristic age is quoted for binary members, whose ``period'' is
orbital and for whom neutron-star dipole formulae are meaningless. Every object with a positive $\dot P$ constraint lies
below the death line (\lptNdeath/\lptNconstr). We report that count as the structural fact it
is, not as \lptNconstr\ independent confirmations: because the death line scales as $P^{3}$,
at minute-to-hour periods the $\dot P$ needed to sit above it exceeds every published
constraint by factors from \lptMinMargin\ (the closest object, GLEAM-X~J1627) to over
$10^{6}$, implying dipole fields far beyond any magnetar; no achievable timing measurement
could have falsified the statement for any member. The committed margin table quantifies the
puzzle per object, and the count is \lptNdeath/\lptNconstr\ at every death-valley constant
from $B/P^{2}=5\times10^{10}$ to $2\times10^{12}$ \citep[minimum margin
\lptValleyMinMargin;][]{chen1993}, so the result does not rest on the
\citet{bhattacharya1991} constant our implementation adopts. The claim-carrying count is the
no-companion subset (\lptNdeathNonbin/\lptNnonbinConstr): for the binaries the criterion is
simply the wrong diagnostic, which is precisely why the diagram needs the class split marked.
The period distribution spans \lptPmin~min--\lptPmax~hr (median \lptPmed~min).

\paragraph{The hinted binary boundary, with the labelling stated.} Three objects' companion
status is unknown (GCRT~J1745$-$3009 and the two 2026 VASTER discoveries, the latter being
two of the three longest periods in the sample), so where they are counted moves the contrast.
The primary comparison excludes them: \lptNwd\ binaries vs 6 known-no-companion objects give
$\Delta\log P=\lptSplitD$ with an exact, fully enumerated permutation $p=\lptSplitP$. The
labelling variants bound the systematic: counting unknowns with the no-companion class (the
choice an earlier version of this paper made silently) shrinks the contrast to
$\Delta\log P=\lptSplitDPub$ ($p=\lptSplitPPub$); counting them as binaries gives
\lptSplitDAsBin\ ($p=\lptSplitPAsBin$); confirmed-only gives \lptSplitDConf\
($p=\lptSplitPConf$). No labelling reaches significance. The honest power statement replaces
the earlier ``the test has power'': at the observed offset the exact test's power at
$N=\lptN$ is \lptPowerPrimary\ (and \lptPowerPub\ at the smaller published offset),
essentially the size of the test, so the null constrains only a clean, near-disjoint
boundary; a synthetic disjoint split at the real sample composition is detected
($p=$~\lptSynSplitP), which verifies the machinery, not the sensitivity. A hard 78-min
boundary is in fact already disfavoured by inspection: three binaries sit below it and three
non-binaries above.

\section{Honest limits}
(i) This is a compilation: every number is someone else's measurement, carried with its arXiv
provenance; our contribution is the cross-checked table, the diagram, and the statistics.
(ii) $N=\lptN$ is small; the split test is reported with its exact $p$-value, its labelling
sensitivity, and its measured (near-zero) power at the observed offset, not as a detection or
exclusion. (iii) Binary/no-companion labels follow the review plus two
post-review updates and will move as follow-up lands; the CSV carries per-object flags for every
disputed value. (iv) GCRT~J1745$-$3009 (never re-detected since 2004, no timing baseline) is the
weakest member and is flagged as such. (v) Selection effects (survey cadence, image-plane vs
time-domain discovery) shape the period distribution and are not modelled here.
