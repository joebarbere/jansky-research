\title{A Coverage-Corrected Occurrence Census for Type~III Solar Radio Bursts:
Method and Recover-a-Known Validation Toward a Multi-Cycle e-Callisto Census}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Type~III solar radio bursts trace flare-accelerated electron beams, and their occurrence rate is
expected to rise and fall with solar activity over the $\sim$11-year cycle
\citep{sainthilaire2013,reid2014}. Turning a stream of coincidence-vetted e-Callisto \citep{benz2009}
burst detections into an occurrence census that can be compared across epochs requires more than
counting: a burst is confirmed only when enough stations observe it, and the active-station count
varies with time, so the raw count must be normalised by observing coverage before rates from
different epochs can be compared. We build that census as a tested tool, a coverage-corrected rate,
$\mathrm{rate}=N_\mathrm{events}/C$, correlated against the SILSO monthly sunspot number
\citep{clette2014,silso2015}, and validate it on a synthetic event stream in which the true
proportionality $k=\ecsSynKtrue$ is known. The validation states plainly what each arm can and
cannot test: with confirmation linear in coverage the recovery ($r=\ecsSynPearson$, slope
$\ecsSynSlope$ over \ecsSynNperiods\ months) is guaranteed by construction and checks only the
arithmetic; under a station history shaped like the real network's growth the correction genuinely
rescues a broken raw correlation ($r=\ecsSynGrowthRawPearson\to\ecsSynGrowthPearson$); and under
a saturating confirmation (the shape the pipeline's fixed two-station threshold produces)
the estimator measurably fails ($r=\ecsSynSatPearson$, with a spurious coverage
anti-correlation of $\ecsSynSatCovCorr$), so the validation can now return a failing answer. This
is a methods-and-tooling contribution. An attempted 168-day real ingest is reported as an audited
ingest result (\ecsRealNdaysIngested\ days ingested, \ecsRealNdaysFailed\ silently throttled
with data present in the archive), which is the concrete demonstration that the real multi-cycle
census is an ingest-infrastructure task, not a method gap.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Type~III bursts are the radio signature of semi-relativistic electron beams escaping the corona along
open magnetic field lines; they are among the most common solar radio phenomena and were among the
first spectral burst types classified \citep{wildmccready1950,reid2014}. Because the beams are
accelerated in flares and jets, the type~III occurrence rate is a proxy for active-region activity and
is observed to track the solar cycle \citep{sainthilaire2013}. The e-Callisto network \citep{benz2009},
with 150+ low-cost spectrometers feeding a public, two-decade archive, is in principle an excellent
instrument for measuring that occurrence rate over multiple cycles. In earlier work we built a
scheduled Airflow-on-Podman pipeline that ingests the archive day by day and promotes single-station
candidates to confirmed events by cross-station coincidence, on the assumption that a real burst is seen at the
same universal time by many sunlit stations while interference is local to one.

What that pipeline produces is a stream of confirmed events per day. Converting that stream into an
occurrence census, a rate that can be compared honestly across epochs and against a solar activity
index, requires one more step that is easy to get wrong: a completeness correction. A burst is
confirmed only if enough of the network observed it, and the number of active e-Callisto stations
grew over the network's history and varies day to day, so the raw confirmed-event count conflates
real activity with how many stations happened to be watching. We build the census statistic that
removes that confound, and validate it end-to-end on a synthetic observing history before it is
turned on the real archive.

\section{The census statistic}
For each period (here a month) with a confirmed-event count $N_\mathrm{events}$ and an observing
coverage $C$ (the number of active stations contributing that period), the coverage-corrected
occurrence rate is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{rate} = \frac{N_\mathrm{events}}{C},
\label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
with periods of zero coverage left undefined rather than counted as zero, and periods where the
ingest failed (files listed by the archive but none retrieved) recorded as missing, never as zero,
the distinction whose absence corrupted an earlier version of this paper's real leg
(Section~\ref{sec:disc}). Here $C$ is the number of stations successfully
ingested in the sampled window; where retrieval is incomplete this undercounts the active network,
which inflates the rate, so the per-period listed/fetched provenance is committed alongside every
census row. Equation~(\ref{eq:rate}) is
the minimal correction: if a burst's confirmation probability scales linearly with how many
stations are watching, then $N_\mathrm{events}\propto R\,C$ for an underlying rate $R$, and
dividing by $C$ returns an estimator of $R$ that is comparable across epochs with different network
sizes. That linearity is an assumption, not a fact: the upstream pipeline confirms an event at
$\geq$2 coincident stations, a fixed threshold under which confirmation probability
saturates with coverage, and the validation below measures what happens when it is
wrong. We then correlate
the corrected rate against the SILSO monthly sunspot number \citep{clette2014,silso2015}, reporting the
Pearson $r$ (linear association), the Spearman $\rho$ (rank association, robust to a non-linear
activity--rate relation), and the ordinary-least-squares slope of rate on sunspot number. The census
is a small, tested \code{jansky\_research.ecallisto\_census} module (pure NumPy/SciPy); the sunspot
series is parsed directly from the public SILSO CSV.

\section{Validation: three arms, one of which can fail}
To be explicit about what is real in this section: every event count is synthetic, generated from a
model, and the arms below state individually what each one is capable of testing. (The SILSO series
is real data, but it enters the real leg of Section~\ref{sec:disc}, not this validation,
which uses a synthetic activity driver so the ground truth is known.) We generate a realistic
monthly sunspot series (a raised, fast-rise/slow-decay cycle plus noise) and, for each month
with activity $S$ and station coverage $C$, draw the confirmed-event count from a Poisson
distribution with mean $k\,S\,C_\mathrm{eff}$, with $k=\ecsSynKtrue$.

\paragraph{The linear arm is correct by construction, and we say so.} With
$C_\mathrm{eff}=C$, the same linearity Equation~(\ref{eq:rate}) assumes, the corrected rate
has expectation $k\,S$ identically, so recovery cannot fail except through an arithmetic bug or
Poisson noise. Over \ecsSynNperiods\ months (\ecsSynNevents\ events) it does not: Pearson
$r=\ecsSynPearson$, Spearman $\rho=\ecsSynSpearman$, fitted slope $\ecsSynSlope$ against the
injected $\ecsSynKtrue$ (Figure~\ref{fig:census}); over 30 independent realizations the slope is
$\ecsSynSlopeMean\pm\ecsSynSlopeSD$, so the single-seed value is not an outlier. This arm
validates the implementation, not the statistic. Two honesty companions are committed with
it: on this stationary coverage history the uncorrected count already correlates with
activity at $r=\ecsSynRawPearson$ (the correction buys little where coverage barely varies), and
the corrected rate retains a residual coverage correlation of $\ecsSynCovCorr$.

\paragraph{The growth arm shows what the correction is for.} Under a station history shaped like
the real network's growth (2 to 60 stations over the span), the raw count conflates activity with
network size and correlates with the driver at only $r=\ecsSynGrowthRawPearson$; the corrected
rate recovers $r=\ecsSynGrowthPearson$. This, not the stationary arm, is the demonstration
that the correction rescues a genuinely broken raw census.

\paragraph{The misspecification arm can fail, and does.} The upstream pipeline confirms an event
at a fixed $\geq$2-station coincidence, so real confirmation probability saturates with coverage
rather than growing linearly. Injecting that shape
($C_\mathrm{eff}=c_{1/2}(1-e^{-C/c_{1/2}})$) under the same growth history, the $N/C$ estimator
over-corrects: the recovery degrades to $r=\ecsSynSatPearson$ and the corrected rate acquires a
spurious anti-correlation with coverage of $\ecsSynSatCovCorr$. This is the arm that keeps
the validation honest: it measures the failure mode of the correction's own assumption, and its
residual coverage correlation is the committed diagnostic a real census must report before its
rates are believed.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:disc}
The contribution is the census statistic and its validation: a coverage correction that makes
type~III event counts comparable across epochs with different e-Callisto network sizes, and a
recover-a-known demonstration that the corrected rate cleanly reproduces a known solar-cycle
proportionality. The honest limitations are specific and were quantified in the coincidence-QC work
that precedes this:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The validation event stream is synthetic.} The monthly event counts in
Section~3 are drawn from a model, not measured; the linear arm is correct by construction and is
labelled as such. This analysis validates the implementation and measures the correction's
failure mode; it does not report a measured solar-cycle correlation.
\item \textbf{The attempted real ingest is reported as an audited failure, and the failure is
the point.} An earlier version of this paper described ``168 sampled days''. An audit of the
archive's own day indexes (one listing per day, committed per-row in the census table) shows the
ingest succeeded on only \ecsRealNdaysIngested\ of the \ecsRealNdaysAttempted\ attempted days
(\ecsRealSpanStart\ to \ecsRealSpanEnd); on the other \ecsRealNdaysFailed\ days the archive
lists data for tens of stations but the per-file fetch pattern was throttled and every failure was
silently recorded as zero coverage. The ingested days yielded \ecsRealNevents\ confirmed events on
\ecsRealNdaysWithEvents\ days (at a \ecsRealTolS~s coincidence tolerance, twice the 60~s the
pipeline paper publishes, recorded here for that reason); we quote no correlation coefficient for
them, because a statistic that is an exact function of which two days host the events is not a
measurement. Producing the real multi-cycle census therefore requires the scheduled, backfilling
Airflow ingest built for exactly this, an infrastructure task the audited failure demonstrates
concretely, not a method gap.
\item \textbf{Type~III only.} The underlying detector targets fast negative-drift ridges; a type~II
census would need a second, slower-drift template.
\item \textbf{Coverage correction is first-order, and its failure mode is now measured.}
Dividing by the station count assumes confirmation probability scales linearly with coverage; the
misspecification arm shows that under the saturating confirmation a fixed coincidence threshold
actually produces, $N/C$ over-corrects (recovery $r=\ecsSynSatPearson$, coverage anti-correlation
$\ecsSynSatCovCorr$). A real census must therefore report its residual rate--coverage correlation,
and a fuller treatment would model the confirmation probability directly.
\end{itemize}
Every number above is written by the census tool into the macros loaded with \code{\textbackslash
input}; \code{python -m jansky\_research.ecallisto\_census --offline} reproduces the validation
suite, \code{--audit} re-lists the archive day indexes behind the ingest audit, and the same
statistic runs on a real event stream once the multi-year ingest is collected.
