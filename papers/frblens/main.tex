\title{No Lensed Repeaters Among the CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 Sources:
The First Catalogue-Level Search for Recurring Image Delays}
\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
A strongly lensed repeating fast radio burst produces a train of images per intrinsic burst,
arriving in multiplets whose mutual time delay is fixed, at the solar-system barycenter, to
milliseconds \citep{dailu2017,li2018}. The theory is a decade old; no observational catalogue-level search
has been published, and every existing FRB lensing search targets the intra-burst
$\mu$s--ms regime instead \citep{leung2022,kader2022,cat1lens,microlens2026}. We present the
first such search, on the CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 repeaters \citep[83 sources, of which 33 have
the $\ge$5 bursts and $>$2-day spans the statistic needs;][]{cat2}. Per source we barycenter
the catalogue's topocentric TOAs (the annual Roemer term would otherwise drift a fixed delay
by up to $\pm$230\,s in topocentric pair delays, far beyond tolerance), then search
all burst-pair delays $>$1\,d for a recurring
delay $\Delta$ shared by the maximum number of disjoint pairs matching within 5\,s and
consistent in DM with each source's own fitted-DM scatter, against a null that permutes each
burst's position within its transit window while keeping its day, so activity epochs,
clustering, and periodic windows survive into every null realisation and only sub-window
fixed-delay coherence can score, whereas a day-scrambling null gives
false detections for the three most clustered repeaters, a failure mode we document.
Injections at the real burst trains, processed identically to the search, map the
sensitivity region: joint detection
requires $\Delta$ to fall within the transit window of an integer number of sidereal days.
The search returns \flRealNDet\ detections among \flRealNSearched\ repeaters searched
(cleanest non-detection $p=0.806$), giving the first empirical upper limit on the
lensed-repeater fraction: $<$\flRealLimit\ per searched repeater at 95\% confidence within
the mapped sensitivity region, dividing by the summed injection efficiency
$\sum_i \epsilon_i = \flRealEpsSum$ rather than by the source count, which would overstate the
limit fourfold. The expected yield at optical depth $\sim$$10^{-4}$ was zero; the limit, the
machinery, and the mapped selection function are the contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\citet{dailu2017} and \citet{li2018} specify exactly how a strongly lensed one-off FRB appears
in a survey: as a ``repeater'' whose burst arrivals come in pairs (or multiplets) separated
by the lens's image delay, fixed for galaxy-scale lenses at days to months and stable at the
barycenter to far better than a second over years. Every observational FRB lensing search to
date has instead targeted the intra-burst regime, using baseband interferometric echoes at
$\mu$s \citep{leung2022,kader2022} and autocorrelation echoes at ms in Catalog 1
\citep{cat1lens} and in the Catalog-2 one-offs \citep{microlens2026}. The burst-to-burst
regime, the one where a lensed one-off actually contaminates the repeater population
\citep{cook2026}, has not been searched in any catalogue. Catalog 2 \citep{cat2}, with 83
repeaters and burst-train spans up to $\sim$4.8 years, is the first data set for which the
search is worth running; the expected yield at optical depth $\sim$$10^{-4}$ is zero, so we
report the result as it stands: the first empirical upper limit, plus machinery already
tested for catalogues where the optical-depth arithmetic is more favorable.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
Per repeater with $\ge$5 bursts and a span $>$2\,d (33 of the 83), we use the first-component
\code{mjd\_400} TOAs, which are topocentric (Catalog 2, Appendix A). A lens delay is fixed
only at the barycenter. The annual Roemer term makes a fixed barycentric delay drift in
topocentric pair delays by $\sim$$\Delta\times9$\,s per day of delay (e.g.\ $\pm$230\,s at
$\Delta=26$\,d), some 46$\times$ our 5\,s matching tolerance, so the search would be blind to
real signals in the raw TOAs. We therefore barycenter every TOA (astropy, CHIME's location,
per-source catalogue positions; arcminute positional uncertainty contributes $<$0.2\,s) and
search in the barycentric frame. Every pairwise delay $>$1\,d is a candidate $\Delta$; delays
below one transit spacing are excluded, since image pairs and intrinsic same-window
clustering are indistinguishable there. For each candidate delay we greedily count disjoint
pairs $(t, t+\Delta)$ matching within 5\,s. The tolerance budget has four terms. Catalogue TOA
errors are ms-scale (repeater median 1.2\,ms, maximum 0.15\,s); a
1\,pc\,cm$^{-3}$ DM difference shifts the 400-MHz TOA by 26\,ms; residual barycentring error
is $<$0.2\,s; and secular delay drift \citep{dailu2017} is sub-second over the span. A 5\,s
tolerance covers all of these with margin while staying far inside the $\sim$15-min transit
window. Matched pairs must also agree in DM within $3\sqrt{2}\,\sigma_{\rm DM}$ of the
source's own fitted-DM scatter ($\sigma_{\rm DM}$ = robust per-source scatter of
\code{dm\_fitb}, median 0.4 and 0.2--8.4 across the sample, with 9 of 33 sources at the
1\,pc\,cm$^{-3}$ floor). A naive 1\,pc\,cm$^{-3}$ ``measurement floor'' would reject the
majority of genuine image pairs, whose independently fitted DMs scatter just like intrinsic
bursts'. The per-source statistic is the maximum count $M_{\max}$.

The null is the design decision this paper exists to document. Our first implementation
scrambled burst days (preserving sidereal phases), the correct null for a periodicity
census, and it promptly ``detected'' the three most intrinsically clustered repeaters in the
catalogue (FRBs 20220912A, 20180916B, 20201124A, $M_{\max}$ up to 82). Dense activity epochs
put many burst pairs at near-integer sidereal-day separations within a loose tolerance, and a
day-scramble destroys exactly that structure, so the comparison answers the wrong question.
The correct null for a fixed-delay signature keeps each burst's day and permutes the sidereal
phases among bursts (re-applying each day's barycentric offset, which varies by $<$0.01\,s
across a transit window): per-day burst counts survive exactly, and what is destroyed is
only the sub-window coherence that lensing, and nothing intrinsic, enforces. With the
phase-permutation null and the 5-s tolerance, the same three sources are clean nulls, and
synthetic lensed trains (each burst duplicated at a fixed barycentric $+\Delta$; magnification
ratio applied against the source's empirical fluence floor; topocentric transit-window
detectability enforced; image DMs drawn with the source's own fit scatter so the DM cut costs
injections what it costs real pairs) are recovered at the scramble floor. The injection grid
also exposes the transit-survey selection function honestly: a delay that is not an integer
number of sidereal days (within the $\sim$15-min window) puts every trailing image outside
the beam, so those cells are dark and the quoted limit is scoped to the sensitive region.

\section{Results}
Zero of \flRealNSearched\ searched repeaters show a significant recurring delay (all
$p>0.8$ against 200 phase scrambles; $p=(k{+}1)/(n{+}1)$). With \flRealNDet\ detections the 95\% Poisson upper
limit on the mean lensed count is 2.996, and the fraction follows on dividing by the summed
injection efficiency:
\begin{equation}
f < \frac{2.996}{\sum_i \epsilon_i} = \flRealLimit,
\end{equation}
applicable within the mapped region: delays at integer sidereal days out to $\sim$26\,d,
magnification ratios to 0.02.

The denominator matters more than the numerator here. Dividing instead by the source count
gives \flRealLimitNaive, which is what this analysis reported before the efficiency was
measured for anything but the deepest train; that naive figure is wrong by a factor of four,
in the optimistic direction. Injecting into every searched train, at the census's own
detection threshold ($p \le \flRealDetP$, not the fivefold looser one the map previously
used), gives a mean efficiency of \flRealEpsMean\ and $\sum_i \epsilon_i = \flRealEpsSum$
against \flRealNSearched\ sources: the best train reaches \flRealEpsMax, and \flRealEpsZero\
sources have $\epsilon = 0$, too few bursts for any injected image train to beat the
phase-scramble null, so they contribute no constraint at all while inflating a count-based
denominator. A limit is a statement about how many lensed systems could have hidden in the
search, and a source the search cannot see into hides all of them. This is the first
empirical statement about the $\sim$$10^{-4}$ optical-depth expectation, and is consistent with it
\citep{lensreview}.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
Three things are worth keeping. (i) The limit itself: within the mapped delay and
magnification region, the lensed fraction of a repeater sample is below \flRealLimit\ per
searched repeater at 95\% confidence, which is relevant to anyone using repeater counts
\citep{cook2026} as population inputs. (ii) The selection function: a transit survey is nearly blind to lensed repeaters
whose delays are not near-integer multiples of the sidereal day; an all-sky or multi-site
monitor \citep[e.g.\ the science case in][]{lensreview} removes this limitation, and the same
machinery applies unchanged. (iii) The methods lessons, stated because we encountered them
directly: for fixed-delay searches, day-scrambling nulls give false positives on intrinsic
clustering, whereas phase-permutation nulls are the correct control, and the difference
between the two is three spurious detections versus a clean census on identical data.
Topocentric TOAs must be barycentered before any seconds-tolerance delay matching, since the
annual Roemer term is $\sim$30$\times$ our tolerance at month-scale delays.
The per-source $M_{\max}$ and null distributions ship with the pipeline.
Catalog 3 and the CHIME outriggers, which will
provide better TOAs and more repeaters, should sharpen the limit mechanically.
