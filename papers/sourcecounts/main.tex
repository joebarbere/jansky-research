\title{Recovering the Canonical 1.4\,GHz Euclidean-Normalised Source Counts from {NVSS}:
A Reproducible Pipeline}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
The differential source count $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S$ (how many radio sources there are per unit
flux per unit sky) is one of the oldest cosmological tests in radio astronomy: a static Euclidean
universe gives $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S\propto S^{-5/2}$, and the departures of the real counts from
that law trace the cosmological evolution of the radio-source population. We build a small tested tool
that forms the Euclidean-normalised count $S^{5/2}\,\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S$ from a public NVSS
\citep{condon1998} region and compares it, bin by bin, to the polynomial fit of
\citet{hopkins2003}. Applied to an \scArea~sr cone at high Galactic latitude (\scNsrc\ NVSS
catalogue components above \scSmin~mJy), the measured counts match the Hopkins fit over
$\sim$2.5 decades in flux (\scSmin~mJy--1~Jy, \scNbins\ fixed 0.2-dex bins, all committed) at a
median ratio of $\scRatio\pm\scRatioErr$, consistent with unity, with the $\pm$
carrying Poisson resampling and the normalisation further subject to
\scCvLo--\scCvHi\% cosmic variance for a field this size. The agreement is a statistic, not an
impression: weighting by the per-bin Poisson errors and the Hopkins fit's own quoted 0.04-dex
residual gives $\chi^2=\scChiRef$ for \scNbins\ bins, and the bin-to-bin scatter
(\scScatter~dex) matches its computed budget (\scPoissonDex\ Poisson $\oplus$ 0.04 reference
$=\scExpectedDex$). The differential slope, $\scSlope\pm\scSlopeErr$, is shallower than the
Euclidean $-2.5$ and matches the reference put through the identical estimator (\scSlopeRef),
the sub-Euclidean behaviour \citet{condon1984} modelled. This is a reproducibility/method
contribution and a recover-a-known, not a new measurement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Source counts were the first radio evidence for cosmological evolution, established over the
1950s--70s; \citet{condon1984} synthesised the 1.4~GHz differential counts into evolutionary
models of the sub-Euclidean behaviour (a log--log slope shallower than $-5/2$) over the sub-Jy
range, requiring a population of radio galaxies and quasars more numerous or luminous in the
past. The modern compilation \citep{dezotti2010} traces the counts from microjansky star-forming
galaxies to the bright radio-loud AGN. The standard way to display them is the
Euclidean-normalised count $S^{5/2}\,\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S$, which divides out the steep
Euclidean slope so a non-evolving population is a flat horizontal line and the real structure stands
out. We ask the most reproducible version of the classic exercise: with the public NVSS catalogue and a
small tested tool, can one recover the canonical 1.4~GHz Euclidean-normalised counts, with the
statistics handled honestly?

\section{Methods}
\code{sourcecounts} is a pure-NumPy/astropy module that reuses the \code{jansky.sourcecounts} helpers
(\code{differential\_counts}, \code{euclidean\_normalised\_counts}, \code{count\_slope}) wholesale; the
new code is the NVSS region fetch, the solid-angle normalisation, and the published reference curve. It
cuts the sample at a completeness cut, bins it on a fixed 0.2-dex logarithmic grid anchored at
the cut (fixed, because binning out to the brightest source made every reported statistic a
function of one Poisson-random object), forms $\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S$ with Poisson errors,
divides by the cone solid angle $2\pi(1-\cos\theta)$ to get a count per steradian, and
Euclidean-normalises. The differential slope and the ratio to the \citet{hopkins2003} polynomial
are computed only over bins that contain at least five sources and whose centre lies below 1~Jy
(the published validity limit of that fit); in this field only the 1-Jy rule binds: every
sub-Jy bin holds well over five sources. The per-bin errors are used, not just drawn: the
agreement is quantified as a $\chi^2$, both against the errors alone and with the reference
fit's own 0.04-dex residual folded in. A slope shallower than $-2.5$ is equivalent to a
Euclidean-normalised count that rises with $S$, so the sub-Euclidean counts climb from faint
fluxes toward the $\sim$1~Jy bright-end peak. Two tests pin the estimator: a closed-form
power-law sky ($\mathrm{d}N/\mathrm{d}S = k\,S^{-5/2}$ over a known area) must return the
normalisation $k$ in absolute units (the test that fails if the solid angle, the $S^{5/2}$
factor, or the bin-width division is wrong), and a synthetic sky drawn from the Hopkins count
round-trips the binning chain (that second check shares the reference with the comparison, so it
constrains the chain, not the reference).

\section{Data}
We use the NRAO VLA Sky Survey \citep{condon1998} via VizieR (VIII/65): 1.4~GHz integrated flux
densities for the radio sources north of Dec~$-40^\circ$ at $45''$ resolution. Two properties of
the sample are stated rather than assumed. First, an NVSS catalogue row is a component,
not a source: the beam splits extended sources, and the committed friends-of-friends merging
sweep bounds the effect (linking at $60''$/$80''$/$100''$ and summing fluxes moves the median
ratio to \scFofSixtyRatio/\scFofEightyRatio/\scFofHundredRatio, at most $\sim$3\%, with the blind merge
over-merging by construction); this is also a convention match, since above 2.5~mJy the Hopkins
curve is anchored to FIRST counts, i.e.\ both sides of the comparison count components. Second,
the survey's nominal 2.5~mJy is a $\sim$50\%-completeness point, not a completeness limit: the
committed fine-bin profile of this cone shows the 2.1--2.5~mJy bin at 0.59 of the reference,
and a near-threshold residual (flux boosting and resolution effects) persisting at the
$\sim$10--15\% level into the \scSmin--5~mJy range before dying away by $\sim$6~mJy. We therefore
cut at \scSmin~mJy knowing the first bin still carries part of that residual
(the fine-bin value there is \scFirstBinResid), and commit a cut sweep showing the headline
ratio stable to $\sim$1\% for cuts from \scSmin\ to 20~mJy. The surface density above the cut is
uniform to $\pm$\scAnnulusSpreadPct\% across four equal-area annuli, so there is no sensitivity
gradient across the $8^\circ$ footprint. The query cone sits at $(180^\circ, +30^\circ)$,
$b\approx+80^\circ$ (high Galactic latitude, so the sample is extragalactic).

\section{Results}
The cone holds \scNsrc\ NVSS components above \scSmin~mJy over \scArea~sr
(Figure~\ref{fig:counts}; every bin's centre, count, normalised count, error, reference, and
ratio is committed with the results). The Euclidean-normalised counts match the canonical
Hopkins fit over \scNbins\ bins spanning \scSmin~mJy to 1~Jy at a median ratio of
$\scRatio\pm\scRatioErr$, consistent with unity. The agreement budget is computed, and each
term lands where it acts. On the normalisation: Poisson contributes
\scPoissonNormPct\% and large-scale clustering \scCvLo--\scCvHi\% (from the published NVSS
$w(\theta)$, Monte-Carlo integrated over this cone; \citealt{blake2002}), so the measured
$\sim$1\% offset from unity is unremarkable: cosmic variance moves every flux bin together
and belongs here, not in the bin-to-bin scatter. On the scatter: the observed
\scScatter~dex matches the computed budget of \scPoissonDex\ (Poisson, from the committed bin
counts) in quadrature with the Hopkins fit's own quoted 0.04-dex residual, $=\scExpectedDex$.
Weighted by errors alone, $\chi^2=\scChiUnity$ for \scNbins\ bins (the counts are better than
the reference fit's nominal errors); with the reference residual folded in,
$\chi^2=\scChiRef$; the word ``match'' is earned by that number. The differential slope is
$\scSlope\pm\scSlopeErr$ (Poisson bootstrap), against \scSlopeRef\ for the Hopkins curve put
through the identical estimator on the identical bins, so the slope, too, is a
recover-a-known, not just ``shallower than $-2.5$'' (the slope is interval-dependent, steepening
to $-2.07$ above a 20-mJy cut, per the committed sweep); this sub-Euclidean behaviour
(equivalently, the normalised counts rising toward the $\sim$1~Jy peak) is the
cosmological-evolution signature the classic literature established \citep{condon1984}.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is method and reproducibility: a tested tool that recovers the canonical 1.4~GHz
Euclidean-normalised source counts from the public NVSS catalogue, with the statistics computed
rather than asserted. The honest limitations: (i) the Hopkins polynomial is itself a fit to a
multi-survey compilation, FIRST-anchored over this paper's entire comparison range, so the
agreement validates the pipeline and the NVSS data against that compilation, not new physics;
(ii) a single \scArea~sr field carries \scCvLo--\scCvHi\% cosmic variance on the
normalisation, so the median ratio is a field-specific figure at the percent level; (iii) the
sample is unmerged catalogue components with a measured near-threshold residual in the first
bin (both bounded by the committed sweeps in \S3, at the few-percent level on the headline);
(iv) above 1~Jy the cone is Poisson-starved and outside the Hopkins fit, so those bins are
plotted but excluded from the comparison. None of these undermine the recover-a-known: the
canonical counts come back out of a public catalogue with a closed error budget. These
limitations do, however, bound what a single NVSS cone can claim. Every number above is
written by the pipeline into the macros this paper \code{\textbackslash input}s, and
\code{python -m jansky\_research.sourcecounts -\,-ra 180 -\,-dec 30 -\,-radius 8} regenerates them from
VizieR.
