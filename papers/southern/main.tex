\title{Measuring the Turnover: A Southern Peaked-Spectrum Catalogue from GLEAM-X and RACS
Multi-band Spectral Curvature}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Gigahertz-Peaked-Spectrum (GPS) and Compact-Steep-Spectrum (CSS) sources are compact, young radio AGN
whose spectra rise then fall \citep{odea1998,odea2021}, best found where many frequencies pin the
turnover. A companion northern selection was limited by shallow 150~MHz data, which forced the
low-frequency point to be an upper limit and so could only bound the spectral index, never measure the
turnover frequency \nupk. We remove that limitation in the southern sky. GLEAM-X DR2 \citep{rossdr2}
measures each source in 19 sub-bands across 76--227~MHz; combined with the three RACS bands (0.89--1.66~GHz;
\citealt{racs2020,racshale2021,racsmid2024,racshigh2025}) a source has up to 22 flux points over
a factor of $\sim$22 in frequency, with the catalogue's own per-sub-band uncertainties setting
the fit weights and a $3\sigma$ per-point detection requirement. We fit a weighted log-parabola
to each SED. Over a $3\arcdeg$ cone (\soRealNmatched\ GLEAM-X$\cap$RACS sources) the selection
cascade is committed at every stage: \soRealNpeakedNaive\ concave in-band fits, cut to
\soRealNpeakedRising\ by requiring a significantly rising or flat optically-thick side, and to
\soRealNpeaked\ peaked candidates by a compactness cut against the GLEAM$\to$RACS resolution
mismatch (median $\nupk\approx\soRealMedianNupk$~MHz, median reduced
$\chi^2=\soRealMedianChisq$; \soRealGapFrac\ of the turnovers are interpolations across the
unsampled 0.23--0.89~GHz gap), plus \soRealNuss\ ultra-steep-spectrum sources, all published as
a machine-readable table. The candidate density, \soRealDensity~deg$^{-2}$, sits within
$\sim$20\% of the Bayesian RadioSED~II Stripe-82 density \citep{kerrison2025} — an indication,
not a controlled comparison. Against the Callingham et al.\ (2017) sample:
\soRealCallRecovered\ of \soRealCallTried\ covered sources are recovered as peaked, with the
measured \nupk\ matching the published value to a median $\soRealCallDlog$~dex
(\soRealCallWithin\% within a factor of two; unconditionally,
\soRealCallWithinTwoOfTested\% of all tested land within a factor of two). This is a
reproducible, measured-turnover scout catalogue and methodology, not a discovery catalogue.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A turnover in a radio spectrum marks the synchrotron self-absorbed or free--free-absorbed core of a
compact, young radio AGN \citep{odea1998,odea2021}. Selecting these GPS/CSS sources and measuring the
turnover frequency \nupk, which encodes the absorbing column and source size, needs dense frequency
coverage on both sides of the peak. In the north, a three-frequency method (150~MHz, 1.4~GHz, 3~GHz)
had to use the shallow 150~MHz survey as an upper limit and so could only bound the low-frequency
index, ``rising then falling'', but with no measured \nupk. The southern sky removes that limitation:
GLEAM-X DR2 \citep{rossdr2}, the deep extension of GLEAM \citep{gleam2017}, samples 76--227~MHz in 19 clean
sub-bands, and RACS \citep{racs2020} adds three bands up to 1.66~GHz. We ask the weakest, most
reproducible version of the question: with these public catalogues and a small tool, how cleanly can
one select peaked-spectrum candidates and measure their turnovers?

\section{Methods}
\code{southern} is a pure-NumPy module that composes earlier tools (the \code{jansky} helpers;
\citealt{jansky}): the two-point spectral index, positional cross-matching, and the classification of
a companion peaked-spectrum slice. Its new capability is \code{fit\_log\_parabola}, a weighted
least-squares fit of $\log_{10}S = a\,x^2+b\,x+c$ with $x=\log_{10}\nu$ to the full multi-band SED,
using the catalogues' own per-point uncertainties and admitting only $\ge3\sigma$ detections
(a sub-threshold point is not a detection, and keeping only its positive noise excursions — the
log fit cannot take negatives — would censor the noise asymmetrically and manufacture a rising
low-frequency side). We note GLEAM-X DR2 itself publishes a per-source curved-SED fit to
227~MHz; the new lever arm here is the extension through the three RACS bands to 1.66~GHz. The
turnover is $\nupk=10^{-b/2a}$ and is a genuine (concave) peak when $a<0$; we require it to lie
within the fitted band. Two systematics, exposed by the real data, require the fixes that make
the result trustworthy.

\paragraph{Units.} GLEAM-X DR2 integrated fluxes are in Jy while RACS fluxes are in mJy; we convert
GLEAM-X to mJy ($\times10^3$) before building the SED. Uncaught, this $10^3$ offset fakes a steeply
rising spectrum into RACS for every source.

\paragraph{Resolution mismatch.} GLEAM's $2\arcmin$ beam recovers extended emission that RACS
($\sim$15--25\arcsec) resolves out, so the flux drops from GLEAM to RACS and fakes a high-frequency
turnover. A compactness cut (the GLEAM-X integrated-to-peak ratio $<\soCompactMax$) flags these as extended, the
southern analogue of the northern method's resolution floor.

\paragraph{Optically-thick rising side.} An ordinary steep source with mild low-frequency flattening
fits a shallow parabola peaking spuriously at the GLEAM band edge. A genuine peaked source is
optically thick below the turnover, so we require the \emph{fitted} GLEAM in-band index — a
weighted fit over all $\ge3\sigma$ sub-bands, not a two-point ratio anchored on the noisy 76~MHz
band — to be significantly rising or flat: $\alo-\sigma_{\alo}>-0.1$, the GPS/SSA signature. An
earlier version used the two-point 76-vs-227~MHz ratio with an assumed 10\% error; since the
76~MHz noise floor in this field is $\sim$7--8~mJy regardless of source brightness, that gate
selected on noise for the faint majority, and moving its anchor by one sub-band flipped half the
displayed candidates.

Every function is tested against a synthetic 19-sub-band GLEAM-X $+$ 3-band RACS field with
injected peaked / steep / USS SEDs \emph{plus} the contaminants each cut exists to reject — a
below-band-peak flattening class for the rising-side gate, an extended class with
resolution-suppressed RACS fluxes for the compactness cut, and per-band noise floors shaped like
the real catalogue's — so the suite can fail on either cut, and runs offline; the real run
fetches GLEAM-X DR2 (VizieR) and the three RACS catalogues.

\section{Data}
We use GLEAM-X DR2 \citep{rossdr2} (the 19 clean \code{Fint} sub-bands with their
\code{e\_Fint} uncertainties; the 92~MHz column is mislabelled in VizieR and dropped) and the
RACS-low/mid/high integrated fluxes \citep{racshale2021,racsmid2024,racshigh2025}, cross-matched
within 25\arcsec\ on a $3\arcdeg$ cone at high Galactic latitude. The cross-match is
nearest-neighbour and therefore not bijective in principle; in this field no RACS source is
claimed by more than one GLEAM-X source (\soRealNracsMulti\ multiple matches, committed with the
results), though a GLEAM-scale source resolved into several RACS components remains handled only
by the compactness cut.

\section{Results}
Over the field (\soRealNmatched\ GLEAM-X$\cap$RACS sources) the selection cascade, committed at
every stage in the results, runs \soRealNpeakedNaive\ (concave in-band fit) $\to$
\soRealNpeakedRising\ (significantly rising or flat optically-thick side) $\to$ \soRealNpeaked\
peaked-spectrum candidates after the compactness cut, with a measured median
$\nupk\approx\soRealMedianNupk$~MHz and median reduced $\chi^2=\soRealMedianChisq$.
\soRealGapFrac\ of the turnovers fall between the GLEAM band top and RACS-low, where \nupk\ is an
interpolation across the unsampled 0.23--0.89~GHz gap rather than a sampled peak; the
machine-readable table (\code{results/southern\_candidates.csv}: positions, classes, \nupk,
$\alo\pm\sigma$, $\chi^2$, point counts for all \soRealNmatched\ sources) records which. The
selection also returns \soRealNuss\ ultra-steep-spectrum sources ($\alpha<\soUssThresh$ throughout;
candidate high-$z$ radio galaxies) and flags \soRealNextended\ extended resolution artefacts
(Figure~\ref{fig:seds}).

RadioSED II \citep{kerrison2025}, a Bayesian SSA/FFA fit over Stripe~82
(\soRealKerrisonDensity\ peaked sources~deg$^{-2}$), provides the nearest literature comparison:
over our \soRealArea~deg$^2$ our density is \soRealDensity~deg$^{-2}$, a ratio of
\soRealDensityRatio. The two samples differ in survey depth, frequency coverage, and selection
method, so this is an indication of comparable candidate density, not a controlled purity
measurement. An earlier version of this selection, run with an assumed 10\% per-band error in
place of the catalogue uncertainties, returned more than twice as many candidates at a
$\sim$2.5$\times$ over-density — that excess was the error model, not the sky.

\paragraph{Recovery of known sources.} As a validation, we re-derive the turnover of the Callingham
et al.\ (2017) peaked sources \citep{callingham2017}. This is the catalogue against which the northern
slice could previously demonstrate only purity, because it bounded \nupk\ but never measured it. Of
\soRealCallTried\ Callingham sources tested, all with usable coverage
(\soRealCallCovered; fetch failures are counted separately and there were none),
\soRealCallRecovered\ are recovered as peaked, and the measured \nupk\ of the recovered agrees
with the published value to a median $\soRealCallDlog$~dex (\soRealCallWithin\% within a factor
of two). That statistic is conditional on passing the selection gate; unconditionally, every
covered source with a finite fitted peak (\soRealCallNuncond\ of \soRealCallTried) gives a median
of \soRealCallDlogUncond~dex, and \soRealCallWithinTwoOfTested\% of \emph{all} tested sources
land within a factor of two. The per-bin tested/recovered counts are committed: recovery climbs from
\soRealCallBinLowRecovered/\soRealCallBinLowTested\ below 250~MHz through
\soRealCallBinMidRecovered/\soRealCallBinMidTested\ at 250--500~MHz to
\soRealCallBinHiRecovered/\soRealCallBinHiTested\ above, as expected for a
rising-side-gated method whose window opens with \nupk. This is the literature turnover, measured
rather than bounded, using the southern data.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
Multi-band curvature in the south converts the northern slice's unmeasurable turnover into a measured
one: GLEAM-X's 19 sub-bands fix \nupk\ on the optically-thick side and RACS pins the optically-thin
fall. We provide a reproducible, systematics-aware tool (catalogue errors and detection
thresholds, unit conversion, compactness cut, significant-rising-side requirement), a published
per-source table, and a candidate list validated by recovering the Callingham
\citep{callingham2017} turnovers above. The remaining limits are stated in place: half the
turnovers are interpolations across the 0.23--0.89~GHz gap (a sampled point there — e.g.\ SUMSS
at 843~MHz — would convert them), the density comparison to \citet{kerrison2025} is uncontrolled
across survey depths, and this is a single 28~deg$^2$ field, so the density carries
field-to-field variance no single cone can estimate. Every selection number above is written by
the pipeline into the macros this paper \code{\textbackslash input}s, and the analysis
regenerates from a clean checkout on public data.
