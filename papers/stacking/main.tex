\title{An Image-Plane Stacking Tool with Forced Photometry and an Off-Source Control,
Applied to SDSS Quasars in VLASS}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Most members of an optically-selected quasar sample are fainter than the single-source detection limit
of a radio survey, but their population-level radio flux is measurable by image-plane stacking: at $N$
known positions thermal noise averages down as $N^{-1/2}$ while a coherent signal adds
\citep{white2007,karim2011}. Because the positions are known, the stacked flux must be a forced
measurement at the target pixel; a peak searched even within a small aperture is positive almost
always on beam-correlated noise and biases every stack high. We apply a tested, CPU-only tool to
SDSS DR16 quasars \citep{lyke2020} in VLASS Single-Epoch \citep{lacy2020} 3~GHz cutouts: of
\stNqueried\ queried, \stNwithCutout\ have an SE image, \stNdetExcl\ individually-detected sources
are excluded by a per-cutout $5\sigma$ test, and \stN\ enter the sigma-clipped median stack. The
forced central flux is \stFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ at signal-to-noise \stSNR. Two controls
accompany it: an off-source stack at $30\arcsec$-offset positions through the identical pipeline
gives a forced flux of $\stCtlFlux\pm\stCtlRms$~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$, with no positive
centre-common pedestal, and an injection test that can fail recovers
$\stInjRatio\pm\stInjRatioSD$ of the injected flux, with point sources injected at the measured
amplitude and per-cutout sub-pixel astrometric jitter.
Binned by apparent $i$ magnitude, the optically-brightest third is detected
(\stBrightFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$, \stBrightSNR$\sigma$) while the faintest third is not
(\stFaintFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$, \stFaintSNR$\sigma$). The estimator is a clipped median, and we
report it as one; for a skewed radio-loudness distribution the population mean is larger. This is a
tooling and reproducibility contribution, not a new astrophysical claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Stacking turns a non-detection into a measurement of a population's central radio flux, and has long
been used to probe the sub-millijansky sky behind optically/IR-selected samples
\citep{white2007,karim2011,lindroos2015}. The method's weaknesses are procedural, and this revision
of the tool is built around two of them. First, photometry on a stack made at known positions
must be forced: the maximum within even a 3-pixel search radius on beam-correlated noise reads
$+1.57\sigma$ and is positive $\sim$99\% of the time, so a searched peak converts noise into
signal.\footnote{An earlier version of this work quoted searched peaks throughout; every flux and
signal-to-noise here is a forced central-pixel measurement, and the searched maximum is committed
only as a labelled diagnostic.} Second, a stack needs controls that can fail: an off-source stack
through the identical pipeline, and an injection test whose construction does not guarantee its own
answer. We ask the most reproducible version of the question: with public catalogues, public images,
and a small tested tool, what does a forced-photometry stack of optical quasars in VLASS Single-Epoch
imaging measure, and what do its controls say?

\section{Methods}
\code{stacking} is a pure-NumPy module (on the \code{jansky} helpers; \citealt{jansky}). It
(i) median-stacks $N$ centred cutout stamps with a per-pixel sigma-clip, which rejects the rare
bright neighbour or imaging artefact (\citealt{white2007} preferred the median over the mean for
this reason); (ii) measures the stacked forced central-pixel flux against an annulus RMS: on pure
noise this measurement goes negative about half the time, and a committed test asserts
exactly that; and (iii) runs two independent checks.

\paragraph{The off-source control.} For every science position we fetch a second cutout $30\arcsec$
north and push the control cube through the identical stack-and-measure pipeline. A centred annulus
cannot detect a bias common to the cutout centres (a pedestal, a CLEAN-residual level); an
off-source stack can.

\paragraph{An injection test that can fail.} The previous version injected the same PSF plane into
every cutout and differenced two clipped medians. Sigma-clipping is shift-equivariant, so the clip
mask never changes and the recovered flux equals the injected flux identically, a ratio of
1.0 for any input whatsoever, including pure noise; it measured nothing, and we retract the
``injection-recovery calibration'' framing of the earlier version. The replacement injects each
cutout's point source at an independent random sub-pixel offset (Gaussian, $\sigma=\stInjJitter$
pixels, a documented assumption standing in for catalogue-to-image astrometric scatter), at the
measured stacked amplitude, and reads the forced central pixel above the no-injection
baseline over eight independent jitter draws. Centring and pixelization losses now register, and the
result carries a spread.

\paragraph{Sample cut, implemented.} A stack of ``individually undetected'' sources must exclude the
detected ones by measurement, not by a sentence: per cutout, a searched $5\sigma$ test against that
cutout's own annulus RMS (searching is correct for a detection test) flags and removes
individually-detected sources.

Every function is tested offline, including: forced photometry going negative about half the time
on noise; the injection test failing (ratio $<0.8$) when the population is badly centred; and a
log-normal population fixture for which the clipped-median stack recovers the median while
the mean is $\sim$2.6$\times$ larger. The estimator's meaning is a tested property, not a word
choice.

\section{Data}
The target list is SDSS DR16 quasars \citep{lyke2020} (VizieR) in a \stRadius$^\circ$ cone at
$(\alpha,\delta)=(\stFieldRa^\circ,+\stFieldDec^\circ)$, capped at the \stMaxSources\ VizieR rows
the query returns; the full queried list, with per-target cutout/control/excluded flags, is
committed (\code{results/stacking\_targets.csv}) so the denominator is auditable. For each target we
fetch a VLASS Single-Epoch \citep{lacy2020,gordon2021} 3~GHz Stokes-I cutout from the CADC SODA
service, trimmed to a common-grid stamp. Of \stNqueried\ queried, \stNwithCutout\ have an SE image
and \stNdetExcl\ are individually detected and excluded, leaving \stN\ in the stack.

\section{Results}
The forced central flux of the median stack is \stFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ against an annulus RMS
of \stRms\ (signal-to-noise \stSNR; Figure~\ref{fig:stack}): a stacked detection of a population
far below the VLASS single-source limit. The searched maximum within 3 pixels equals the forced
value (\stPeakSearched~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$), as it should when a real source sits at the stack
centre. The off-source control stack (\stCtlN\ positions) gives a forced flux of
$\stCtlFlux\pm\stCtlRms$~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ ($\stCtlSNR\sigma$): there is no positive
centre-common pedestal, so the science signal is not a pipeline artefact. The mildly negative
control is consistent with zero at the $\sim$2$\sigma$ level; to the extent it is real, positions
$30\arcsec$ from real sources sample the negative CLEAN-residual environment of their neighbours,
which would make the science flux conservative rather than inflated. The jittered injection test
recovers $\stInjRatio\pm\stInjRatioSD$ of the injected flux: under the assumed
$\stInjJitter$-pixel astrometric scatter, the forced measurement underestimates a true centred
median by $\sim$8\%. We quote the uncorrected measurement and this ratio separately rather than
folding an assumption into the primary measurement.

\paragraph{Binned by apparent magnitude.} Splitting into \stNbins\ equal-count bins of SDSS
$i$-band magnitude (Figure~\ref{fig:stack}, middle): the optically-brightest third is clearly
detected (\stBrightFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ at \stBrightSNR$\sigma$, median $i\simeq\stBrightMag$),
the middle third marginally, and the faintest third is a non-detection
(\stFaintFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ at \stFaintSNR$\sigma$, $i\simeq\stFaintMag$). We do not quote a
bright-to-faint flux ratio: the denominator is consistent with zero. Nor do we read the run as the
radio--optical luminosity correlation: these are apparent magnitudes over a wide redshift
range ($z\sim0.9$--$2.6$ across the bins), so distance and luminosity are entangled, and the binned
run is reported as a demonstration of the machinery on observed-frame quantities.

\paragraph{Binned by redshift.} Re-binning the same cutouts by redshift (Figure~\ref{fig:stack},
right): the observed-frame median flux is comparable in the two lower-$z$ bins
(\stLowzFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ at $z\simeq\stLowzZ$) and undetected in the highest
(\stHighzFlux~$\mu$Jy\,beam$^{-1}$ at $z\simeq\stHighzZ$, $0.9\sigma$). An observed-frame flux
folds luminosity distance, $K$-correction, and evolution together; this is machinery, not an
evolutionary measurement.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
The contribution is tooling and reproducibility: a stacking pipeline whose photometry is forced,
whose sample cut is implemented rather than asserted, and whose two controls (an off-source
stack and a jittered injection) are each capable of returning a failing answer. The estimator is
a sigma-clipped median: for a skewed radio-loudness distribution the population mean is
larger (in the committed log-normal fixture, $\sim$2.6$\times$), so this number is the population's
central flux, not its mean, and the noise on a median is $\approx1.25\sigma/\sqrt{N}$ rather than
$\sigma/\sqrt{N}$. Several limitations remain. The result depends on SE coverage at the target
positions, the injection ratio is conditional on an assumed astrometric scatter, the control offset
samples the environment $30\arcsec$ from quasars rather than arbitrary blank sky, and the binned
runs are observed-frame demonstrations. Every number above is written by the pipeline into the macros this
paper \code{\textbackslash input}s, and the analysis regenerates from a clean checkout
(\code{python -m jansky\_research.stacking --ra \stFieldRa\ --dec \stFieldDec\ --radius \stRadius}).
