\title{A Reproducible Three-Frequency Curvature Selection for Peaked-Spectrum Radio Sources:
TGSS, NVSS, and VLASS, with Two Recover-a-Known Validations}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
Gigahertz-Peaked-Spectrum (GPS) and Compact-Steep-Spectrum (CSS) sources are compact, young radio
AGN whose spectrum rises then falls, peaking in the $\sim$0.1--3~GHz band \citep{odea1998,odea2021}.
Identifying them needs at least three frequencies to see the turnover. We build a small, tested, CPU-only
tool that selects peaked-spectrum candidates from three public continuum surveys (TGSS
150~MHz \citep{intema2017}, NVSS 1.4~GHz \citep{condon1998}, and VLASS 3~GHz \citep{lacy2020,
gordon2021}) by their spectral curvature. The \ahi\ arm never uses TGSS, so it is unaffected by a constant
TGSS flux-scale offset, though the candidate count is set by the \alo\ arm's assumed limit,
which is treated below. The selector
uses a low-frequency index (\alo, 150$\to$1400~MHz) minus a
high-frequency index (\ahi, 1400$\to$3000~MHz). A naive ``detected in all
three, rising then falling'' selection returns zero candidates; we show this is two selection
effects with clean fixes. (i) Peaked sources are faint at 150~MHz by definition, so a TGSS detection
excludes them; we treat a non-detection as an upper limit $S_{150}<$ the field's local
$7\sigma$ floor, a lower bound on \alo.
(ii) The NVSS$\to$VLASS resolution mismatch (45\arcsec\ versus 2.5\arcsec) fakes steep turnovers for
extended sources; a floor $\ahi>-2$ rejects \pkRealNextended\ of \pkRealNnvssvlass\ in the field.
The upper-limit trick makes the candidate count limit-dominated, so the limit is taken from the
field itself (the TGSS local-rms column gives a median $7\sigma$ floor of
\pkRealLimitUsed~mJy in this cone), and the count is committed across a limit sweep rather than
quoted at one assumed value. Over a $2\arcdeg$ cone the method retains \pkRealNpeaked\ source
as peaked and \pkRealNghzpeaked\ as GHz-peaked (still rising at 3~GHz, a class this field
gained when the VLASS flux-scale correction was applied); every rising candidate is published
with fluxes, both indices and their errors, and SIMBAD/NED identifications, the vetting running
in the committed pipeline. Under the looser survey-median limit the list grows to include a
known \textit{Fermi} BL~Lac that only the catalogue cross-ID identifies, so blazar rejection
needs the cross-ID (single-survey epoch variability at the VLASS flux-scale floor excludes
nothing). Two literature comparisons calibrate the selection function: against
the GLEAM-selected, MHz-peaked Callingham et al.\ (2017) sample it flags
\pkRealCallLowFlagged\ of \pkRealCallLowTotal\ sources with $\nu_{\rm pk}<250$~MHz and
\pkRealCallMidFlagged/\pkRealCallMidTotal\ at 250--500~MHz (its own claimed window); against
the Dallacasa et al.\ (2000) High-Frequency-Peaker candidate list, using each source's
measured TGSS flux (\pkRealHfpTgssDet\ of \pkRealHfpN\ are TGSS-detected; an earlier version
substituted the survey limit for all of them, which made the test a flux cut that could not
fail), \pkRealHfpRising\% are optically-thick-rising and \pkRealHfpGhz\% still rising at 3~GHz,
while \pkRealHfpFallingLow\% fall from 150~MHz to 1.4~GHz. This is a tooling and methodology
contribution with a published candidate list, not a discovery.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The radio spectra of most extragalactic sources are simple power laws; a small population
turns over, rising at low frequency and falling at high, marking the synchrotron
self-absorbed or free--free-absorbed cores of compact, young radio AGN, the GPS and CSS sources
\citep{odea1998,odea2021}. They are signposts of the earliest phase of jet activity, so clean samples
matter. But a turnover cannot be seen with two frequencies: a single steep or inverted index is
degenerate with a flux-scale error between surveys. The ultra-steep-spectrum exercise that preceded this work ended
exactly there: a 150~MHz/1.4~GHz index alone cannot separate a real turnover from
a TGSS flux-scale artefact, and confirming a turnover needs a third frequency. This is that
follow-up. With a third frequency (VLASS 3~GHz) we measure curvature and ask the weakest,
most reproducible version of the question: how cleanly can one select peaked-spectrum candidates
from public catalogues alone, and what does the method actually recover?

\section{Methods}
\code{peaked} is a pure-NumPy module that composes two earlier tools rather than reimplementing them
(the \code{jansky} helpers; \citealt{jansky}): the two-point spectral index with errors, positional
cross-matching, and the survey cone-search/fetch from a spectral-index module, and the VLASS 3~GHz
peak-flux fetch and multi-epoch variability metrics from a VLASS module. For each NVSS source we form
\begin{equation}
\alpha = \frac{\ln(S_2/S_1)}{\ln(\nu_2/\nu_1)},
\end{equation}
twice: \alo\ over 150$\to$1400~MHz and \ahi\ over 1400$\to$3000~MHz. The curvature $\ahi-\alo$ and the
signs of the two indices classify the SED: peaked (\alo\ rising, \ahi\ falling, a turnover
in band), ghz\_peaked (both rising, a High-Frequency Peaker whose turnover is above 3~GHz),
steep, flat, or an extended resolution artefact. Two systematics, exposed by the
real data, require fixes that are the heart of the method.

\paragraph{TGSS as an upper limit.} Peaked sources are faint at 150~MHz, so requiring a TGSS detection
selects against the target population; this is why the naive triple-detection cut returns
nothing. We instead anchor on NVSS$\cap$VLASS and treat a TGSS non-detection as $S_{150}<25$~mJy (the
survey's $\sim$7$\sigma$ floor; \citealt{intema2017}), which gives a lower bound on \alo: a
source brighter at 1.4~GHz than the 150~MHz limit, yet undetected at 150~MHz, must be rising.
This can select only peaked sources brighter than $\sim$30~mJy at 1.4~GHz, an honest depth limit, not
a measured turnover frequency.

\paragraph{Resolution floor.} NVSS (45\arcsec) recovers extended emission that VLASS (2.5\arcsec)
resolves out, manufacturing impossible spectra (e.g.\ 690$\to$6~mJy, $\ahi=-6$) that masquerade as
sharp turnovers. A floor $\ahi>-2$ rejects them; \pkRealNextended\ of \pkRealNnvssvlass\ sources in the field
are flagged extended/artefact. Because curvature compares two indices and \ahi\ never uses TGSS,
the \ahi\ arm is insensitive to the flux-scale offset that sank the two-point steep cut.

Every function is tested against a synthetic three-survey field with injected peaked/steep/flat SEDs,
so the suite runs offline; the real run adds NED/SIMBAD vetting and a VLASS-variability blazar flag.

\section{Data}
We use TGSS ADR1 150~MHz \citep{intema2017}, NVSS 1.4~GHz \citep{condon1998}, and the VLASS Epoch-1
Quick-Look catalogue at 3~GHz \citep{gordon2021}, cross-matched within 15\arcsec\ on a $2\arcdeg$ cone
(\pkRealNnvssvlass\ NVSS$\cap$VLASS sources). VLASS Epoch-1 Quick-Look peak fluxes
underestimate the true flux by $\sim$13\% \citep{gordon2021}; we apply the same
Perley--Butler-scale correction (and 7\% cross-epoch scatter term) as this toolkit's VLASS
variability slice, without which every \ahi\ is low by 0.16. The TGSS detection limit is
field-local: ADR1's per-source local rms in this cone has median \pkRealNoiseMed~mJy/beam
(maximum \pkRealNoiseMax), giving the $7\sigma$ floor of \pkRealLimitUsed~mJy the selection
uses; the committed metrics carry the candidate count across a sweep of defensible limits
(\pkRealLimitSweep), because the count is limit-dominated. The comparison samples are the
Callingham et al.\ (2017) peaked-spectrum catalogue \citep{callingham2017} and the Dallacasa
et al.\ (2000) High-Frequency-Peaker candidate list \citep{dallacasa2000} (their
Table~1 is the VLA target list, pre-selected on an inverted NVSS$\to$87GB index), each
augmented with corrected VLASS 3~GHz and, for Dallacasa, each source's measured TGSS flux where
detected.

\section{Results}
\paragraph{Field candidates.} At the field-local limit the selection retains
\pkRealNpeaked\ peaked and \pkRealNghzpeaked\ GHz-peaked source (the latter appearing once
the VLASS flux-scale correction is applied; its corrected $\ahi=+0.23$ sat at $+0.07$
uncorrected), shown in the spectral-curvature plane of Figure~\ref{fig:curv}; every rising
candidate, including two extreme resolution artefacts kept for reference, is published with
positions, fluxes, both indices with their propagated errors, and SIMBAD/NED identifications in
\code{results/peaked\_candidates.csv}; the vetting runs in the committed pipeline, not as a
side analysis. Each candidate's \alo\ is a lower limit (no TGSS detection), so its turnover is
bounded, not measured, and no compactness is claimed beyond the VLASS component match. The count
is limit-dominated, and the committed sweep says so plainly: 8 candidates at an assumed 20~mJy
limit, 6 at the survey-median 25~mJy an earlier version used, \pkRealNpeaked\ at this field's
own median $7\sigma$ floor, and 0 at its worst-case floor. The earlier six-candidate list,
which included a known \textit{Fermi} BL~Lac that only the catalogue cross-ID identified, was
therefore mostly a statement about the assumed limit, and the cross-ID lesson survives while
the list does not.

\paragraph{Comparison I: MHz-peaked sources.} The Callingham et al.\ (2017) catalogue
is GLEAM-selected (70--230~MHz) and dominated by MHz-peaked sources whose turnover lies below
the 150~MHz floor of this method; across 150~MHz--3~GHz those sources are simply falling and the
method flags \pkRealCallLowFlagged\ of \pkRealCallLowTotal\ below 250~MHz. We note the
honest scope of that number: those sources are all TGSS-detected with strongly falling measured
indices (median $\alo\approx-0.55$ against a $+0.1$ cut), so their rejection restates the
sample rather than stresses the boundary. The informative bins are the higher ones, committed
in full: \pkRealCallMidFlagged/\pkRealCallMidTotal\ at 250--500~MHz and
\pkRealCallHiFlagged/\pkRealCallHiTotal\ at 500--1000~MHz, the recovery inside the method's
own claimed $\sim$0.7--2~GHz window, previously computed and left out of the committed
evidence.

\paragraph{Comparison II: the Dallacasa HFP candidate list.} Over \pkRealHfpN\ Dallacasa
et al.\ (2000) sources with corrected VLASS fluxes, using each source's \emph{measured} TGSS
flux (\pkRealHfpTgssDet\ are TGSS-detected, some at Jy level; the limit is used only for the
rest), \pkRealHfpRising\% are optically-thick-rising from 150~MHz and \pkRealHfpGhz\% still
rising at 3~GHz, while \pkRealHfpFallingLow\% fall from 150~MHz to 1.4~GHz. An earlier
version of this test substituted the TGSS survey limit for every source, under which
``rising'' reduced to an NVSS flux cut the sample (faintest member 60.6~mJy) could not fail,
and it reported 100\%. The corrected numbers say something weaker and more interesting: a
list pre-selected on a 1.4$\to$4.85~GHz inverted index is heterogeneous at 150~MHz, and this
method's classes separate its genuinely still-rising members from those already turning over.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
Three-frequency curvature turns the unfinished business of a two-point steep cut into a
maximal-reuse peaked-spectrum selector that separates resolution artefacts and publishes its
candidates with errors and identifications. The honest scope of its calibration: the
\ahi\ arm is TGSS-independent, but the candidate count is set by the assumed TGSS limit
through the \alo\ arm, which is why the limit is measured from the field and the count is
committed across a sweep; and the two literature comparisons probe the regimes far from the
selection boundary (TGSS-detected steep sources; bright pre-selected risers), so the regime
that actually produces the candidates (TGSS non-detections within $\sim$30\% of the effective
floor) is calibrated by neither, and the candidates are correspondingly labelled candidates.
Confirming any as a true GPS source needs higher-resolution or more-frequency follow-up (VLBI
compactness, a sampled turnover). The method is depth-limited (the upper-limit trick can only
select sources brighter than $\sim$$e^{0.225}$ times the local limit at 1.4~GHz), and its
turnover is bounded rather than measured for TGSS non-detections. Every selection and
comparison number above is written by the pipeline into the macros this manuscript
\code{\textbackslash input}s, and the analysis regenerates from a clean checkout on public
data.
