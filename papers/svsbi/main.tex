\begin{abstract}
Neural simulation-based inference (SBI) has been applied to the populations of radio pulsars
\citep{graber2024,pardoaraujo2024} and magnetars \citep{sautron2025}, but not to the coherent,
circularly-polarized radio emission of M dwarfs, whose population parameters (the radio
luminosity function and the beaming fraction, the fraction of coherent emitters that point a
detectable beam at us) are known only qualitatively \citep{callingham2021,pritchard2021}. We
present the first such inference, built on a RACS Stokes-V census of \svbRealNTargets\ nearby M
dwarfs with two-epoch photometry (of 60 targeted; the rest were lost to archive-access failures
rather than to non-detection, a selection the inference treats as independent of source
brightness without being able to test it), of which \svbRealNDet\ is a confident detection, the
single unresolved GJ 65 system. A forward model draws a coherent-emitter population (a power-law
luminosity function with an exponential break, plus a beaming fraction), folds it through the
real per-target distances, Stokes-V noise, and instrumental-leakage floor of the census, and a
neural posterior estimator \citep{papamakarios2016,sbi_toolkit} infers the three population
parameters. The forward model and its summary statistics pass monotonicity recover-a-knowns, and
the trained posterior passes simulation-based calibration \citep[SBC;][]{talts2018} at the formal
Kolmogorov threshold (max KS $= \svbRealKsMax$ vs the 95\% critical value $\svbRealKsCrit$ at 150
trials), consistent with nominal coverage, though at that sample size the test cannot
exclude appreciable miscalibration, and SBC is by construction blind to the failure mode at issue here:
a posterior that returns the prior passes it exactly. The result is deliberately modest and
honestly bounded: with a single detection the beaming
fraction is barely updated from the prior (posterior $f_{\rm beam} = \svbRealFbeam$, 90\% CI
$\svbRealFbeamLo$--$\svbRealFbeamHi$, a width \svbRealFbeamWidthRatio\ of the prior's), and the
luminosity-function break is not constrained at all: re-inferring under a widened prior box moves
its median by \svbPriorLogLShift\ dex (\svbPriorLogLPub\ $\to$ \svbPriorLogLWide) against a
seed-to-seed scatter of \svbPriorLogLSeedSd, so the published value was reporting where the box
was closed. The data support a one-sided statement only, $\log_{10} L^* \gtrsim \svbLogLLimit$,
set by the single detection's own luminosity. We claim not a measurement of the beaming fraction
but the first calibrated framework for it: the deliverable is the SBC-validated pipeline, not a
tight number. The pipeline conditions on any RACS Stokes-V target list and sharpens directly
as the censuses grow.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A minority of M dwarfs produce bright, highly circularly-polarized, coherent radio emission
(electron-cyclotron maser or plasma emission), and wide-field Stokes-V surveys (LOFAR
\citep{callingham2021}, RACS \citep{pritchard2021}, and the Sydney Radio Star Catalogue
\citep{driessen2024}) have turned it into a population science. But the two numbers that
govern how many such emitters a survey should find, the radio luminosity function and the
fraction of emitters whose beam sweeps across us, have never been inferred with a calibrated
posterior; they enter the literature as assumed or order-of-magnitude values. Simulation-based
inference is the natural tool: the forward problem (draw a population, observe it through a
survey's real selection) is easy to simulate but has no tractable likelihood, exactly the
setting where neural posterior estimation \citep{papamakarios2016,sbi_toolkit} excels. SBI has
been applied to pulsar and magnetar populations \citep{graber2024,pardoaraujo2024,sautron2025}
but not to coherent stellar emitters. We build that inference on a RACS Stokes-V census and
report its honest, wide uncertainties.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Forward model}
A population is parameterized by
$\theta = (\mathrm{slope}, \log_{10} L^*, f_{\rm beam})$: each star in the real parent sample is
assigned a coherent luminosity drawn from $dN/dL \propto L^{-\mathrm{slope}}$ with an exponential
break at $L^*$ (a Schechter-like taper), and beams toward us with probability $f_{\rm beam}$ (a
non-beaming star contributes zero flux). The observed Stokes-V flux is $S = L/4\pi d^2$ at the
star's Gaia DR3 distance; we add Gaussian noise at each target's measured V rms over two epochs
(keeping the brighter, as a variability census does) and call a source detected if $|V|$ clears
both $5\sigma$ and the target's instrumental-leakage floor. The distances (Gaia parallaxes),
noise, and leakage floor are the real per-target values from the merged \code{stokesv\_discovery}
census; the leakage floor is the selection function, reused from that slice. Unresolved binaries
whose forced photometry returns identical fluxes at both epochs (the RACS beam does not resolve
the $\sim$2\arcsec\ GJ 65 pair) are deduplicated to one physical source, so the detection count
that drives the beaming inference is not inflated. Each simulation is reduced to a four-number
summary (detection count, brightest and summed detected $|V|$, detected fraction).

\subsection{Inference}
We train a neural posterior estimator (masked autoregressive flow,
\code{sbi} 0.26; \citealt{sbi_toolkit}) on \svbRealNSims\ prior draws, conditioning on the summary
of the real census. Training runs unchanged on a consumer AMD GPU under ROCm, since \code{sbi} is
pure PyTorch. Two validations gate the result. Forward-model recover-a-knowns confirm the
detection count rises monotonically with both $f_{\rm beam}$ and $L^*$ (the pipeline is
sensitive to what it claims to infer). Simulation-based calibration
\citep[SBC;][]{talts2018}, which ranks each of 150 injected true $\theta$ within its own
posterior, confirms the ranks are uniform (max KS distance from uniform $= \svbRealKsMax$
across the three parameters, below the formal 95\% Kolmogorov critical value $\svbRealKsCrit$ at
150 trials), so the posterior's credible intervals cover at their nominal rate. Only a calibrated
posterior is worth reporting; SBC is the gate.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
The census contributes \svbRealNDet\ confident Stokes-V detection among \svbRealNTargets\ M dwarfs
(the single unresolved GJ 65 system). Conditioned on that, the posterior is calibrated
(SBC passes the formal threshold) and, as expected for one detection, mostly prior-wide.
\begin{itemize}
\item Beaming fraction: $f_{\rm beam} = \svbRealFbeam$, 90\% credible interval
$\svbRealFbeamLo$--$\svbRealFbeamHi$, against a prior of
$\mathcal{U}(\svbPriorFbeamLo, \svbPriorFbeamHi)$. The interval is
\svbRealFbeamWidthRatio\ of the prior's width, and the median sits near the prior midpoint: a
single detection barely updates the beaming prior. Note that this parameter is not the beaming
fraction alone: the forward model draws a luminosity for every star in the parent sample and
then applies one Bernoulli trial, so the inferred quantity is the product of the
coherent-emitter fraction, the beaming fraction, and the duty cycle over the two epochs.
The census cannot separate the three. We therefore claim the first calibrated constraint on
this quantity (the interval's coverage is validated) while stating plainly that it is
near-vacuous, not a measurement. It is consistent with the tens-of-percent values the
literature assumes \citep{callingham2021,pritchard2021}.
\item Luminosity function: the break appears better constrained,
$\log_{10}L^* = \svbRealLogLbreak$ (90\% CI $\svbRealLogLbreakLo$--$\svbRealLogLbreakHi$), but
it is not. Its posterior piles against the top of the prior box, and the test for that is to
move the box: re-inferring with the break prior extended to $\svbPriorBoxHi$ and the slope
floor dropped below its theoretical value, over \svbPriorNSeeds\ training seeds each, moves the
median from \svbPriorLogLPub\ to \svbPriorLogLWide, a shift of \svbPriorLogLShift\ dex
against a seed-to-seed scatter of \svbPriorLogLSeedSd. The median tracks the wall. What the
census actually supports is the one-sided statement $\log_{10}L^* \gtrsim \svbLogLLimit$, which
is set by the single detection's own luminosity and is stable under both tests.

The same experiment moves $f_{\rm beam}$ from \svbPriorFbeamPub\ to \svbPriorFbeamWide. The
beaming posterior is therefore not merely prior-wide but prior-located: it responds to priors
on parameters other than its own, the beaming--luminosity degeneracy, visible directly in this
shift. Only the slope median survives the widening (\svbPriorSlopePub\ $\to$
\svbPriorSlopeWide, against a seed scatter of \svbPriorSlopeSeedSd), though its lower credible
bound was itself truncated by the floor at $\svbPriorSlopeLo$ and is not meaningful either.

With a single detection the population cannot separate ``a few luminous beamers'' from ``many
faint ones''. The honest reading is that no parameter of this model is measured by one
detection; what the census delivers is a lower limit on $L^*$ and a calibrated framework.
\end{itemize}
The scientific content is deliberately modest and honestly bounded: a one-detection census
yields a posterior that is calibrated but, for the beaming fraction, near-prior-width. The value
is the calibration itself, in that the credible intervals mean what they say, and the pipeline,
which conditions on any RACS Stokes-V target list and tightens as the censuses grow.

\section{Discussion and Conclusions}
We have built and calibrated the first SBI population inference for M-dwarf coherent radio
emitters, delivering the first SBC-validated framework for their beaming fraction
($f_{\rm beam} = \svbRealFbeam$, 90\% CI $\svbRealFbeamLo$--$\svbRealFbeamHi$, near-prior-width
at one detection, a calibrated constraint rather than a measurement). The population is
beaming-degenerate at the one-detection level and awaits a larger census. Two extensions are
immediate and require only more data through the same pipeline. The RACS-low2 Stokes-V
catalogue and the Sydney Radio Star Catalogue \citep{driessen2024} supply hundreds of targets,
which would break the degeneracy and turn the beaming interval into a percent-level measurement.
The forward model also extends unchanged to other coherent-emitter classes (ultracool dwarfs,
chromospherically active binaries), given their parent samples and survey selection. The
contribution is the calibrated method and its honest uncertainty widths, not a tight number.
