\title{The Inner Milky Way Rotation Curve from Public LAB HI~21\,cm Tangent-Point
Velocities, Validated Against the VGPS Terminal-Velocity Curve}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We measure the inner Milky Way rotation curve from \hiNlong\ Galactic-longitude $(b,v)$ slices of
the Leiden/Argentine/Bonn (LAB) HI survey \citep{kalberla2005}, spaced \hiStep\arcdeg, taking the
terminal velocity of each $b=0$ profile with two estimators on the same spectra: a fixed 2~K
brightness-temperature threshold, and an error-function fit to the profile edge. Beyond the
bar-dominated region, over the \hiRealNflat\ points from $R\approx\hiRealRmin$ to
$\hiRealRmax$~kpc, the rotation speed is $V=\hiRealVflat$~km\,s$^{-1}$ (point-to-point scatter
\hiRealVflatScatter, standard error of the mean \hiRealVflatSem), far from the Keplerian decline
that would reach \hiRealKepler~km\,s$^{-1}$ at the outermost point. It is not flat: the fitted
slope is $\hiRealSlope\pm\hiRealSlopeErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$ ($\hiRealSlopeSigma\sigma$).
Against the independently measured VGPS curve of \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}, at the
\hiRealRefN\ longitudes the two surveys share, the edge fit agrees to
$\hiRealRefEdgeOffset\pm\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ and gives a slope of
$\hiRealSlopeEdge\pm\hiRealSlopeEdgeErr$ against their $\hiRealRefSlope\pm\hiRealRefSlopeErr$, so
an all-sky single-dish survey reproduces an interferometric one in level and in shape. The two
estimators differ by \hiRealThrMinusEdge~km\,s$^{-1}$, since the threshold crossing sits on the
profile wing; the edge fit brings the level to \hiRealVflatEdge~km\,s$^{-1}$, within
$\hiRealExcessPctEdge$\% of the \citet{reid2019} $V_0=\hiVzero$~km\,s$^{-1}$, so the threshold
estimator's $\hiRealExcessPct$\% excess is a systematic measured on this data rather than a cited
one. Geometry follows $R = R_0\sin\ell$, $V(R) = v_\mathrm{term} + V_0\sin\ell$ with
$R_0=\hiRzero$~kpc. An inner-Galaxy non-Keplerian curve is not by itself a dark-matter detection.
This is a tooling and reproducibility contribution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
That spiral-galaxy rotation curves stay flat to large radii, rather than declining with the visible
mass, is one of the cornerstones of the dark-matter case. For our own Galaxy the inner rotation curve
is measured from the HI~21\,cm line by the tangent-point (terminal-velocity) method
\citep{mccluregriffiths2007}: along an inner sightline the gas at the subcentral point moves fastest
along the line of sight, marking the high-velocity edge of the profile. We test whether this classic
measurement is reproducible from public survey data (each $(b,v)$ slice is a sub-megabyte file) with
a small, tested tool whose core estimator and geometry fit in a few dozen lines, and we check the
answer against an independent survey rather than against a textbook expectation:
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} publish a uniformly measured first-quadrant terminal-velocity curve
from the VLA Galactic Plane Survey, sampled far more finely than ours, which serves as the
comparand throughout. This is a validation, not a new measurement.

\section{Method}
\code{hi} is a pure-NumPy/astropy module (on the \code{jansky} helpers; \citealt{jansky}). For each
of the \hiNlong\ longitudes $\ell$, spaced \hiStep\arcdeg, it reads the LAB $(b,v)$ slice, takes the
$b=0$ spectrum, and estimates the terminal velocity two ways on the same spectrum: (i) the threshold estimator, the most positive LSR
velocity with brightness temperature above 2~K (a conventional choice; sweeping the threshold over
1.5--5~K moves the flat mean only \hiRealSweepLoThr\ to \hiRealSweepHiThr~km\,s$^{-1}$, small
against the estimator systematic below); and (ii) the edge estimator, an error-function fit
$T(v)=\frac{A}{2}\,\mathrm{erfc}\!\big((v-v_0)/\sqrt{2}w\big)+c$ to the profile's falling edge,
whose half-height velocity $v_0$ sits inside the edge rather than out on its wing, in the spirit of
the edge fitting of \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}. In \hiRealNNoncontig\ of the \hiNlong\ LAB spectra the
emission above 2~K breaks into more than one velocity run, so for those sightlines the threshold
crossing may sit on a detached local-emission island rather than on the top edge of the main
emission; the edge fit, which is seeded there but then fits the profile itself, is the estimator we
quote. The edge fit fails to converge on \hiRealNEdgeFail\ sightline, which is dropped from the edge
curve. The tangent point then gives
\begin{equation}
R = R_0\sin\ell, \qquad V(R) = v_\mathrm{term} + V_0\sin\ell,
\end{equation}
with $R_0=\hiRzero$~kpc and $V_0=\hiVzero$~km\,s$^{-1}$ from \citet{reid2019} (their best-fit solar
circular speed, not the older IAU 220~km\,s$^{-1}$). LAB velocities are tabulated in the kinematic
LSR frame, which uses the standard solar motion; the \citet{reid2019} constants are tied to their
fitted solar motion, and the residual frame mismatch injects a few km\,s$^{-1}$ at $\sim\sin\ell$,
small against the estimator terms tracked below. A synthetic $(\ell,v)$ slice with a known injected
flat curve and realistic edge widths (3--8~km\,s$^{-1}$, spanning the range LAB shows) drives the
offline tests: the fixture reproduces the threshold estimator's wing overshoot
(\hiSynThrMinusEdge~km\,s$^{-1}$ against \hiRealThrMinusEdge\ measured on LAB) while the edge
estimator recovers the injected curve to \hiSynRefEdgeOffset~km\,s$^{-1}$. The offline leg runs the
same comparison machinery as the real one, against the injected curve in place of the VGPS curve, so
that code is exercised without a network.

\paragraph{Comparison with the VGPS curve.} We compare our terminal velocities against
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} Table~1: \hiRealRefN\ of our longitudes fall inside their
$18.4\arcdeg < \ell < 67.0\arcdeg$ coverage with enough samples to average. Their curve is sampled
every $0.065\arcdeg$, far finer than the LAB beam, so each of our longitudes is compared against the
mean of their values within $0.5\arcdeg$, comparable to the $36\arcmin$ LAB beam; longitudes
outside their coverage are dropped, never extrapolated. The comparison is made on $v_\mathrm{term}$ itself rather than on
$V(R)$, so no $R_0$ or $V_0$ choice enters it and the two surveys are compared on what each
measured. Their estimator is of the same family as our edge fit (a sum of two error functions,
seeded from a 20~K threshold crossing on continuum-masked, latitude-averaged spectra), so this is
a cross-survey check of a shared method, not a comparison of independent methods.

\section{Data}
We use the LAB survey \citep{kalberla2005}, the all-sky combination of the Leiden/Dwingeloo and
Instituto Argentino de Radioastronom\'ia surveys with stray-radiation correction, retrieved as
per-longitude $(b,v)$ FITS slices from VizieR. We sample \hiNlong\ longitudes from $\ell=10\arcdeg$ to
$80\arcdeg$ in the first quadrant, spaced \hiStep\arcdeg, and read the mid-plane ($b=0$) spectrum of
each. For the comparison of \S\ref{sec:results} we use the tabulated VGPS terminal velocities of
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016}, also from VizieR (748 rows over
$18.4\arcdeg < \ell < 67.0\arcdeg$).

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Table~\ref{tab:curve} lists the curve at $5\arcdeg$ intervals under both estimators (the full
\hiNlong-point curve is in the committed results file). Beyond the bar-dominated region
($R\lesssim\hiRealRmin$~kpc, dropped), the \hiRealNflat\ points from $R\approx\hiRealRmin$ to
$\hiRealRmax$~kpc give $V=\hiRealVflat$~km\,s$^{-1}$ on the threshold estimator, where the
point-to-point scatter is \hiRealVflatScatter~km\,s$^{-1}$ and the standard error of the mean
\hiRealVflatSem\ (Figure~\ref{fig:rc}). The level is insensitive to the hand-chosen analysis
parameters: sweeping the threshold 1.5--5~K moves the mean from \hiRealSweepLoThr\ to
\hiRealSweepHiThr, dropping the innermost point beyond the cut gives \hiRealDropInner, and dropping
the two outermost points (the longitudes where local emission blends most with the terminal edge)
gives \hiRealDropOuterTwo~km\,s$^{-1}$. All variants are committed with the results.

\paragraph{The curve is non-Keplerian, but it is not flat.} A Keplerian $V\propto R^{-1/2}$
normalised at the innermost point beyond the bar cut would fall to \hiRealKepler~km\,s$^{-1}$ at the
outermost; the measured value stays $\sim$90~km\,s$^{-1}$ above it, which is the dark-matter-relevant
contrast and is not in doubt. The curve is nonetheless rising: a least-squares slope over the
\hiRealNflat\ points gives $\hiRealSlope\pm\hiRealSlopeErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$
($\hiRealSlopeSigma\sigma$) on the threshold estimator and
$\hiRealSlopeEdge\pm\hiRealSlopeEdgeErr$ ($\hiRealSlopeEdgeSigma\sigma$) on the edge fit. This
supersedes an earlier version of this analysis which sampled only eight longitudes and reported a
slope consistent with zero; that sample was too small to resolve a rise this gentle, and the
conclusion of flatness was a statement about its power, not about the Galaxy. The reference curve
agrees: \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}, on their own \hiRealRefSlopeN\ points beyond the same cut, give
$\hiRealRefSlope\pm\hiRealRefSlopeErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$. That the inner curve is
curved rather than flat is not itself new. \citet{reiddame2016} show that fitting a flat
curve to HI terminal velocities biases the inferred $\Theta_0$ for exactly this reason, and
\citet{davis2026} examine what terminal-velocity curves do and do not recover in simulated
galaxies. What is corrected here is this analysis's own earlier claim.

\paragraph{Cross-survey validation.} Over the \hiRealRefN\ overlapping longitudes the edge-fit
terminal velocities sit
$\hiRealRefEdgeOffset\pm\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ from the VGPS values
(scatter \hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$), so an all-sky single-dish survey with a $36\arcmin$
beam reproduces an interferometric survey's terminal-velocity curve at the km\,s$^{-1}$ level, in
both normalisation and slope. The threshold estimator does not: it sits
$\hiRealRefThrOffset\pm\hiRealRefThrOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ high, with a scatter of
\hiRealRefThrOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$ that is itself large, so the offset is not a constant that
could be subtracted. That gap is the wing overshoot and it closes as the threshold climbs the profile
edge: raising it to the 20~K that \citet{mccluregriffiths2016} seed their own fit from leaves
\hiRealSweepRefTwenty~km\,s$^{-1}$. Consistent with the error-function picture, the per-sightline
overshoot grows with the fitted edge width (slope \hiRealWidthSlope, correlation
$r=\hiRealWidthCorr$ across the \hiNlong\ sightlines). The relation is real but loose, since the
fitted width is itself noisy.

\section{Limitations}
\paragraph{The normalization is an estimator statement, measured here.} The threshold curve sits
$\hiRealExcessPct$\% above $V_0=\hiVzero$; the edge fit, run on the same spectra, sits at
\hiRealVflatEdge~km\,s$^{-1}$ ($\hiRealExcessPctEdge$\%). The difference,
\hiRealThrMinusEdge~km\,s$^{-1}$ on the sample beyond the bar cut, is the wing overshoot of a
threshold crossing on a finite-width edge, reproduced by the synthetic fixture
(\hiSynThrMinusEdge~km\,s$^{-1}$) and confirmed against the VGPS curve above. Note this is measured
in-house rather than imported: the $\sim$7~km\,s$^{-1}$ offset in \citet{mccluregriffiths2016} is
between the \citet{clemens1985} CO terminal curve and their fitted HI curve (a cross-tracer,
cross-survey comparison they attribute only partly to the threshold method) and is not a
substitute for the comparison done here. The residual $\sim$$\hiRealExcessPctEdge$\% under the edge
estimator is within the streaming-motion and frame-convention terms below. The quoted
\hiRealVflatScatter~km\,s$^{-1}$ is point-to-point scatter, orthogonal to this shared systematic:
an estimator bias moves all \hiRealNflat\ points together and leaves the scatter unchanged.
\paragraph{The cross-survey check is not a check of independent methods.} Our edge fit and the
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} estimator are both error-function fits to the profile edge, so their
agreement bounds survey-to-survey and pipeline-to-pipeline differences (beam, sampling,
continuum treatment, absorption masking) and does not test the choice of estimator family. Both
would move together under a different definition of the terminal velocity.
\paragraph{Circular orbits assumed.} $V = v_\mathrm{term} + V_0\sin\ell$ assumes the tangent-point gas
is on a circular orbit; spiral-arm and bar-driven streaming biases individual points and cannot be
removed without a model.
\paragraph{Bar region excluded.} Points at $R\lesssim\hiRealRmin$~kpc are dominated by the bar's
non-circular motions and are dropped from the mean and the slope fit. The fitted slope is a
description of $V(R)$ over $\hiRealRmin < R < \hiRealRmax$~kpc under a straight-line model, not a
physical parameter; a real rotation curve is not a line, and the value would change with the fitted
range.
\paragraph{Not, by itself, a dark-matter detection.} An inner flat curve is consistent with a
dark halo but can also arise from the disk's own mass; the unambiguous case needs the curve beyond
$R\approx R_0$, where the visible-mass contribution falls, a regime requiring a different method
and not covered here. The result is also first-quadrant, $b=0$ only, and the tangent-point method
applies solely for $R<R_0$.

\section{Discussion and reproducibility}
This is a validation: a small, tested, offline-capable tool recovers the inner Milky Way rotation
curve from public LAB HI data, with the Keplerian alternative excluded by $\sim$90~km\,s$^{-1}$ at
the outermost point, the gentle rise resolved and matched to the VGPS curve rather than asserted
flat, the absolute level agreeing with that curve to
$\hiRealRefEdgeOffset\pm\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$, and the normalization traced to a
measured estimator systematic rather than a citation. The practical consequence for anyone reading
terminal velocities off an all-sky survey is that LAB is adequate to the km\,s$^{-1}$ level provided
the profile edge is fitted; a fixed low brightness-temperature threshold costs
$\hiRealRefThrOffset$~km\,s$^{-1}$ and the cost varies from sightline to sightline. The analysis
regenerates from a clean checkout, and every number traces to the pipeline-written macros and
table \code{\textbackslash input}ted at the top of the source.
