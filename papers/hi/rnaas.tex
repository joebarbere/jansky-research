\title{The LAB and VGPS HI Terminal-Velocity Curves Agree to 1~km\,s$^{-1}$ When the Profile
Edge Is Fitted}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We compare inner-Galaxy HI terminal velocities measured from the all-sky Leiden/Argentine/Bonn
(LAB) survey against the VLA Galactic Plane Survey curve of \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}, at the
\hiRealRefN\ first-quadrant longitudes the two cover in common. An error-function fit to the
profile edge agrees with the interferometric curve to
$\hiRealRefEdgeOffset\pm\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$, with a slope of
$\hiRealSlopeEdge\pm\hiRealSlopeEdgeErr$ against their
$\hiRealRefSlope\pm\hiRealRefSlopeErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$. A fixed 2~K
brightness-temperature threshold on the same spectra sits
$\hiRealRefThrOffset\pm\hiRealRefThrOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ high, with a scatter of
\hiRealRefThrOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$ that rules out a constant correction. The gap closes as the
threshold climbs the profile edge.
\end{abstract}

\section{}

Terminal velocities at the tangent point remain the standard route to the inner Milky Way
rotation curve \citep{mccluregriffiths2007}, and what a terminal velocity is depends on how the
high-velocity edge of the HI profile is reduced to one number. \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}
fit that edge with a sum of two error functions and publish a uniformly measured first-quadrant
curve from the VLA Galactic Plane Survey, warning in the same paper that any threshold method
is subject to errors. The size of those errors on an all-sky single-dish survey has not been
measured against their curve, and neither has the agreement. Both are worth knowing, because
LAB \citep{kalberla2005} covers the whole sky at sub-megabyte cost per sightline and the VGPS
does not.

We read the $b=0$ spectrum of \hiNlong\ LAB $(b,v)$ slices from $\ell=10\arcdeg$ to
$80\arcdeg$, spaced \hiStep\arcdeg, and estimate the terminal velocity of each twice on the
same spectrum: the most positive velocity with $T_B$ above 2~K, and the half-height velocity
$v_0$ of an error-function fit $T(v)=\frac{A}{2}\mathrm{erfc}((v-v_0)/\sqrt{2}w)+c$ to the
falling edge. \hiRealRefN\ of these longitudes fall inside the $18.4\arcdeg<\ell<67.0\arcdeg$
coverage of \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}, whose curve is sampled every $0.065\arcdeg$; each is
compared against the mean of their values within one LAB half-beam ($0.5\arcdeg$), and
longitudes outside their coverage are dropped rather than extrapolated. The comparison is made
on $v_\mathrm{term}$ itself, so no $R_0$ or $V_0$ enters it.

The edge fit reproduces the interferometric curve
(Figure~\ref{fig:cmp}, left): the mean difference is
$\hiRealRefEdgeOffset\pm\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ with a scatter of
\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$, and the result is insensitive to the matching window,
moving between \hiRealRefWindowMin\ and \hiRealRefWindowMax\ over half-widths of $0.25\arcdeg$ to $1.0\arcdeg$ under
either a mean or a median. The shapes agree as well as the levels. Converting to
$V(R)=v_\mathrm{term}+V_0\sin\ell$ with $R_0=\hiRzero$~kpc and $V_0=\hiVzero$~km\,s$^{-1}$
\citep{reid2019} and fitting beyond the bar-dominated region, LAB gives
$\hiRealSlopeEdge\pm\hiRealSlopeEdgeErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$ against the
$\hiRealRefSlope\pm\hiRealRefSlopeErr$ of the reference curve on its own \hiRealRefSlopeN\ points. That the
inner curve rises rather than staying flat is not new \citep{reiddame2016,davis2026}; that a
$0.5\arcdeg$ beam recovers the rise measured at $1\arcmin$ is the point.

The 2~K threshold does not reproduce it. Those terminal velocities sit
$\hiRealRefThrOffset\pm\hiRealRefThrOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ above the reference, and the
scatter of \hiRealRefThrOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$ is large enough that no single subtracted offset
would repair them. The excess is the overshoot of a threshold crossing on a finite-width edge,
and it behaves accordingly (Figure~\ref{fig:cmp}, right): raising the threshold from 1.5~K to
40~K walks the mean offset from \hiRealSweepRefLo\ to \hiRealSweepRefHi~km\,s$^{-1}$, passing
\hiRealSweepRefTwenty~km\,s$^{-1}$ at the 20~K that \citet{mccluregriffiths2016} seed their own
fit from. Per sightline the overshoot grows with the fitted edge width, at
\hiRealWidthSlope~km\,s$^{-1}$ per km\,s$^{-1}$ of width, though the correlation is loose
($r=\hiRealWidthCorr$) because the fitted width is itself noisy.

Two limits bound this. The \citet{mccluregriffiths2016} estimator and ours are both
error-function fits to the profile edge, so the agreement bounds survey and pipeline
differences (beam, sampling, absorption and continuum treatment) and says nothing about the
choice of estimator family, which would move both together. And the LAB spectra are not
uniformly clean: in \hiRealNNoncontig\ of the \hiNlong\ the emission above 2~K breaks into more
than one velocity run, so the threshold crossing there may sit on a detached local-emission
island, and the edge fit fails to converge on \hiRealNEdgeFail. Both counts are small, and both
are the kind of defect an eight-longitude sample hides.
