\title{The LAB and VGPS HI Terminal-Velocity Curves Agree to One Velocity Channel When the
Profile Edge Is Fitted}

\author[0009-0008-3289-4447]{Joseph Barbere}
\affiliation{Independent researcher}

\begin{abstract}
We compare inner-Galaxy HI terminal velocities from the all-sky Leiden/Argentine/Bonn (LAB)
survey against the VLA Galactic Plane Survey curve of \citet{mccluregriffiths2016}, at the
\hiRealRefN\ first-quadrant longitudes the two cover in common. Fitting the profile edge with an
error function, as they do, the two agree to
$\hiRealRefEdgeOffset\pm\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSemCorr$~km\,s$^{-1}$, one LAB channel, and vary
by \hiRealEstSysSpan~km\,s$^{-1}$ across reasonable choices of the fit itself. Over the same
longitudes the curves have the same slope, $\hiRealMatchSlope\pm\hiRealMatchSlopeErr$ against
$\hiRealMatchSlopeRef\pm\hiRealMatchSlopeRefErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$. A fixed 2~K
brightness-temperature threshold on the same spectra sits
$\hiRealRefThrOffset\pm\hiRealRefThrOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ high, with a scatter of
\hiRealRefThrOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$ that rules out a constant correction.
\end{abstract}

\section{}

Terminal velocities at the tangent point remain the standard route to the inner Milky Way
rotation curve \citep{mccluregriffiths2007}, and what a terminal velocity is depends on how the
high-velocity edge of the HI profile is reduced to one number. \citet{mccluregriffiths2016} fit
that edge with a sum of two error functions and publish a uniformly measured first-quadrant
curve from the VLA Galactic Plane Survey, warning in the same paper that any threshold method is
subject to errors. The size of those errors on an all-sky single-dish survey has not been
measured against their curve, and neither has the agreement. Both are worth knowing, because LAB
\citep{kalberla2005} covers the whole sky at sub-megabyte cost per sightline and the VGPS does
not. Small-telescope tangent-point curves are measured against textbook expectations rather than
against a survey \citep{lihu2024}.

We read the $b=0$ spectrum of \hiNlong\ LAB $(b,v)$ slices from $\ell=10\arcdeg$ to $80\arcdeg$,
spaced \hiStep\arcdeg, at the survey's $36\arcmin$ resolution and \hiRealChannel~km\,s$^{-1}$
channels, and estimate the terminal velocity of each twice on the same spectrum: the most
positive velocity with $T_B$ above 2~K, and the half-height velocity $v_0$ of an error-function
fit $T(v)=\frac{A}{2}\mathrm{erfc}((v-v_0)/\sqrt{2}w)+c$ to the falling edge. \hiRealRefN\ of
these longitudes fall inside the $18.4\arcdeg<\ell<67.0\arcdeg$ coverage of the
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} table (VizieR J/ApJ/831/124), whose curve is sampled every
$0.065\arcdeg$; each is compared against the mean of their values within $0.5\arcdeg$, and
longitudes outside their coverage are dropped rather than extrapolated. The comparison is made
on $v_\mathrm{term}$ itself, so no $R_0$ or $V_0$ enters it.

The edge fit reproduces the interferometric curve (Figure~\ref{fig:cmp}, left). The mean
difference is \hiRealRefEdgeOffset~km\,s$^{-1}$, one LAB channel, with a scatter of
\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$; the residuals are correlated between neighbouring
sightlines (lag-1 \hiRealResidAuto), which puts the standard error at
\hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSemCorr\ rather than the \hiRealRefEdgeOffsetSem\ an independent-sample
count would give. Three choices inside our own estimator matter more than that. Widening or
narrowing the fitted window, or averaging over $|b|<0.5\arcdeg$ as
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} do instead of taking the single $b=0$ row, moves the offset across
a range of \hiRealEstSysSpan~km\,s$^{-1}$. The agreement is therefore at the one-channel level
and no better, and the \hiRealRefEdgeOffset\ is not a resolved offset between the surveys. The
residual does drift with longitude, at
$\hiRealResidTrend\pm\hiRealResidTrendErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$ per degree, which is unexplained.

The shapes agree as well as the levels, provided both are measured over the same radii. Our
sightlines run to $R=\hiRealRmax$~kpc and the reference stops at \hiRealRefRmax~kpc, so a slope
fitted to each over its own range compares different chords of a curve that has real structure
in it. Fitted over the \hiRealMatchSlopeN\ shared longitudes beyond
$R=\hiRealRmin$~kpc, LAB gives $\hiRealMatchSlope\pm\hiRealMatchSlopeErr$ and the reference
$\hiRealMatchSlopeRef\pm\hiRealMatchSlopeRefErr$~km\,s$^{-1}$\,kpc$^{-1}$. Neither is a stable
property of the Galaxy: moving the inner cut from 3 to 6~kpc swings the fitted slope from
\hiRealRminSweepLo\ to \hiRealRminSweepHi, consistent with the $\sim$2~kpc undulation
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} report. It swings the reference identically
(\hiRealRminSweepRefLo\ to \hiRealRminSweepRefHi), which is the result here. That the inner
curve rises rather than staying flat is not new \citep{reiddame2016,davis2026}.

The 2~K threshold reproduces neither. Those terminal velocities sit
$\hiRealRefThrOffset\pm\hiRealRefThrOffsetSem$~km\,s$^{-1}$ above the reference, and the scatter
of \hiRealRefThrOffsetSd~km\,s$^{-1}$ is large enough that no single subtracted offset would
repair them. The excess is the overshoot of a threshold crossing on a finite-width edge, and it
behaves accordingly (Figure~\ref{fig:cmp}, right): raising the threshold from 1.5~K to 40~K
walks the mean offset from \hiRealSweepRefLo\ to \hiRealSweepRefHi~km\,s$^{-1}$, reaching
\hiRealSweepRefTwenty~km\,s$^{-1}$ at 20~K. A residual there is expected, since
\citet{mccluregriffiths2016} use their 20~K crossing only to seed a fit and publish the fitted
value. Per sightline the overshoot grows with the fitted edge width, at
\hiRealWidthSlope~km\,s$^{-1}$ per km\,s$^{-1}$ of width, loosely ($r=\hiRealWidthCorr$). It
does not leak into the estimator we validated: the edge-fit residual is uncorrelated with
fitted width, $r=\hiRealEdgeWidthCorr$.

Two limits bound this. The \citet{mccluregriffiths2016} estimator and ours are both
error-function fits to a profile edge, so the agreement bounds survey and pipeline differences
and says nothing about the choice of estimator family, which would move both together. And the
LAB spectra are not uniformly clean: \hiRealNDefective\ of the \hiNlong\ have emission above 2~K
in more than one velocity run or no converged edge fit. One of those falls inside the compared
sample, so the agreement above is essentially defect-free; three fall in the outer sightlines
the reference cannot check, and dropping all of them moves the full-range slope to
$\hiRealSlopeDropDefective\pm\hiRealSlopeDropDefectiveErr$.
