# Mechanical triage of all 46 papers (2026-08-13)

Deep refereeing costs about a session per paper and had covered 7. `scripts/triage_papers.py`
is the cheap first pass over the remaining 39: it checks only for failure modes detectable
without understanding the science, each corresponding to a defect found in a real paper this
month. Run it with `uv run python scripts/triage_papers.py`.

Validated before use against a deliberately broken fixture — all five detectors fire.

## What it checks, and why

| check | the defect it is for |
|---|---|
| `cited-placeholder` | a macro rendering as `--` that the prose cites, so the sentence reads as a finished claim with the number gone (four papers shipped this) |
| `unnamespaced` | a mode-dependent macro emitted outside the Syn/Real loop, so an offline rebuild writes a synthetic value under a real name — a *wrong* number, invisible to the `--` guard (`\tiiNEvents`, `\svbNTargets`) |
| `hard-typed` | a prose number equal to a committed macro's value, i.e. one that will drift silently on the next run |
| `bad-doi` | a DOI whose Crossref title does not match the `.bib` title (two already found pointing at entirely different papers) |
| `bare-author` | `author = {others}`, which renders as "others. 2025, arXiv e-prints" |
| `no-evidence` | results file missing, unparseable, or `is_real: false` |
| `overclaim` | a verb this repo has had to retract, near a number |

## DOI sweep outcome (2026-08-13)

Attempted with automated Crossref matching. **Only 2 of 16 could be corrected safely.**

The first attempt used the same containment metric as the triage's detector (`|A∩B| / min(|A|,|B|)`)
at a 0.85 threshold, and it was badly unsafe — it scores 1.00 whenever the shorter title's
tokens are a subset of the longer one. Its "unambiguous" proposals included an **Oxford
English Dictionary entry for the word *Saturn*** offered as the replacement for a Saturn
kilometric radiation paper, and a book chapter titled *"4. The interpretation of radio bursts"*
for a type III burst paper. Applying those would have been far worse than leaving the wrong
DOIs in place.

Requiring **all three** of near-identical title (Jaccard ≥ 0.75, symmetric), first-author
surname present in the Crossref author list, and year within ±1 leaves exactly two, both
adjacent-DOI typos, both then verified by direct lookup:

| entry | was | is | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `skr/provan2019` | `10.1029/2018JA026349` | `10.1029/2018JA026079` | Provan, Lamy, Cowley, Bunce; JGR Space Physics **124**, 1157–1172 (2019). The old DOI resolved to *"A Revised Look at Relativistic Electrons in the Earth's Inner Radiation Zone"*. |
| `vlass/rowlinson2019` | `10.1016/j.ascom.2019.03.004` | `10.1016/j.ascom.2019.03.003` | Rowlinson, Stewart, Broderick, Swinbank; Astronomy and Computing **27**, 111–129 (2019). Exact title match; the old DOI is the adjacent article in the same issue. |

Both entries carry a comment recording the correction and its evidence.

**Note the asymmetry this exposes:** the triage's containment metric is right for *detecting*
a mismatch (it is deliberately permissive, so it does not flag markup variants) and wrong for
*confirming* a match. Detection and repair need different thresholds.


## Resolution (2026-08-13, overnight round 2): all 19 HIGH findings cleared

The automated sweep had stalled at 2 of 16 DOIs because fuzzy title search cannot confirm a
match. The unlock was using the coordinates already recorded in each entry — I wrote these
`.bib` entries, so the author/journal/volume/pages fields identify the intended paper even
where the title or DOI is wrong. Procedure per entry: pull the current DOI's full Crossref
metadata, compare against the bib's coordinates, and adjudicate **which field is the defect**.

That reframing mattered: **five of the fourteen were title-wrong, not DOI-wrong** — the DOI,
authors, volume and pages all agreed, and the transcribed title belonged to a different paper
(e.g. `chime25repeaters` carried the baseband-catalog title over the correct
25-repeaters DOI/coordinates; `fischer2015`'s real title is *"Saturn kilometric radiation
periodicity after equinox"*). A DOI-repair pass that trusted titles would have "corrected"
five right DOIs.

The DOI-wrong cases were mostly **adjacent-identifier errors** — the recorded DOI is the
article next to the intended one (Ho et al. at ApJ 541, 120 beside Taylor at 112; Koza at
SoPh 292:98 beside the intended; Riggi at PASA e085 beside Driessen's e084; Suto & Fujita at
10.1086/169090 beside Vallée's 169089; Rowlinson `.004` beside `.003`). The DOIs were
constructed by pattern instead of looked up, which is the durable lesson.

One entry was **author-wrong on top of DOI-wrong**: the "Gurnett et al. 2016" rotational-
modulation paper is Ye et al. 2016 (Gurnett is last author) — the prose in `skr` naming
"Gurnett et al." is corrected to "Ye et al." as well.

The five `author = {others}` entries all carried arXiv IDs in their `note` fields, and the
arXiv API resolves every one (the earlier "blocked" was an unfollowed HTTP→HTTPS redirect).
`racslow2` turned out to be published since the entry was written and now carries its PASA
coordinates (43, e092) and DOI. The previously unverifiable `fashi_groups` is confirmed real:
Yan, Ma, Yu, Fang, He & Zhu, arXiv:2510.22902 — the fashienv novelty claim's citation now
resolves.

Every corrected entry carries an inline comment recording the evidence. All affected papers
recompile with zero undefined citations. **The triage now reports zero HIGH findings across
all 46 papers.** What remains is MED/LOW: hard-typed numbers duplicating macros, absent
offline evidence files for a few tool papers, and flagged verbs — the deep-review queue is
now defined by science content, not by mechanical defects.


## Round 3 (2026-08-13): MED tier cleared — zero HIGH, zero MED remain

**The seven `no-evidence` flags were all naming artifacts.** The evidence exists under
pre-convention names, each verified against the module's own write call: `metrics.json`
(frbstats, the oldest slice), `rotation_curve.json` (hi), `uss_metrics.json` (spectra),
`drift_metrics.json`, `period_metrics.json`, `singlepulse_metrics.json` (torchfdmt),
`ecallisto_metrics.json`. The triage now carries an `EVIDENCE_ALIAS` map rather than the
files being renamed — the modules and their tests read those paths, and renaming committed
evidence for a cosmetic gain would churn real history.

**Twelve `hard-typed` findings adjudicated: nine fixed, three were coincidences.** The nine
real ones (a prose literal duplicating a macro that records the same quantity) are now the
macro: Lamy's refined Uranus period in vgpra, the VLASS match radius and beam in dr20radio,
their ρ_DM = 0.107 in innerrc, both Wind/WAVES band edges, the Rayleigh tail percentage in
offsets, the USS threshold in spectra, the S/WAVES band edge, and the Crab DM in torchfdmt.

The three coincidences are allowlisted **with reasons**, because substituting a macro would
have been wrong, not just unnecessary: pte2's `p<0.05` is the conventional significance
threshold that the synthetic false-positive rate merely happens to equal — writing
`p<\ptSynFP` would claim the threshold was measured; vgpra's `17.24 h` is the Voyager-era
literature constant the synthetic injection was *chosen* to match; dr20radio's `2.5×` is a
flux ratio (3.0/1.2), not the 2.5″ beam. Equality of a number and a macro is not provenance.

**Final state: 18 papers fully clean; 28 carry only LOW `overclaim` verb flags.** Those are
prompts for a science read, not mechanical defects — and several sit in already-reviewed
papers where the flagged verb was kept deliberately (svsbi's "consistent with nominal
coverage" trips the "nominal coverage" pattern that was added *because of* svsbi). The
mechanical debt is settled; anything further is refereeing.
